# The two worksheets

## Quick Performance Analysis Worksheet — nine sections

### Section 1: Unicorn ad identification and analysis

> "Identify the single creative that has maintained the highest performance over the longest period
> while consuming significant ad spend."

**Unicorn ad details:** creative name/ID · format · launch date · days or weeks active · total spend
· overall ROAS/CPA · current status (still running or paused).

**Then dissect it** using the building-blocks framework, in five groups:

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | First 3 seconds description · hook type · hook rate |
| **Messaging and pain points** | Primary pain point or desire addressed · key benefit highlighted · emotional triggers used · objections tackled |
| **Persona and presentation** | Who delivers the message · presentation style (face-to-camera, voiceover, text-only) · tone (authoritative, friendly, urgent) |
| **Format and production** | Length · production quality (high-end, moderate, UGC-style) · visual style (bright, dark, colourful, minimal) · music and sound · text overlay approach |
| **Demonstration and proof** | How the product is demonstrated · social proof elements · results or transformation shown |
| **Call to action** | CTA phrasing · urgency or scarcity tactics · offer presentation |

**Closes with:** *"What specific elements do you believe make this creative your 'unicorn'?"* — three
numbered slots.

⭐ **Those three slots are the deliverable of the whole section.** Everything above them is
description; these are the claim. Each should be an element that could be reproduced in another ad,
not an observation about this one.

### Section 2: 30-day top performers

A table of the top 5 to 10 by primary KPI: creative name/ID · format · KPI result · brief description
· key message · **similarities to unicorn ad**.

Then:
- **Format breakdown** — videos, images, carousels, others, each as a % of top performers
- **Common elements** — visual themes · messaging approaches · creative styles
- **Comparison to unicorn** — elements that match the unicorn's success factors · notable differences
  · potential new success factors identified

### Section 3: 7-day versus 30-day comparison

Per creative: in 30-day top? · in 7-day top? · performance trend (↑/↓/→) · notes on change.

**Creative fatigue assessment:**
- % of 30-day winners still in the 7-day top performers
- Average performance decline in aging creatives (%)
- Newly emerging top performers
- **Fatigue rate comparison: unicorn versus recent ads**

⭐ **The cheapest diagnostic in the course.** Two reports and a subtraction, and it separates a
creative problem (winners decaying) from a scaling problem (winners fine, nothing new arriving).

### Section 4: Hook rate analysis

> **Hook rate = (3-second video views ÷ impressions) × 100%**

Top 5 to 10 by hook rate: creative name/ID · hook rate · primary KPI · **correlation to conversion**
· opening frame description.

**Hook patterns:** most effective visual elements · most effective opening messages · correlation
between hook rate and primary KPI · comparison to the unicorn's hook approach.

⚠️ **The correlation column is the one that matters.** A high hook rate that does not move the KPI
means the opening is stopping the wrong people, and that is a finding, not a failure of the metric.

### Section 5: Hold rate evaluation

> **Hold rate = (ThruPlays ÷ impressions) × 100%**

Top 5 to 10 by hold rate: creative name/ID · hold rate · primary KPI · length · key content
structure.

**Hold rate patterns:** most effective storytelling approaches · most effective pacing and editing
styles · correlation between hold rate and primary KPI.

### Section 6: 6-month trend analysis

How winning approaches have changed · which evergreen concepts held · which formats or styles grew or
declined · seasonal patterns.

### Section 7: Quick wins and action items

Immediate iteration opportunities · creative gaps · red flags · low-hanging fruit.

## In-Depth Creative Audit Worksheet — nine parts

### Part 1: Audit objectives and performance benchmarks

**Brand goals and KPIs:** secondary objectives · **North Star metric for creative success** ·
secondary performance metrics to track.

**Current performance benchmarks**, a table with four rows (hook rate, hold rate, conversion rate,
ROAS/CPA) and four columns: **current average · top performer · bottom performer · industry
benchmark.**

⚠️ **The industry benchmark column is left blank by ACA and the course supplies no numbers for it.**
If you have a defensible source, use it and cite it. If not, leave it empty. **Do not fill it from
memory** — a fabricated benchmark in this cell silently re-rates every creative in the audit.

**Campaign and optimization alignment check** — do the current objectives and optimization settings
match the stated goals?

⛔ **Part 1 comes first because everything inherits it.** An audit run against the wrong North Star
metric is internally consistent and wrong throughout.

### Part 2: Creative format performance analysis

**Format breakdown and performance:** video · image · carousel · collection · other.

**Video length analysis** by band. ⚠️ The course's script gives *0-15s, 15-30s, 30-60s, 60s+*; the
delivered session says *10-15s* for the first band, which leaves a gap. Use 0-15s.

**Creative diversity assessment.**

### Part 3: Creative elements comparison

Three tables, each with columns: **top performer · bottom performer · performance difference ·
insight.**

1. **Visual elements** — face to camera, product close-up, and so on
2. **Messaging elements** — feature-focused, and so on
3. **Hook type performance**, with columns for example and *why it works*: question · surprising
   statement · visual pattern interrupt · problem statement · direct address · other

### Part 4: Audience response patterns

Demographic performance breakdown, top-performing and worst-performing per segment. Then funnel
stage: cold versus warm, prospecting versus retargeting.

### Part 5: Cost effectiveness and lifespan analysis

Production investment against return, and the average effective lifespan by creative type.

⭐ **Lifespan is the number most accounts never calculate**, and refresh cadence is guesswork without
it. *"Do static images last longer than video ads? That usually tends to be the case."*

### Part 6: Landing page and post-click experience

⚠️ **This part exists in the worksheet and is not taught in the recording.** The course covers
creative up to the click. If you fill it, say the framework for it is not in the course.

### Part 7: Strategic insights and action plan

- **3 to 5 creative principles** that should guide the approach
- Optimal creative mix by format and style
- Which elements to standardise: hooks, CTAs
- **A testing plan**: 3 to 5 elements, prioritised by impact and resource, each with a **clear
  hypothesis**
- **An optimization roadmap**: 2 to 3 iteration opportunities per top performer, a refresh schedule
  based on observed fatigue, and improvement targets

## Producing the audit

Deliver as a document with:

1. **The North Star metric and the date range**, at the top. Every number below is relative to them
2. **The filled sections**, in order
3. **The three action outputs** — principles, testing plan with hypotheses, roadmap
4. ⛔ **A "what the data could not tell us" section.** Which sections were left blank, which
   conclusions rest on creative descriptions rather than on the creatives, and any window with too
   few creatives to be meaningful

⚠️ **Mark thin evidence in the section where it sits, not only in the summary.** An audit gets
excerpted into a deck. A conclusion drawn from three creatives needs `⚠️ n=3` next to it, because
that is the only place the caveat survives being copied.
