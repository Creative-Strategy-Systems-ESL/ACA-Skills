# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — three assets shipped with **Course 302: Research for Creative Strategy**,
module 1, taught by **Mirella Crespi** of Creative Milkshake, ACA's founder:

1. **Quick Performance Analysis Worksheet** — 11 pages, 9 sections
2. **In-Depth Creative Audit Worksheet** — 9 parts
3. **AI Prompts for Creative Assistance** — 12 prompts in 3 categories

⭐ **Course 302 is the most asset-heavy course in the programme.** ACA's tracked-download list runs to
46 rows covering 45 distinct titles across 12 of the 26 numbered courses, and **12 of those rows
belong to this course** — more than a quarter of everything ACA ships.

## ⚠️ The prompt pack has two names

The PDF titles itself **"AI Prompts for Creative Assistance"** on every page. ACA's tracked-download
list and the course narration call it **"AI Prompts for Creative Audit Assistance"**. Same document.
Flagged so a search for either finds it.

## ⚠️ The PDFs stamp a different course number than the curriculum does

All three assets footer themselves *"Course 301: Research for Creative Strategy"*. ACA's shipping
curriculum numbers this course **302**; 301 is Zach Murray's Reverse Engineering Winning Ads.

**This is not a filing error.** ACA renumbered for launch and the worksheet PDFs were stamped before
that. The titles agree in both schemes; only the numbers moved. This skill uses **302**.

⚠️ Anyone opening the PDFs will read 301 on every page, so expect to explain it.

## ⛔ The most important thing in here: the course sets no thresholds

Part 1 of the In-Depth worksheet has an **"industry benchmark"** column for hook rate, hold rate,
conversion rate and ROAS/CPA. **ACA leaves it blank and the recording supplies no numbers for it
anywhere.** What the course asks for instead is your own current average, top performer and bottom
performer, and targets set from those.

⛔ **Any hook-rate or hold-rate benchmark attributed to this course is invented.** The blank column is
an invitation to fill it from a defensible source, not a gap to paper over from memory. A fabricated
number in that cell silently re-rates every creative in the audit.

## What is reproduced and what is this skill's

**Reproduced as written:** both worksheets' full section and part structure with every field, both
metric formulas, the three prompt categories and all twelve prompts with their inputs and outputs.

**This skill's, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The refusal to audit without data.** ACA says the prompts are not a replacement for pulling
  reports; the instruction to decline and say what to export instead is this skill's, as is the
  minimum-export table.
- ⛔ **Naming creative descriptions as the input people forget.** Without them an audit can rank
  numbers but cannot say why, which is the difference between an audit and a spreadsheet summary.
- ⭐ **Carrying the unicorn as a risk as well as a prize.** The worksheet frames section 1 purely as
  finding your best ad. The concentration-risk reading comes from the delivered recording (*"we don't
  really want that... strive for some balance"*), which the worksheet does not carry, and joining
  them is this skill's.
- **The reading of section 1's three "key success factors" slots as the section's actual
  deliverable**, and the test that each should be reproducible in another ad.
- **The note that hook rate's correlation-to-conversion column is the one that matters**, and that a
  high hook rate which does not move the KPI means the opening is stopping the wrong people.
- **The 7-versus-30-day comparison read as separating a creative problem from a scaling problem.**
- **The five output checks** in `the-prompts.md`: sample size, correlation stated as cause,
  invented benchmarks, confounded elements, and untestable hypotheses. ACA gives no guidance on
  reading the prompts' output.
- ⚠️ **Flagging prompt 9 as the odd one out**, because it is the only prompt in the pack that asks
  about data you do not have.
- **The note that prompt 10 needs a clean testing history** or it produces correlations dressed as
  impact.
- **The observation that part 6 of the in-depth worksheet (landing page and post-click) is not taught
  anywhere in the recording.**
- **The instruction to mark thin evidence in the section where it sits**, because audits get
  excerpted.

## ⚠️ How old this material is

ACA stamps no date on the assets. They were received into this library in **2026-W31**, which is a
"no later than" and not a publication date.

**What dates fastest here is thinner than elsewhere in the programme**, which is a point in these
assets' favour. The worksheets are structure, and the prompts operate on data you supply.

⛔ **What does date:** the metric names. **ThruPlays** and **3-second video views** are Meta's terms
and Meta renames metrics. If the platform's reporting no longer offers them under those names, the
formulas still work on the equivalents, but the field names in the worksheet will not match the
export.

## Deliberate overlap with `aca-302-research-for-creative-strategy`

Both relate to Course 302, on purpose, and each must run with nothing else installed.

- **`aca-302-research-for-creative-strategy`** teaches all four research passes: the account audit,
  audience research, competitors and trends. It explains what hook rate is and why the audit is
  ordered as it is.
- **This skill** runs pass 1 on a real account with real exported data, and does not teach the other
  three passes.

**Route on whether numbers are on the table.** Exported performance data is this skill. "What is hook
rate and why does it matter" is the course skill.
