# Brief a UGC creator

Produces **Ad Creative Academy's Course 502 artifacts** — Chloe Rhys's. Two documents, two moments.

| Mode | Document | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The casting call** | Nobody is cast yet. This is the public post that decides who applies |
| **B** | **The Content Creator Brief** | A creator is chosen. This is what they film from |

⭐ **This skill emits ACA's real template filled**, not a description of one.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required for either mode:**

1. **The product**, what it does, and the problem it solves
2. **The niche** — the category the creator talks about themselves in. ACA's own worked example uses
   skincare, where `[NICHE]` becomes "skin" and "skincare"

**Required for mode A, the casting call:**

3. **The rate.** ⛔ Ask for it. The course requires the field and gives no benchmark, so a rate this
   skill supplies would be invented
4. **What you need submitted** — a final edited video, raw clips, or both

**Required for mode B, the brief:**

5. **Who is filming it**, and whether they are self-directed or on a live shoot
6. **Where files go** — the upload link. ⛔ Leave it as a marked placeholder rather than inventing a
   URL
7. **The deadline**

**Optional, and each is a field:**

8. The emotional benefit, in customer language
9. Reference content that nailed the vibe
10. Brand constraints: words to avoid, claims needing disclaimers, competitor rules
11. Usage rights and term, and whether the creator posts organically
12. Capture spec, if the brand has one

⛔ **Never invent a rate, a URL, a usage term or a legal claim.** These four are the fields that turn
a draft into an offer someone sends to a real person. If they were not supplied, write
`[TO CONFIRM: ...]` and list them at the top of the output.

## How to run it

**Ask for what is missing, then produce it.** Do not hold the whole document hostage to a
questionnaire: the product and the niche are enough to produce a complete draft with the unknowns
marked.

1. **Get the product and the niche.** Those two are the minimum, and they are enough to draft from.
2. **Decide whether this is a casting call or a brief.** If nobody is cast yet, start with
   **Mode A: the casting call** (below) — briefing an uncast creator is out of order.
3. **Emit the brief section by section** from **Mode B: ACA's Content Creator Brief** (below): the opening, the eleven-item
   checklist, the two footage requests, the twelve shooting tips, the prompt steps, the annexes. **All of
   them** — a brief missing its checklist is the one the creator ignores.
4. ⛔ **Choose which capture spec you are using and say so.** ACA's two briefs disagree — 1080/30fps against
   4K, 25-30 clips against 20+ B-roll plus 10 testimonials. ⚠️ **Provenance** (below) has the full
   contradiction; pick one and state it rather than emitting both.
5. **Add commercial terms only if they were supplied.** ⚠️ The $500 in ACA's example is a placeholder, not a
   rate.
6. **List every surviving placeholder at the end** as open questions, per the rule below.

⛔ **Mark every placeholder that survives.** ACA's own template ships bracketed —
`[INSERT DROPBOX LINK]`, `[PRODUCT NAME]`, `[NICHE]` — and a bracket left visible is a question; a
bracket quietly filled with a guess is a wrong instruction the creator follows.

## ⚠️ What this skill is not

**It does not decide whether to use creators at all**, what job the content should do, or who to
cast. Those come first and belong to `aca-202-creator-advertising`, which produces the Creator
Casting Brief: the campaign's requirements, scored, before anyone applies. **That is a different
document from either of these**, written at a different moment, and filling one does not fill it.

**It does not score applicants.** The casting grid is in `aca-502-ugc-talent`, along with the method
behind everything here.

**It does not produce a storyboarded shoot document.** A creator filming alone from prompts and a
crew shooting a boarded concept are different jobs; the boarded one is `aca-403-storyboarding`.

## Mode A: the casting call

The public post. **It is a filter, not an advert** — its job is to make the wrong people not apply.

> "Be specific, but not rigid. If you're too broad, you'll waste time reviewing people who aren't a
> fit. But if you're too narrow, you'll limit the number of submissions, especially if you're on a
> tight timeline."

### The six required fields

| # | Field | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What the product is** | Plainly. One or two lines |
| 2 | **Who the brand is** | ⚠️ *"Ok if you don't want to disclose the actual brand, but it helps"* |
| 3 | **The tone or vibe** | The register you want on camera |
| 4 | **Who you're looking for** | Energy, look and/or persona |
| 5 | **Exactly what to submit** | *"A final edited video? Raw clips? Both?"* |
| 6 | **The rate** | ⛔ Supplied by the user. Never invented |

### Emit it in this shape

```
[BRAND OR CATEGORY] — UGC Creator Call

THE PRODUCT
<one or two lines: what it is, what problem it solves>

THE BRAND
<name, or the category if the brand is undisclosed>

THE VIBE
<the tone you want on camera, in plain words>

WHO WE'RE LOOKING FOR
<energy, look, persona — traceable to the brief, nothing extra>

WHAT YOU'LL SUBMIT
<edited video / raw clips / both, and how many>

RATE
<supplied by the user, or [TO CONFIRM: rate]>

TO APPLY
<where to send it, plus the sample request below if used>
```

### ⭐ The 30-second sample, and when to ask

> "You can also explicitly request a quick **30-second video sample** — ideally speaking unscripted
> about a product they like. This gives you a fast read on camera presence, vocal tone, energy level
> and authenticity. **You'll learn more in 30 seconds of off-the-cuff talking than in an entire
> polished reel.**"

⚠️ **It has a cost and Chloe names it:** *"Requiring a video sample will no doubt reduce the overall
number of submissions you'll receive, and often if they have a solid UGC reel this step can be
avoided."*

**So it is conditional, not standard:**

| Ask for the sample | Skip it |
|---|---|
| Posting somewhere broad, where most applicants will not be UGC creators | The applicant already has a real UGC reel |
| The reels you are seeing are acting or glam work | The timeline is tight and submissions are scarce |

**Wording to use:** *"Please include a 30-second video, filmed on your phone, talking unscripted
about any product you actually like. No script, no editing."*

### Where to post, and what each route returns

| Route | What you get | The trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Backstage** | Volume. Actors, models, voiceover artists, dancers | ⚠️ *"A very wide mix of experience levels."* Expect heavy manual filtering, and many applicants unfamiliar with short-form UGC |
| **Trend.io · Billo · Cohely** | Creators already fluent in vertical, to-camera, prompt-led work. Pay per deliverable, platform handles some logistics | ⭐ Best when the timeline or the budget is tight |
| **Organic outreach, especially TikTok** | ⭐ Creators who already hook attention. Search your product niche and message micro-creators directly | Slower, one at a time, but *"nuance and potential long-term collaborators"* |

⭐ **Match the route to what you need, not to habit.** *"Backstage gives you volume. Trend, Billo and
Cohely give you speed and relevance. TikTok gives you nuance and potential long-term
collaborators."*

⚠️ **And the option nobody posts:** in fast sprints, strategists, copywriters and even VPs step in
front of the camera themselves. *"It's fast. It's real. And often, it works."* If the brief is a
quick testimonial or a hands-only POV demo, ask whether a casting round is needed at all.

### ⛔ Before delivering the call

- **Every trait named in "who we're looking for" traces to the brief.** A trait that is not in the
  brief is not filtering for the campaign, it is filtering on taste
- **The rate is real or marked `[TO CONFIRM]`.** A posted rate is an offer
- **The submission ask is unambiguous.** "Send us your work" produces a mixture nobody can score
- **Say what happens next**, including whether unsuccessful applicants hear back. People are giving
  you unpaid work when you ask for a sample

## Mode B: ACA's Content Creator Brief

> "**80% of great UGC is in the brief.**"

> "A vague brief is a wasted budget. If you don't tell creators what's important to include, what to
> avoid, or how to structure the video, they'll guess. And their guess might not be what you needed."

⛔ **Emit the document below, filled.** Not a summary of what a creator brief contains.

⚠️ **ACA's own worked example is a skincare brand, Skintific**, where `[NICHE]` becomes "skin" and
"skincare", `[PRODUCT NAME]` becomes "Skintific", and one prompt is adapted to the product —
*"Explain how Skintific works. What each **light** does"* where the template says "what each
**feature** does". **Adapt the wording to the product; keep the structure.**

### The template, section by section

#### Opening

> "[Brand Name] Content Creator Brief
>
> Thank you for joining our UGC content creator program! We're so excited to see what you'll make!
> Your first brief is dedicated to [BRAND NAME]. Please find all instructions below and feel free to
> reach out with any questions."

⭐ **Keep the warmth.** Chloe's directing method opens with warmth for a reason, and the brief is the
first contact a self-directed creator has. A cold brief gets a stiff performance.

#### Checklist — eleven items

The at-a-glance list. It mirrors the two prompt steps below, which is why it works as a checklist.

| # | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Upload original video files to this **[INSERT DROPBOX LINK]** |
| 2 | Your main concerns/problems about your **[NICHE]** |
| 3 | Things YOU HATE or WILL never do again around **[NICHE]** |
| 4 | Your experience receiving the **[PRODUCT NAME]** and first impressions |
| 5 | Tell us about your **[PRODUCT NAME]** ritual |
| 6 | Tell us what IT FEELS LIKE to use **[PRODUCT NAME]** |
| 7 | Show us how / where you use **[PRODUCT NAME]** |
| 8 | Explain how **[PRODUCT NAME]** works. What each feature does, and the benefits |
| 9 | Results |
| 10 | Calls to Action! |
| 11 | Upload all new footage video files to this **[INSERT DROPBOX LINK]** |

#### 1. Your Original Video (no music or graphics)

> "This part is dedicated to your original video that we already loved so much! Please upload to
> this [INSERT DROPBOX LINK] (you can do this from your phone, any raw video/audio clips you used to
> create your original video submission)."

- Original video clips **without overlays / graphics or text or music**
- Original voiceover audio files, if a separate voiceover was recorded
- If the voiceover was recorded in-app while editing, **export a version that is voiceover + video
  without any additional music or graphics**
- *"If you no longer have saved or cannot locate the original video clips, please reach out to let
  us know!"*

⚠️ **This section only applies when the creator was cast off an existing video you liked.** Cut it
when they were cast from a reel or a sample, rather than leaving a request for a video that does not
exist.

#### 2. New Footage

> "For the second part please capture new footage following the prompts below. Please capture each
> prompt/question in a few clips each around a minute or less long (**in total you should have at
> least 25-30 clips to send — more is great!**)"

> "No need to edit anything! Just upload the raw clips to the dropbox folder provided!"

> "**Important: No music and no graphics, captions, filters or emojis. Do not mention an affiliate
> code.**"

#### Shooting Tips — twelve

| # | Tip |
|---|---|
| 1 | Make sure there's plenty of storage space on your phone |
| 2 | Choose a time and place to shoot in with the best lighting |
| 3 | Shoot in a bright / well-lit flattering area |
| 4 | If recording audio, choose a quiet area without noise interruptions. **Be aware of fans, AC and traffic** |
| 5 | Remember you're not alone! You can reach out to us at any time with questions |
| 6 | ⚠️ Set your camera settings to record at **HD 1080 30 fps** |
| 7 | **Always shoot vertically** |
| 8 | When recording yourself, try to keep your eyes near the top line |
| 9 | **Lock your exposure** |
| 10 | Try to keep your recordings under a minute each |
| 11 | Feel free to get creative with your shot styles — close ups, point of view, selfie |
| 12 | For the styling/setting of your video, make sure your shoot location is clean and well-styled |

⛔ **Tip 6 is the one to check before sending.** ACA's other creator template says to film in 4K, not
1080p, and they cannot both be followed. Pick one, state it once, and see **Provenance** (below).

#### Step 1: You and your [NICHE]

> "While holding/using the [PRODUCT NAME], speak to the camera and tell us a bit about yourself and
> your [NICHE] peeves / concerns / no-gos. **Feel free to go deep, get personal, vent, tell us your
> hot takes.**"

**1. What are your main concerns/problems about your [NICHE]?**

*"Any personal anecdotes or stories that people could relate to are great!"* — Did you have issues
growing up? · Notice any changes recently? · Have any unique stories? · How have these struggles
affected you?

**2. What are some things YOU HATE or WILL never do again around [NICHE]?**

What did you try that did not work, or disappointed you? · Any expensive or harsh products or
treatments? · Do you hate how long routines take, or not have time for appointments? · Are you
scared of certain treatments, or had bad experiences? · Are you SHOCKED at how expensive everything
is?

⭐ **Step 1 question 2 is the load-bearing one.** It produces the "what else I tried that failed"
beat, which is what makes the product's claim credible rather than another promise. Adapt its
sub-prompts to the category properly rather than pasting the skincare ones.

#### Step 2: You and your [PRODUCT NAME]

> "This section will focus around introducing [PRODUCT NAME] to your viewers, telling them how you
> use it, why you love it. **Feel free to be very expressive, and let your facial expressions convey
> how you feel** as you use it or the surprise upon seeing the results."

| # | Prompt |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tell us about your experience receiving the **[PRODUCT NAME]** and your first impressions |
| 2 | Tell us about your **[PRODUCT NAME]** ritual |
| 3 | Tell us what **IT FEELS LIKE** to use **[PRODUCT NAME]** |
| 4 | **Show us** how / where you use **[PRODUCT NAME]** |
| 5 | Explain how **[PRODUCT NAME]** works. What each function does, and the benefits |
| 6 | **Results** — have you seen any initial results yet? Show to the camera where you've seen improvement. **If not, where are you hoping to see results?** |
| 7 | **Call to Action** |

⭐ **Prompt 6 is written honestly and should stay that way.** It asks for results *or* hopes, which
means a creator with no results yet has a truthful answer available. Rewriting it to assume results
is how a brief starts manufacturing testimonials.

the do's and don'ts, and the creator's end-of-brief checklist below.**

### ⛔ What ACA's template leaves out, and the brief needs

The template covers the prompts. Chloe's Module 4 covers four things the template does not carry, and
a brief missing them is the brief that produces unusable footage.

#### Product information

Three sentences: what it is, what customers say about it, who it is for. ⭐ **Including the emotional
benefit** — *"how it makes the customer feel."* Her worked example:

⛔ **The middle sentence is customer language, and you may not invent it.** *"Customers love it for
how gentle it feels"* is a claim about what real buyers said. A creator reads this brief and
paraphrases it to camera, so an invented customer sentiment becomes **a spoken claim in a shipped
ad** — the one place in this document where a plausible guess turns into someone else's testimonial.
**If no reviews, survey answers or support quotes were supplied, write
`[TO CONFIRM: what customers actually say — send reviews or quotes]`** and leave it unfilled. The
same applies to any benefit written as though customers reported it.

> "This is a skincare mist designed to calm redness and replenish the skin barrier. Customers love
> it for how gentle it feels and how quickly it takes down inflammation. It's perfect for anyone
> who's self-conscious about redness."

#### Structure guidance

| # | Beat |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Before state / problem state** |
| 2 | **Category discovery** — other things I tried that didn't work |
| 3 | **Product discovery / experience** |
| 4 | **Solution / resolved state** |

#### Visual and audio instructions

Vertical 9:16 · natural light, shoot facing a window, avoid dark or blown-out · no background music
unless requested · no cluttered backgrounds or visible brands · no jump cuts unless specified ·
stable framing.

⭐ **Include a visual reference** — past winning content, or a format that nailed the vibe.

#### Do's and don'ts

Words to avoid, like *"miracle"* or *"cure"* · claims that require disclaimers · no competitor
mentions, profanity or sensitive topics.

⛔ **This section is the cheapest insurance in the process.** A compliance problem found in the edit
costs a reshoot; prevented, it costs three lines.

### The creator's end-of-brief checklist

> "Always include a checklist at the end of the brief."

| ☐ Filmed in vertical 9:16 |
|---|
| ☐ Spoke clearly and confidently |
| ☐ No music or filters |
| ☐ Product shown or used clearly |
| ☐ Hook, story and CTA all included |

⚠️ **This is the creator's checklist, in their voice.** Your own check of the returned footage
against the seven clip types is a different document and lives in `aca-502-ugc-talent`.

### ⛔ Before delivering the brief

1. **Every `[BRACKET]` is either filled or visibly marked** as a question. A quietly guessed link or
   product name is an instruction the creator follows
2. **A `[TO CONFIRM: ...]` list at the top** naming the rate, the upload link, the usage term and any
   legal line that was not supplied
3. **Prompts, never a script.** ⛔ If the user hands you exact lines to be read, say plainly that the
   course advises against it and offer the same content as prompts
4. **The niche sub-prompts are rewritten for this category**, not pasted from skincare
5. **Section 1 is cut if the creator was not cast off an existing video**
6. **One capture spec, stated once**

### ⛔ ACA's two creator briefs contradict each other

**Measured, not inferred.** A creator sent both would not know which to follow:

| | Content Creator Brief Template | External Creator Script Template |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture format** | *"Set your camera settings to record at **HD 1080 30 fps**"* | *"Film all content in **4K** (change your camera settings to 4K instead of HDR)"* |
| **Volume asked for** | *"At least **25-30 clips**"* | *"**20+ RAW B-Roll Shots**, **10x Scripted Testimonials**"* |
| **Head and tail** | Not specified | *"Add an extra **1-3 seconds** to the beginning and end of all raw clips"* |
| **Commercial terms** | None | Deadline, *"$500 USD after content submission"*, a signature line, and usage: ads only, Facebook and Instagram, up to 12 months |

⛔ **1080p against 4K is the one that cannot be fixed after the shoot.** Neither is wrong: 4K gives
reframing and punch-in headroom, which matters when one deliverable is cut to several aspect ratios;
1080p is smaller, faster to upload from a phone, and less likely to fill a creator's storage.
**Sending both is what is wrong.** This skill emits the Content Creator Brief's spec by default and
flags it for confirmation.

⚠️ **They agree on everything else that matters**: vertical framing, good light, no music, no
filters, no graphics, a quiet room, and watching for fans and AC.

⭐ **The two are not versions of one document.** They share only **21.4%** of their content despite
near-identical original filenames, and the shorter one is **not on ACA's shipping asset list** at
all. Anyone matching on filename will conflate them.

⚠️ **The `1-3 seconds` head-and-tail instruction is worth borrowing** even when emitting the other
template. It costs the creator nothing and it is what lets an editor cut cleanly.
