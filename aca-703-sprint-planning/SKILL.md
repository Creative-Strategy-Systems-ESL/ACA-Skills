**Chloe Rhys**, Ex-TubeScience, now at OpenStore. 55m 41s, 3 modules — the largest course in the

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what a MORE request is, how to define a win, or when a
result is readable, and get an answer.

**To plan a sprint, required:**

1. **Daily or monthly ad spend.** ⛔ Say which — this course indexes on **daily** spend and Course
   601 indexes on **monthly**, and confusing them is a 30× error
2. **Your team's actual weekly output capacity**, honestly stated

**Optional, and each one changes the plan:**

3. Brand maturity — new to paid social, or with a library of learnings
4. How campaigns are structured: dedicated testing campaigns, or new ads fed into evergreen ones
5. The sprint goal: growth, efficiency or stabilisation
6. The CPA target, which sets the win threshold
7. What is currently winning, which sets the MORE pipeline

⛔ **Never invent a volume number.** This course gives five variables and one worked account, not a
lookup table. A number produced without the five is a guess wearing a framework.

⛔ **Never plan past the team's stated capacity.** ⭐ Chloe's own rule: *"Launching 6 Concepts a week,
every week, will outperform 12 one week and none for the next month."*

## The through-line

> "You don't scale by finding one winner. **You scale by turning that winner into a series.**"

⭐ **That is what a sprint is for.** The four request types exist so that a week of creative is a
portfolio — mostly building on what works, some rescuing near-misses, some genuinely new — rather
than a pile of fresh ideas competing for the same budget.

## How to run it

**Volume comes from the account, not from a benchmark.** The five variables exist so the number is derived
rather than borrowed.

1. **Work the five variables** in **How much, and how often** (below) to get this account's volume. ⛔ Indexed
   on **daily** spend — Course 601's ladder is monthly, and mixing them is a 30× error.
2. **Set launch cadence and turnaround times**, same file.
3. ⛔ **Establish when a result is readable** and when it is still noise, before anything launches. This is
   what stops ads being killed early, which is the failure the course names.
4. **Define a win** with the four criteria in **Wins, and what a sprint contains** (below), including the 20× CPA scale rule.
   ⭐ Note that Course 601's $2,000-per-test at a $100 CPA is the same threshold, reached independently.
5. **Set the request mix** — MORE, FIX, ADAPT, NEW — to the target proportions in the same file.
6. **Cover the ego-less factory-line mindset**, which is what makes the cadence survivable rather than a
   burnout schedule.
7. ⭐ **To fill the board, route to `aca-plan-creative-sprint`** if installed. ⛔ It emits structure only,
   never ACA's client data.
8. **Check the volume number came from their variables.** A number quoted from another course is the mistake
   this sequence exists to prevent.

## Answering well

**Volume is derived, not looked up.** Five variables set it: spend level, brand maturity, team
bandwidth, testing structure and goal type. ⭐ *"There's no universal number that works across the
board."*

**Consistency beats peaks.** The single most actionable line in the course is about cadence, not
volume: a steady six a week beats twelve then nothing.

**A win has four parts, and three of them are about durability.** Beating CPA is only the first;
holding at scale, across multiple variants, and over time are what separate a winner from a lucky
edit.

**Not every dead ad is bad creative.** ⚠️ *"Sometimes ads just don't get spend, or media buyers turn
off your ads before they get a fair shot... This happens more often than you'd think — and it's not
always the creative's fault."* There is a protocol for it rather than a shrug.

⚠️ **This course competes with Course 601 on creative volume and neither mentions the other.** They
use different units and different time bases. **Provenance** (below) sets out both;
`aca-plan-creative-volume` carries the comparison.

## Automating the sprint system

⚠️ **What this section is, and what it is not.** ACA ships an asset for this course called the
*Sprint Planning Operation System Worksheet*. **It is not a worksheet and there is nothing to fill
in.** It is engineering documentation for the internal creative-operations stack OpenStore built
around the sprint board, credited to **Billy Bjork** alongside Chloe.

⭐ **It is still worth having**, because it is the only place in the programme that shows what the
sprint board looks like once a team has outgrown doing it by hand.

### The problem it was built for

> "Our challenge was to scale creative operations to deliver **150+ unique ad creatives per week
> across 40+ brands.**"

⭐ **That number is the context for everything in Course 703.** The five volume variables, the
cruising-speed argument and the four request types all come from a team operating at that scale. A
brand running one account does not need any of this machinery — but the concepts underneath it still
apply.

### The three components

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Creative Sprint Planning** | *"Central project management system that tracks projects through all stages, implements naming conventions, and facilitates collaboration between strategists, editors and designers"* |
| **Automated Notifications** | Slack notifications triggered by status changes, ⭐ *"allowing users to update project statuses directly from Slack messages"* |
| **Performance Dashboard** | Browsing every ad in the portfolio launched on Meta and TikTok. Users take their own copy, then filter and sort by metrics like spend, CTR and ROAS |

**The stack named:** Google Sheets Apps Script with external API integrations including **iconik** for
media asset management · **n8n** for node-based workflow orchestration · a data pipeline doing
**daily metric refreshes from Meta and TikTok**.

### The six workflow steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Job creation** | Jobs are added with **dynamically generated names** from user input. On a status change, a script **auto-increments a brand-specific counter** to assign the job number. Supports batch creation |
| **2. Review link generation** | After the first revision, assets are exported to the media asset manager and **review links are generated automatically by looking up the job name** |
| **3. Trigger Slack notifications** | Status changes send payloads onward, which route to the right Slack message |
| **4. Interactive Slack messages** | When notes are left on a first creative, editors and designers get a notification **they can change the job status from** |
| **5. Processing Slack interactions** | Those interactions route back and update the sheet |
| **6. Performance analytics** | The dashboard, filtered |

⭐ **Steps 1 and 2 are the ones worth copying even without the engineering.** Auto-generated job names
and auto-assigned numbers are what make the naming convention hold — Course 601's whole argument is
that a convention only works if it is followed every time, and a system that generates the name
removes the chance to get it wrong.

⭐ **Steps 3 to 5 solve the real operational problem**, which is that status only stays current if
updating it is easier than not updating it. A board nobody updates is worse than no board, because it
reports confidently and wrongly.

### The dashboard's filters

**And this is the payoff, because it shows what the board's columns are actually for:**

| Filter |
|---|
| Brand |
| Channel — Meta or TikTok |
| Requester (strategist) |
| Creator (editor/designer) |
| Job Format — video or static |
| ⭐ **Job Type — New, More, Fix, Adapt** |
| Date range (launch date) |
| ⭐ **Wins and Pre-Wins** — internal top-performer criteria |

⭐ **Every filter is a column on the sprint board.** That is the argument for filling the board
properly: each field you leave blank is a question you cannot ask later. Filtering performance by
**Job Type** is what tells you whether your MOREs actually out-hit your NEWs, which is the number
that should set next quarter's mix.

⚠️ **"Pre-Wins" appears only here.** Course 703 defines a win with four criteria and never mentions a
pre-win, so its threshold is not held. Treat it as OpenStore's internal tier, not a taught concept.

### ⛔ What to take from this if you are not building it

**You do not need Apps Script, n8n or Snowflake.** The transferable rules:

1. **Generate job names and numbers rather than typing them**, however you do it
2. **Make the status update happen where the person already is**, not in a sheet they have to open
3. ⛔ **Record job type on every job**, because it is the filter that makes the sprint mix
   answerable rather than aspirational
4. **Define your win criteria once and store them as a flag**, so "which of these won" is a query
   rather than an argument

⚠️ **The documentation names a public repository and an individual's contact details.** They are not
reproduced here; the architecture above is what matters and it is described in full.

## How much, and how often

> "If there's one question every creative strategist gets asked, it's this: **'How many ads should we
> be making every week?'**"

> "Not every brand needs the same volume of output. **There's no universal number that works across
> the board.**"

### ⛔ Before any number: which spend, and what unit

**This course indexes on DAILY spend.** Course 601's ladder indexes on **monthly**. ⛔ A reader
converting between them without noticing is out by roughly 30×.

**And the unit differs too.** This course counts **Concepts**, each launched in its own ad set with
**3-6 Variants**, so a weekly concept count multiplies into a much larger ad count. Always say which
you are quoting.

---

### The five variables

#### 1. Spend level

> "The more you spend, the faster your ads fatigue. And the faster your ads fatigue, **the more
> creative you need in rotation.**"

> "A brand spending over **$10,000/day** is going to hit fatigue much faster than a brand spending
> **$100/day.**"

⭐ **Her worked account, and the only concrete volume figure in the course:**

> "For **Jack Archer**, we often launch **9 to 12 new Concepts per week.** Each Concept is launched
> in its own Ad Set, with **4-6 Variants.** So, across a week, that's typically anywhere from **50 to
> 70 ads** hitting the account."

**Below that:** *"If you're spending... under **$500/day**, you can operate with a lower volume. But
remember, **lower volume means slower learnings.** You have to be more intentional about what you
test."*

⚠️ **She gives 4-6 variants here and 3-6 under variable 4.** Both are hers; treat the range as 3-6
and say so.

#### 2. Brand maturity

| State | Mode | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **New to paid social** | ⭐ **Exploration** | *"prioritize diversity—different angles, formats, tones, and audiences. You're still learning what works. So, volume helps you explore that surface area."* |
| **Mature**, with a library of learnings | **Refinement** | *"You're exploiting known winners, iterating more on top performing styles and angles"* |

⭐ *"So your volume might stay high — **but the type of creative you make will shift.**"* Maturity
changes the *mix*, not necessarily the count. That is what connects this variable to the four request
types.

#### 3. Team bandwidth

> "Let's say you've scoped out your ideal sprint: you want to launch 10 new Concepts a week, each
> with 5 Variants. That's 50 ads a week. **But… can your team handle that?**"

> "This is where a lot of teams break down. **They build a creative strategy that doesn't match their
> actual resources.**"

⭐ **The line to quote when someone wants to scale output:**

> "**Consistency beats chaos. Launching 6 Concepts a week, every week, will outperform 12 one week
> and none for the next month.** Find your team's creative **'cruising speed.'** That's how you
> define your ideal volume target."

#### 4. Testing structure

> "Your campaign setup has a big impact on how much creative you can test."

**The question:** are ads launched into dedicated testing campaigns with flexible budgets, or fed
into evergreen campaigns alongside existing winners?

⛔ *"If your new ads are fighting with high-spending BAU ads in the same campaign? **Your new ads
might never get spend or impressions.**"*

⭐ **Her recommendation:** *"Launching each Concept in its own Ad Set, with 3-6 Variants. That
isolates performance and ensures each idea gets a clean read. And when you're able to do that across
multiple Concepts per week — you start building real **learning velocity.**"*

#### 5. Goal type

| Sprint goal | Volume posture |
|---|---|
| **Growth** — scale aggressively | ⭐ **Test more.** Push volume, look for net-new winners, new personas, audiences to expand into |
| **Efficiency** — tight budget, maybe Q1 | **Iterate more.** Reduce volume, increase precision, focus on proven structures |
| **Stabilisation** | *"Do both, carefully"* |

> "Volume should reflect your sprint goal."

---

### Launch cadence

> "There is **no single “best” day** to launch creative from having tested thousands of ads. I've
> tested Mondays, Fridays, mid-week, weekends. And what we've found is that **the day itself doesn't
> matter as much as the cadence and consistency—without**"

#### You do not need a fixed launch day

> "At OpenStore, we aim to get creative into the ad account **as soon as it's ready.** If a Concept
> has gone through review, passed QA, and been uploaded — it gets launched."

**The exceptions, where date alignment does matter:** timed promos · back-in-stock pushes · new
product launches.

⭐ *"For business-as-usual creative, there's no benefit to holding a great concept until Monday if
it's ready on Thursday. **In fact, waiting can slow your feedback loop.**"*

#### Expected turnaround, not arbitrary deadlines

| Work | Expected turnaround |
|---|---|
| **A net-new Concept** | **5-7 business days** from brief to first draft |
| **A quick iteration** — alternate CTAs or hooks | **1-3 days** |

> "I use these timelines as **guidelines, not hard rules.** And the benefit of this model? It allows
> your designers and editors to work with more autonomy, and it prevents unnecessary pressure just
> to hit a random calendar date."

⭐ **And it buys responsiveness:** *"If a new angle is working, you can submit a brief for an
iteration immediately — without waiting for the next 'launch day.'"*

#### Two priority tags, and only two

Every job is tagged either **Business-as-usual (BAU)** or **Time-sensitive / high-priority**, the
latter paired with a specific due date.

⛔ *"**If you treat every single Concept as urgent, you'll burn your editors out and miss deadlines
anyway.**"*

---

### When a result is readable

> "Instead of sitting impatiently refreshing Ads Manager waiting to see immediate results, **you need
> to give Meta time to optimize.**"

**The Learning Phase:** *"when Meta is still figuring out how to deliver your ad. This phase
typically lasts until the ad set gets **50 optimization events** — for example, 50 purchases or
adds-to-cart. During Learning, performance is unstable. You could possibly see big swings in CPA, CTR
and spend."*

**Her rule of thumb:**

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Days 2-3** | Start checking **early indicators** |
| **Days 7-14**, or once the ad exits Learning | ⭐ **Don't make final calls before this** |
| Low-spend brands | ⚠️ *"Be patient with low-spend brands—it might take much longer to gather enough data"* |

---

### ⛔ When ads get turned off early, or never spend

> "Let's address the elephant in the room: sometimes ads just don't get spend, or media buyers turn
> off your ads before they get a fair shot."

⚠️ **She takes the media buyer's side first**, which is what makes the protocol usable: *"That's
fair, they're responsible for efficiency, or may have missed some early engagement indicators."*

**If an ad was prematurely paused:**

1. **Ask directly why.** Spend limits? Performance? A pacing issue?
2. **Check if it launched in a test campaign.** If not, it may have been competing with other high
   performers
3. **Request a retest** in a dedicated ad set if it did not get enough delivery
4. ⭐ **Tag it "Not Enough Spend" in your tracker**, so it stays on the radar for a future retest

**If an ad gets zero spend at all**, three things to check:

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| **Ad set audience overlap** | *"If you've got other ads targeting the same audience, Meta might prioritize the ones with more history"* |
| **Bid cap or manual bidding** | *"Aggressive bid caps can restrict delivery — especially on new ads"* |
| **Is the account in efficiency mode?** | *"During periods of reduced spend, media buyers often cut back on testing. **New ads are the first to go**"* |

⭐ **Do not write it off:** *"At OpenStore, we sometimes re-launch promising Concepts a few weeks
later — especially if we believe the creative is strong but just didn't get a fair shot."*

> "**This happens more often than you'd think — and it's not always the creative's fault.**"

⛔ **This is the section that protects a strategist's judgement from a delivery artefact.** An ad that
never spent produced no evidence, and filing it as a loss corrupts every pattern you later draw from
the tracker.

## Wins, and what a sprint contains

### ⭐ The four criteria for a winning concept

> "This is one of the most critical skills for any creative strategist: being able to **clearly
> define what a winning Concept is** — and turn that win into more momentum for the brand."

⚠️ **She frames it as one house's definition:** *"Every agency or brand tends to have a slightly
different version of this, but generally I define a win using four key criteria."*

| # | Criterion | The test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It meets or beats your CPA goal** | *"That's the baseline. If your CPA target is $50 and an ad is converting below that — great start. If it's over target, it's not a win. Simple as that"* |
| 2 | ⭐ **It does this at scale** | *"We need to see performance hold through **at least 20x your CPA.** So for a $50 CPA, that's **$1,000 of spend.** If it holds through $2,000, $3,000, even better."* |
| 3 | **It performs across multiple Variants** | *"One strong ad could just be a fluke. But if **2 or 3 Variants** from the same Concept are hitting? That tells you **the idea—the** Concept itself—is driving performance."* |
| 4 | **It holds over time** | *"We're not interested in 24-hour spikes. We want to see sustainable performance. Ideally, that means the Concept delivers results across **5–7 days** or more."* |

> "If your Concept hits all four? **You've got a win.**"

⭐ **The 20× CPA rule is the most portable number in the course**, because it scales with the
account instead of asserting a fixed budget. And it is the reason criterion 1 alone is not enough:
*"We don't call something a winner based on $50 of spend. **That's noise, not signal.**"*

⭐ **Criterion 3 is what makes the win a *concept* win rather than an *ad* win** — which is what
justifies the whole MORE pipeline below. One lucky edit cannot be expanded; a validated idea can.

### What to do when you have a winner

> "Once a Concept meets win criteria, **the worst thing you can do is… nothing.**"

**Step 1 — Document everything.** Flag it, then record: what was the hook? the format? the persona or
offer? *"What really made this one stand out?"*

⭐ *"This ensures everyone on the team can reference it — editors, strategists, media buyers. And it
helps you **spot patterns across wins.**"*

**Step 2 — Launch a MORE request.** Take the same Concept and expand it.

## The four types of ad request

> "you can't just make random concepts each week. You need to be intentional with **what types of
> work you prioritize** and how that work ladders up to your performance goals."

⭐ **These four are also the Job Type values in ACA's sprint planning board**, which is how the
course and the template connect.

### 1. MORE — expanding on what's working

> "This is your **highest-confidence creative type.** MORE means taking a winning Concept and
> producing iterations. You're not coming up with a new idea—you're doubling down on what already
> works."

**Her worked trigger:** a Variant hit a 1.8% CTR and beat the CPA goal by 30%. *"You don't just let
it run. You expand it."*

| Ways to expand |
|---|
| Swap the voiceover with a new voice or a different greenscreen UGC |
| Change the first 3 seconds to a different hook |
| Re-shoot the same script with different talent |
| Add urgency with a promo offer or countdown |
| Recut it in a new aspect ratio — 1x1, 9x16, 4x5 |

> "The goal of a MORE request is to **extend the shelf life of a Concept.** And because you already
> know the idea works, **your hit rate for iterations is usually higher.** That's why every
> high-performing account we manage runs on 'MORE's."

⛔ **Isolate the variable.** *"When you're launching iterations, **don't change too many things at
once.** Keep your tests clean. Change one variable at a time — whether that's a hook visual, a
headline, the CTA."*

### 2. FIX — iterating on a near-miss

> "Sometimes, an ad is **almost there.** Maybe the hook is strong but the CTA is weak. Maybe the
> watch time is high but people aren't clicking."

| Symptom | The fix |
|---|---|
| **Thumbstop is low** | A new, more engaging opening hook headline or visual |
| **CTR is low** | Refine the value prop, add urgency or a promo, or highlight a different benefit |
| **CVR is low** | Explain the USPs better — and where possible, align the landing page with the ad's message |

⛔ **Be surgical.** *"Don't overhaul the entire Concept. **Change one or two key things based on
data, then retest.**"*

⭐ **The economic argument:** *"You've already paid to concept, script and shoot the ad. **A FIX gives
you another shot at ROI.**"*

### 3. ADAPT — repurposing a proven strategy

> "Take something that worked **elsewhere** — either inside your own account or from a competitor —
> and apply the same structure, angle or format to a new concept."

Two shapes: a competitor's format applied to your product with your own content; or a top-performing
ad for Product A rebuilt for Product B, keeping the format and angle while switching the visuals and
copy.

> "They're not totally original, but they're not pure copy/paste either. They're **smart reuse**
> adaptation **of what's proven to work.**"

⭐ *"ADAPTing is a great tool when you need to **fill your pipeline with reliable concepts** —
especially if you're in a slump or trying to stabilise performance quickly."*

### 4. NEW — testing unproven ideas

> "**No past data to back it up.** These are your riskiest requests — but also the most exciting."

A NEW concept might explore a new persona or use case, take a creative risk with humour, weirdness or
bold visuals, focus on a different product feature, or show the product in a way you have not before.

⚠️ *"Because there's no proof yet, **be selective about how many NEWs you launch each sprint.** They
take the most effort — and usually have the **lowest initial success rate.** But when a NEW concept
hits? **It can redefine your entire performance strategy.**"*

---

### ⭐ The balanced sprint mix

> "Here's a rule of thumb, for most brands:"

| Type | Share | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **MORE** | **30-40%** | *"Keeps your winners fresh and profitable"* |
| **FIX** | **20-30%** | *"Don't waste near-misses. Make them better"* |
| **NEW** | **20-30%** | *"Always test something wild"* |
| **ADAPT** | **10-20%** | *"Use this to stay consistent and efficient"* |

⚠️ **The ranges sum to 80-120%**, so this is a shape rather than an allocation. Pick points inside
each band that total 100 and say what you picked.

⭐ **The stated exception, and it is a big one:** *"If you're working with a brand that is relatively
new to paid social advertising, and has yet to unlock a clear win, you might have a mix of **40% New
and 60% Adapts.**"* — which is 0% MORE and 0% FIX, because there is nothing yet to build on or
rescue. **The mix follows brand maturity.**

> "**Just make sure every sprint includes a mix.** That's how you balance stability and exploration."

### Producing a sprint plan

**Deliver:**

1. **The volume**, derived from all five variables with each one's contribution named
2. ⛔ **The unit and the time base stated** — Concepts per week, Variants per Concept, and the ad
   count that implies
3. **The mix**, as actual counts per type, summing to the volume
4. **The win criteria for this account**, with the CPA target and the 20× spend threshold calculated
5. **The read date** — when early indicators get checked and when a final call can be made
6. ⛔ **A line naming every input that was assumed rather than given**

⚠️ **If the team's capacity was not stated, do not plan a volume.** Ask. The course's own failure
mode is a strategy that does not match the resources, and a plan that ignores capacity produces the
twelve-then-nothing pattern it exists to prevent.

⭐ **They are not really contradicting each other, they are counting different objects** — 601 counts
*tests* and 703 counts *ads*, with concepts in between. But nothing in either course says so, and a
team handed both will produce a plan that is wrong by a multiple.

⚠️ **Course 601's is a lookup table and this one is a derivation.** When both are available, this
course's five variables are the more defensible instrument, because they include team capacity and
campaign structure — the two things that actually cap output and that a spend-indexed ladder cannot
see.

### ⭐ Where the two courses independently agree

**Course 601:** *"roughly **$2,000 per test** to identify a winner"*, alongside a **$100 CPA**.

**Course 703:** a win must hold through *"at least **20x your CPA.**"*.

**20 × $100 = $2,000.** ⭐ **Two faculty, two courses, no cross-reference, the same number.** That is
the strongest corroboration anywhere in this programme, and it makes the 20× rule the more useful
form: it scales with the account instead of asserting a fixed budget.
