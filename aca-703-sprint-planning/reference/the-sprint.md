# Wins, and what a sprint contains

## ⭐ The four criteria for a winning concept

> "This is one of the most critical skills for any creative strategist: being able to **clearly
> define what a winning Concept is** — and turn that win into more momentum for the brand."

⚠️ **She frames it as one house's definition:** *"Every agency or brand tends to have a slightly
different version of this, but generally I define a win using four key criteria."*

| # | Criterion | The test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It meets or beats your CPA goal** | *"That's the baseline. If your CPA target is $50 and an ad is converting below that — great start. If it's over target, it's not a win. Simple as that"* |
| 2 | ⭐ **It does this at scale** | *"We need to see performance hold through **at least 20x your CPA.** So for a $50 CPA, that's **$1,000 of spend.** If it holds through $2,000, $3,000, even better."* |
| 3 | **It performs across multiple Variants** | *"One strong ad could just be a fluke. But if **2 or 3 Variants** from the same Concept are hitting? That tells you **the idea—the** Concept itself—is driving performance."* |
| 4 | **It holds over time** | *"We're not interested in 24-hour spikes. We want to see sustainable performance. Ideally, that means the Concept delivers results across **5–7 days** or more."* |

> "If your Concept hits all four? **You've got a win.**"

⭐ **The 20× CPA rule is the most portable number in the course**, because it scales with the
account instead of asserting a fixed budget. And it is the reason criterion 1 alone is not enough:
*"We don't call something a winner based on $50 of spend. **That's noise, not signal.**"*

⭐ **Criterion 3 is what makes the win a *concept* win rather than an *ad* win** — which is what
justifies the whole MORE pipeline below. One lucky edit cannot be expanded; a validated idea can.

## What to do when you have a winner

> "Once a Concept meets win criteria, **the worst thing you can do is… nothing.**"

**Step 1 — Document everything.** Flag it, then record: what was the hook? the format? the persona or
offer? *"What really made this one stand out?"*

⭐ *"This ensures everyone on the team can reference it — editors, strategists, media buyers. And it
helps you **spot patterns across wins.**"*

**Step 2 — Launch a MORE request.** Take the same Concept and expand it.

# The four types of ad request

> "you can't just make random concepts each week. You need to be intentional with **what types of
> work you prioritize** and how that work ladders up to your performance goals."

⭐ **These four are also the Job Type values in ACA's sprint planning board**, which is how the
course and the template connect.

## 1. MORE — expanding on what's working

> "This is your **highest-confidence creative type.** MORE means taking a winning Concept and
> producing iterations. You're not coming up with a new idea—you're doubling down on what already
> works."

**Her worked trigger:** a Variant hit a 1.8% CTR and beat the CPA goal by 30%. *"You don't just let
it run. You expand it."*

| Ways to expand |
|---|
| Swap the voiceover with a new voice or a different greenscreen UGC |
| Change the first 3 seconds to a different hook |
| Re-shoot the same script with different talent |
| Add urgency with a promo offer or countdown |
| Recut it in a new aspect ratio — 1x1, 9x16, 4x5 |

> "The goal of a MORE request is to **extend the shelf life of a Concept.** And because you already
> know the idea works, **your hit rate for iterations is usually higher.** That's why every
> high-performing account we manage runs on 'MORE's."

⛔ **Isolate the variable.** *"When you're launching iterations, **don't change too many things at
once.** Keep your tests clean. Change one variable at a time — whether that's a hook visual, a
headline, the CTA."*

## 2. FIX — iterating on a near-miss

> "Sometimes, an ad is **almost there.** Maybe the hook is strong but the CTA is weak. Maybe the
> watch time is high but people aren't clicking."

| Symptom | The fix |
|---|---|
| **Thumbstop is low** | A new, more engaging opening hook headline or visual |
| **CTR is low** | Refine the value prop, add urgency or a promo, or highlight a different benefit |
| **CVR is low** | Explain the USPs better — and where possible, align the landing page with the ad's message |

⛔ **Be surgical.** *"Don't overhaul the entire Concept. **Change one or two key things based on
data, then retest.**"*

⭐ **The economic argument:** *"You've already paid to concept, script and shoot the ad. **A FIX gives
you another shot at ROI.**"*

## 3. ADAPT — repurposing a proven strategy

> "Take something that worked **elsewhere** — either inside your own account or from a competitor —
> and apply the same structure, angle or format to a new concept."

Two shapes: a competitor's format applied to your product with your own content; or a top-performing
ad for Product A rebuilt for Product B, keeping the format and angle while switching the visuals and
copy.

> "They're not totally original, but they're not pure copy/paste either. They're **smart reuse**
> adaptation **of what's proven to work.**"

⭐ *"ADAPTing is a great tool when you need to **fill your pipeline with reliable concepts** —
especially if you're in a slump or trying to stabilise performance quickly."*

## 4. NEW — testing unproven ideas

> "**No past data to back it up.** These are your riskiest requests — but also the most exciting."

A NEW concept might explore a new persona or use case, take a creative risk with humour, weirdness or
bold visuals, focus on a different product feature, or show the product in a way you have not before.

⚠️ *"Because there's no proof yet, **be selective about how many NEWs you launch each sprint.** They
take the most effort — and usually have the **lowest initial success rate.** But when a NEW concept
hits? **It can redefine your entire performance strategy.**"*

## ⭐ The balanced sprint mix

> "Here's a rule of thumb, for most brands:"

| Type | Share | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **MORE** | **30-40%** | *"Keeps your winners fresh and profitable"* |
| **FIX** | **20-30%** | *"Don't waste near-misses. Make them better"* |
| **NEW** | **20-30%** | *"Always test something wild"* |
| **ADAPT** | **10-20%** | *"Use this to stay consistent and efficient"* |

⚠️ **The ranges sum to 80-120%**, so this is a shape rather than an allocation. Pick points inside
each band that total 100 and say what you picked.

⭐ **The stated exception, and it is a big one:** *"If you're working with a brand that is relatively
new to paid social advertising, and has yet to unlock a clear win, you might have a mix of **40% New
and 60% Adapts.**"* — which is 0% MORE and 0% FIX, because there is nothing yet to build on or
rescue. **The mix follows brand maturity.**

> "**Just make sure every sprint includes a mix.** That's how you balance stability and exploration."

## Producing a sprint plan

**Deliver:**

1. **The volume**, derived from all five variables with each one's contribution named
2. ⛔ **The unit and the time base stated** — Concepts per week, Variants per Concept, and the ad
   count that implies
3. **The mix**, as actual counts per type, summing to the volume
4. **The win criteria for this account**, with the CPA target and the 20× spend threshold calculated
5. **The read date** — when early indicators get checked and when a final call can be made
6. ⛔ **A line naming every input that was assumed rather than given**

⚠️ **If the team's capacity was not stated, do not plan a volume.** Ask. The course's own failure
mode is a strategy that does not match the resources, and a plan that ignores capacity produces the
twelve-then-nothing pattern it exists to prevent.
