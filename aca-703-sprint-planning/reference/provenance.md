## ⛔ This course and Course 601 answer the same question incompatibly

**Neither cites the other.** Both are ACA, both are titled around creative volume.

| | **Course 601**, Mirella Crespi | **Course 703**, Chloe Rhys |
|---|---|---|
| **Indexed on** | ⛔ **Monthly** spend | ⛔ **Daily** spend |
| **Shape** | A six-tier lookup ladder | **Five variables** you derive from |
| **Unit** | "creatives", or concepts with 3-5 variations | **Concepts**, each in its own ad set, × **3-6 Variants** |
| **Worked case** | $250k/month × 40% ÷ $2,000 per test = **50 tests/month** | Jack Archer: **9-12 Concepts/week**, **50-70 ads/week** |

⛔ **The time base is the trap.** $10,000/day is roughly $300,000/month — Course 601's tier 5. That
tier derives ~50 tests a month; this course puts a comparable account at 9-12 concepts and 50-70 ads
**per week**. Read as ads, that is roughly 4-5× denser.

⭐ **They are not really contradicting each other, they are counting different objects** — 601 counts
*tests* and 703 counts *ads*, with concepts in between. But nothing in either course says so, and a
team handed both will produce a plan that is wrong by a multiple.

⚠️ **Course 601's is a lookup table and this one is a derivation.** When both are available, this
course's five variables are the more defensible instrument, because they include team capacity and
campaign structure — the two things that actually cap output and that a spend-indexed ladder cannot
see.

## ⭐ Where the two courses independently agree

**Course 601:** *"roughly **$2,000 per test** to identify a winner"*, alongside a **$100 CPA**.

**Course 703:** a win must hold through *"at least **20x your CPA.**"*.

**20 × $100 = $2,000.** ⭐ **Two faculty, two courses, no cross-reference, the same number.** That is
the strongest corroboration anywhere in this programme, and it makes the 20× rule the more useful
form: it scales with the account instead of asserting a fixed budget.

## What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Chloe's, quoted directly** — the five variables, the cadence rules, the
turnaround times, the learning-phase guidance, the no-spend protocol, all four win criteria, all four
request types and the mix.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the three-file split, the routing table, rendering the
variables and request types as tables, and the sprint-plan output checklist.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The daily-against-monthly warning** and the comparison with Course 601. Neither course knows
  the other exists.
- ⭐ **The observation that 20 × $100 = $2,000**, and that two ACA courses reached the same threshold
  independently.
- ⭐ **The reading that win criterion 3 is what makes it a *concept* win rather than an *ad* win**,
  and therefore what justifies the MORE pipeline. She states the criterion and not its consequence.
- ⭐ **The note that brand maturity changes the mix rather than the count**, which links variable 2 to
  the four request types — a connection the course makes implicitly across two modules.
- ⭐ **Reading the 40% NEW / 60% ADAPT exception as 0% MORE and 0% FIX**, because a brand with no win
  has nothing to build on or rescue.
- ⚠️ **The note that the mix percentages sum to 80-120%** and need points chosen inside the bands.
- ⛔ **The point that an ad which never spent produced no evidence**, so filing it as a loss corrupts
  every pattern later drawn from the tracker. She gives the protocol and not this reason.
- ⛔ **The rule not to plan a volume without the team's stated capacity**, since the course's own
  named failure mode is a strategy that does not match the resources.
- ⚠️ **Flagging the two different variant ranges.**
