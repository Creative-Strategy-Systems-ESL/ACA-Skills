# How much, and how often

> "If there's one question every creative strategist gets asked, it's this: **'How many ads should we
> be making every week?'**"

> "Not every brand needs the same volume of output. **There's no universal number that works across
> the board.**"

## ⛔ Before any number: which spend, and what unit

**This course indexes on DAILY spend.** Course 601's ladder indexes on **monthly**. ⛔ A reader
converting between them without noticing is out by roughly 30×.

**And the unit differs too.** This course counts **Concepts**, each launched in its own ad set with
**3-6 Variants**, so a weekly concept count multiplies into a much larger ad count. Always say which
you are quoting.

---

## The five variables

### 1. Spend level

> "The more you spend, the faster your ads fatigue. And the faster your ads fatigue, **the more
> creative you need in rotation.**"

> "A brand spending over **$10,000/day** is going to hit fatigue much faster than a brand spending
> **$100/day.**"

⭐ **Her worked account, and the only concrete volume figure in the course:**

> "For **Jack Archer**, we often launch **9 to 12 new Concepts per week.** Each Concept is launched
> in its own Ad Set, with **4-6 Variants.** So, across a week, that's typically anywhere from **50 to
> 70 ads** hitting the account."

**Below that:** *"If you're spending... under **$500/day**, you can operate with a lower volume. But
remember, **lower volume means slower learnings.** You have to be more intentional about what you
test."*

⚠️ **She gives 4-6 variants here and 3-6 under variable 4.** Both are hers; treat the range as 3-6
and say so.

### 2. Brand maturity

| State | Mode | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **New to paid social** | ⭐ **Exploration** | *"prioritize diversity—different angles, formats, tones, and audiences. You're still learning what works. So, volume helps you explore that surface area."* |
| **Mature**, with a library of learnings | **Refinement** | *"You're exploiting known winners, iterating more on top performing styles and angles"* |

⭐ *"So your volume might stay high — **but the type of creative you make will shift.**"* Maturity
changes the *mix*, not necessarily the count. That is what connects this variable to the four request
types.

### 3. Team bandwidth

> "Let's say you've scoped out your ideal sprint: you want to launch 10 new Concepts a week, each
> with 5 Variants. That's 50 ads a week. **But… can your team handle that?**"

> "This is where a lot of teams break down. **They build a creative strategy that doesn't match their
> actual resources.**"

⭐ **The line to quote when someone wants to scale output:**

> "**Consistency beats chaos. Launching 6 Concepts a week, every week, will outperform 12 one week
> and none for the next month.** Find your team's creative **'cruising speed.'** That's how you
> define your ideal volume target."

### 4. Testing structure

> "Your campaign setup has a big impact on how much creative you can test."

**The question:** are ads launched into dedicated testing campaigns with flexible budgets, or fed
into evergreen campaigns alongside existing winners?

⛔ *"If your new ads are fighting with high-spending BAU ads in the same campaign? **Your new ads
might never get spend or impressions.**"*

⭐ **Her recommendation:** *"Launching each Concept in its own Ad Set, with 3-6 Variants. That
isolates performance and ensures each idea gets a clean read. And when you're able to do that across
multiple Concepts per week — you start building real **learning velocity.**"*

### 5. Goal type

| Sprint goal | Volume posture |
|---|---|
| **Growth** — scale aggressively | ⭐ **Test more.** Push volume, look for net-new winners, new personas, audiences to expand into |
| **Efficiency** — tight budget, maybe Q1 | **Iterate more.** Reduce volume, increase precision, focus on proven structures |
| **Stabilisation** | *"Do both, carefully"* |

> "Volume should reflect your sprint goal."

## Launch cadence

> "There is **no single “best” day** to launch creative from having tested thousands of ads. I've
> tested Mondays, Fridays, mid-week, weekends. And what we've found is that **the day itself doesn't
> matter as much as the cadence and consistency—without**"

### You do not need a fixed launch day

> "At OpenStore, we aim to get creative into the ad account **as soon as it's ready.** If a Concept
> has gone through review, passed QA, and been uploaded — it gets launched."

**The exceptions, where date alignment does matter:** timed promos · back-in-stock pushes · new
product launches.

⭐ *"For business-as-usual creative, there's no benefit to holding a great concept until Monday if
it's ready on Thursday. **In fact, waiting can slow your feedback loop.**"*

### Expected turnaround, not arbitrary deadlines

| Work | Expected turnaround |
|---|---|
| **A net-new Concept** | **5-7 business days** from brief to first draft |
| **A quick iteration** — alternate CTAs or hooks | **1-3 days** |

> "I use these timelines as **guidelines, not hard rules.** And the benefit of this model? It allows
> your designers and editors to work with more autonomy, and it prevents unnecessary pressure just
> to hit a random calendar date."

⭐ **And it buys responsiveness:** *"If a new angle is working, you can submit a brief for an
iteration immediately — without waiting for the next 'launch day.'"*

### Two priority tags, and only two

Every job is tagged either **Business-as-usual (BAU)** or **Time-sensitive / high-priority**, the
latter paired with a specific due date.

⛔ *"**If you treat every single Concept as urgent, you'll burn your editors out and miss deadlines
anyway.**"*

## When a result is readable

> "Instead of sitting impatiently refreshing Ads Manager waiting to see immediate results, **you need
> to give Meta time to optimize.**"

**The Learning Phase:** *"when Meta is still figuring out how to deliver your ad. This phase
typically lasts until the ad set gets **50 optimization events** — for example, 50 purchases or
adds-to-cart. During Learning, performance is unstable. You could possibly see big swings in CPA, CTR
and spend."*

**Her rule of thumb:**

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Days 2-3** | Start checking **early indicators** |
| **Days 7-14**, or once the ad exits Learning | ⭐ **Don't make final calls before this** |
| Low-spend brands | ⚠️ *"Be patient with low-spend brands—it might take much longer to gather enough data"* |

## ⛔ When ads get turned off early, or never spend

> "Let's address the elephant in the room: sometimes ads just don't get spend, or media buyers turn
> off your ads before they get a fair shot."

⚠️ **She takes the media buyer's side first**, which is what makes the protocol usable: *"That's
fair, they're responsible for efficiency, or may have missed some early engagement indicators."*

**If an ad was prematurely paused:**

1. **Ask directly why.** Spend limits? Performance? A pacing issue?
2. **Check if it launched in a test campaign.** If not, it may have been competing with other high
   performers
3. **Request a retest** in a dedicated ad set if it did not get enough delivery
4. ⭐ **Tag it "Not Enough Spend" in your tracker**, so it stays on the radar for a future retest

**If an ad gets zero spend at all**, three things to check:

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| **Ad set audience overlap** | *"If you've got other ads targeting the same audience, Meta might prioritize the ones with more history"* |
| **Bid cap or manual bidding** | *"Aggressive bid caps can restrict delivery — especially on new ads"* |
| **Is the account in efficiency mode?** | *"During periods of reduced spend, media buyers often cut back on testing. **New ads are the first to go**"* |

⭐ **Do not write it off:** *"At OpenStore, we sometimes re-launch promising Concepts a few weeks
later — especially if we believe the creative is strong but just didn't get a fair shot."*

> "**This happens more often than you'd think — and it's not always the creative's fault.**"

⛔ **This is the section that protects a strategist's judgement from a delivery artefact.** An ad that
never spent produced no evidence, and filing it as a loss corrupts every pattern you later draw from
the tracker.
