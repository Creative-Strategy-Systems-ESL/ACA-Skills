# The 10 types of angles

The angle is the **why**: the reason someone should buy right now. It is the single most useful
lever in creative, because the same offer aimed at the same audience performs completely
differently depending on the angle chosen.

ACA's ten, each with its core message, when to use it, the psychological triggers it pulls, and
example applications.

**One angle per ad.** An ad attempting three angles communicates none of them clearly. Test angles
against each other rather than stacking them inside one execution.

---

## 1. Promotion
> **"Because it's on sale"**

Emphasises limited-time offers, discounts, special deals, or exclusive pricing. Creates urgency and
appeals to value-conscious customers.

**When to use:** during sales events or seasonal promotions · to clear inventory or increase
short-term revenue · when targeting price-sensitive audiences · to reactivate dormant customers

**Psychological triggers:** scarcity · loss aversion · value perception

**Examples:**
- `[PRODUCT]`: "Flash Sale: 48 hours only! Save 30% on all [PRODUCT] bundles"
- `[SERVICE]`: "Join today and get your first month free - limited spots available"
- `[APP]`: "Unlock all premium features at 50% off - this weekend only!"

---

## 2. Education
> **"Because it does this"**

Focuses on product functionality, features, and unique mechanisms. Appeals to rational
decision-making and works well for complex or innovative products.

**When to use:** for products with unique technical features · when targeting logical,
information-driven buyers · for higher-priced items that require justification · when your solution
has a unique mechanism

**Psychological triggers:** curiosity · desire for mastery and knowledge · rational problem-solving

**Examples:**
- `[TECH PRODUCT]`: "How our proprietary algorithm analyzes 50+ sleep factors to improve your rest quality"
- `[SKINCARE]`: "The science behind our patent-pending delivery system that reaches 7 skin layers"
- `[SOFTWARE]`: "See how our platform automates your workflow in 3 simple steps"

---

## 3. Social Proof
> **"Because everyone loves it"**

Leverages the wisdom of crowds and demonstrates widespread adoption. Shows that others have already
made the purchase decision and are satisfied.

**When to use:** when you have impressive customer numbers · for products with strong reviews and
ratings · in competitive markets where trust is crucial · for status-oriented products

**Psychological triggers:** conformity bias · fear of missing out · desire for belonging

**Examples:**
- `[PRODUCT]`: "Join the 250,000+ customers who've transformed their [problem area] with [product]"
- `[SERVICE]`: "Why 98% of customers renew their subscription after the first year"
- `[APP]`: "The #1 rated solution in the App Store with over 10,000 five-star reviews"

---

## 4. Influencer
> **"Because this person loves it"**

Uses trusted personalities, experts, or celebrities to build credibility. Leverages transferred
authority and trust from a **specific individual** rather than the crowd.

**When to use:** when endorsed by relevant experts or celebrities · for products where expertise
matters in the decision · when targeting communities that follow influencers · to establish
authority in new markets

**Psychological triggers:** authority bias · aspirational identity · credibility through association

**Examples:**
- `[PRODUCT]`: "The exact [product] that [celebrity/expert] uses daily for [benefit]"
- `[FITNESS]`: "Developed with Olympic trainer [name] to maximize your workout results"
- `[BEAUTY]`: "The secret weapon in [celebrity]'s skincare routine for the past 5 years"

⚠️ Distinct from Social Proof: the crowd versus one trusted person. They fail differently too, since
an influencer angle inherits that individual's reputation, good and bad.

---

## 5. Gifting
> **"Because someone you love needs it"**

Positions the product as a perfect gift for someone else. Leverages the buyer's desire to please
others and be seen as thoughtful.

**When to use:** during holiday seasons or special occasions · for products with gifting potential ·
**when targeting gift-givers rather than end-users** · for premium products that make impressive
gifts

**Psychological triggers:** altruism · social signaling · relationship reinforcement

**Examples:**
- `[PRODUCT]`: "The gift they'll actually use every day (unlike last year's present)"
- `[SUBSCRIPTION]`: "Give the gift of [benefit] that keeps giving all year round"
- `[EXPERIENCE]`: "Create unforgettable memories for someone special with our [product]"

⚠️ The buyer and the user are different people here, so the pain point that converts belongs to the
giver, not the recipient.

---

## 6. New Release
> **"Because it's new"**

Emphasises novelty, innovation, or being first. Appeals to early adopters and those who want to
stay ahead of trends.

**When to use:** for product launches or feature updates · when targeting innovators and early
adopters · when your product represents a genuine innovation · to create renewed interest

**Psychological triggers:** novelty seeking · status from being first · curiosity about new
solutions

**Examples:**
- `[PRODUCT]`: "Introducing the first-ever [product] that [unique benefit]"
- `[TECH]`: "Just released: The next generation [product] with [new feature]"
- `[SERVICE]`: "Be among the first to experience our revolutionary approach to [problem]"

---

## 7. Emotional Benefit
> **"Because it'll make you feel better"**

Focuses on the emotional transformation or relief the product provides. Speaks directly to feelings
rather than function.

**When to use:** for products that address emotional needs · when targeting emotionally-driven
buyers · for lifestyle and personal development offerings

**Psychological triggers:** desire for emotional states (joy, relief, confidence) · pain avoidance ·
identity reinforcement

**Examples:**
- `[FASHION]`: "Walk into any room with the confidence that comes from knowing you look your best"
- `[WELLNESS]`: "Finally experience the calm and peace you've been searching for"
- `[HOME]`: "The joy of coming home to a space that feels truly yours"

---

## 8. Functional Benefit
> **"Because it will make this easier"**

Emphasises practical value, efficiency, and problem-solving. Focuses on how the product makes life
easier.

**When to use:** for utility-focused products that save time or effort · when targeting practical,
solution-oriented buyers · for products with clear functional advantages

**Psychological triggers:** desire for ease and convenience · problem-solution alignment ·
practical value perception

**Examples:**
- `[TOOL]`: "Complete your [task] in half the time with none of the frustration"
- `[SOFTWARE]`: "Automate the 3 most time-consuming parts of your [process]"
- `[APPLIANCE]`: "The smart solution that handles [task] while you focus on what matters"

⚠️ Emotional and Functional Benefit are the pair most often confused. The test: does the promise
name a **feeling** or a **task**? Most products can run both, and running both as separate ads is
usually more informative than trying to blend them.

---

## 9. Comparison
> **"Because it's better than our competitors"**

Directly or indirectly contrasts your offering with the alternatives. Highlights competitive
advantages and unique selling points.

**When to use:** in competitive markets with clear alternatives · when you have specific advantages
over competitors · when targeting customers with competing options · for differentiation in
saturated markets

**Psychological triggers:** contrast effect · loss aversion · maximization tendency

**Examples:**
- `[PRODUCT]`: "Why our [product] outperforms leading brands in all 5 key performance tests"
- `[SERVICE]`: "The only [service] that guarantees [benefit] without [common competitor limitation]"
- `[SOFTWARE]`: "What our customers say after switching from [competitor]"

---

## 10. Seasonality
> **"Because now is the time of year you need this"**

Connects to seasonal needs, timely relevance, or calendar-based urgency. Creates time-specific
relevance.

**When to use:** for products with seasonal utility · during specific times of year (holidays,
seasons, events) · when targeting seasonal pain points or desires · for creating calendar-based
urgency

**Psychological triggers:** timeliness · preparedness · cultural and seasonal associations

**Examples:**
- `[SUMMER PRODUCT]`: "Be ready when the temperatures rise with the only [product] that [summer benefit]"
- `[HOLIDAY]`: "Make this [holiday] season memorable with our exclusive [offering]"
- `[SEASONAL CHALLENGE]`: "The solution to [seasonal problem] that works even in the harshest [season]"

⚠️ Distinct from Promotion, though they often ship together. Seasonality says the *need* is now;
Promotion says the *deal* is now. A seasonal angle works without a discount.

---

## Choosing an angle

The worksheet asks, for each type: **how could you apply this angle to your offer?** Working all
ten is the exercise, and it is worth doing even for the angles that seem wrong, because the
forced attempt often surfaces a claim the obvious angles miss.

For choosing between them:

- **What does the evidence support?** Social Proof without numbers and Comparison without a real
  advantage are claims the audience can check.
- **What is the awareness stage?** Education and Emotional Benefit reach earlier stages; Promotion
  and Comparison assume the person already knows what the product is.
- **What has the account already run?** Angle exhaustion is real. If every ad for six months has
  been Functional Benefit, the untested angles are where the upside is, regardless of which
  performs best in theory.

## The Multi-Angle Strategy Worksheet

The worksheet does not stop at the ten. It closes with a section that turns ten filled-in options
into a plan, and it is the half that makes the exercise worth doing.

**Your top 3 most promising angles** — based on product, audience and objectives, which three, and
**why**? The "why" column is the section. Three angles named without reasons is a shortlist; three
with reasons is an argument someone can disagree with.

**Audience-angle matching** — different segments respond to different angles. Map them:

| Audience segment | Primary angle | Secondary angle |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

This is the part most often skipped and it is where the leverage is. The same offer aimed at two
segments frequently wants two different angles, and running one angle at everyone is how an account
plateaus.

**Angle combinations** — sometimes combining creates stronger messaging. List two or three:

> `1. [angle] + [angle] = `
> `2. [angle] + [angle] = `
> `3. [angle] + [angle] = `

⚠️ **This sits in tension with "one angle per ad", and both are right.** A combination is one
angle carrying a second as support — Social Proof + Promotion is a promotion made credible, not two
competing arguments. If both angles want to be the headline, it is not a combination, it is a
cluttered ad.

**Angle testing plan** — how will you test to identify the most effective? Sequence and a way to
read the result, not just a list.

**Final notes & insights** — anything else about angle strategy for this specific product.
