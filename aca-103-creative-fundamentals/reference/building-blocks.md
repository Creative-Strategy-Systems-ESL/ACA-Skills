# The modular building blocks framework

The core of Course 103. An ad is not a monolith, it is an assembly of interchangeable parts. Once
you see the parts, you can diagnose which one is failing and swap it without rebuilding the ad.

## The three-part structure

Every successful direct response ad has it.

### The Hook (0-3 seconds)

The scroll-stopping moment. Roughly three seconds to convince someone not to scroll past. The hook
must immediately signal relevance and generate curiosity.

Great hooks do one or more of these:

- **Identify a specific pain point** — *"Tired of struggling with patchy beard growth?"*
- **Challenge a common belief** — *"The one skincare ingredient dermatologists say you should NEVER use"*
- **Make a bold promise** — *"How we generated 427 qualified leads in 24 hours without paid ads"*
- **Create a pattern interrupt** — something unexpected that breaks the scroll rhythm

### The Body

The main persuasive content. Where the argument is made, the product is shown, and objections are
handled.

### The CTA

The action-driving closer.

## The 14 blocks, in three categories

### Structure blocks — the backbone

| Block | Is |
|---|---|
| **Hook Block** | Your attention-grabbing opener |
| **Body Block** | Your main persuasive content |
| **CTA Block** | Your action-driving closer |

### Product blocks — these showcase your offering

| Block | Is |
|---|---|
| **Product Introduction** | How you first reveal the product |
| **Demonstration** | Showing the product in action |
| **Features / Benefits** | Highlighting key selling points |
| **Buying Experience** | Making the purchase process clear |
| **Unboxing** | Creating anticipation and trust |

### Person blocks — these create emotional connection

| Block | Is |
|---|---|
| **Problem Statement** | Articulating the pain point |
| **Failed Alternatives** | What did not work before |
| **Desired Outcome** | The transformation promised |
| **Before & After** | Visual proof of change |
| **Social Proof** | Testimonials and social validation |
| **Storytelling** | Narrative that ties everything together |

### Why the categories matter

Product blocks argue; Person blocks connect. An ad built only from Product blocks is a
specification sheet, and one built only from Person blocks never explains what is being sold. The
category split is a fast diagnostic for an ad that feels flat: check whether one category is
missing entirely.

## Why modularity is the point

> "The power of this approach is its flexibility. Current hook has a low hook rate? Swap in a
> different one. Seeing a big drop off in the part of the video with a testimonial? Switch it for a
> visual product demo. Test both and let the data decide."

And the consequence for optimisation:

> "Performance drops by 20%? Perhaps it's time to test a new hook while keeping the high-performing
> body and CTA intact. This targeted approach to optimization is far more efficient than scrapping
> entire ads and starting over. **It's surgical rather than shotgun.**"

## Proven block combinations

Three high-performing formats:

**Problem-Solution format**
- Hook: Problem Block (emotional pain point)
- Body: Demonstration Block + Failed Alternatives Block
- CTA: Desired Outcome Block + CTA Block

**Authority-Driven format**
- Hook: Social Proof Block (expert endorsement)
- Body: Features/Benefits Block + Demonstration Block
- CTA: CTA Block with risk reversal

**Transformation format**
- Hook: Before & After Block (visual impact)
- Body: Storytelling Block + Product Introduction
- CTA: Social Proof Block + CTA Block

These are starting points, not a menu to pick from and stop. The value of naming them is that they
give you a baseline to vary deliberately.

## Building your block library

The implementation process, in four steps.

**1. Audit your existing assets**
- Review current content and categorise elements into the appropriate blocks
- Note which blocks you are missing or need to strengthen

**2. Create block variations**
- Develop 3 to 5 different hooks addressing various pain points
- Record multiple product demonstrations from different angles
- Collect diverse testimonials highlighting different benefits

**3. Test systematically**
- Create A/B tests that isolate specific blocks
- Example: same body and CTA, test Hook A against Hook B
- Let data determine which blocks perform best for which audiences

**4. Document performance**
- Track which blocks perform best together
- Build a performance library to reference for future campaigns

> "Different audiences may respond to different block combinations. What works for cold traffic
> might not work for retargeting audiences who are already familiar with your brand."

The library is the compounding asset here. A team that has run fifty ads without cataloguing blocks
has fifty ads; a team that catalogued them has a reusable inventory and a record of what
combinations worked on whom.

## Five real-world success patterns

Drawn from analysing thousands of high-performing ads across industries.

**1. The First 3 Seconds Rule.** The most successful ads deliver their core promise within the
first three seconds. *"Never save your best for last - viewers won't stick around."*

**2. Authenticity trumps production value.** Authentic, believable content consistently outperforms
high-production, overly polished ads. Especially true for testimonials and demonstrations.

**3. Emotional → Logical → Emotional.** Top performers open with emotional appeal (the pain or
desire), provide logical support (features, proof), then close with emotional reinforcement (the
transformation).

**4. One message, one ad.** Communicating too many benefits dilutes impact. The best ads focus on a
single core message, hammered home through multiple angles.

**5. Visual reinforcement.** When visuals directly support the spoken or written message,
comprehension and retention increase sharply. *"Misalignment between visuals and message creates
cognitive dissonance and reduces performance."*

Pattern 3 and pattern 4 are the two most often violated together: an ad that opens logically with a
feature list and covers four benefits fails both, and it is the single most common shape of a
mediocre ad.

## Reverse engineering any ad

The framework's other use: taking apart someone else's winner.

**1. Identify the three-part structure.** Where is the hook? Where does the body begin? When does
it transition to the CTA?

**2. Catalog the building blocks.** Which specific blocks are used? Is it leading with a problem
block or social proof? Demonstration or storytelling in the body?

**3. Note the execution details.** How long is each section? What visual techniques? What emotional
triggers are activated?

**4. Create your swipe file.** Build a collection of reverse-engineered ads that resonated,
organised by block structure.

`worksheet.md` turns this into the formal analysis ACA ships, and it ends by asking for the ad's
**core building block formula**, for example:

> Hook (Question) → Problem → Failed Alternatives → Demonstration → Social Proof → CTA with Scarcity

That formula is the transferable artifact. The specific ad is not reusable; its block sequence is.
