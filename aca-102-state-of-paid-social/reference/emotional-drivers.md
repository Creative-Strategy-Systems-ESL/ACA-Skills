# Emotional drivers

The second layer. Awareness tells you what the person can hear; this tells you what they actually
want underneath the purchase.

## Why feelings before features

The course's position is that people do not buy features, they buy the feeling a feature produces.
A feature is evidence for a feeling; on its own it is a specification. The practical consequence is
that the emotional driver should be chosen **before** the copy is written, because the driver
determines which features are even worth mentioning.

Two frameworks are used to name the driver.

## Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Five levels, from the base up:

1. **Physiological** — survival basics
2. **Safety** — security, stability, protection
3. **Belonging** — love, connection, community
4. **Esteem** — respect, recognition, status, achievement
5. **Self-actualization** — fulfilment, growth, creativity, purpose

Maslow is the coarse cut. It is useful for locating roughly which register a product operates in,
and less useful for writing a specific line, which is what the Life-Force 8 is for.

## The Life-Force 8

Eight drives described as hard-wired rather than learned. Each carries the explanation as the
course gives it, because the explanation is what makes the drive usable.

**1. Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension**
Products promising health, vitality, or safety tap this drive.

**2. Enjoyment of food and beverages**
We are programmed to seek pleasurable taste experiences.

**3. Freedom from fear, pain, and danger**
People spend heavily to eliminate worry, whether financial, physical or emotional.

**4. Sexual companionship**
Beyond romance, this includes feeling desirable and attractive.

**5. Comfortable living conditions**
Products that make life easier, more efficient, or more luxurious.

**6. To be superior; winning**
Appeals to status, achievement, and recognition.

**7. Care and protection of loved ones**
Particularly powerful for parents, pet owners, and caregivers.

**8. Social approval**
We crave acceptance and respect from our peers.

### Using them without flattening the product

Most products touch several drives. The worksheet asks you to check all that apply, then identify
**the primary** transformation, and the second step is the one that matters. A message aimed at
three drives at once usually reads as aimed at none.

The tie-breaker worth applying: which drive explains why they would buy **now**, rather than
agreeing that the product is nice in principle. Freedom from fear and care of loved ones tend to
produce urgency; comfortable living conditions and social approval tend to produce agreement.

## The emotional transformation

The frame the worksheet uses to turn a driver into copy direction:

> **FROM:** [current negative emotion] → **TO:** [desired positive emotion]

And two supporting lists:

- What the product helps customers **avoid feeling**
- What the product helps customers **feel**

**Write these in the customer's language, not the brand's.** Research, reviews and support tickets
carry the actual words, and those words outperform any paraphrase, because they were produced by
someone in the state you are trying to name. "I'm tired of waking up tired" is worth more than
"combats fatigue".

**Emotions, not outcomes.** "From tired to energised" is an emotional transformation. "From no
subscription to subscribed" is a transaction, and it will not generate a hook. If the FROM state
reads like a description of not owning the product yet, it needs another pass.

## How this feeds the rest of the worksheet

The emotional driver chosen here is the raw material for:

- **Section 4, the message framing matrix** — the same driver expressed as a benefit, a pain
  avoidance, a status claim, a mechanism, and a story
- **Section 5, hook development** — hooks that trigger the driver, pitched at the right awareness
  stage
- **Section 6, sequencing** — the emotional trigger column, exposure by exposure

Getting the driver wrong is expensive, because everything downstream inherits the error and each
piece will look internally consistent.
