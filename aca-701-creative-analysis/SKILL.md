**Evan Lee**, head of partnerships at **Motion**. 16m 25s, 1 module.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.**

**To analyse an account, required:**

1. **The performance data**, per creative — at minimum spend and the goal metric
2. **The goal metric itself**, and its target. ⭐ Every report in this course is built by choosing one

**Optional, and each one opens a report:**

3. Video play rates at 25/50/75/100%, for drop-off analysis
4. 1-second, 3-second and 15-second view counts, for the engagement metrics
5. What each ad actually was — format, creator, message — since patterns are what you are looking for
6. Landing pages, if you want the funnel view

⛔ **Never invent a benchmark.** This course gives several and they are Motion's observations across
its customer base, not universal standards — and two of them conflict with other ACA courses.

## The through-line

> "**Study your top performing ads to raise the ceiling** of your ad performance, and some of your
> weakest ads **to raise the floor.**"

⭐ **That is the whole analysis method in one line**, and it is why the course builds two reports
rather than one. Most teams only ever look at winners, which improves the best case and leaves the
worst case exactly where it was.

## How to work through it

**Definitions before readings.** Every metric here has a specific denominator, and reading a report with the
wrong one in mind produces a confident wrong conclusion.

1. ⛔ **Define the metrics first** from **The metrics, and the five reports** (below), hold rate especially. ⚠️ **This
   course divides 15-second views by impressions; Course 402 divides 10-second views by 3-second views.**
   They are not comparable, and nothing warns you at the point of use.
2. **Split engagement from conversion**, same file. This is what stops a high-thumbstop ad being called a
   winner.
3. **Walk the play-rate waterfall** and the drop-off points, which is where the actual diagnosis happens.
4. **Then the reports** — leaderboard, top performers, and the what-is-working and what-is-not filters — so
   the method has somewhere to land.
5. **Finish on the hook iteration report**, which turns the analysis into the next brief rather than a
   summary.
6. **Check they can say which three seconds to change and why.** "The ad underperformed" means the waterfall
   step did not land.

## Answering well

**Two metric families, and they answer different questions.** Engagement metrics tell you how ads
perform in the upper funnel — *"even in conversion campaigns, you should keep an eye out for these."*
Conversion metrics tell you what it cost.

**Group by asset, not by ad.** ⭐ *"What creative teams really care about is how the asset performed
holistically"*, rather than split across the one-off ads an ad account creates.

**Patterns are the output, not rankings.** *"If half of your top ten ads use the same format,
messaging or creators, that's something you might want to double down on."*

**Losers are data.** *"The ones that fail can be valuable too... Is it the message? Do they have weak
hooks, or is the CTA unclear?"*

⭐ **Set the goal as a trade, not an improvement.** His worked example: an ad with a great conversion
rate and a low thumbstop. *"The goal I would set with my creative team is to **increase the thumbstop
ratio while maintaining the conversion rate**"* — then swap only the first three seconds.

### ⛔ Its metric definitions contradict other ACA courses

**This is the most important thing in this file.** Three ACA courses define or benchmark the same

#### Hold rate — two incompatible formulas

| Course | Formula |
|---|---|
| **This course (701)**, Evan Lee | ⛔ **15-second views ÷ impressions** |
| **Course 402**, Chloe Rhys | ⛔ **10-second views ÷ 3-second views** |

⛔ **Different numerator and different denominator.** They are not two ways of expressing one number;
they measure different things. 402's is a *retention* rate among people who already stopped; 701's is
a *reach-weighted* rate across everyone served. **The same account will produce wildly different
values, and only 701 attaches a benchmark** — 8-12% typical, 20-30% for top performers, which is
meaningful **only** against 701's formula.

⚠️ **Evan flags the ambiguity himself:** *"There are a bunch of ways to calculate this metric, but one
of the most popular ways is..."* ⭐ **So the honest handling is: always state the formula beside the
number.** A hold rate quoted without its definition is unusable across teams.

#### CTR — three benchmarks across three courses

| Course | The figure |
|---|---|
| **This course (701)** | *"The average click-through rate for a Meta ad is around **1 to 2%**"* |
| **Course 402**, Chloe Rhys | Sweet spot **0.9-1.3%**; above 1.4%, question whether the traffic is qualified |
| **Course 210**, TikTok | **Above 2%** for ecommerce |

⛔ **An ad at 1.5% CTR is above average by 701, a qualification risk by 402, and failing by 210.**
Three courses, three positions, no cross-references. Platform and CTR variant explain some of it —
210 is TikTok, 402 is explicitly CTR (Link Click) — but **701 does not say which CTR its 1-2% refers
to**, and it discusses both CTR (all) and outbound CTR immediately beforehand.

## The metrics, and the five reports

> "The ultimate goal of an ad is to generate a conversion, but along the way, there are going to be
> **two categories of metrics** that will help you understand what's happening across your"

---

### Engagement metrics

> "Engagement metrics tell us how our ads are performing in the **higher stages of our funnel.**
> EVAN Even in conversion campaigns, you should keep an eye out for these data points"

| Metric | Evan's definition | His benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Thumbstop rate** | *"The percentage of people who were served the ad and watched for at least the first three seconds"* | None given |
| ⭐ **First frame retention** | **1-second views ÷ impressions** | ⚠️ *"According to Morella, this always should be **above 90%.** If it's not, analyze the first second. Frame by frame and figure out how to make it more engaging."* |
| ⛔ **Hold rate** | ⛔ **15-second views ÷ impressions** — *"the percentage of people who stuck around to watch at least 15 seconds"* | **8-12% typical**, *"top performers can get up to **20 or 30%**"* |
| **Waterfall rate** | Comparing **25 / 50 / 75 / 100%** play rates | *"Gives you an idea of the point in the video where most people are dropping off"* |
| **CTR (all)** and **outbound CTR** | Outbound *"measures only the percentage of viewers who can click through to your landing page"* | ⛔ *"The average click-through rate for a Meta ad is around **1 to 2%**"* |

⛔ **Two of these contradict other ACA courses outright.** Course 402 defines **hold rate as
10-second views ÷ 3-second views** — a different numerator *and* a different denominator — and puts
CTR's sweet spot at 0.9-1.3%. **Read **Provenance** (below) before quoting either.**

⚠️ **He hedges the hold-rate formula himself:** *"There are a bunch of ways to calculate this metric,
but one of the most popular ways is..."* — so even inside this course it is offered as one convention
among several.

⭐ **And his advice supersedes his own benchmarks:** *"I would suggest you look at the hold rates
among **your top performing creatives**, and figure out a benchmark that makes sense for your
brand."* He says the same about CTR: *"it's better to measure against your own internal
benchmarks."* **That instruction is the safe one and it is his.**

### Conversion metrics

> "Depending on your industry, your conversion metric is likely going to change."

| Business | Optimise for |
|---|---|
| **Ecommerce** | **ROAS** or **CPA** |
| **Apps, media, subscription** | **CPL** (cost per lead) · **CPD** (cost per demo) · **CPI** (cost per app install) |

> "To figure out what's profitable for you and to set your goals, you have to analyze a bunch of
> things, including **cost of goods sold and lifetime value.**"

⭐ **If you have no conversion data yet:** *"I recommend going a bit higher in the funnel... **Add to
cart** will give you some signal into how much volume is almost there — how close you are to your
first purchase."*

### The five reports

#### 1. The leaderboard — for the weekly check-in

**Last 7 days against the 7 before.** Total spend, the goal metric, and how many creatives launched
this week.

| Section | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Top creative** — *"like a Billboard top ten for your ads"* | How long an ad has been on it, how far it has moved, spend and goal metric |
| ⭐ **Patterns among the top ten** | *"If half of your top ten ads use the same format, messaging or creators, that's something you might want to double down on"* |
| **New entries** | *"You're always looking for winners. So if you have something that's cracked into the top ten, you need to take note"* |
| **Performance trend** | How many ads are scaling against declining, recently launched, or paused |

⭐ **The performance-trend section solves an unglamorous problem that wastes real time:** *"Sometimes
there's a ton of creative floating around and you're not always sure what's live and isn't."*

#### 2. Top performers — and 3. what is *not* working

**Filter to what is working:** his worked example is *"more than $500 in spend and a ROAS of 2× or
higher"* — a threshold plus a spend floor, so results are readable rather than noise.

⭐ **Then invert the filter.** *"Don't make the mistake of only looking at winners. **The ones that
fail can be valuable too.** Think critically why something might not be working. Is it the message?
Do they have weak hooks, or is the CTA unclear?"*

> "Study your top performing ads to **raise the ceiling** of your ad performance and some of your
> weakest ads to **raise the floor.**"

⚠️ **Group by asset.** *"Motion is automatically grouping your ads and metrics together, because what
creative teams really care about is **how the asset performed holistically**"* — rather than split
across duplicate ads in the ad account, which skews results.

#### 4. Group by landing page

> "this is aggregate performance. So groups all the landing pages connected to your ads."

**When one performs well, two moves:**

1. ⭐ **Lift its language into an ad.** *"If it's got a strong headline or CTA, apply this as a text
   overlay to an ad. It's a **low-lift way to capitalise on strong messaging**"*
2. **Pair more creative with it**, as long as the page is generic enough. If it fits none of your
   existing ads, make ads that do

⛔ *"You never want to send a customer to a landing page that feels out of place with the ad they've
clicked on."*

#### 5. ⭐ The hook iteration report

**Two axes, one from each metric family:** *"stop rate aka hook rate"* against *"click-to-
purchase ratio, aka conversion rate."*

> "I use this report to **identify low-hanging fruit.**"

**His worked example, and it is the model for every iteration decision in the course:**

> "take a look at the ad on the far right. It has **a great conversion rate but a lower thumbs stop
> rate.** The goal I would set with my creative team is to **increase the thumbs off ratio, while
> maintaining the conversion rate,** because the story is so strong. EVAN So let's go ahead and
> create two new versions where all we're doing is **swapping out the first**"

⭐ **Three things make that a good brief.** It names the metric to move, it names the metric that must
not move, and it names the only thing allowed to change. That is a testable instruction rather than
"make it better".

⭐ **And where to look for the replacement:** *"If your team is looking for inspiration on what to do
in the first three seconds, take a look at the ad on the left"* — the one already winning on
thumbstop. The report supplies both the problem and the reference.
