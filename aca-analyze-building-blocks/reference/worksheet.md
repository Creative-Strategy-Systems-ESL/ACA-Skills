# The worksheet

Five sections, in order. Every field, plus what a good answer looks like.

**Open the filled sheet with what you were working from**, so the analysis can be weighed later:

```
ANALYSED FROM: [the ad itself / a chronological description with timestamps /
                a partial description — say which parts were not observable]
PERFORMANCE DATA: [supplied, and what / none, so all ratings are craft judgement]
```

Two lines, and they change how every rating below them should be read. A teardown that does not say
what it was working from invites the reader to assume the strongest case.

## Section 2 — Product Block Identification

Five blocks. For each: **present (Y/N) · timestamp · style · effectiveness (1-10) · notes.**

| Block | Style options |
|---|---|
| **Product Introduction** | direct product shot · problem-then-product · benefit-first approach · other |
| **Demonstration** | how-to usage · before/after results · real-time results · comparison to alternative · user demonstration · other |
| **Features / Benefits Presentation** | feature → advantage → benefit · problem → solution → outcome · pain → remedy → relief · list/bullet points · animated graphics/text · other |
| **Buying Experience** | price reveal · shipping information · purchase process explanation · guarantee / risk reversal · limited time offer · other |
| **Unboxing** (if applicable) | customer unboxing · professional unboxing · packaging highlights · initial reaction · other |

**Mark every block, including the absent ones.** `Present: N` is a finding. A block left off the
sheet entirely is ambiguous between "not there" and "not looked at", and Section 4 depends on
knowing which.

`blocks.md` has what to look for in each and what its absence usually means.

## Section 3 — Person Block Identification

Six blocks. Same fields.

| Block | Approach options |
|---|---|
| **Problem Statement** | personal story / experience · common pain point · question-based · other |
| **Failed Alternatives** | prior solutions tried · competing product comparison · common misconceptions · "I've tried everything" approach · other |
| **Desired Outcome** | emotional transformation · physical/tangible results · lifestyle improvement · status enhancement · problem elimination · other |
| **Before & After** | side-by-side comparison · sequential reveal · testimonial transformation · results timeline · other |
| **Social Proof** | customer testimonials · reviews/ratings · user count/statistics · expert endorsements · awards/certifications · other |
| **Storytelling** | personal journey · customer success story · origin/founding story · problem-solution narrative · day-in-the-life scenario · other |

⚠️ **ACA's printed worksheet labels this section's first block "Product Introduction", repeating
the Section 2 heading.** Its fields are all problem-framing, and ACA's own AI prompt calls it
**Problem Statement**. Use Problem Statement. See `provenance.md`.

## Section 4 — Overall Analysis & Insights

Six questions. This is where the teardown becomes useful rather than merely complete.

- **Block combination assessment** — which blocks work particularly well together in this ad?
- **Missing blocks** — which blocks are notably absent that might have strengthened it?
- **Modular replication potential** — which specific blocks or combinations could be reused
  elsewhere?
- **Structure effectiveness** — how well does the Hook-Body-CTA structure work here? What could be
  improved?
- **Key performance drivers** — which 2-3 blocks contribute most to this ad's effectiveness?
- **Modular improvement suggestions** — how could this ad be improved by modifying, adding or
  rearranging specific blocks?

⭐ **Missing blocks is the question that earns the sheet.** It is easy to describe what an ad does
and hard to notice what it declines to do, and the absent block is frequently the opportunity.

Answer it with judgement, not by listing every unticked box. An ad with no Unboxing block is not
missing anything if it sells software. The useful version names one or two absences and says what
each would have bought:

✅ *"No Failed Alternatives block, in a category where the audience has demonstrably tried three
other things. That block is how an ad earns the right to make its claim here, and its absence is
why the proof section has to work so hard."*

⛔ `Missing: Unboxing, Before & After, Buying Experience.`

⚠️ **Key performance drivers is a judgement about the creative unless performance data was
supplied.** If it was not, say "contributes most" rather than "drove the results". The two are
different claims and only one of them is evidenced.

The part worth keeping.

**Primary awareness stage targeted:**
- [ ] Unaware
- [ ] Problem Aware
- [ ] Solution Aware
- [ ] Product Aware
- [ ] Most Aware

**Dominant emotional driver:**

**Core building block formula** — the essential sequence that makes this ad work:

> Example: `Hook (Question) → Problem → Failed Alternatives → Demonstration → Social Proof → CTA with Scarcity`

**Transferable creative principles** — what 2-3 principles from this ad could apply to other
products or services?

**Final notes & observations:**

### Writing the formula well

The formula is the deliverable. Someone who reads only this section should get the transferable
thing.

- **Include only load-bearing blocks.** A formula listing every block present is an inventory, not
  a formula. If removing a block would not change how the ad works, leave it out.
- **Keep the order.** The sequence is the content. The same blocks in a different order are a
  different ad.
- **Qualify the blocks that carry a mechanism.** `CTA with Scarcity` says more than `CTA`.
- **Sanity check it against Section 1.** If the formula opens with a block that appears at 0:12,
  something upstream is wrong.

**Transferable principles should not be the formula restated.** The formula is what this ad did; a
principle is why it worked and applies to a product with a different shape.

---

## What to do with a completed sheet

The ad is not reusable. Copying it produces a worse version of someone else's creative. The block
sequence is reusable, and running it against a different offer, angle and audience is the actual
technique.

**Three or four teardowns of the same advertiser beat one done in depth.** The blocks they repeat
show what they have found works. The blocks nobody in the category uses show what nobody is trying,
which is usually the more valuable of the two findings.
