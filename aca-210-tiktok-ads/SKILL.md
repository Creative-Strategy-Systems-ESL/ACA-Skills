## Answering well

**Lead with the format argument, not the tactics.** The most common failure the course describes is
treating TikTok as a placement to resize into. ACA's figure: a study of 3,500 ads found that
swapping TV-style spots for TikTok-first videos nearly doubled watch time and lifted purchase intent
37%.

**Sub-culture mining is the distinctive idea here.** Not "target interests" — find the weirdly
specific hashtag communities, watch their content, and take *the language* back into the hook.
That is the part people skip and it is what the platform rewards.

**The thresholds are the useful bit.** Most advice about creative fatigue is vague; this course
gives numbers — CPA up 20% on its week-one average, or CTR down 30%, means rotate. Give those.

**Do not oversell the AI tooling.** Symphony and the rest are described as they were; treat named
features as time-stamped rather than current.

## How the algorithm decides

⚠️ **The course's own heading is "How the 2025 Algorithm Decides Your Fate."** Treat everything in
this section as describing that point in time.

Three pillars the ranking model feeds on:

1. **User interactions** — swipes, rewatches, comments, shares, profile taps
2. **Video information** — captions, on-screen text, hashtags, sounds
3. **Device and account settings** — language, locale, device type

### ⭐ But the power curve is the first three seconds

> "Your opening frame drives the earliest watch-time signal, and **that signal tells the system
> whether or not to push your video to the next thousand people.**"

Two consequences the course draws:

- **Hook visually** — a reveal, a shock, a question — *"before the viewer even decides if they'll
  keep the sound on."* The visual has to work before the audio decision is made.
- **Speak to a niche, not the masses.** *"The algorithm now shows preference for sub-communities
  like #BookTok, #CarTok, #STEMTok. When the niche explodes, you ride the wave organically."*

## The six metrics that matter

> "**Vanity metrics make you feel busy; algorithmic metrics make you rich.**"

| Metric | Healthy | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **3-second hold rate** | **≥ 70%** | *"If lower, your hook is ice-cold"* |
| **Average watch time** | **1.4× video length** | Rewatches trump single passes |
| **Completion rate** | **18-25%** for conversion ads | Higher for storytelling |
| **Engagement rate** | **4-8%** for consumer brands | (likes + comments + shares) ÷ impressions |
| **Click-through rate** | **> 2%** for ecommerce | Below it, *"your CTA or landing page mismatches the promise"* |
| **ROAS / CPA trend line** | — | ⚠️ **A 20% week-over-week CPA rise is the canary for creative fatigue.** Kill or refresh |

⚠️ **These thresholds are ACA's, given without a source or a vertical.** They are usable as
starting reference points and should be replaced by the account's own baselines once it has them.
Do not present them to a client as industry benchmarks.

⛔ **Two of them collide with ACA's own Course 402, and neither course mentions the other.**

- **CTR.** This course puts the ecommerce floor at **above 2%**. Course 402 puts the sweet spot at
  **0.9% to 1.3%** and treats anything over 1.4% as a reason to check whether the traffic is
  qualified. An ad at 1.1% is healthy by one course and broken by the other. **They are different
  platforms** — this is TikTok, 402 measures Meta link clicks out to a store — **and near-certainly
  different CTR definitions**, since 402 establishes that Ads Manager holds five distinct CTRs and
  this course names no variant.
- **The 3-second hold rate.** This course gives no formula for it. If it means 3-second views ÷
  impressions, then a 70% floor cannot be squared with 402's *"50% or higher is very rare"*. If it
  means something else, the two numbers are not about the same thing.

⭐ **So: never carry a threshold from this table onto another platform, and say which CTR you mean.**
`aca-402-key-metrics` has the formulas and the full comparison.

## Creative at scale: the tooling

Section 4. ⚠️ **Named product features, and the most time-bound content in the course.** Given as
described; verify anything before relying on it.

TikTok One and **Symphony**:

- **Generate & Remix** — paste a product page URL, receive five ready-to-run videos in minutes
- **Avatar Videos** — a multilingual host lip-syncing your script. ACA's cited result: *"Vodafone
  slashed cost-per-lead 45%"*
- **Translate & Dub** — one click, thirty languages, lip alignment
- **Daily Video Generations** — Symphony watches yesterday's winners and proposes fresh spins

Also named as *"your cheapest and fastest staff creators and editors"*: **CapCut templates**, the
in-app Video Editor, and Symphony's Daily Generations.

### ⭐ The minimum viable habit

> "Keep **five to seven unique creatives live in every conversion campaign** and **refresh them
> weekly.** That single habit is the cheapest antidote to creative fatigue."

⚠️ **Weekly is a TikTok rhythm.** ACA's Meta course puts fatigue at *"week four, week six, or week
eight"*. Both are their platform's own guidance — TikTok's feed turns over faster — so do not apply
a weekly refresh budget to a Meta account, or a six-week one here.

## The creative iteration loop

Section 11. *"TikTok rewards volume and variation."*

**Structure:** 3 to 5 creatives per ad group, multiple ad groups per campaign.

| Week | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1** | Launch varied hooks |
| **2** | Double down on winners |
| **3** | Remix — sound, captions, first three seconds |

⚠️ Note that week 3 changes *only* the hook layer of a proven concept. That is a different move
from generating new concepts, and the course treats them as separate activities.

## The six-day action plan

Section 12. The runnable part of the course, built for a small budget.

> "how to use TikTok's tools and ecosystem to quickly test and and unlock winning concepts in only 1
> week without much budget and effort."

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| **1** | Pull Audience Insights; **list three sub-cultures, write three scripts** |
| **2** | Spin five variants in Symphony + CapCut; **tag each with the principle it exemplifies** |
| **3** | Launch a test budget — **$50 per ad set, auto-placement** ⚠️ see note |
| **4** | Check 3-second hold rate and CTR; **kill the bottom two** |
| **5** | Remix the best performer twice; **change hook or sound only** |
| **6** | Scale top ads, archive losers, document learnings, repeat |

⭐ **Day 2's tagging instruction is the part that makes the week worth repeating.** Tagging each
variant with the principle it demonstrates means day 4's kill decision teaches you which *principle*
worked, not merely which file won.

⚠️ **And it is what keeps day 2 from contradicting ACA's Meta course.** Course 201's Andromeda module
is blunt that *"more ads is not automatically a strategy"* and that five cosmetic variants of one
idea give the system nothing to work with. Day 2 asks for five variants **before** anything is
proven — which looks like exactly that.

**The reconciliation is the tag.** Five variants each demonstrating a *different principle* are five
distinct creative inputs, which is what both courses actually ask for. Five variants that differ
only in colour are the thing 201 warns against, on either platform. **Spin five, but make them five
different arguments.**

⚠️ **Day 5 changes one layer only** — hook or sound — which keeps the test readable. Changing the
concept and the hook together tells you nothing about either.

⚠️ **The $50 per ad set is ACA's illustrative figure**, not a platform minimum. Check TikTok's
current minimums, and scale the number to the account.

#### ⚠️ Auto-placement: this course says yes, ACA's Meta course says no

Day 3 runs on auto-placement, and the campaign-architecture section says **95% of advertisers can
leave placements automatic** because *"TikTok's auction is placement-agnostic."*

ACA's Course 201 says the opposite for Meta: ⛔ *"do not lean on auto-placement... often the resized
ads don't look their best. Not really great at all."*

⭐ **Both are right, for different reasons, and the reason is the point.** Meta's auto-placement
**resizes one asset into placements it was not built for**, which is why it degrades. TikTok's
placement choice is about *where the auction serves it*, not about reformatting your creative — and
TikTok is one dominant vertical format rather than a spread of shapes.

**So: automatic on TikTok, per-placement builds on Meta.** Do not carry either rule across.

⚠️ **Choose the objective first and carefully.** *"Choose wrong and every setting downstream —

## Audience Insights, and sub-culture mining

> "**Stop guessing who your buyer is; ask TikTok directly.**"

Open Analytics → Audience Insights.

⭐ **The distinctive idea in this course**, and the part people skip:

> "Demographics are the appetizer; **the main course is sub-culture mining.**"

### What to actually look at

**Interest and hashtag clouds.** Export the list and highlight *"not only the obvious (#running,
#skincare) but the weirdly specific tags — #GoblinCore, #SlowLiving, #TinyDeskDIY. These are
self-organising micro-communities that the algorithm loves to serve niche content."*

**Community overlap graph.** TikTok shows what else your buyers binge.

> "A plant-based snack brand may discover its audience also lives inside #BookTok; that's a hint to
> pair recipe content with cosy reading vibes."

### ⭐ Insights give you language, not just targeting

The move that makes this operational rather than interesting:

> "Insights don't just point to targeting; **they give you language.** If #copperhair is trending
> inside your cohort, open your next video by literally stating it: *'Copper hair lovers, this one's
> for you…'*"

### The drill

Mirella sets it as homework, and it is the cheapest useful thing in the course:

> "Right after this session, pick one unexpected hashtag from your Insights, click through to its
> feed, **watch ten videos, and write down the inside jokes.** Your next ad starts there."

⚠️ **This is research you have to actually do.** The hashtags above are ACA's examples, not a
starter list for your brand — and a sub-community you have not watched cannot supply you with its
language. Do not invent the inside jokes of a community you have not observed; that is precisely
the failure that makes brand content on TikTok read as tourism.

## The six creative principles

Section 7. ACA attributes these to TikTok's internal *"Creative Bootcamp"*, which *"distilled six
principles from thousands of top ads."*

---

### Principle 1 — TikTok-First

> "Go lo-fi, vertical, human."

- **9 × 16 framing with no UI overlap**
- **Real people over studio actors**
- **Natural lighting over studio light**
- **Trending sound or authentic voice** drives memory

⭐ ACA's figure: *"A/B tests showed TikTok-First assets doubled engagement and conversions."*

### Principle 2 — Trends: Force → Moment → Signal

> "Think of a trend as an iceberg."

| Layer | What it is | Example given |
|---|---|---|
| **Forces** | Big societal shifts | Sustainability, AI |
| **Moments** | Flash-wave memes | The "We do not care" transition |
| **Signals** | Subtle patterns | A popular editing style, a sound effect |

**The process: Identify, Isolate, Iterate.** Scroll the For You Page, scan hashtags, check Creative
Center, pick a Moment, inject brand relevance, then iterate three versions.

⚠️ Moments have the shortest shelf life of the three by definition. A Force is safe to build a
campaign on; a Moment is only usable while it is live, and the example above will be long dead.

### Principle 3 — Structure: the Golden Formula

> "**Hook → Key Message → CTA.** The Golden Formula lives or dies in order."

| Stage | Timing | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0-3 s | Suspense, surprise, or strong emotion. ⭐ Increases watch time **up to 1.7×** |
| **Key Message** | 4-15 s | Product benefit, social proof, or transformation |
| **CTA** | Final 5 s | A direct ask |

⭐ **The statistic worth quoting at anyone who leaves the CTA out:**

> "Ads with a strong CTA drive **208% higher purchase intent** — yet **half of TikTok ads skip
> one.**"

#### The three creative structures

> "Three easy creative structures:"

1. **Talent-Engagement Forward** — personality first, product woven through
2. **Problem / Product Forward** — pain point, instant reveal, demo, urgent CTA
3. **No Visible Talent** — lo-fi hands-and-product with voice-over

Useful when a brand has no on-camera talent, or when testing whether a face helps at all.

### Principle 4 — Stimulation

> "Fast cuts, kinetic captions, emoji bursts, camera movement, dramatic zooms — each is a
> micro-reward that keeps the brain's attention system firing."

ACA's supporting claim: *"Neuromarketing data shows these devices spike activity in areas tied to
memory and decision-making."*

### Principle 5 — Sound

> "**Sound on TikTok is half the experience.**"

- **Music** sets mood. *"Faster BPM boosts memorability."*
- **Voice-over** clarifies complex value propositions. *"Use text-to-speech if budget is zero."*
- **ASMR and SFX** parallel the action — *"think can-crack or foundation squish."*

⭐ ACA cites Kantar's 2021 cross-platform study: TikTok *"was the only platform where sound-on ads
lifted both purchase intent and brand favourability."*

⚠️ **Note the tension with other platforms.** ACA's Meta course insists you never assume audio is
on and subtitle everything. Both are right for their platform — TikTok is sound-on by default in a
way feed placements elsewhere are not. Do not carry the Meta rule here, or this rule there.

### Principle 6 — Creative Refresh

> "**Even perfect creative dies.**"

The triggers are numeric, which is what makes them usable:

> "Track CPA and CTR. When **CPA rises 20% above its week-one average** or **CTR drops 30%**,
> rotate in fresh assets."

Sources of fresh ideas named in the course: Symphony's *Daily Video Generations* and Creative
Center's *Top Ads Spotlight*.

## Production best practices

Section 10 of the script. **Five practices** — note that "10" is the section number, not a count.

1. **Show one product**; max three if they belong together.
2. **Disclose price or promo early** — transparency drives trust.
3. **Showcase diversity** — let your consumer see themselves.
4. **Sweet-spot length: 20-35 seconds.** Under 15s is for teaser hype; over 40s drains attention.
5. **Keep text and key elements in the safe zone** — the top 20% and bottom 20% are UI land.

⚠️ **Point 4 sits oddly beside the hook guidance and both are in the course.** The Golden Formula
puts the CTA in the final five seconds of a 20-35 second ad, so the two are consistent — but if
someone has absorbed "keep it under six seconds" from a Meta context, flag the difference rather
than blending them. This platform tolerates, and often wants, longer.

⚠️ **Safe-zone percentages and specs date.** Check TikTok's current ad specs before building to
them.
