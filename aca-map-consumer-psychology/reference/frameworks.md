## The 5 awareness stages

Section 1. Place the audience, and say what the evidence was.

| Stage | They are | Message focus | Funnel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Don't know they have a problem | Problem identification, disruption, curiosity | Top |
| **Problem Aware** | Know the problem, not the solution | Problem validation, education, hope | Top |
| **Solution Aware** | Know solutions exist, not yours | Differentiation, unique mechanism, comparison | Middle |
| **Product Aware** | Know your product, haven't bought | Objection handling, proof, specific benefits | Middle |
| **Most Aware** | Ready to buy with the right offer | Offers, urgency, easy action steps | Bottom |

Tactical detail per stage:

- **Unaware** — raise awareness of the problem itself, use pattern interrupts and curiosity
  triggers, lead with stories that illuminate an unrecognised pain point.
  *Example: "The hidden reason you're exhausted even after 8 hours of sleep"*
- **Problem Aware** — validate the experience ("yes, this is real, and you're not alone"), educate
  about the problem's nature, introduce solution categories **without pitching your product yet**
- **Solution Aware** — differentiate from alternatives, focus on unique mechanisms, show why your
  category is superior.
  *Example: "Why natural supplements outperform synthetic options for sustainable energy"*
- **Product Aware** — address specific objections, provide proof (studies, testimonials,
  demonstrations), compare directly to what they are considering
- **Most Aware** — urgency, scarcity, offers, reduce friction, reinforce with guarantees

⚠️ **One audience is rarely at one stage.** Broad targeting puts all five in the same ad set. Name
the *primary* stage for the worksheet, and note if a second is significant.

## Market sophistication

Awareness is about the person; sophistication is about the market. As a category matures, plain
claims stop working because everyone makes them.

Two escapes:

1. **A unique mechanism** — *"how our proprietary technology works differently"*. Not a bigger
   claim, a different reason the claim is true.
2. **Reframe the problem** — *"you've been solving the wrong problem all along"*. Moves the
   conversation rather than competing inside it.

Diagnostic: if every competitor ad makes the same promise, the promise is spent.

## Maslow's hierarchy

Section 2, the coarse cut. Check all that apply.

1. **Physiological** — basic survival needs
2. **Safety** — security, stability, protection
3. **Belonging** — connection, relationships, community
4. **Esteem** — recognition, status, respect, achievement
5. **Self-actualization** — growth, creativity, purpose

## The Life-Force 8

Section 2, the sharp cut. These are the ones that produce copy.

| Drive | What it is |
|---|---|
| **1. Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension** | Products promising health, vitality or safety |
| **2. Enjoyment of food and beverages** | We are programmed to seek pleasurable taste |
| **3. Freedom from fear, pain, and danger** | People spend heavily to eliminate worry: financial, physical, emotional |
| **4. Sexual companionship** | Beyond romance: feeling desirable and attractive |
| **5. Comfortable living conditions** | Products that make life easier, more efficient, more luxurious |
| **6. To be superior; winning** | Status, achievement, recognition |
| **7. Care and protection of loved ones** | Powerful for parents, pet owners, caregivers |
| **8. Social approval** | Acceptance and respect from peers |

**Tie-breaker when several apply:** which drive explains why they would buy **now**, rather than
agreeing the product is nice in principle? Freedom from fear and care of loved ones tend to produce
urgency; comfortable living and social approval tend to produce agreement.

## The 14 persuasion principles

Section 3. Select **2 to 3** and say how each will be used.

⚠️ **The printed worksheet's grid lists only 10.** All fourteen are here. The four the grid omits
are marked, and two of them (distinctiveness, self-centeredness) are among the most commonly
violated.

**Cialdini's seven:**

| Principle | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | Giving something valuable first creates obligation |
| **Commitment and consistency** | Small yeses lead to bigger ones |
| **Social proof** | When uncertain, people follow others. Strongest when the others are recognisably *like them* |
| **Authority** | Credentials transfer confidence, fastest where buyers cannot judge quality themselves |
| **Liking** | Rapport and relatability. Much of why creator-led ads work |
| **Scarcity** | Limited availability or time. Converts, and easiest to abuse |
| **Unity** | Shared identity, a sense of *us*. Strongest and slowest to build |

**The seven the course adds:**

| Principle | Mechanism | On the grid? |
|---|---|---|
| **MAYA** (Most Advanced Yet Acceptable) | The most novel thing they will still accept. Useful for unfamiliar mechanisms | ⛔ omitted |
| **Loss aversion** | Losing hurts more than gaining pleases. Reframe the same fact as what they lose | ✅ |
| **Anchoring** | First information sets the reference point. Set value before naming price | ✅ |
| **Pratfall effect** | Minor flaws make strong claims believable. A claim with no admitted downside reads as advertising | ✅ |
| **Distinctiveness** | What stands out gets remembered. A memory argument as much as persuasion | ⛔ omitted |
| **Self-centeredness** | People care about their problems, not your product. The corrective for copy opening with the company | ⛔ omitted |
| **Primacy and recency** | Beginning and end are remembered. The hook and CTA carry disproportionate weight | ⛔ omitted |

**Choosing:** pick what the *evidence* supports. Social proof without numbers, authority without
credentials and scarcity without real limits are claims the audience can check.

Pairings that compound: authority + social proof · scarcity + loss aversion (and both push urgency,
so overuse reads as pressure) · pratfall + authority (admitting a limitation makes expertise more
credible). Liking + authority pull against each other; warm-and-relatable sits awkwardly beside
credentialed-and-expert, and the ad has to pick a register.

## The 5 framing angles

Section 4. One core message, five ways to say it. Not five claims.

| Frame | Answers | Pulls from |
|---|---|---|
| **Benefit** | What do I get? | The "TO" side of the transformation |
| **Pain avoidance** | What do I stop suffering? | The "FROM" side |
| **Status / identity** | Who does this make me? | Social approval, to be superior, esteem |
| **Process / mechanism** | Why does it work? | The sophistication escape |
| **Testimonial / story** | Who else has done this? | Social proof |

Two checks: could a competitor put their name on any line unchanged (then it describes the
category, not the product)? And do all five read as one argument from five angles, or as five
different products (then the core message was never settled)?

## The two hook menus

Section 5. Use when a hook will not come, or to force variety past the usual two or three shapes.

**Ten psychological approaches** — what the hook *does to the viewer*:
FOMO · curiosity gap · identity reinforcement · unexpected statement or statistic · problem
agitation · dream scenario · pattern interrupt · status elevation · loss aversion · before/after
contrast

**Ten structural approaches** — what *shape the sentence takes*:
question-based · statistic-based · story-based · identity-based · pain-point · curiosity ·
counterintuitive statement · bold promise · future-pacing · exclusivity

Picking one from each list produces combinations the obvious approach misses.

⚠️ A hook containing a specific claim (a number, a timeframe, a result) needs that claim to be
real. If the research does not support it, soften it or mark it as needing verification before
ship.

## The 4-exposure sequence

Section 6, for multi-touch campaigns only.

| Exposure | Job |
|---|---|
| **1st** | Introduce the problem |
| **2nd** | Reveal your unique solution |
| **3rd** | Provide proof it works |
| **4th** | Present the offer with urgency |

This walks someone from problem aware to most aware across four touches instead of asking one ad to
do all four jobs, which is what produces the overstuffed ad.

⚠️ The awareness target should **advance** down the rows. Same target in row 1 and row 4 means the
plan repeats rather than sequences.

Each ad still has to work standing alone: someone may enter at exposure 3.
