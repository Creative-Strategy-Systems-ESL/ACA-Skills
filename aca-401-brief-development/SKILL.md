# Course 401: Brief Development

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 40m 52s, 4 modules.

> "This is my absolute favorite part of the process. Coming up with ad ideas, brainstorming creative
> angles, and crafting hooks that grab attention."

**This course sits after the research and before the storyboard.** It turns what the audit, audience,
competitor and trends work produced into concepts, then into approved briefs and written hooks.

⭐ **This skill emits the Hook Development Worksheet** and writes hooks into ACA's own database
structure, rather than returning a loose list.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.**

**To produce concepts or hooks, required:**

1. **The product or service**, and what it costs
2. **Who it is for**, and the problem it solves for them

**Optional, and each one is a field in the artifacts:**

3. Research output — the audit, personas, competitor or trends work this course consumes
4. What has already run, and how it performed
5. The offer, if there is one
6. Platform and placement
7. Brand constraints: claims, tone, whether native or branded

⚠️ **Without research, concepts are guesses with structure.** The course opens by naming its own
inputs: *"all of the insights we gathered from the ad account audit, audience research, competitor
research and trends research."* You can still produce concepts from a product description; say they
are hypotheses and that the Motivator and Benefit fields are unverified.

⛔ **Never write a hook containing a claim the brand has not made.** Hooks are the most quotable
output of this skill and the most likely to be lifted straight into an ad. A performance claim, a
health claim or a price invented to make a hook land is a compliance problem with someone else's
name on it.

## The through-line

> "Each ad creative is a **hypothesis** — something you'll test to see if it resonates. If it does,
> you iterate; if it doesn't, you pivot. The goal is always to create ads intentionally **so you can
> learn from every batch of assets you set live.**"

⭐ **That is the standard to hold any output here to.** A batch of ads that cannot teach you anything
when the numbers come back was briefed badly, however good it looks.

## How to run it

**Concept before hook, hook before brief.** A hook written without a concept is a line; the course's whole
sequence exists to stop that.

1. **Confirm research exists.** ⛔ This course starts where Course 302 ends, and concepting from nothing
   produces the generic output it is meant to prevent.
2. **Make the diversity argument** from `reference/concepts.md` — it is an auction argument, not a taste one,
   and it is what justifies the volume that follows.
3. **Work the five-axis Creative Diversification Guide** and the four-part Creative Strategy Map, same file.
4. **Build the concept** and take it through the presentation stakeholders sign off, still in
   `reference/concepts.md`.
5. **Write hooks** using `reference/hooks.md` — the five elements, Meta's categories, the five copy formulas.
6. ⭐ **Produce the Hook Development Worksheet** from the section at the end of `reference/hooks.md`: target
   audience, core problem and solution, hook brainstorming, hook testing plan. **All four sections, filled.**
7. **Add sound-first** from `reference/sound-first.md` where the format allows it, which is where the course
   is most distinctive.
8. **Check one concept has become ten ads on paper.** If it is still one idea, the diversification step was
   agreed to rather than done.

## Answering well

**Creative diversity is an auction argument, not an aesthetic one.**

> "Because of how the Facebook and TikTok auctions work, **if your ads are too similar, they'll
> essentially target the same subset of users.** That can be good for reinforcing a strong performer,
> but it also risks over-saturation."

So diversity is how you reach new segments and delay fatigue. *"Knowing when to lean into similarity
versus when to diversify is essential."*

**The hook is the targeting mechanism.** *"When someone stops on your ad, the algorithm serves it to
similar users, effectively building your audience based on who finds your hook compelling."*

**One concept, many ads.** *"The same concept can become a video ad, a static ad, a carousel, a big
budget production or a simple UGC video."* Do not stop at one execution.

⚠️ **Ad type names are shared language, not doctrine.** *"These names are what make sense to me and
my team. You might call them something different, and that's perfectly fine. The goal is to create a
shared language."*

## ⚠️ Scope

Concepting, briefing and hook writing. It stops at the storyboard: shot lists, scripts and shooting
documents are Course 403. It does not cover media buying, testing methodology or measurement.
