# Course 401: Brief Development

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 40m 52s, 4 modules.

> "This is my absolute favorite part of the process. Coming up with ad ideas, brainstorming creative
> angles, and crafting hooks that grab attention."

**This course sits after the research and before the storyboard.** It turns what the audit, audience,
competitor and trends work produced into concepts, then into approved briefs and written hooks.

⭐ **This skill emits the Hook Development Worksheet** and writes hooks into ACA's own database
structure, rather than returning a loose list.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.**

**To produce concepts or hooks, required:**

1. **The product or service**, and what it costs
2. **Who it is for**, and the problem it solves for them

**Optional, and each one is a field in the artifacts:**

3. Research output — the audit, personas, competitor or trends work this course consumes
4. What has already run, and how it performed
5. The offer, if there is one
6. Platform and placement
7. Brand constraints: claims, tone, whether native or branded

⚠️ **Without research, concepts are guesses with structure.** The course opens by naming its own
inputs: *"all of the insights we gathered from the ad account audit, audience research, competitor
research and trends research."* You can still produce concepts from a product description; say they
are hypotheses and that the Motivator and Benefit fields are unverified.

⛔ **Never write a hook containing a claim the brand has not made.** Hooks are the most quotable
output of this skill and the most likely to be lifted straight into an ad. A performance claim, a
health claim or a price invented to make a hook land is a compliance problem with someone else's
name on it.

## The through-line

> "Each ad creative is a **hypothesis** — something you'll test to see if it resonates. If it does,
> you iterate; if it doesn't, you pivot. The goal is always to create ads intentionally **so you can
> learn from every batch of assets you set live.**"

⭐ **That is the standard to hold any output here to.** A batch of ads that cannot teach you anything
when the numbers come back was briefed badly, however good it looks.

## How to run it

**Concept before hook, hook before brief.** A hook written without a concept is a line; the course's whole
sequence exists to stop that.

1. **Confirm research exists.** ⛔ This course starts where Course 302 ends, and concepting from nothing
   produces the generic output it is meant to prevent.
2. **Make the diversity argument** from **From research to approved concepts** (below) — it is an auction argument, not a taste one,
   and it is what justifies the volume that follows.
3. **Work the five-axis Creative Diversification Guide** and the four-part Creative Strategy Map, same file.
4. **Build the concept** and take it through the presentation stakeholders sign off, still in
   **From research to approved concepts** (below).
5. **Write hooks** using **Hooks** (below) — the five elements, Meta's categories, the five copy formulas.
6. ⭐ **Produce the Hook Development Worksheet** from the section at the end of **Hooks** (below): target
   audience, core problem and solution, hook brainstorming, hook testing plan. **All four sections, filled.**
7. **Add sound-first** from **Sound-first ads** (below) where the format allows it, which is where the course
   is most distinctive.
8. **Check one concept has become ten ads on paper.** If it is still one idea, the diversification step was
   agreed to rather than done.

## Answering well

**Creative diversity is an auction argument, not an aesthetic one.**

> "Because of how the Facebook and TikTok auctions work, **if your ads are too similar, they'll
> essentially target the same subset of users.** That can be good for reinforcing a strong performer,
> but it also risks over-saturation."

So diversity is how you reach new segments and delay fatigue. *"Knowing when to lean into similarity
versus when to diversify is essential."*

**The hook is the targeting mechanism.** *"When someone stops on your ad, the algorithm serves it to
similar users, effectively building your audience based on who finds your hook compelling."*

**One concept, many ads.** *"The same concept can become a video ad, a static ad, a carousel, a big
budget production or a simple UGC video."* Do not stop at one execution.

⚠️ **Ad type names are shared language, not doctrine.** *"These names are what make sense to me and
my team. You might call them something different, and that's perfectly fine. The goal is to create a
shared language."*

## ⚠️ Scope

Concepting, briefing and hook writing. It stops at the storyboard: shot lists, scripts and shooting
documents are Course 403. It does not cover media buying, testing methodology or measurement.

## From research to approved concepts

### Why creative diversity, mechanically

> "Remember how we said **'creative is your targeting'**? People buy the same products for different
> reasons. Because of how the Facebook and TikTok auctions work, **if your ads are too similar,
> they'll essentially target the same subset of users.** That can be good for reinforcing a strong
> performer, but it also risks over-saturation."

> "By testing multiple angles across different emotional motivators, ad formats, and execution
> styles, you can **unlock entirely new audience segments, keep your CPAs stable, and avoid creative
> fatigue** in the long run."

⭐ **This is not "make different-looking ads".** Similar creative lands on the same people, so a
saturated segment and a fatiguing account are the same problem seen from two angles. Diversity is
the lever on both.

### The Creative Diversification Guide — five axes

> "Diversity doesn't just mean a random mix of ads. It's about **systematically exploring different
> possibilities** to appeal to varied audience mindsets."

#### 1. Message

| Sub-axis | What varies |
|---|---|
| **Human desires** | Motivators and barriers, emotional benefits, situational triggers. *"A fitness app might focus on achievement in one ad, and fear of missing out in another"* |
| **Functional vs emotional** | Features and benefits. *"One ad might focus on usability, while another highlights a specific demo"* |
| **Awareness level** | ⭐ *"You want to have creatives that speak to all stages of the funnel."* |
| **POV** | Brand voice · customer · expert |
| **Promotions** | Offers and events. *"Not every ad needs a promotion, but when you have one, it deserves its own creative approach"* |

#### 2. Formats

Core: **static · video · carousel**. Bonus: shop ads, product tags. And the dimensions — **1:1, 4:5,
9:16** — *"each have their place"*.

#### 3. Execution styles

**Lo-fi UGC** that feels authentic · **native platform language** that blends in · **creator-led**
content that leverages someone else's audience · **polished, high-production** product and lifestyle
shoots.

#### 4. Placements

> "Meta has feed, stories, and reels. TikTok has multiple creative entry points. YouTube has various
> ad formats from skippable to shorts. **Each deserves creative tailored to its environment.**"

#### 5. Best practices checklist

Highlight the brand quickly · use strong hooks · show the problem-solution dynamic · optimise for
platform-specific requirements.

> "This guide isn't just for reference. It's a **systematic checklist** to ensure your creative
> strategy covers all the bases."

⭐ **Run it as a gap audit, not an inspiration list.** Lay the current creative against the five axes
and the empty cells are the brief. That is faster and more defensible than brainstorming.

### The Creative Strategy Map — what a concept is

Four components. **All four together are the concept.**

| Component | The question |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | What is the audience you are targeting? |
| **Motivator** | What core emotion or desire are you tapping into? Security, excitement, belonging |
| **Benefit** | The tangible payoff or outcome your product delivers |
| **Offer** | The specific reason to act now. A discount, a free trial, an exclusive bonus |

> "A concept can become SO many ads if they are executed in so many different ways. The same concept
> can become a video ad, a static ad, a carousel, it can be a big budget production or a simple UGC
> video."

> "The goal here is to take all your learnings from your audit and research and **generate as many
> concepts as possible, to really build a database of concepts** you can always refer to when you
> need new ad ideas."

⚠️ **A concept missing its Motivator is an execution idea.** "UGC unboxing video" names a format, not
a concept, and it cannot be varied because there is no thesis in it to hold constant.

⚠️ **Offer can legitimately be empty**, and should be marked so rather than invented. Not every
concept carries a promotion, and a discount written in to fill the field becomes a discount someone
expects to run.

### The concept presentation, for stakeholder approval

> "Before diving into production, we need to properly present our concepts to stakeholders."

**One slide per video concept, eight fields:**

| Field |
|---|
| The concept name |
| Ad type |
| Target persona |
| Placement or channel |
| Brief description of the concept |
| Emotional motivator or pain point addressed |
| USPs or benefits to highlight |
| Visual references |

**Static concepts follow the same structure**, focused on ad types, formats and clear direction on
execution.

> "This structured approach ensures **everyone understands the strategic intent** behind each
> creative concept before moving to production."

⭐ **Fields 6 and 7 are what make this an approval document rather than a mood board.** A stakeholder
signing off a concept is agreeing to the motivator and the benefit, which is exactly what should not
be renegotiated after the shoot.

⚠️ **Approval belongs before storyboarding.** Course 403 assumes it has happened.

### The ad type libraries

> "Having a library of proven ad types is the fastest and most efficient way to execute your
> concepts."

⚠️ **The names are hers, and she says so:** *"These names are what make sense to me and my team. You
might call them something different, and that's perfectly fine. The goal is to create a **shared
language** that helps categorize references and formulate best practices."* Treat the library as a
vocabulary to adopt or rename, not a taxonomy to defend.

#### Video ad types

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Before & After** | The transformation or results from using your product |
| **Storytelling** | Narrative-driven, creating emotional connection |
| **Greenscreen Tutorial** | Presenter explains benefits with visual aids |
| **Social Proof Mashup** | Compilation of user testimonials and reviews |
| **Reaction** | Authentic first reactions to the product |
| **Customer Review** | In-depth testimonial from a single customer |
| **Unboxing** | Receiving and opening the product |
| **Listicle** | Numbered list of benefits, features or tips |
| **POV** | The product experience from the user's perspective |
| **Styling Reel** | Multiple ways to use or style the product |

#### Static ad types

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Bold Claim** | Strong statement about the product or its benefits |
| **Key Benefits** | The main advantages |
| **Comparison (Us vs. Them)** | Direct comparison with alternatives |
| **Before & After** | Visual transformation results |
| **Polished Review** | Professionally formatted customer feedback |
| **Native Review** | A review that appears user-generated or organic |
| **Split Screen** | Contrasting situations, problems or solutions |
| **Grid Swap** | Transformation shown in a grid |
| **Price Breakdown** | Value proposition and cost structure |
| **Text Message** | Formatted as a mobile messaging conversation |
| **Notes App** | Styled like phone notes |
| **Search** | Mimicking search results or the search process |
| **Post-Its** | Information organised as sticky notes |
| **Promo** | Special offers or discounts |
| **Problem vs. Solution** | Directly contrasting the issue and the fix |
| **News/Media** | Formatted as a PR feature, billboard or newspaper |

⭐ **Sixteen static types against ten video types is worth noticing.** Statics are cheaper, faster and
under-used by most accounts, and this library is the fastest route to volume when production budget
is the constraint.

### Producing concepts

Deliver:

1. **A concept table** — one row per concept, with all four Strategy Map components filled
2. **The presentation fields** for each concept taken forward, all eight
3. **Format and execution options per concept** — how this one concept becomes several ads
4. ⛔ **A line naming which fields are inferred** rather than drawn from supplied research

⚠️ **Aim for a bank, not a shortlist.** The course's own instruction is to *"generate as many
concepts as possible"* and build a database. Three polished concepts is a worse output than twelve
rough ones with the Motivator column properly varied.

## Hooks

> "those critical first 1-3 seconds that determine whether someone keeps watching your ad or scrolls
> right past it."

### Why the hook is the highest-leverage element

Three reasons, and the first is the one people miss:

1. ⭐ **It is your real targeting mechanism.** *"When someone stops on your ad, the algorithm serves
   it to similar users, effectively building your audience based on who finds your hook
   compelling."*
2. **Different hooks reach different segments.** *"By testing multiple approaches for the same
   creative, you can tap into entirely new customer groups."*
3. **Refreshing hooks prevents fatigue.** *"Even when your core message is working, a new hook can
   revitalize a campaign that's starting to see diminishing returns."*

⭐ **Reason 3 is the cheapest performance fix in the programme.** A fatiguing ad with a working body
needs a new opening, not a new concept. Check whether that has been tried before briefing anything
new.

### Five pattern interrupt techniques

| Technique | ACA's example |
|---|---|
| **Trigger curiosity** | *"The one mistake costing most advertisers thousands..."* |
| **Trigger emotions** | *"We were shocked when we saw these results..."* |
| **Be relatable** | *"That moment when your ad account performance suddenly tanks..."* |
| **Be shocking or surprising** | Unusual visuals, surprising statements, counterintuitive claims |
| **Showcase an opportunity** | *"The three-step process that cut our CPAs by 60%..."* |

### The five elements of an effective hook

Not just the words. **All five, working together.**

| Element | What it carries |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | Who appears. *"leverage actors who resemble your target audience, and who display emotions in the way that your target audience would when faced with their problem."* |
| **Message** | The bold problem statement. *"In a few words, your audience should clearly be able to connect this problem to a personal experience"* |
| **Visual** | *"The engaging, shocking or satisfying visual will tell your audience what emotions they should feel"* |
| **Sound** | *"Pattern-interrupting sounds, voice tonality, or even strategic silence"* |
| **Thumbnail** | ⚠️ *"The static image that appears before a video plays... This single frame needs to generate enough interest to earn that initial click"* |

> "When these five elements work together, you create a hook that's much harder to scroll past."

⭐ **Thumbnail is the element nobody briefs**, and it is the only one that does work before the video
plays at all.

### Meta's hook categories

> "A powerful framework developed by **Meta's Creative Shop in Israel**. Led by our faculty member
> **Udi Avital**, this team identified... hook categories that consistently drive engagement."

⚠️ **ACA says "twelve distinct hook categories" and then lists seventeen.** Counted from the course
itself. All seventeen are below; the count in the narration is wrong, not the list.

| # | Category | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dramatize the Problem** | Amplifies the pain point. *"Makes the problem feel impossible to ignore"* |
| 2 | **Motion Tricks** | Eye-catching transitions and visual effects that stop the scroll |
| 3 | **Podcast** | Mimics a podcast format. *"The “expert voice” introduction in the first seconds establishes authority and signals valuable information ahead"* |
| 4 | **Reminder** | Time-sensitive. Connects the product to an upcoming event, sale or season |
| 5 | **The Absurd Alternative** | An exaggerated or ridiculous alternative, making your solution obvious |
| 6 | **In Real Life** | *"Perfect for gaming and tech."* Bridges virtual experiences with real situations |
| 7 | **Reaction in Action** | Authentic reactions, often split-screen or green-screen |
| 8 | **Evolution** | Visualises transformation over time, problem to solution |
| 9 | **Question** | Opens on a question targeting the exact pain point |
| 10 | **On Trend (FOMO)** | Taps a current trend, or triggers the desire to participate |
| 11 | **Teaser Hook** | *"The classic don't-show-everything approach."* Withholds key details |
| 12 | **Make Me Laugh** | Humour disarms and builds positive association |
| 13 | **Emphasize the Solution** | A *"magic moment"* visualising the product's impact |
| 14 | **Negative Hook** | A negative statement or warning, creating urgency |
| 15 | **Satisfying Intro** | Sensory-rich visuals that are inherently pleasing to watch |
| 16 | **Destroy, Toss & Burn** | *"Destruction is a powerful pattern interrupt."* Breaking or smashing |
| 17 | **Skeptical Voice** | Acknowledges hesitation upfront, *"lowering resistance"* |

⭐ **Use the list as a coverage check.** Ten hooks that are all Question and Dramatize the Problem is
two hooks written five times. The point of a categorised list is to force range.

⚠️ **Categories 5, 15 and 16 carry brand risk** and the course does not flag it. Absurdity,
destruction and sensory content each work by being unlike the category norm, which is also what makes
them the ones a cautious brand rejects. Propose them knowing that.

### Five hook copy formulas

Templates, with ACA's own examples across three verticals.

| Formula | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Start with the problem** | *"Stop using makeup to hide breakouts."* · *"If you're struggling to breathe, you need to see this!"* · *"Guys, stop wearing uncomfortable jeans"* |
| **Ask a question about the problem** | *"Are you tired of breakouts and skin bumps?"* · *"Did you know your snoring can lead to serious health risks?"* · *"Do your jeans look like this?"* |
| **Make a list** | *"3 Ways This Serum Fights Acne"* · *"Here are 3 reasons why you need to try this anti-snoring device!"* · *"5 things your dermatologist won't tell you"* |
| **Start with the product** | *"This acne serum sold out 5x!"* · *"This anti-snoring device can end your snoring overnight"* · *"Jeans that feel like sweatpants"* |
| **Solve the problem** | *"This serum cleared my acne in 2 weeks."* · *"This simple anti-snoring trick can add years to your life!"* · *"How to dress like a real man"* |

⛔ **Several of ACA's own examples make claims that would need substantiation.** *"Cleared my acne in
2 weeks"*, *"can add years to your life"*, *"sold out 5x"* are results, health and sales claims. They
are reproduced here because they are ACA's, and **a hook you write must not invent one.** If the
brand has not made the claim, the hook cannot.

### The Hook Development Worksheet, and producing it

ACA's artifact. Four sections.

⛔ **Section 1 is research output, not a field to fill from the product description.** A filled
demographics and pain-point table reads as findings, and it travels: the audience written here is
the audience every hook below it is aimed at. **Mark each of its four fields `supplied`, `inferred`
or `not known`** and put the marker in the cell, not in a note underneath. If nothing was supplied,
the worksheet still gets produced, with Section 1 marked inferred throughout and a line saying the
hooks are aimed at a guess.

#### 1. Target Audience

| Field |
|---|
| Primary audience |
| Key demographics |
| Main pain points |
| Current solutions they use |

#### 2. Core Problem & Solution

| Field |
|---|
| Problem your product solves |
| Unique solution you offer |

#### 3. Hook Brainstorming

Five buckets, and the buckets are the diversity mechanism:

| Bucket |
|---|
| **Problem-Focused Hooks** |
| **Results-Focused Hooks** |
| **Curiosity Hooks** |
| **Social Proof Hooks** |
| **Humor Hooks** |

#### 4. Hook Testing Plan

⛔ **Emit the worksheet with these sections and fields**, filled. Not a description of what a hook
worksheet contains.

### Writing into the Comprehensive Hook Database structure

ACA's database is organised **by category, then sub-category, then bracketed templates**:

> **Problem/Pain Hooks → Direct Problem Statements**
> - *"Stop scrolling if you suffer from [PROBLEM]"*
> - *"[TARGET AUDIENCE], we gotta stop doing [BAD HABIT]"*
> - *"Don't do [ACTIVITY] without doing [SOLUTION] first"*
> - *"[PROBLEM] making it hard to go anywhere?"*
> - *"Break up with your [PROBLEM]"*
> - *"If you have [PROBLEM], you need to hear this"*

⭐ **Keep the `[BRACKETS]` when the value is unknown.** ACA's own database is bracketed, and a
template with the placeholder intact is reusable; one with a guessed value filled in reads as a
finished hook and gets shipped.

**So when producing hooks, deliver both:**

1. **Filled hooks** for this brand, ready to test
2. **The bracketed template** each came from, so the brand keeps a reusable pattern

### Delivering hooks

- **Tag every hook with its category** from the seventeen, and **check the spread** before delivering
- **Ten hooks minimum for a test batch**, spread across at least four categories
- **Flag any hook carrying a claim** the brand has not supplied evidence for
- **Note which of the five elements each hook depends on** — a hook that only works with a specific
  visual or sound is a production requirement, not just a line

## Sound-first ads

> "Sound is arguably one of the most underutilized elements in ad creative development."

### Why it deserves attention

Three arguments, in ACA's order:

1. **Platform behaviour.** *"Over 90% of TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts users engage with sound
   on."*
2. **Performance.** *"Our creative tests with clients running ads on Meta and TikTok show that videos
   with bespoke sound can **lower CPAs by up to 50%** compared to generic background tracks or silent
   videos."*
3. **It matches organic behaviour.** *"Songs and sounds go viral because creators dance to them,
   lip-sync to them, and build their own content around audio trends."*

> "Think of the hook as not just your 'scroll stopper' but your **'ear stopper'** as well."

⚠️ **Both figures are ACA's and unsourced**, and the second is their own client data with an "up to"
attached. See **Provenance** (below).

⛔ **The 90%-sound-on claim is platform-specific and does not generalise.** ACA's own Meta course
warns never to assume audio, and both are right about their own platform. Carrying the TikTok figure
into a Meta feed brief is a real error, and the courses do not reconcile it.

### Reversing the process

| Traditional | Sound-first |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify the target persona | 1. **Start with the sound concept** |
| 2. Develop the visual hook | 2. Build the visual elements around the audio |
| 3. Create the messaging | 3. Ensure the messaging complements the sound-driven narrative |
| 4. Add sound as the final element | 4. Optimise the whole package for the platform |

> "This reversal mimics how organic content often develops on social platforms, where creators
> frequently start with a trending sound and build their creative around"

### The three worked examples

| Brand | The sound concept |
|---|---|
| **Wise**, *"Spend Like a Local"* | *"What does it sound like to use Wise?"* The beeps, chimes and confirmations of payment machines, cut against quick location changes. ⭐ *"The sound drives the pace of the edits"* |
| **Roman**, telehealth | The contrast between **a cat's meow and a lion's roar**, representing hesitance to confidence. *"Communicates the emotional benefit without explicitly stating it"* |
| **Spoiled Child** | Took the trending *"man in finance"* sound, but **worked with a music producer on their own remix** rather than using the original |

⭐ **The Roman example is the clearest argument for the whole approach.** An ED brand cannot state
the emotional benefit plainly; a meow becoming a roar says it in a second and clears compliance,
because a metaphor is not a claim.

⚠️ **The Spoiled Child example is the practical one.** Remixing rather than lifting a trending sound
gets the familiarity while sidestepping the rights problem and the expiry date on the original.

### The four-step framework

⛔ **ACA ships a fillable version of this and it is not in this folder.** The *Sound-First Ads:
Creative Development Worksheet* is named on their download list for this course and is not held. What
follows is the method as taught in the recording, complete; what is missing is their form for it.

⚠️ **It is a different asset from the two sound-first items that ARE here** — *AI Tools for
Sound-First Creative Development* and *AI Prompts for Sound-First Creative Development*. The titles
are close enough to be read as the same thing, and they are not. If ACA sends the worksheet later, it
belongs here rather than in place of either of those.

#### Step 1: Sound concept exploration

Three prompts:

| Prompt | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Emotional soundscapes** | What emotions are associated with using the product? Calm, confident, relieved, powerful, sexy? **What transformation occurs before to after?** Stressed → calm · exhausted → energized · insecure → confident |
| **Product sound reality** | What does it literally sound like to receive and use? Packaging sound, unboxing, unwrapping? Does it make a sound when used (**ASMR potential**)? For digital, the app or game sound effects |
| **Cultural sound connections** | Seasonal themes? Trending sounds you can adapt or remix? **Sound metaphors** that relate to your benefits? |

#### Step 2: Sound production approaches

| Approach | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Original production** | Custom sound effects · original music embodying the brand emotion · voice with distinctive delivery or catchphrases |
| **Trend adaptation** | Remix or reinterpret a trending sound · a brand version of a popular format · the structure of a viral sound with new content |
| **Sound design** | Layer elements · use contrast for surprise · ⭐ **build audio patterns that create recognition across multiple ads** |

⭐ **That last one is the strategic option.** A recurring audio signature makes a whole account
recognisable in a feed, which no single ad can do.

#### Step 3: Visual integration

- Match visual cuts to audio beats
- Create visual metaphors that reinforce sound metaphors
- **Show reactions to sounds within the ad itself**
- Use on-screen text to highlight key sound moments
- Consider how the visuals explain or enhance the sound

#### Step 4: Testing variations

| What to vary |
|---|
| Different sound intensity levels |
| Various pacing of the same sound concept |
| **Male vs. female voices**, if using voiceover |
| Subtle vs. obvious sound effects |
| **Original sound vs. trending sound adaptations** |

⭐ **These are variations on one concept, not five concepts.** That is what makes it a clean test:
the sound is the variable and everything else holds.

### The checklist

**Audio hook: does the sound grab attention in the first 1 to 2 seconds?**

⚠️ **The transcript's checklist is cut off after this first item** in the filed material, so the rest
of ACA's criteria are not held. The first one is the load-bearing one and is reproduced as given.

### Briefing sound-first

**Name the sound concept before anything else in the brief**, and say which of the three production
approaches it needs — original, adaptation or design. Those have very different costs and lead times,
and a brief that says "great sound" without choosing gets a stock track.

⛔ **A sound-first ad needs a silent fallback, and the course does not mention one.** Some placements
autoplay muted regardless of platform norms. Say what the ad does with the sound off: if the answer
is nothing, that is a placement restriction the media buyer needs before launch, not after.

### ⛔ ACA says twelve hook categories and lists seventeen

The narration introduces Meta's framework as *"twelve distinct hook categories"*. The module then
teaches seventeen, each with its own name and description:

Dramatize the Problem · Motion Tricks · Podcast · Reminder · The Absurd Alternative · In Real Life ·
Reaction in Action · Evolution · Question · On Trend (FOMO) · Teaser Hook · Make Me Laugh · Emphasize
the Solution · Negative Hook · Satisfying Intro · Destroy, Toss & Burn · Skeptical Voice

**Counted directly from the course.** This skill carries all seventeen and flags the count, rather
than trimming the list to match the number or repeating the number as given.

⚠️ **Attribution:** the framework is credited to **Meta's Creative Shop in Israel**, led by **Udi
Avital**, who is also ACA's Course 702 faculty. It is Meta's work, not ACA's, and should be cited
that way.

⭐ **Two of the seventeen appear as dropdown values in ACA's storyboard template** — *Skeptical
Voice* and *Dramatize the Problem* — which is how the 401 hook work and the 403 production template
connect in practice.

### ⚠️ The figures are ACA's

| Claim | As given |
|---|---|
| Sound-on behaviour | *"Over 90% of TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts users engage with sound on"* |
| Sound-first performance | *"Videos with bespoke sound can **lower CPAs by up to 50%**"* — their own client tests, no sample given |
| Hook copy examples | *"This serum cleared my acne in 2 weeks"* · *"can add years to your life"* · *"This acne serum sold out 5x!"* |

⛔ **The "up to 50%" is a ceiling from unnamed client tests**, which is the weakest form a
performance claim can take. Quote it as ACA's claim about their own work or not at all.

⛔ **The hook copy examples contain results, health and sales claims.** They are reproduced because
they are ACA's teaching examples, and this skill states plainly that **a hook written for a real
brand must not invent a claim the brand has not made.** ACA does not raise this anywhere.

### ⚠️ A conflict with ACA's own Meta course

This course says over 90% of short-form users watch with sound on, and builds a method on it. ACA's
**Course 201** teaches the opposite discipline for Meta: never assume audio.

**Both are correct for their platform**, and neither course mentions the other. Carrying the TikTok
figure into a Meta feed brief is a real and easy error. Flagged in **Sound-first ads** (below).

### What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Mirella's, quoted directly.** The scripts are written prose and need
almost no tidying.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the four-file split, the routing table, rendering the
Diversification Guide's five axes and the ad type libraries as tables, and numbering the seventeen
hook categories so the count discrepancy is visible.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The rule that a hook must not carry a claim the brand has not made**, and the observation that
  ACA's own examples do. Hooks are the most liftable output of this course.
- ⭐ **Reading the Diversification Guide as a gap audit rather than an inspiration list** — lay the
  current creative against the five axes and the empty cells are the brief.
- ⭐ **The point that refreshing hooks is the cheapest performance fix in the programme**, and should
  be checked before briefing anything new.
- ⚠️ **The note that a concept missing its Motivator is an execution idea**, not a concept, and
  cannot be varied because it has no thesis to hold constant.
- **The note that the Offer field can legitimately be empty** and should be marked so, because an
  invented discount becomes one someone expects to run.
- **The observation that presentation fields 6 and 7 are what make it an approval document**, since
  signing off a concept means agreeing the motivator and benefit.
- **The point that sixteen static ad types against ten video types** makes statics the fastest route
  to volume when production budget is the constraint.
- **Using the seventeen categories as a coverage check**, and the note that three of them carry brand
  risk the course does not flag.
- ⭐ **Keeping the `[BRACKETS]` in database templates**, so a pattern stays reusable rather than
  reading as a finished hook.
- **The reading of the Roman example as the clearest argument for sound-first**, because a metaphor
  is not a claim and clears compliance where a stated benefit would not.
- ⭐ **Naming the recurring audio signature as the strategic option** in sound design.
- ⛔ **The requirement for a silent fallback on a sound-first ad.** Some placements autoplay muted
  regardless of platform norms, and the course never addresses it.
- **The note that Step 4's variations are one concept tested cleanly, not five concepts.**

### Scope

Concepting, briefing and hook writing. It stops at the storyboard: shot lists, scripts and shooting
documents are **Course 403**. It does not cover media buying, testing methodology or measurement, and
it names no metric thresholds.
