# Course 230: Cross Channel Strategies

**Carlotta Costanzo**, GAIN. 33m 08s, 3 modules, 9 case studies.

The course about **orchestration** — how platforms and funnel stages fit together, rather than how
any one of them works.

> "Your customers aren't sticking to just one channel. They're scrolling through social media,
> searching on Google, networking on LinkedIn, and listening to music on YouTube. That's why
> multi-channel strategies are not optional — they're essential."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none.** Ask a cross-channel question and get an answer.

**To run the AI workflow and produce adapted messaging, required:**

1. **The brand**, and what makes it different from the obvious alternatives
2. **The audience**, and what you already know about them

**Optional, and it sharpens everything:**

3. Which channels are running now, and how budget currently splits
4. Whether this is a peak trading moment or steady state
5. The core message, if one already exists

## The three principles the course ends on

> **1. Be where your customers are.** They move between platforms seamlessly; effective campaigns
> meet them on their terms.
>
> **2. Align creative with context.** Tailor format and messaging to both the journey stage **and**
> the platform's strengths.
>
> **3. Stay consistent, but evolve your story.** Anchor to one core message; change how you tell it
> as people move from awareness to consideration to conversion.

⭐ **Principle 3 is the one that does the work**, and the one people get wrong in both directions —
either running identical creative everywhere, or running four unrelated campaigns that share a logo.

## Answering well

**Push and pull are not either/or.** The course's most useful correction: Booking.com is the pull
example and Dr. Squatch the push example, and *both brands do both.* The question is the balance and
what it should be right now.

⭐ **The seasonal rhythm is the actionable part.** Ramp push before and during a peak to build
demand, then let pull capture the high-intent audience that creates — *"often at higher efficiency
and lower cost."* Someone planning Black Friday should hear this.

**Do not map one platform to one funnel stage.** The course is explicit that while platforms have
natural strengths, *"the most effective cross-channel strategies use all relevant platforms
throughout the journey"*, with content and objectives tailored per stage.

**Metrics change by stage, and judging a stage by the wrong metric is the standard failure.**
Awareness work measured on ROAS will always look like it failed.

## The case for multi-channel, and the push/pull balance

### Why more than one channel

⭐ **ACA's supporting evidence:** a **Harvard Business Review study of 46,000 shoppers** found that
omnichannel customers spend significantly more than single-channel ones — **9% more in-store and
10% more online.**

Four arguments, and the third is the one people underrate:

1. **Visibility and memorability.** Being present in several places makes the message stick.
2. **Engagement and trust.** *"When people see your brand in different places, they're more likely
   to trust you and take action."*
3. ⭐ **Resilience and risk diversification.** *"If one channel's performance drops, algorithm
   changes, or costs spike, your other channels keep your brand in front of your audience."* This
   is an argument for multi-channel even when a single channel is currently working well.
4. **Richer data.** Seeing which messages and platforms work where enables faster, better decisions.

⚠️ **The cost of opening channels, named honestly:** *"when you open up new channels, you also enter
the world of multi-channel attribution — the beauty and the complexity. Understanding which channel
truly drove a conversion can be challenging."*

> "A multi-channel strategy isn't just about being everywhere — it's about **being everywhere that
> matters**."

## Push versus pull

The distinction that decides how budget splits.

| | **Pull** | **Push** |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Captures demand that already exists | Creates demand that does not yet exist |
| The moment | Someone is actively searching | Someone is scrolling, not looking for you |
| Typical form | Search, Shopping | Video in feed, display, notifications |
| Intent | Highest | None yet |

**Pull** *"meet existing demand and help customers find you when their intent is highest."*
**Push** is *"proactively putting your message in front of people who may not be looking for your
product or service yet... creating demand and sparking interest."*

### The two examples, and the correction that follows them

**Booking.com — pull.** You type *"best hotels in Paris"* into Google and Booking.com is the
sponsored result at the top. Classic demand capture.

**Dr. Squatch — push.** You are scrolling TikTok, Instagram or YouTube and a funny video about
natural soap for men appears. *"Maybe you weren't even thinking about soap, but the ad hooks you
with humor and storytelling."*

⭐ **Then the course immediately corrects the obvious inference:**

> "Does this mean Booking.com only uses pull strategies, and Dr. Squatch only uses push? **Of course
> not!** Why? Most successful brands blend both approaches."

- **Booking.com** also runs demand-gen ads, targets broad audiences, and retargets on social —
  *"classic push tactics."*
- **Dr. Squatch** invests in search ads and content marketing to capture people actively looking for
  natural soap — *"so they're not just creating demand, but also capturing it when it appears."*

> "Push and pull aren't either/or—they **work best together.** As a brand, your goal is to find the
> right balance and allocate your budget in a way that guarantees the best return."

That balance depends on brand, product, audience and objectives. There is no percentage in the
course, and inventing one would be worse than saying so.

### ⭐ The seasonal rhythm, which is the actionable part

The balance **is not static**, and this is the most immediately usable idea in the section:

> "Brand seasonality and key sales moments — like Black Friday — can significantly influence your
> approach. During peak sales periods or seasonal spikes, brands often **ramp up push marketing** to
> maximize reach and create urgency among broader audiences. This push builds awareness and
> stimulates demand ahead of or during these critical moments."
>
> "After this push phase, **pull strategies become especially important** to capture high-intent
> audiences who are now actively searching and ready to convert, **often at higher efficiency and
> lower cost.**"

**The sequence, plainly:** push creates the demand, then pull harvests it — and the harvest is
cheaper than the creation because intent has already been manufactured.

> "A dynamic, data-driven approach to balancing push and pull — adjusted for seasonality and sales
> events — is essential for sustainable growth."

⚠️ **The failure this prevents:** a brand that only runs pull during a peak is competing for a
demand pool it did nothing to fill, at the moment that pool is most contested. A brand that only
runs push has manufactured intent and then left it uncaptured.

## The four platform personalities

> "Every channel has its own style, audience mindset, and creative best practices."

Carlotta gives each platform a character. The label is a memory aid; the useful part is the
creative implication attached to each.

---

### Meta — The Data-Driven Diplomat

> "Sophisticated, versatile, and always equipped with insights."

- **Balances broad reach with precise targeting**
- Massive, diverse audience across Facebook, Instagram and Messenger
- Format variety: images, video, carousels, stories
- ⭐ **Robust analytics and conversion tracking** — *"allowing you to measure impact at every stage
  of the customer journey"*
- *"The diplomat knows how to speak to different audiences"* — adapts messaging across demographics
  while holding brand consistency

Suits both broad awareness and highly targeted conversion.

**What makes it stand out creatively:** the diversity of formats, and the ability to tailor content
per audience and placement *"ensuring your creative always fits the context."*

---

### TikTok — The Trendsetter

⚠️ **ACA's script calls this platform two different things** — the slide list says *"The Show
Stopper"* and the section itself says *"The Trendsetter"*. Same platform, same description. See
`provenance.md`.

> Began as a hub for dance challenges for younger generations, *"but it quickly evolved into a
> global stage for creativity, discovery, and cultural influence that now embraces all age groups."*

- Short-form video plus a powerful algorithm that **rewards originality and authenticity**
- Favours **native, lo-fi and user-generated content**
- Thrives on **immediacy and relevance**
- *"Where brands can shape culture"* — sparking trends, driving engagement, connecting playfully

**What makes it stand out creatively:** it encourages *"unconventional, trend-generating ideas"* and
gives brands room to experiment with native, creator-driven content.

---

### Google — The Interpreter

> "Translating customer intent into opportunities for your brand to provide solutions at exactly the
> right moment."

- **Captures high-intent users via Search and Shopping** — *"exceptionally powerful for
  consideration and conversion stages"*
- ⚠️ **But its reach is not only search.** Display, YouTube and Demand Gen work *"across the entire
  funnel"* — a point people miss when they file Google under "bottom of funnel only"
- Advanced demographic and contextual targeting, reaching people on immediate needs and interests

**What makes it stand out creatively:** dynamically adapting creative assets *"using AI and
automation — to serve the most relevant, visually compelling, and contextually tailored ads at
scale."*

---

### YouTube — The Storyteller

> "While YouTube is part of the Google ecosystem, it deserves its own spotlight."

- **A full spectrum of formats** — from quick Shorts to in-depth product demos and reviews
- Short-form **and** long-form video, so it can drive *"everything from instant awareness to deeper
  consideration and action"*
- Reaches **both active searchers and passive viewers**, at every funnel stage
- Long watch times on traditional video plus high engagement on Shorts make it good for testing
  creative and scaling

**What makes it stand out creatively:** *"unmatched versatility — enabling brands to deliver
everything from snappy, trend-driven content to immersive, story-driven experiences."*

## ⭐ Uber, adapting across six channels

The course's masterclass example. Same brand, same promise, six different executions.

| Channel | What Uber does |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | A mix of static visuals, UGC, and meme-inspired creative. *"Statics remain a winning format here, while UGC adds authenticity"* |
| **TikTok** | Native, creator-driven — quick *"travel hack"* videos showing how easy Uber is on the go. Fun, helpful, trend-driven |
| **Google Search** | Direct and functional. Immediacy and convenience, headlines like *"A Ride When You Need It"*, meeting people at the moment of intent |
| **YouTube** | Cinematic storytelling. The *"On Our Way"* campaign uses emotionally charged long-form video around universal moments |
| **LinkedIn** | A business shift — Uber for Business, employee experience and programme management, aimed at decision-makers. Statics and carousels |
| **Out of home** | Airport billboards with simple evocative visuals: *"You're almost there"* — reaching travellers *"in a moment that matters"* |

> "Uber's approach is a masterclass in cross-channel adaptation: **the core promise is always
> consistent, but the format, tone, and message are tailored for each platform and audience.**"

⚠️ **Note what is being adapted and what is not.** The promise — a ride when you need one — never
changes. What changes is the register: functional on Search, emotional on YouTube, professional on
LinkedIn, situational on a billboard. That is the difference between adaptation and running six
unrelated campaigns.

## Funnel-to-channel mapping

> "The key to an effective cross-channel strategy isn't just being everywhere — **it's being in the
> right places at the right times with the right messages.**"

### The three stages

| Stage | The customer is | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| **Top — awareness** | Discovering your brand, becoming aware of their problem | Capture attention, leave a memorable first impression |
| **Middle — consideration** | Evaluating potential solutions, including yours | **Build trust.** Enable deeper engagement and education |
| **Bottom — conversion** | Ready to decide, needing a final push | Targeted, personalised messages to high-intent prospects |

*"Each stage demands distinct messaging, creative formats, and calls to action."*

### ⛔ Do not park one platform at one stage

The most important correction in this section, and the one that contradicts how most people use a
platform-personality framework:

> "While certain platforms may naturally excel at different funnel stages, **the most effective
> cross-channel strategies use all relevant platforms throughout the journey** — with content and
> objectives tailored to each stage."

Awareness examples the course gives: entertaining TikTok videos, visually striking Instagram
Stories, YouTube's immersive formats, Google Demand Gen campaigns. Note that Google appears in the
awareness list, not only at the bottom.

> "What makes the difference isn't just which platform you choose for each stage, but **how you
> adapt your creative approach, messaging, and campaign objectives** to fit both the platform's
> environment and the user's position in their journey."

---

## ⭐ Metrics belong to stages

Judging a stage by another stage's metric is the standard failure here.

| Stage | Measure |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Reach · impressions · brand awareness · engagement · website traffic · social engagement |
| **Consideration** | Engagement rates (video watch time, comments) · time on site · lead conversion rate · **lead quality** |
| **Conversion** | Conversion rate · **ROAS** · **CAC** |

> At the awareness stage, *"you're not trying to sell yet — **you're trying to get remembered.**"*

⚠️ **So awareness work measured on ROAS will always look like a failure**, and conversion work
measured on reach will always look like a success. Fix the metric before judging the creative.

Note that **lead quality** sits in the middle column, not just lead volume — the course is explicit
that it matters.

## The awareness case studies

Two campaigns the course uses to show what awareness work looks like when it works.

### Nike, "Just Do It", 1988

The original spot featuring **Walt Stack**, an 80-year-old marathon runner, jogging across the
Golden Gate Bridge sharing his daily routine. No flashy editing, no superstar athlete.

> "In many ways, this ad feels like an early version of what we now call user-generated content."

**Why it worked:**

1. The slogan is **simple and sticks**
2. It taps something deeper — *"determination and self-belief matter more than age or ability"*
3. Nike **kept the message alive for decades**, always finding new ways to keep it relevant

⭐ **The point being made:** *"Nike wasn't focused on selling a specific shoe or product. Instead,
they were building a brand mindset."* A textbook awareness campaign — memorable and emotionally
resonant, not pushing an immediate sale.

### Dollar Shave Club, 2012

Founder **Mike Dubin** walking through a warehouse, cracking jokes, explaining the razor
subscription *"in a totally unfiltered, hilarious way."*

**Why it worked:**

1. **The value proposition is crystal clear within the first 10 seconds** — *"no confusion, just
   straight talk"*
2. **Humour and authenticity are disarming** — it feels real, not like a traditional ad
3. **So entertaining and unexpected that people wanted to share it**

⭐ **The result:** *"Twelve thousand new customers in the first 48 hours, and a billion-dollar
acquisition just a few years later."*

> "Both Nike and Dollar Shave Club prove that great awareness campaigns don't just introduce a
> product — **they create a feeling, a memory, or a laugh that sticks with people** long after the
> ad is over."

## ⭐ Ffern, carried across all three stages

The course's most useful example, because it is **one brand** shown shifting, so the changes are
visible rather than theoretical.

**The brand:** a London-based fragrance house that *"treats perfume like fine wine"* — small-batch,
seasonal releases, subscribers only. A spot on the **"ledger"** costs **$129 per season** and
guarantees four new scents a year. *"This model creates both exclusivity and anticipation — and
it's reflected through all their marketing."*

| Stage | Creative | Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| **Top** | **Exclusivity and intrigue.** Striking visuals — *"blood-orange sunsets"* — that catch the eye and spark curiosity. *"The goal here isn't to sell, but to make people stop scrolling and wonder what Ffern is all about"* | Reach · impressions · website traffic · social engagement |
| **Middle** | **Trust and credibility.** Real people sharing first impressions in short-form video. Testimonials that feel *"premium but relatable, bridging the gap between curiosity and purchase intent"* | Engagement rates (watch time, comments) · time on site · lead conversion rate · lead quality |
| **Bottom** | **Direct and actionable.** Simple product-focused statics highlighting **scarcity and FOMO**, plus elegant unboxing visuals. *"The message is clear: spots are limited, and you don't want to miss out"* | Conversion rate · ROAS · CAC |

**The outcome**, as the course reports it: the New York Times headline *"A Half-Million People Are
Waiting for Ffern, a Fragrance They Have Never Smelled."*

> "That's the power of a well-executed, cross-funnel creative strategy."

⭐ **What to take from it.** The brand promise — scarce, seasonal, crafted — never changes. What
changes is the *job*: intrigue, then reassurance, then urgency. The scarcity that reads as mystique
at the top reads as a deadline at the bottom, and that is the same fact doing two different jobs.

## The four-step AI workflow

Carlotta's answer to the question the course sets up:

> "How can I move faster, come up with more creative ideas, and make sure my messaging is always
> relevant — no matter the channel or stage of the funnel? **This is where AI can become your secret
> weapon.**"

Four steps, each with a prompt, reproduced as written. They run in sequence — each takes the
previous step's output as its input, which is what makes it a workflow rather than four prompts.

> "You can save these prompts and reuse them for any campaign, adjusting the details for your brand
> and audience."

### ⚠️ Before running any of them

**Step 1 is the only step that touches reality.** Steps 2, 3 and 4 all build on its output, so if
step 1 is run thin, everything downstream is confident and ungrounded — and it will not look it.

Two specific cautions:

- **Step 1 asks for "five surprising statistics about the product or service category."** A model
  will supply five. ⛔ **Treat every one as unverified until checked** — a fabricated category
  statistic that reaches a client deck is the most likely way this workflow causes real damage.
- **The output of step 2 is a claim about your brand.** Check it is true of the actual product
  before it becomes the anchor for everything else.

---

### Step 1 — Research & Insight Generation

> "Our goal is to uncover what really drives your audience — their frustrations, desires, and unmet
> needs — not just with your brand, but across the whole category. This ensures your messaging is
> rooted in real, relevant insights."

```
Act as a creative strategist.

Identify the main pain points, unmet needs, and emotional drivers that lead potential customers to
brands like [brand X]. Do not focus on pain points about [brand X] itself; instead, focus on the
broader frustrations, desires, or human needs that would motivate someone to seek out a brand in
this category. List key language around benefits, pain points, features, objections, and failed
solutions. Provide five surprising statistics about the product or service category.
```

⭐ **Note the deliberate instruction to look past the brand.** *"Do not focus on pain points about
[brand X] itself"* — the point is category-level human need, which is what a cross-channel message
has to connect to. A brand-level answer produces messaging only existing customers understand.

⚠️ **The five statistics are the risk.** Everything else this prompt returns is a hypothesis you can
test cheaply; a statistic is a claim that will get repeated. Verify or drop them.

---

### Step 2 — Core Message Generation

> "Now it's time to condense those insights into a single, powerful message that sets your brand
> apart and connects emotionally and rationally with your audience. **This core message anchors your
> campaign and ensures consistency.**"

```
Based on the previous research insights, write one clear, compelling core message for [brand X]
that:
- Clearly differentiates the brand from mainstream competitors
- Appeals to both the emotional and rational needs of the target audience
- Uses vivid, memorable language
- Addresses the audience's main pain points and aspirations
- Is concise enough to be used as the foundation for campaign messaging
```

> "With this, you're not just creating a tagline — you're defining the story you'll tell across every
> channel and touchpoint."

⭐ **This is the thing principle 3 says to hold constant.** Everything in step 3 and step 4 is a
restatement of it, so a weak or untrue core message multiplies rather than dilutes.

---

### Step 3 — Funnel Stage Message Adaptation

> "Adapt your core message for each stage of the funnel, so it remains relevant as your audience
> moves from discovery to decision."

```
Take this core message: [insert message].

Rewrite it for each stage of the funnel:
- Awareness: curiosity-driven and emotionally resonant
- Consideration: informative and trust-building
- Conversion: urgent and action-oriented.
```

**Note the register named for each stage** — curiosity, then information, then urgency. That is the
Ffern progression in three words, and it is the concrete version of *"stay consistent but evolve
your story."*

⚠️ **Check the three still say the same thing.** The failure mode is three messages that read as
three different products. If the conversion version could belong to another brand, the core message
was not carried through.

---

### Step 4 — Channel-Specific Creative Tests & Hypotheses

> "The purpose here is to make sure your campaign is not only consistent, but also optimized for how
> people interact on platforms like Meta, TikTok, and Google."

```
Based on the adapted funnel messages, suggest one creative test idea for Meta, TikTok, and Google.
For each, specify the funnel stage (TOFU, MOFU, BOFU), describe the creative concept, define the
target audience, and state the main hypothesis for why it should work on that platform and at that
funnel stage.
```

⭐ **The hypothesis requirement is what makes this a test rather than a list of ideas.** *"State the
main hypothesis for why it should work on that platform and at that funnel stage"* — an idea with no
stated reason cannot teach you anything when it wins or loses.

⚠️ **The output is a starting point for a media conversation, not a media plan.** It has no budget,
no duration, no measurement design. Take the concepts and hypotheses; build the test properly with
whoever owns the buy.

---

### What the workflow is and is not

> "By moving through these steps — research, core messaging, funnel adaptation, and channel-specific
> testing — you can use AI not just to speed up your process, but to create smarter, more effective
> campaigns."

**It is:** a fast route from category insight to a consistent, stage-adapted, platform-specific set
of testable concepts. Genuinely useful, and the sequencing is the value.

**It is not:** research. Step 1 asks a model what it thinks the category's pain points are; that is
a hypothesis generator, not evidence from your customers. Where real customer research exists, paste
it in rather than asking the model to imagine it.

> "Start with these prompts, adapt them to your brand, and watch how much faster and more
> confidently you can move from insight to execution."

### Scope

This course is about **orchestration** — how channels and funnel stages fit together. It is not a
platform how-to, and it says nothing about budget percentages, channel benchmarks, or attribution
methodology beyond naming multi-channel attribution as *"the beauty and the complexity."*

For platform mechanics, ACA has dedicated courses on Meta and TikTok. Where those skills are
installed alongside this one, route mechanics to them and keep this one for the orchestration
question.
