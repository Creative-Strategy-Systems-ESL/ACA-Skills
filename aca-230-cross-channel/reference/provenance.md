## ⚠️ The source is a production script, not a delivered recording

**Two consequences:**

1. **It contains stage directions rather than content** — *"Show the video"*, *"Let's have a look at

   included, so this skill carries Carlotta's analysis of them and not the creative itself.

## ⚠️ How old this material is

ACA stamps no recording date. What can be established: the course was received into this library in
**2026-W31**, which is a "no later than" and not a recording date.

Two internal markers give a floor: it cites a **Harvard Business Review** study of 46,000 shoppers,

## ⚠️ Two discrepancies in ACA's own script

**1. TikTok is given two different names.** The slide list reads *"TikTok: The Show Stopper"* and
the section heading four lines later reads *"TikTok: The Trendsetter"*. Same platform, same
description. This skill uses **The Trendsetter**, because that is the name attached to the actual
write-up and the body text repeats it (*"As the trendsetter, TikTok is where brands can shape
culture"*). Flagged rather than silently reconciled.

**2. The HBR figure is stated twice, differently.** First as *"9% more in-store and 10% more
online"*, then in the recap compressed to *"up to 10% higher spend"*. Both are carried where they
appear; the fuller version is the one to quote.

## What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Carlotta's, quoted directly.** The script is written prose rather than
speech, so quotes need almost no tidying.

**This skill's organisation:** the four-file split, the routing table, and grouping the case studies
into the funnel-mapping file rather than leaving them where they fall in the script.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- The framing of the **push/pull seasonal rhythm as a sequence** — push manufactures the demand,
  pull harvests it more cheaply — and the note on what goes wrong when a brand runs only one of them
  into a peak. Carlotta describes the rhythm; the failure modes are drawn out here.
- The note on **what Uber changes and what it does not** — that the promise is fixed and only the
  register moves, which is the line between adaptation and six unrelated campaigns.
- The observation that **Ffern's scarcity does two different jobs** — mystique at the top of the
  funnel, deadline at the bottom — which is the clearest illustration of principle 3 in the course.
- The warning that **awareness work measured on ROAS will always look like failure**, which follows
  from the stage-metrics table but is not spelled out.
- The cautions in `ai-workflow.md` about **step 1's five statistics** and about the workflow being a
  hypothesis generator rather than research.

## Scope

This course is about **orchestration** — how channels and funnel stages fit together. It is not a
platform how-to, and it says nothing about budget percentages, channel benchmarks, or attribution
methodology beyond naming multi-channel attribution as *"the beauty and the complexity."*

For platform mechanics, ACA has dedicated courses on Meta and TikTok. Where those skills are
installed alongside this one, route mechanics to them and keep this one for the orchestration
question.
