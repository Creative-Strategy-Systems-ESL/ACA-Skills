# The three kinds of skill, and how to use them

⭐ **This is the question new students ask that the curriculum map cannot answer.** They know what
ACA teaches; they do not know what a skill is or which one to open. Answer from here.

## Tell them apart from the name

There is one rule and it is reliable:

| The name | What it is |
|---|---|
| A **number** after `aca-` — `aca-402-key-metrics` | Follows a course. The number is the course code |
| A **verb** after `aca-` — `aca-build-angles` | Makes a document. Named for what it does |
| `aca-welcome` | This one. The front door |

## The three kinds

**Teach.** Ask a question in plain words, get an answer. It is the faculty member's material on
call. It never asks for a form first, and it does not need a brand or an ad to be useful. Use it to
understand something, settle a disagreement, or check what the course actually says.

**Teach and do.** Explains the method, asks what the document needs, then produces it — teaching on
the way through. Same material as the course, except the student finishes with the worksheet filled
in rather than notes about it. Use it when they want both the understanding and the artifact.

**Do.** No lesson. It says what it needs, asks for anything missing one question at a time, and
hands back the finished worksheet for a real brand. Use it when they already know the method and
just want the output.

⚠️ **A course skill and a "do" skill can cover the same ground on purpose.**
`aca-103-creative-fundamentals` teaches the ten angle types; `aca-build-angles` produces angles for
a real offer. Neither is a lesser version of the other — route on whether there is a real brand on
the table.

## What to tell someone opening their first one

**Just describe the problem.** Skills load themselves when they are relevant, so
`which part of ACA covers hooks` or `break this ad down into its building blocks` is enough. A
skill can also be named directly.

**The document skills ask questions first, and they will not invent.** They refuse to make up a
price, a customer count, a discount, or an ad they cannot see. A missing input gets a question, not
a plausible guess. ⭐ **Say this out loud to a new student** — someone expecting an instant answer
reads the questions as the skill being broken, when it is the skill working.

**Each one stands alone.** No skill needs another installed, there is no order, and nothing has to
be run first.

⚠️ **This skill is the exception.** Its job is pointing at others, so on its own it will name
skills the student may not have. Always check what is installed alongside before naming one — see
`routing.md`.

## Where the skills came from

Every skill was built from ACA's own recordings and worksheets. Quotations are verbatim, including
the transcription slips, which are flagged rather than silently corrected so any quote can be
checked against its source. Each skill carries a `reference/provenance.md` naming the material it
came from, the faculty member who taught it, and — where a named ACA asset was not available —
saying so plainly rather than papering over the gap.
