# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — Course 104: How to Make Terrible Great Ads (100-Level), taught by
**George Mack (The Ad Professor)**. 17 minutes 29 seconds, 4 modules, 2 case studies.

The four modules as ACA lists them:

1. Talk to Real People, Not "Customers" (4m 09s)
2. Track Emotion Through Facial Reactions (3m 56s)
3. Design Ads for Sharing (3m 46s)
4. Obsess Over Unit Economics (5m 38s)

⚠️ **Note the direction.** ACA's module titles state the principles the right way round; on the
recording George states each one inverted, as an instruction for making ads worse, and flips it at
the end. This skill keeps the inverted framing because it is the technique, not a stylistic choice.
Anyone comparing the module list against the recording will find them mirrored.

## The title

The course is listed as "How to Make Terrible Great Ads", and in course artwork "Terrible" appears
struck through. George opens by saying "welcome to how to make terrible ads", which is the honest
description of the content.

## Credits George gives

- **Jeff Bezos** — asking what will *not* change in the next ten years, the illustration of
  inversion
- **Dwarkesh Patel** — the writing test: "what are you trying to say here? Then just say that",
  which George notes was about writing and applies equally to advertising
- **Sean** (a friend, no surname given) — the compression idea, that great advertising reduces to a
  sound or short acronym
- **Adam Mosseri**, head of Instagram — that most content consumed on Instagram arrives via sharing
  and DMs
- **Alex Hormozi** — the gross-margin model, which George introduces as "a model that blew my mind
  that I stole from Alex"

The midwit meme is referenced as the motivation for inversion generally.

## What is quoted and what is organised

The four principles, their reasoning, the margin arithmetic, the factory map, the art/science/maths
split and every example are George's, quoted or reproduced.

`the-pre-ship-check.md` is this skill's organisation. George does not present his material as a
checklist; it is arranged that way here so it can be run against a real ad. The severity ordering
in that file (4, then 2, then 1, then 3) is also this skill's, derived from George's own statement
that principle 4 "might be the most important one because of how common it is", plus the observation
that the arithmetic determines whether creative changes can help at all.

## A note on scope

This is a short, deliberately reductive course. George's method is to identify the few things that
decide outcomes and ignore the rest, which makes it a strong pre-ship check and a poor creative
strategy. It will not tell you what your angle is, who your audience should be, or how to structure
an ad. Other courses in the programme cover those.
