# Build audience personas from copy mining

**Ad Creative Academy**, Course 302 (Mirella Crespi), module 2.

> "Most marketers make the mistake of guessing what their audience cares about. But here's the
> secret — you don't have to guess."

> "We're not inventing messaging — we're just translating our audience's exact pain points into
> solution-focused hooks."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **The product or category**, and roughly what it costs
2. **Source material to mine**, or somewhere to mine it. Reviews, forum threads, ad comments,
   support tickets, survey responses, search questions. The more raw and unedited the better

**Optional, and each sharpens the personas:**

3. Competitors whose reviews and ad comments can also be mined
4. Existing customer data — who actually buys, and any segments already known
5. What has been tried in creative and what fell flat
6. The platform the creative is destined for

⛔ **NEVER WRITE A QUOTE YOU DID NOT FIND. In any mode, for any reason, under any deadline.**

This is the one rule with no exception. Every other prohibition in this skill is about *hooks*, and a
hook is checked against the research — so fabricating a customer quote and then deriving a hook from
it defeats every check, because the phrase now "appears in the research". **The quote is the thing
that must be real.** If a KEY LANGUAGE cell has no source, it stays empty and says why.

⛔ **The whole method is other people's words, so the personas are only as good as the raw material.**
If none is supplied, do not present personas as findings. Say what to collect and where, using the
source list in **The copy mining expedition** (below).

**What you can still deliver with nothing mined**, which is not the same thing:

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **A hypothesis scaffold** | The persona *shapes* you expect to find, so the client can sanity-check the direction and you know which clusters to look for. Every cell marked `⚠️ INFERRED` |
| ⛔ **Never** | The KEY LANGUAGE row. Mark it `⛔ NO SOURCE QUOTE — HYPOTHESIS` and leave it empty |
| ⛔ **Never** | Hooks. They derive from language that does not exist yet |
| ✅ **A collection plan** | Which sources, in what order, and what to send back |

**Say what it is in the document**: a scaffold to aim the research, not the deliverable. The empty
KEY LANGUAGE row is the honest signal of that, so do not fill it to make the artifact look finished.

**Deliver the scaffold and ask, rather than asking and waiting** — unless the person is plainly
mid-conversation with you and a question will get an answer in minutes.

## ⭐ The thing that makes these personas work

The slide template has **two columns, not one**:

| Primary Persona Details | Creative Translation |
|---|---|
| What is true about this person | **What to do about it in an ad** |

**Every attribute is paired with an instruction.** A motivator is not "desire for efficiency", it is
"desire for efficiency → emphasise time-saving benefits, show the before/after mental state".

⛔ **A persona with an empty right-hand column is a description, not a tool.** That column is the
entire reason the artifact exists, and it is the one people leave blank.

## How to run it

1. **Mine first, and collect verbatim.** **The copy mining expedition** (below). Exact phrases, not summaries.
2. **Cluster the language**, not the demographics. Patterns form around desires, frustrations and
   life situations.
3. **Build a persona per cluster**, with all six rows filled on both sides.
4. **Do the creative translation exercise** — turn the collected phrases into hooks.
5. **Check every persona against the test below.**

## The test each persona has to pass

> "When you're writing a script or designing creative, you should be able to ask, 'Would Busy Mom
> Brenda connect with this message?' and know the answer **instantly**."

⭐ **If the answer is not instant, the persona is too vague to be worth having.** That test is not
just about the creative; it is the quality check on the persona itself. A persona that cannot decide
anything is a paragraph with a name on it.

## Answering well

**Use their words, literally.** *"The most powerful copy comes directly from your customers' mouths,
not your marketing team's brainstorms."* If a persona's language section reads like marketing
English, the copy mining did not happen.

⭐ **Check every hook against the research.** In ACA's own worked example, each hook keeps the
customer's own noun phrase (`the 5pm panic`, `guilty about takeout`, `my to-do list`) and
changes only the tense or the outcome. **Nothing new is introduced. A hook containing a phrase that
appears nowhere in the research was invented, not mined** — rewrite it or drop it.

**Go past demographics.** *"Understanding your audience goes way beyond basic demographics like age
and location."* Age and location belong on the slide; they are not the finding.

**Trigger events are the most actionable row and the most skipped.** They are when someone is
receptive, which makes them the row that changes campaign timing rather than just messaging.

⚠️ **Mirella's own caution about doing this with AI, which is unusually firm:** *"Mastering the
manual process first is absolutely essential. I've seen too many marketers jump straight to AI
without understanding what they're actually looking for, and the results are... not great."* The
prompts summarise material you supply. **They do not find it, and they cannot invent it honestly.**

## The copy mining expedition

> "There's a goldmine of information already out there just waiting to be discovered. We call this
> process **'Copy Mining'** — literally digging up the exact words, phrases, and emotions your ideal
> customers are already using."

The goal Mirella names is *"developing your customer empathy muscle"*: the ability to think, feel and
talk like your customers. What you are collecting is **zero-party data** — *"direct insights straight
from your audience's mouths."*

### The sources, and what each one gives you

#### Customer reviews and surveys

- **Ask open questions.** *"What made you finally decide to buy?"* · *"What's your biggest
  frustration with products like this?"* Nothing answerable yes or no
- ⭐ **"Don't just look at 5-star reviews! The 3-star ones are where you'll find the most honest
  feedback about what's working and what's not."**
- **Hunt emotional words** — *frustrated, finally, game-changer, disappointed*

> "I once found a review where someone said they were 'literally in tears' when our product solved
> their problem. That exact phrasing became the foundation for an entire campaign."

#### Amazon

*"Even if you don't sell on Amazon, it's a treasure trove of research."*

- Search the category, take the **top 3 to 5 bestsellers**
- **Filter 2-3 stars for pain points, 4-5 stars for the benefits that actually matter**
- Build a swipe file of *"I love how it..."* and *"I wish it would..."*

#### Forums and Reddit

> "Unlike reviews, these conversations happen **before** someone's bought anything, so you see raw
> pain points."

- Search the category, competitor names, or the problem
- Threads with many comments and emotional language
- Save posts where people vent or describe an ideal solution

> "Find the subreddits where your audience hangs out and just observe. **Don't post or sell — just
> listen.**"

⭐ **The pre-purchase versus post-purchase distinction is the sharpest idea in the module.** A review
is written by someone who owns the thing and is partly justifying the decision. A forum post is
written while the problem is still unsolved, which is the state your ad has to meet them in. **If you
mine only reviews, your hooks will speak to people who have already bought something.**

#### Support tickets and emails

⚠️ **Not one of ACA's named sources, and often the only first-party language a young brand has.**
Included here because the skill accepts it as raw material and the course gives no guidance for it.

**It is a third state, and the pre/post-purchase split above does not cover it.** Someone contacting
support has bought *and* is mid-frustration. That combination makes it the best source for two rows
and a poor source for the rest:

| Row | Value from support tickets |
|---|---|
| **Objections** | ⭐ Highest of any source. These are the doubts that survived the purchase |
| **Pain points** | ⭐ High, but note they are *post-purchase* pain, which may be product friction rather than the life problem the ad has to speak to |
| **Trigger events** | ⚠️ Occasionally, when someone explains why they bought |
| **Motivators** | ⚠️ Weak. People rarely explain what they wanted when reporting a fault |
| **Key language** | ✅ Genuine verbatim, but skewed to the vocabulary of complaint |

⛔ **The skew is severe and must be stated on the persona.** Support contacts are people who had a
problem. A persona built only from them is a persona of customers in difficulty, which is a real
segment and not the market.

**Also read the contact rate itself as a finding.** A high ratio of support contacts to units sold
says something about the product, the onboarding or the anxiety level of the category, and that
changes what the ads have to pre-empt.

#### Ad comments

- Your own **highest and lowest** performing ads
- Competitors' ads in the public ad library
- Note **objections, questions, and enthusiastic responses**

#### Search and question tools

- **Answer the Public** — what questions people actually ask
- **Google Trends** — seasonal interest and related terms that reveal angles
- **Pinterest** — most-saved pins and their descriptions. Strong for lifestyle, home and DIY

#### Social platform search

- Hashtags and keywords on TikTok and Instagram
- Note **style, pacing, language** in the top content
- Read the comments: what are people asking for more detail about?

### ⭐ The no-review brand, which is the common case

**A brand with no reviews of its own is not blocked**, and this is worth stating plainly because it
is one of the most frequent reasons anyone commissions personas at all.

**The category has the language even when the brand does not.** Two sources carry it:

1. **Amazon on the category**, not the brand. Top 3 to 5 bestsellers in the same job-to-be-done,
   filtered 2-3 stars for pain and 4-5 for benefits. Their customers are your customers.
2. **Forums on the problem**, not the product. And for a new brand this is the *better* source
   anyway, not the consolation prize: your buyer is pre-purchase, which is exactly the state forum
   posts are written in.

⚠️ **What you lose is brand-specific objection language** — why someone hesitated over *this*
product at *this* price. The fastest fix is a two-question survey to whoever has bought: *"What made
you finally decide to buy?"* and *"What's your biggest frustration with products like this?"*. Sent
to a small list, it returns in days and the first question is a trigger-event question.

### The four questions to hold throughout

| | Question | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Who are they really?** | Psychographics over demographics. Values, communities, life stage |
| **2** | **What do they genuinely want?** | The deeper desire. *"They don't just want a vacuum cleaner; they want a clean home without back pain or wasted time"* |
| **3** | **What triggered them to search or buy?** | A move, a recurring frustration, a comparison with friends. *"These trigger moments are when people are most receptive"* |
| **4** | **How do they naturally speak?** | Technical or casual. *"The closer your ad language matches their natural speech, the more they'll trust you"* |

### How much is enough

⚠️ **ACA gives no volume target anywhere**, while asking you to police frequency claims. These are
this skill's, offered as working floors rather than as anything the course says:

| | Floor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbatims in total** | ~50 | Below this you cannot tell a pattern from a coincidence, which is the same standard the skill applies to competitors' ads |
| **Per source type** | ~15 | Enough that one unusual reviewer does not become a persona |
| **Sources used** | 2 minimum | One source is one sampling bias |
| **Quotes per persona** | 3 to 4 | ACA's own number, from the slide template |

**Stop when new material stops surprising you.** If the last twenty verbatims added no new
frustration, objection or phrase, the clusters are stable and more collecting is procrastination.

### Collecting properly

**Verbatim, always.** A summary of what customers say is your words again. The whole method depends
on keeping theirs.

**Record where each quote came from.** Source and date. A phrase from a 2019 review may describe a
product that has changed.

**Keep the ugly ones.** Bad grammar, run-on sentences and odd word choices are the texture that makes
a hook sound native. Cleaning them up is how mined language turns back into marketing English.

⚠️ **Watch for the sample you are actually drawing.** Reviewers are people motivated enough to write
a review, which skews to the delighted and the furious. Forum posters skew to the engaged and the
frustrated. Neither is the median buyer, and the quiet middle is invisible in both. **Say so when it
matters** — a persona built entirely from 1-star reviews is a persona of people who complain.

### ⛔ Regulated categories, and mining communities of people who are unwell

⚠️ **ACA says nothing about any of this**, and the method walks straight into it: health, supplements,
finance, legal and gambling are a large share of paid social, and this technique's instruction is to
go and quote the affected community directly.

**Two separate problems, and both bite before the personas are finished.**

#### 1. The most quotable line is often the one you cannot run

In a regulated category the verbatims with the most emotional force are frequently the ones making
the claims advertising rules forbid: recovery, cure, coming off medication, replacing professional
treatment, guaranteed outcomes, income results.

⛔ **Get the client's position on permissible claims BEFORE writing hooks, not after.** Otherwise you
produce a deck that is excellent and unusable, and the strongest hook in it is the one legal strikes
first.

**Mine the quotes anyway.** They are true things people said and they tell you what the audience
actually wants. The constraint is on what the *ad* may claim, not on what the research may record —
so keep them in the language collection and mark which ones cannot be said in market.

#### 2. You are handling other people's health and money disclosures

The instruction to record source and date is right for provenance and wrong if applied naively to a
forum post about someone's illness or debt: it puts a link to an identifiable person's disclosure
into a client deliverable.

| Do | Not |
|---|---|
| Quote the phrasing | Quote the whole post |
| Record the source *type* and date | Paste a link to an identifiable individual's medical or financial disclosure into a client deck |
| Strip usernames, locations, employers, and details that identify one person | Reproduce a story specific enough to find its author |
| Keep a private, sourced record if verification may be needed | Assume "it was public" settles it |

⛔ **Public is not the same as consented.** Someone describing their back pain to a support community
did not post it as marketing research, and a client deck is a different room.

| Part | Prompts | Tools ACA names |

| **2. Forum and social media analysis** | Reddit discussion analysis · Social media comment extraction · Competitor ad comment analysis | GigaBrain · Brandwatch |

⚠️ **The prompt text is in ACA's own PDF, not reproduced here.** This skill carries what each prompt
is for and how to check its output; the wording is ACA's asset and ships with the course. If someone
needs the exact prompts, point them at that PDF rather than reconstructing them, because a
reconstructed prompt is a different prompt.

⛔ **Every prompt takes material you paste in.** None of them find anything. That is what makes the
pack safe, and it is also the limit: **a prompt asked to describe an audience it has no data on will
produce fluent marketing English**, which is precisely the failure copy mining exists to prevent.

⚠️ **Mirella's caution, in her own words:** *"Mastering the manual process first is absolutely
essential... AI is your research partner, not a replacement for developing genuine customer empathy.
Use these tools to scale what you've learned, not to skip learning the fundamental skills."*

#### Checking what the prompts return

**1. Every claimed pattern should be traceable to quotes.** Ask for the supporting verbatims. A theme
with no quotes under it was inferred from the category.

**2. Watch for smoothed language.** If the output's "customer language" is grammatical and
well-balanced, it has been paraphrased. Go back to the raw source for the actual phrasing.

**3. Frequency claims need counting.** `Most reviewers mention...` is only true if something counted
them. Ask how many.

## The two artifacts

### The Audience Research and Persona Development Worksheet — six parts

#### Part 1: Copy Mining Expedition

Four research surfaces, each with a capture table and a **"key insights"** block underneath:

| Surface | What the table captures |
|---|---|
| **Customer reviews analysis** | 5-star reviews (what they love) · 3-star reviews (pain points and improvements) · recurring phrases · **emotional words used** |
| **Forum and social discussions** | Discussion topic · common questions · common objections · patterns |
| **Competitor ad comments** | Competitor · positive reactions · questions asked · objections · common language |
| **Search and question analysis** | Search term · common questions · related searches · seasonal trends · notable insights |

⭐ **Every one of the four tables has a column for the customers' actual words**, and that column is
the output. The rest is filing.

#### Part 2: Language Collection

Where the verbatims from part 1 are pooled and grouped, before any persona exists. **This is
deliberately separate from persona building**: the clusters should emerge from the language rather
than the language being sorted into personas you already decided on.

#### Part 3: Persona Development

One persona per cluster. Each carries:

1. **A memorable name** — ACA's examples are *"Fitness Enthusiast Ethan"*, *"Busy Mom Brenda"*
2. **Primary emotional drivers** — security, belonging, achievement
3. **Key pain points and objections**
4. **The specific language they use**
5. **Their trigger moments for purchasing**

#### Part 4: Trigger Events Identification

Its own part, because it is the row that changes *when* you spend rather than what you say.

#### Part 5: Creative Translation Exercise

Turning the mined phrases into hooks. ACA's worked example, for a meal delivery service and the
persona **"Overwhelmed Working Parent Olivia"**:

| The customer's words, from research | The hook |
|---|---|
| "The 5pm 'what's for dinner' panic is the worst part of my day." | **"End the 5pm dinner panic.** Healthy meals delivered with zero prep time." |
| "I feel so guilty ordering takeout again, but who has time to cook after a full day?" | **"Stop feeling guilty about takeout.** Start feeling proud of what you're feeding your family." |
| "I want my family to eat healthy, but meal planning is just one more thing on my endless to-do list." | **"What if meal planning never appeared on your to-do list again?"** |

⭐ **Read the transformation, because it is consistent and copyable.** Each hook keeps the customer's
own noun phrase and changes only the tense or the outcome:

- *the 5pm panic* → **end** the 5pm panic
- *guilty about takeout* → **stop** feeling guilty about takeout
- *my to-do list* → never appearing on your to-do list

**Nothing new is introduced.** No new metaphor, no invented benefit, no adjective the customer did
not use.

⛔ **The test that follows: a hook containing a phrase that appears nowhere in the research was
invented, not mined.** Either find the source phrase or drop the hook.

#### Part 6: Next Steps and Action Plan

### The Audience Persona Slide Template

One slide per persona. **Two columns, six rows**, and the second column is the point.

| Row | Primary Persona Details | Creative Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **PERSONA** | Name, age, brief description | **How this persona should be represented visually** |
| **MOTIVATORS** | 2 to 3 core emotional drivers | **How to leverage them in creative** |
| **PAIN POINTS** | 2 to 3 key frustrations | **How to acknowledge them in messaging** |
| **OBJECTIONS** | 1 to 2 primary hesitations | **How to address them preemptively** |
| **TRIGGER EVENTS** | 1 to 2 moments that prompt search | **How to time or reference them in creative** |
| **KEY LANGUAGE** | 3 to 4 direct quotes or phrases | **How to incorporate the authentic language** |

ACA's filled example runs down both sides:

> **PERSONA** — *"Sarah, 35, working mother of two who prioritizes convenience and quality"* →
> *"Relatable mid-30s woman in casual business attire, often shown in transition moments"*
>
> **MOTIVATORS** — *"Desire for efficiency, avoiding guilt, maintaining control"* → *"Emphasize
> time-saving benefits, show 'before/after' mental state"*
>
> **PAIN POINTS** — *"Never enough time, overwhelmed by options, worried about making wrong choices"*
> → *"Open with scenario showing time-pressure, validate the overwhelm"*
>
> **OBJECTIONS** — *"It's too expensive", "I've tried similar solutions that failed"* → *"Include
> clear price justification, show testimonial addressing previous failures"*
>
> **TRIGGER EVENTS** — *"Beginning of school year, after particularly stressful week"* → *"Create
> seasonal campaign targeting back-to-school period"*
>
> **KEY LANGUAGE** — *"I just need something that works without adding to my mental load"* → *"Use
> 'mental load' phrase in hook, mirror conversational tone"*

⭐ **Look at what the KEY LANGUAGE row does.** The instruction is not "sound conversational", it is
*use this specific phrase in the hook*. That is the level of specificity the right-hand column needs
to be worth writing.

⛔ **A persona with an empty Creative Translation column is a description, not a tool.** It is the
column that gets left blank and the only one a designer or copywriter can act on.

⚠️ **The header says the number of slots and they are small on purpose** — 2 to 3 motivators, 1 to 2
objections, 3 to 4 quotes. A persona listing eight pain points has not been through part 2's
clustering; it is the raw research with a name attached.

### Producing it

Deliver:

1. **A short note on what was mined** — which sources, roughly how many items, over what period. This
   is the evidence base and it belongs at the top
2. **The personas**, one slide-equivalent each, both columns filled
3. **The hooks**, each paired with the verbatim it came from
4. ⛔ **A gaps line** — which rows had no supporting quote and are therefore inference

⚠️ **Mark unsupported rows in the row itself, not only in the covering note.** Personas get pasted
into decks and briefs and read months later by people who never saw the research. Use `⚠️ INFERRED —
no source quote` inside the cell, because that is the only place the caveat survives the copy-paste.

**How many personas?** ACA does not give a number, and the clusters should decide it. But a set of
seven personas usually means the language was never really clustered, and one usually means the
research was too narrow to find a second.

ACA stamps no date. Received into this library in **2026-W31**, a "no later than" rather than a
publication date.

⛔ **The tool lists date fastest**, and there are four of them. **GigaBrain, Reviewbox, Bazaarvoice,
MonkeyLearn, Brandwatch, BuzzSumo, SEMrush, SparkToro, People Data Labs, Dovetail** and **Answer the
Public** are named as current. Availability, pricing and access all move, and several are small
enough to disappear. Check before recommending any of them.

⭐ **The method dates slowly.** Copy mining, the four questions, the persona structure and the
two-column translation do not depend on any tool. Where a named tool is gone, the source type
(reviews, forums, ad comments, search questions) still exists.

### Deliberate overlap with two other skills

Each must run with nothing else installed.

- **`aca-302-research-for-creative-strategy`** teaches all four research passes and explains why
  audience research sits second. It does not produce the artifacts.
- **This skill** runs pass 2 for a real brand and produces the personas.
- **ACA's Course 102 has its own consumer-psychology worksheet**, which is a different job: awareness
  stages, persuasion principles and message framing, working from theory toward a message. This one
  works from the audience's recorded words toward a persona. **Route on whether there is raw customer
  language to mine.**
