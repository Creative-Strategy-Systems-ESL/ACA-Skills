# Build audience personas from copy mining

**Ad Creative Academy**, Course 302 (Mirella Crespi), module 2.

> "Most marketers make the mistake of guessing what their audience cares about. But here's the
> secret — you don't have to guess."

> "We're not inventing messaging — we're just translating our audience's exact pain points into
> solution-focused hooks."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **The product or category**, and roughly what it costs
2. **Source material to mine**, or somewhere to mine it. Reviews, forum threads, ad comments,
   support tickets, survey responses, search questions. The more raw and unedited the better

**Optional, and each sharpens the personas:**

3. Competitors whose reviews and ad comments can also be mined
4. Existing customer data — who actually buys, and any segments already known
5. What has been tried in creative and what fell flat
6. The platform the creative is destined for

⛔ **NEVER WRITE A QUOTE YOU DID NOT FIND. In any mode, for any reason, under any deadline.**

This is the one rule with no exception. Every other prohibition in this skill is about *hooks*, and a
hook is checked against the research — so fabricating a customer quote and then deriving a hook from
it defeats every check, because the phrase now "appears in the research". **The quote is the thing
that must be real.** If a KEY LANGUAGE cell has no source, it stays empty and says why.

⛔ **The whole method is other people's words, so the personas are only as good as the raw material.**
If none is supplied, do not present personas as findings. Say what to collect and where, using the
source list in `reference/copy-mining.md`.

**What you can still deliver with nothing mined**, which is not the same thing:

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **A hypothesis scaffold** | The persona *shapes* you expect to find, so the client can sanity-check the direction and you know which clusters to look for. Every cell marked `⚠️ INFERRED` |
| ⛔ **Never** | The KEY LANGUAGE row. Mark it `⛔ NO SOURCE QUOTE — HYPOTHESIS` and leave it empty |
| ⛔ **Never** | Hooks. They derive from language that does not exist yet |
| ✅ **A collection plan** | Which sources, in what order, and what to send back |

**Say what it is in the document**: a scaffold to aim the research, not the deliverable. The empty
KEY LANGUAGE row is the honest signal of that, so do not fill it to make the artifact look finished.

**Deliver the scaffold and ask, rather than asking and waiting** — unless the person is plainly
mid-conversation with you and a question will get an answer in minutes.

## ⭐ The thing that makes these personas work

The slide template has **two columns, not one**:

| Primary Persona Details | Creative Translation |
|---|---|
| What is true about this person | **What to do about it in an ad** |

**Every attribute is paired with an instruction.** A motivator is not "desire for efficiency", it is
"desire for efficiency → emphasise time-saving benefits, show the before/after mental state".

⛔ **A persona with an empty right-hand column is a description, not a tool.** That column is the
entire reason the artifact exists, and it is the one people leave blank.

## How to run it

1. **Mine first, and collect verbatim.** `reference/copy-mining.md`. Exact phrases, not summaries.
2. **Cluster the language**, not the demographics. Patterns form around desires, frustrations and
   life situations.
3. **Build a persona per cluster**, with all six rows filled on both sides.
4. **Do the creative translation exercise** — turn the collected phrases into hooks.
5. **Check every persona against the test below.**

## The test each persona has to pass

> "When you're writing a script or designing creative, you should be able to ask, 'Would Busy Mom
> Brenda connect with this message?' and know the answer **instantly**."

⭐ **If the answer is not instant, the persona is too vague to be worth having.** That test is not
just about the creative; it is the quality check on the persona itself. A persona that cannot decide
anything is a paragraph with a name on it.

## Answering well

**Use their words, literally.** *"The most powerful copy comes directly from your customers' mouths,
not your marketing team's brainstorms."* If a persona's language section reads like marketing
English, the copy mining did not happen.

⭐ **Check every hook against the research.** In ACA's own worked example, each hook keeps the
customer's own noun phrase (`the 5pm panic`, `guilty about takeout`, `my to-do list`) and
changes only the tense or the outcome. **Nothing new is introduced. A hook containing a phrase that
appears nowhere in the research was invented, not mined** — rewrite it or drop it.

**Go past demographics.** *"Understanding your audience goes way beyond basic demographics like age
and location."* Age and location belong on the slide; they are not the finding.

**Trigger events are the most actionable row and the most skipped.** They are when someone is
receptive, which makes them the row that changes campaign timing rather than just messaging.

⚠️ **Mirella's own caution about doing this with AI, which is unusually firm:** *"Mastering the
manual process first is absolutely essential. I've seen too many marketers jump straight to AI
without understanding what they're actually looking for, and the results are... not great."* The
prompts summarise material you supply. **They do not find it, and they cannot invent it honestly.**
