# Course 501: Content Creation & Production

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 17m 28s, 1 module.

> "**Production quality does not always mean high production value or big budgets.** It means the
> right style for your audience and platform."

**This is the craft layer.** It sits between an approved concept and a delivered ad, and it exists
because the strategist has to describe a shoot they will not personally run.

> "As a creative strategist, **you are in charge of overseeing the production of your creative
> assets** and passing them on to the paid media team."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what a canted angle is, which aspect ratios to deliver,
or how to name files, and get an answer.

**To plan a shoot or a delivery, required:**

1. **The concepts or storyboards** to be produced
2. **Where the ads will run** — the platforms and placements decide the ratios, the safe zones and
   half the framing decisions

**Optional, and each one sharpens the plan:**

3. Who is shooting: a creator, an agency, a studio, or you
4. Budget and timeline
5. What already exists in the content bank
6. Brand constraints: fonts, colour, disclaimers
7. The tools the team already has

⛔ **Never invent a platform specification.** Aspect ratios, safe zones and placement lists change,
and the ones here are current-as-of-recording. Give them as ACA's starting point and say to check
the platform's current spec before delivery.

## The through-line

> "The most important part of effective collaboration between creative strategy and creative
> production is **a shared common language.**"

⭐ **That is what the whole course is for.** Every list in it exists so a strategist can say exactly
what they want to a creator, an agency or a studio, and be understood. A brief that says "make it
feel premium" and a brief that says "extreme close-up, low angle, track left" cost the same to write.

## How to run it

**Vocabulary first.** Most production waste in this course's telling comes from a brief the shooter had to
guess at.

1. **Give the shot vocabulary** from **The shared language of production** (below) — thirteen native shot types across four
   families, nine cinematography types, three camera movements. ⭐ This is the course's real gift: a
   strategist who can name the shot gets the shot.
2. **Plan the shoot** with **Planning, managing and delivering** (below) — batching, modular shooting, and what makes a
   day efficient rather than long.
3. **Decide the platform adaptations and delivery specs**, same file, including aspect ratios and safe zones.
   ⛔ Decide these before the shoot, not in the edit.
4. **Set up asset management and tagging** so the footage can be found again. ⚠️ This is the step teams skip
   and pay for six months later.
5. **Cover thumbnails**, which the course treats as part of production rather than an afterthought.
6. ⭐ **For the pre-launch QA pass, route to `aca-run-creative-qa`** — it holds ACA's Creative QA Checklist
   and needs the finished asset. ⚠️ Confirm it is installed before naming it.
7. **Check the brief names shot types, not moods.** "Make it feel premium" is the brief this course exists to
   replace.

## Answering well

**Polish is not the goal.** *"The best performing ads aren't always the most polished. They're the
ones that speak the native language of the platform and connect authentically with your audience."*

**Shoot the bank, not the ad.** ⭐ *"Having these shots in your content bank allows you to create
endless variations of successful ads."* And: *"When shooting content, **B-roll is your best friend.**
Always try to capture as much B-roll as possible."*

**Plan for production before you plan the concept.** *"Great concepts mean nothing if they're not
production-friendly."* Batching, modular components and platform adaptations are decisions made
before a camera is booked, not after.

**Tag as you go, or lose it.** *"How you organize and tag your assets is absolutely critical"* — and
tagging talent name and date is how usage rights stay traceable.

## ⚠️ Scope

Producing and delivering the assets. It does not generate concepts (Course 401), build the
storyboard (Course 403), cast or direct the creator (Course 502), or read the results (Course 402).
It stops at a delivered, QA'd file handed to the paid media team.

⛔ **The pre-ship quality check has its own skill.** ACA ships an eight-page Creative QA Checklist
for this course, and `aca-run-creative-qa` runs it against a real finished asset.

## The shared language of production

> "The most important part of effective collaboration between creative strategy and creative
> production is **a shared common language.** Remember those storyboard templates we covered? The
> sections for shot type and visual direction are where this shared language comes into play."

> "After producing **tens of thousands of paid social ads**, I've identified essential shot types
> that every native producer or content creator must master. **Think of this as your visual
> vocabulary.**"

---

### The visual vocabulary: four families

⚠️ **ACA's two recordings of this course list a different number of shots.** The written script gives
twelve; the delivered recording adds **Before & After** as a thirteenth. All thirteen are carried
here, and the discrepancy is flagged rather than resolved. See **Provenance** (below).

#### Talking head shots

| Shot | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Selfie** | The creator filming themselves directly. ⭐ *"Ideally with some natural movements and fun angles, so it really feels authentic"* |
| **Showcase** | Talking while demonstrating the product |
| **Greenscreen** | Using platform background effects for context or emphasis. Shot on the effect, then layered onto a background |

#### Product shots

| Shot | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Product in Hand** | Showing scale and real-world context. ⭐ *"Amazing for videos and static ads"* both |
| **Product Demo** | Demonstrating how it works |
| **Unboxing** | Capturing the opening experience. ⭐ *"Always try to shoot these with sound on for that extra ASMR effect"* |
| **Macro Texture** | Close-ups of materials, textures and important details |

#### Action shots

| Shot | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Lifestyle** | The product or service being used in daily life |
| **Mirror Selfie** | ⭐ Showing before/after or full view. *"Amazing to show the before and after effect in full view"* |
| **Creator Demo** | Walking through the usage steps of the product, service or app |
| **Before & After** | ⚠️ *"actions that highlights the problem or the problem being solved."* **In the delivered recording only** |

#### POV shots

| Shot | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Digital Interaction** | Point-of-view shots of browsing websites, apps, or playing a game |
| **Receiving Product** | *"Those first impression moments of picking up the package from the door and opening it from the POV angle"* |

#### ⭐ Why the list is a bank, not a menu

> "Having these shots in your content bank allows you to **create endless variations of successful
> ads.** Master these, and you'll always have the building blocks you need."

> "When shooting content, **B-roll is your best friend.** Always try to capture as much B-roll as
> possible. Having a rich content bank you can pick from when creating ad variations gives you so
> much creative freedom and flexibility."

⭐ **The economic argument sits underneath this.** A shoot planned around one ad produces one ad; the
same shoot planned around the thirteen shot types produces a bank that feeds a quarter of testing.
The marginal cost of the extra shots, while the creator and the product are already in the room, is
close to zero.

### The filmmaker's toolkit

> "Even though we're often creating low-fi, native content that doesn't look like traditional ads,
> **we still need to think like expert filmmakers.**"

> "For a Creative Strategist to effectively communicate their vision, you need to understand basic
> cinematography techniques and language."

#### Shot types

| Shot type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Close-up** | Tight framing on specific details |
| **Wide Shot** | Capturing the full environment |
| **Extreme Close-up** | Dramatic detail focus |
| **Low Angle** | Filming from below — worm's eye view |
| **High Angle** | Filming from above — bird's eye view |
| **Over the Shoulder** | Viewing from behind a subject |
| **Canted Angle** | Tilted frame, for tension or energy |
| **Point of View (POV)** | Showing what the subject sees |
| **American Shot** | Framing from mid-thigh up |
| **Level Shot** | ⚠️ Looking at the subject directly. **In the delivered recording only** |

#### Camera movements

| Movement | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Pan** | Moving side to side |
| **Tilt** | Moving up and down |
| **Track** | Moving alongside or around a subject |

> "Using this shared language ensures everyone on your team understands exactly what you're
> envisioning."

⭐ **Three movements is the whole list, and that is the point.** The vocabulary is deliberately small
enough to be learned in an afternoon by someone who is not a filmmaker, which is what makes it get
used in briefs rather than admired in a document.

⚠️ **ACA ships a guide of these definitions with visual examples and it is not in this folder.** The
recording says *"we created a super handy guide, available in your downloads below. It has all of the
definitions with visual examples of each shot."* The definitions above are complete as taught; the
visual examples are not held.

## Planning, managing and delivering

> "**Great concepts mean nothing if they're not production-friendly.**"

---

### The four planning moves

#### 1. Batching similar concepts

Group what can be shot together:

- All UGC concepts with **the same creator**
- All product-focused videos that need **the same setup**
- All testimonial videos that need **the same location**

> "This approach **dramatically reduces setup time and logistics costs.**"

#### 2. ⭐ The modular production approach

> "Think of your creative elements as **modular components**."

- Shoot **multiple hooks** that can be mixed with different bodies
- Film **various product demonstrations** that can be used across concepts
- Capture **a range of CTAs** that can be attached to different videos

> "This modular approach lets you **produce many more variations without proportionally increasing
> production time or budget.** It's how you scale creative testing efficiently."

⭐ **This is the highest-leverage idea in the course.** Hooks, bodies and CTAs shot as separate
components multiply: five hooks against three bodies is fifteen ads from one shoot day. Shot as three
finished films, it is three.

⚠️ **Modularity has to be designed into the shoot, not discovered in the edit.** Components only
recombine if they were framed, lit and paced to. A hook filmed in a different room from the body
cannot be cut against it.

#### 3. Platform-specific adaptations

> "Plan for how each concept will be adapted across platforms: **Meta feed vs. stories vs. reels.
> TikTok vs. YouTube Shorts.** Each requires slight modifications in framing, pacing and text
> treatment."

> "Building these adaptations into your production plan **prevents scrambling later.**"

#### 4. Asset management system

> "Create a clear system for organizing all these assets."

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Naming conventions** | That reflect the concept and the variation |
| **Folder structure** | That makes it easy to find specific elements |
| **Tracking document** | That maps which assets have been used in which ads |

> "Production-friendly planning is how you turn great creative concepts into reality **without
> blowing your budget or timeline.**"

---

### Digital asset management

> "In order to streamline your creative production, post-production, and most importantly,
> **iteration**, how you organize and tag your assets is absolutely critical."

**What to tag:**

| Tag | Why |
|---|---|
| **Shot type** | Talking head, product shot, and the rest of the vocabulary |
| ⭐ **Talent name and date** | *"To track usage rights"* |
| **Everything, into one searchable database** | Raw assets, working files and final ads together |

⭐ **Tagging talent name and date is the one with consequences.** Usage rights expire, and an asset
library that cannot answer "who is in this and when did we license them" is a library you cannot
safely reuse from. It is a legal record disguised as a filing convention.

**Tools she names:** *"**Air** is the simplest version of this, but there are many Digital Asset
Management tools in the market."* And **Uplifted**, *"a particularly great tool to organize and tag
your assets. They have AI tagging capabilities that automatically identify your content type and make
your entire asset library searchable."*

⚠️ **The Uplifted recommendation comes with a coupon in ACA's resources**, which is a commercial
relationship the course does not name as one. The capability is real; treat the endorsement as an
endorsement.

### Post-production tools

⚠️ **Her own framing of the tooling question:** simplified tools like **CapCut** *"are more than
enough to create ads for social."* But for cross-team collaboration and faster iteration, *"using
more pro tools like **Adobe Premiere** is the best approach."*

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Frame.io** | Share creatives for review · capture feedback **directly on specific frames** · syncs with Adobe Creative Cloud |
| **Moises.io** | Extract background music from existing assets. *"Also available in CapCut Pro"* |
| **Adobe Podcast (Enhance Speech)** | One-click audio clean-up, removing background noise and static. *"Also available in CapCut Pro"* |
| **Mister Horse** | After Effects and Premiere plug-in for transitions and animation presets |
| **CapCut** | Text-to-speech, background removal, AV sync |
| **Premiere Pro plugins** | **Fire Cut** — silence cutting, captions, B-roll integration · **Social Media Text Tools** — presets for native styles and colours |

⭐ **Frame.io is the one that changes how a team works rather than what it can make.** Feedback
attached to a frame instead of a paragraph is the difference between one revision round and three.

### Platform-optimised delivery

> "When editing your videos, you want to make sure they're optimized for **every placement.**"

**Deliver every ad in multiple aspect ratios:**

| Platform | Ratios |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | **9:16 · 4:5 · 1:1** |
| **TikTok** | **9:16** |
| **YouTube** | **9:16 · 16:9** |

#### ⛔ Safe zones

> "Following safe zones is extremely important because **every platform and placement has UI
> elements that could cover your ad.** Every key title and visual should always be within the safe
> space."

**ACA's own margins**, from the Safe Spaces & Ad Specs reference:

| Ratio | Keep text, logos and CTAs |
|---|---|
| **1:1 · 4:5** | ⭐ Out of the **top and bottom 14%** of the frame |
| **9:16** | ⭐ Inside the **central 80% vertically and 90% horizontally** |

**Why the margin exists at all**, in their words: *"Every platform (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, etc.) adds
interface overlays (like buttons, captions, or progress bars) on top of your ad."* A safety zone is
*"the central area of your video or image where all critical content will always remain visible, no
matter the placement."*

**Their four instructions:**

1. *"Use platform templates that already account for safety zones."*
2. *"Preview ads across placements before launch (feed, Stories/Reels, Tiktok, etc.)."*
3. *"Keep subtitles, logos, and CTAs inside safe areas."*
4. *"Always export multiple aspect ratios (1:1, 4:5, 9:16) so the ad looks good everywhere."*

⛔ **The three crop-safe templates ACA links here — Meta, TikTok, and a combined Meta × TikTok — are
not in this folder.** The margins above are the whole specification we hold. Build your own guides
from them, or ask ACA for the templates.

⚠️ **Platform-native fonts are a separate ACA reference and are also not held**, so a font question
has no answer here.

⛔ **These ratios and placements are current-as-of-recording.** Platform specs change. Give them as
the starting point and verify before delivery.

### Thumbnail design

> "This is something that often gets overlooked in the production process, but it's **critically
> important.** While it shouldn't be the first thing you focus on, you definitely shouldn't ignore
> it either."

**Why it matters:** *"In Meta, **not all placements are autoplay.** In many cases, users will see
your thumbnail before they decide whether to watch your video. That initial frame needs to work as a
standalone **'advertisement for your advertisement.'**"*

| Element | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Visual impact** | A high-contrast, attention-grabbing image that communicates your value instantly |
| **Clear subject** | Make sure your product or key visual is immediately identifiable |
| **Text overlay** | If using text, keep it short, large enough to read, and **placed in safe zones** |

> "Remember to **intentionally design that first frame** for maximum impact. Don't just let it be
> whatever happens to be at the beginning of your video."

⭐ **"Don't just let it be whatever happens to be at the beginning" is the whole instruction.** A
thumbnail is a decision or it is an accident, and the default is an accident.

### Quality assurance

> "Before any asset goes live, it needs to pass a comprehensive quality assurance process. **This
> isn't just about catching errors. It's about ensuring every asset reaches its full potential.**"

**Her four categories:**

| Category | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Technical specifications** | Time length for the platform · correct aspect ratios per placement · safe space compliance · proper rendering and export quality |
| **Brand consistency** | Brand guidelines where they apply · font usage, branded or platform-native · consistent colour and visual identity |
| **Content quality** | Spelling and grammar · sound quality and audio levelling · hook effectiveness, all five elements present · sufficient variation between hook versions · a clear CTA aligned to the conversion objective |
| **Engagement optimisation** | Sound pattern interrupts and "ear stoppers" · strategic sound effects at key moments · pacing without dead spots · visual hierarchy · message congruency throughout |

⭐ **Two rules she attaches, and they pull in opposite directions on purpose:**

> "I recommend getting **at least two sets of eyes on every asset** before it goes live."

> "The QA process should be **thorough but not endless.** Establish clear guidelines and timeframes
> to prevent perfectionism from delaying your testing and learning cycles."

⛔ **ACA ships an eight-page Creative QA Checklist for this**, and it is held. When there is a real
finished asset to check rather than a process to explain, `aca-run-creative-qa` runs it.
