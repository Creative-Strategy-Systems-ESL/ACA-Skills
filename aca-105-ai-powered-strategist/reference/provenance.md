# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — Course 105: The AI-Powered Creative Strategist (100-Level). 27 minutes 49
seconds, 1 module, 2 case studies.

An interview, not a lecture. **Reza Khadjavi**, CEO of **Motion**, interviewed by **Evan Lee**,
Motion's head of partnerships. The recording is timecoded and speaker-labelled.

ACA's curriculum lists three learning objectives for it: understand AI's role in creative strategy,
recognise the human advantage, and leverage AI to amplify creative output.

⚠️ **ACA's curriculum also lists "2 case studies" for this course. The recording contains none.**
No branded example, no before-and-after, no named company walked through. Reza reasons from
categories and personal observation throughout. Flagged because a student expecting two worked
cases will not find them, and because the count comes from ACA's own spreadsheet rather than from
the material.

## ⚠️ Vendor context, stated because a listener should know it

**Motion sells creative analytics software.** Reza is its CEO and Evan works for him, so this is an
interview conducted by an employee with his own chief executive, hosted inside a course.

Two things worth saying about that, both true:

- **He does not pitch Motion in this session.** No product mentions, no feature claims, no call to
  action. The advice is given generically and stands without the product.
- **His argument favours instrumenting and measuring creative work**, which is the category Motion
  sells into. That does not make it wrong, but it is a position he has commercial reasons to hold.

**What can be checked from inside this skill:** he makes no product claim, names no tool, and the
advice would be unchanged if Motion did not exist. The argument's load-bearing parts (the 90/10
research split, process-before-automation, the two career skills) do not route to buying anything.

**What cannot be checked from here, offered as a pointer rather than as proof:** the same origin
story, with the same two causes and the same conclusion, is told independently by Mirella Crespi in
ACA's Course 102. If you have that course, comparing the two is the strongest available test of
whether this account is vendor-shaped. If you do not, treat this as a claim to verify rather than
as settled corroboration.

Mention the vendor context where it is relevant. Do not suppress it, and do not treat it as
disqualifying.

## What is quoted and what is organised

Everything substantive is Reza's, quoted directly. **Quotes are verbatim**, and a conversational interview transcribed verbatim reads like one: filler, restarts and the transcript's own slips are all present, because a tidied quotation is no longer evidence of what was said. Where a claim is his personal forecast rather than an observation, it is flagged as such.

The four-way split into role, foundations, AI discipline and career moves is this skill's
organisation. The interview moves through these in roughly that order but conversationally, with
topics recurring, so the reference files gather each thread rather than following the recording
linearly.

The routing table, the practical tests derived from his advice, and the note that the risk lands on
progression before employment are this skill's additions, drawn from what he says rather than
stated by him.

## ⚠️ One transcription artefact

Mirella Crespi's name is rendered as "Marella" in the recording's transcript. It refers to Mirella,
ACA's founder.

## What this course is not

There are no frameworks, worksheets or prompt packs. It is a conversation about the role, and it
names no specific AI tools deliberately, since Reza's argument is that tools change while the
fundamentals do not. Anyone needing a method for actually making, analysing or briefing creative
needs a different course.
