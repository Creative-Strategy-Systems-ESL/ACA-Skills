# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — the **Creative QA Checklist**, the downloadable artifact of Course 501:
Content Creation & Production. **Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake.

**An eight-page PDF**, seven of content. Its structure is reproduced exactly: two halves (Technical
Review and Performance Optimization), nine sections between them, a separate image-ad page, and the
two-column **Good to Go / Needs attention** marking on every row.

**The course recording backs it**, and names the checklist as the resource for this module.

## ⛔ The checklist carries numbers the course narration does not

This matters because the recording is where most of Course 501's material comes from, and it names
**no thresholds at all**. The checklist does:

| Figure | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| **TikTok 7-40s · Meta 15-45s · YouTube 20-45s** | ⛔ Checklist only |
| *"Only VSL ads can be longer than 1 minute"* | ⛔ Checklist only |
| ⭐ **The heartbeat method — something changes every 1.5 seconds** | ⛔ Checklist only. The phrase appears nowhere in either recording |
| **No longer than 2-3 seconds on the same visual** | ⛔ Checklist only |
| The aspect ratios per platform | Both the checklist and the recording |

⚠️ **So attribute them to the checklist, not to the course.** Someone who watched Course 501 and
someone who downloaded its worksheet did not receive the same guidance, and only the second one has
runtime bands.

⛔ **All of them are ACA's, given without a source or a vertical**, and the document is undated.
Platform specs change. Score against them and say the current platform documentation is the final
word.

## ⭐ What the document says it is for

> "Use this checklist to ensure quality and performance optimization for all creative assets before
> launching them in your ad accounts. **Have at least two team members review each asset** but keep
> the QA process efficient and focused."

> "This tool serves two purposes: **catching technical errors and optimizing for performance.** The
> performance sections are particularly valuable for **training team members to develop their
> creative intuition.**"

⚠️ **The two-reviewer instruction is in the document itself**, so an automated pass does not satisfy
it. This skill says so in its output rather than implying the check is complete.

## What is reproduced and what is added

**Every row is ACA's, in ACA's wording and ACA's order**, across all nine sections and the image-ad
page. The two-column marking is theirs.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** rendering the sections as tables, the emitted CSV shape,
and the summary counts.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the document rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The `Cannot assess` and `N/A` marks.** ACA gives two columns, Good to Go and Needs attention,
  and **no way to record that a row could not be judged.** With only two options, an unverifiable row
  gets ticked, and a filled checklist then certifies an asset nobody actually checked. This is the
  most important addition here.
- ⛔ **Naming which sections collapse without their inputs**: Creative Integrity needs the storyboard,
  Brand Compliance needs the guidelines, Hook Variation needs the rest of the batch, and the CTA
  alignment row needs the conversion objective. The document assumes an internal reviewer who has all
  four to hand.
- ⛔ **The rule that a failed Technical row means `hold` regardless of the performance half.** ACA
  orders the two halves and never says one gates the other.
- ⭐ **The observation that the six Hook Effectiveness rows are the five elements of a hook plus the
  pattern interrupt**, so failing three of them is an opener problem rather than six separate notes.
- ⭐ **The point that the heartbeat row is the most checkable in the document**, because a change
  every 1.5 seconds is countable from a timeline rather than judged.
- ⭐ **Reading the Final Gut Check as the part that cannot be gamed by a spec sheet**, and noting that
  ACA wrote *"(Be honest)"* into the document, which says what usually happens to it.
- ⛔ **Flagging the Branded / Native / Expert row as the only one naming a failure mode** rather than
  a quality, and the one with real consequences for a static.
- ⚠️ **The note that the captions row is two tests in one**, accuracy and placement.
- ⚠️ **Marking format-conditional rows `N/A` with a reason** rather than passing them by default.
- ⚠️ **The instruction not to soften a Needs attention into a suggestion**, since the document exists
  to stop assets going live.
- **The reminder to look at statics on a phone**, which is ACA's own instruction and is easy to read
  past.

## ⚠️ Two small things in ACA's own document

- The Creative Integrity section carries a **typo**, *"IIf the ad is a street interview"*. Reproduced
  as the row it plainly is.
- **Thumbnail Assessment appears twice** under different framings — once in the video half and once
  on the image-ad page, where it is scoped to non-autoplay placements. Both are kept, because the
  questions differ.

## ⚠️ How old this material is

ACA stamps no date on the checklist. Received into this library in **2026-W31**, a "no later than".

⭐ **The durable parts:** the section structure, the craft questions, the gut check and the image-ad
pass. None depends on a platform.

⛔ **What dates fastest:** every runtime band, the aspect ratios, the safe-zone assumption, and the
implicit claim that these are the placements worth delivering for.

## Scope

A craft check on a finished asset. Not a performance prediction, not a strategy review, and not a
legal or compliance sign-off — it flags missing disclaimers and copyright risk as craft items and
does not replace whoever approves claims.
