## ⛔ ACA's two creator briefs contradict each other

**Measured, not inferred.** A creator sent both would not know which to follow:

| | Content Creator Brief Template | External Creator Script Template |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture format** | *"Set your camera settings to record at **HD 1080 30 fps**"* | *"Film all content in **4K** (change your camera settings to 4K instead of HDR)"* |
| **Volume asked for** | *"At least **25-30 clips**"* | *"**20+ RAW B-Roll Shots**, **10x Scripted Testimonials**"* |
| **Head and tail** | Not specified | *"Add an extra **1-3 seconds** to the beginning and end of all raw clips"* |
| **Commercial terms** | None | Deadline, *"$500 USD after content submission"*, a signature line, and usage: ads only, Facebook and Instagram, up to 12 months |

⛔ **1080p against 4K is the one that cannot be fixed after the shoot.** Neither is wrong: 4K gives
reframing and punch-in headroom, which matters when one deliverable is cut to several aspect ratios;
1080p is smaller, faster to upload from a phone, and less likely to fill a creator's storage.
**Sending both is what is wrong.** This skill emits the Content Creator Brief's spec by default and
flags it for confirmation.

⚠️ **They agree on everything else that matters**: vertical framing, good light, no music, no
filters, no graphics, a quiet room, and watching for fans and AC.

⭐ **The two are not versions of one document.** They share only **21.4%** of their content despite
near-identical original filenames, and the shorter one is **not on ACA's shipping asset list** at
all. Anyone matching on filename will conflate them.

⚠️ **The `1-3 seconds` head-and-tail instruction is worth borrowing** even when emitting the other
template. It costs the creator nothing and it is what lets an editor cut cleanly.

## ⚠️ The $500 is a placeholder, not a benchmark

The course requires the rate as a casting-call field and **names no benchmark anywhere**. The only
figure in any of this material is the External Creator Script Template's `$500 USD after content
submission`, which is one template's example for one deliverable package.

⛔ **So this skill never supplies a rate.** A rate it invented would be an offer someone sends to a
real person.

## ⚠️ Claims that are Chloe's and carry no source

| Claim | As given |
|---|---|
| Brief quality | *"**80% of great UGC is in the brief**"* |
| Scripting and platform enforcement | Handing a creator a full script *"increases your risk of content rejection on Meta or TikTok. Platforms know when something is scripted, and often flag that content as low-quality or **'inauthentic behavior'**"* |

⛔ **The platform-enforcement claim is the one to be careful with.** Neither Meta nor TikTok
documents penalising scripted creator content as such. **The craft argument needs no platform to
back it**: scripted delivery reads as performed and performs worse. That is what this skill says
when it declines to write a word-for-word script.

## What is quoted and what is organised

**ACA's template is reproduced in its own structure and wording** — the greeting, all eleven
checklist items, both footage requests, all twelve shooting tips, both prompt steps with their
sub-prompts, and the annexes. **The casting call's six fields are Chloe's**, as are the sourcing
routes, the four-beat structure, the five brief parts and the creator's end-of-brief checklist.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the two-mode split, the casting call's emitted layout,
rendering the checklist and shooting tips as tables, and the pre-delivery checks.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The `[TO CONFIRM]` convention and the four fields it guards** — rate, upload link, usage term,
  legal lines. These are the fields that turn a draft into an offer sent to a real person, and ACA
  never addresses what to do when they are unknown.
- ⛔ **The instruction to cut section 1** when the creator was not cast off an existing video. ACA's
  template assumes they were, and leaving it in requests footage that does not exist.
- ⭐ **The reading that the eleven-item checklist mirrors the two prompt steps**, which is what makes
  it usable as a checklist rather than a second list to maintain.
- ⭐ **The note that Step 1 question 2 is load-bearing**, because it produces the "what else I tried
  that failed" beat that makes the product's claim credible.
- ⭐ **The observation that Step 2 prompt 6 is written honestly** — it asks for results *or* hopes, so
  a creator with no results has a truthful answer. Rewriting it to assume results is where a brief
  starts manufacturing testimonials.
- ⚠️ **The warning to rewrite the niche sub-prompts** rather than pasting the skincare ones, which is
  the most likely way this template gets used badly.
- ⚠️ **The point that the sample request is unpaid work**, so the call should say whether
  unsuccessful applicants hear back.
- ⚠️ **The distinction between the creator's end-of-brief checklist and your own clip check.** ACA
  gives both and never says they are different documents used at different moments.
- **The rule that every trait in "who we're looking for" must trace to the brief**, since a trait
  that is not in the brief filters on taste rather than on the campaign.
- **Keeping ACA's warm opening**, because a self-directed creator's first contact sets the register
  of the performance.
