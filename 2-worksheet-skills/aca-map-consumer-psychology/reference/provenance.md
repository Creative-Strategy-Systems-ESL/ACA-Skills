# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — Course 102: State of Paid Social Advertising & Consumer Psychology
(100-Level), taught by **Mirella Crespi**, ACA's founder, of **Creative Milkshake**.

Two artifacts from that course are what this skill runs:

- **The Creative Strategist's Consumer Psychology Worksheet** — 8 sections, reproduced in
  `reference/worksheet.md` with every field intact.
- **AI Prompts for Creative Strategists: Consumer Psychology Edition** — 20 prompts, all reproduced
  verbatim in `reference/ai-prompts.md`.

The lookup tables in `reference/frameworks.md` come from the course itself rather than from the
worksheet, which prints the checkboxes but not the explanations behind them.

## Whose frameworks these are

The course is explicit about this, and so should anyone using the worksheet be. None of it is
ACA's own invention, and none of it is this skill's:

| Framework | Origin, as the course gives it |
|---|---|
| **The 5 awareness stages** and **market sophistication** | Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, 1960s |
| **The hierarchy of needs** | Abraham Maslow, "A Theory of Human Motivation", 1943 |
| **The Life-Force 8** | Dr Drew Eric Whitman, *Cashvertising* |
| **The first seven persuasion principles** | Dr Robert Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*, 1984; **Unity** added in *Pre-Suasion*, 2016 |
| The remaining seven principles, the framing matrix, the hook menus, the 4-exposure sequence, the evaluation checklist, the winning formulas | Assembled by ACA for this course |

Mirella's framing of why sixty-year-old material still applies is worth keeping: *"advertising
mediums change, but consumer psychology doesn't."*

## ⚠️ A discrepancy inside ACA's own materials

**The course teaches 14 persuasion principles. The worksheet's Section 3 grid prints 10.** Missing
from the grid: **MAYA**, **distinctiveness**, **self-centeredness**, and **primacy and recency**.

`reference/frameworks.md` carries all fourteen and marks which four the grid omits. This is a note
about the source, not a correction to it: someone filling the printed PDF will see ten and never
know the other four were taught.

## What is reproduced and what is this skill's

**Reproduced as written:** every worksheet field and section heading, the checkbox lists, the
evaluation checklist's ten questions, the four winning formulas, all 20 AI prompts including their
`[BRACKETED]` placeholders, and the closing line about connection rather than manipulation.

**Both worked examples are ACA's** — the supplement brand and the productivity app. Their Sections
1 to 3 are exactly as the course gives them. **Sections 4, 5 and 7 of the supplement example are
this skill's extension**, carrying ACA's example through to the end of the sheet because the course
stops at Section 3 and the later sections are the harder ones to demonstrate. They are marked
illustrative in `worksheet.md` and should stay marked: no real product is behind them, and the
mechanism claim in particular would need to be true of an actual formulation.

**This skill's, drawn from the course rather than stated by it:**

- The regrouping of the 20 prompts by worksheet section. ACA groups them by activity; their own
  ordering guidance is reproduced at the end of `ai-prompts.md` so the change is visible.
- The three-state input rule, and specifically the middle state: a research source **named but not
  supplied**. That distinction is not in the course. It is here because "we have 400 reviews" reads
  like evidence arrived when nothing did, and the worksheet produced from it is indistinguishable
  from a grounded one.
- The `GROUNDED IN:` / `⚠️ ASSUMPTION, NOT VERIFIED:` provenance markers, and the rule that they
  live inside each section rather than in the surrounding conversation.
- The line between what may be reasoned towards (how a category generally behaves) and what must be
  supplied (what **this** brand's customers actually say).
- The tie-breakers, the ✅/⛔ examples, the funnel positions on the awareness table, and the notes on
  which principle pairings compound or pull against each other.

## Deliberate overlap with `aca-102-state-of-paid-social`

Both skills carry this worksheet and this prompt pack, and that duplication is on purpose. Each
skill has to run with nothing else installed, so neither can point at the other for its content.

The split is by job, not by material:

- **`aca-102-state-of-paid-social`** teaches the psychology. Someone who wants to understand
  awareness stages, or why market sophistication matters, wants that one.
- **This skill** produces the artifact for a specific brand and does not teach. Someone with a
  product, an audience and a pile of reviews wants this one.

If both are installed, either will do the other's job badly. Route on whether there is a real brand
on the table.
