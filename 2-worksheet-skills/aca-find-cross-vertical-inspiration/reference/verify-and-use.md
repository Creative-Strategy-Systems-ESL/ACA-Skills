# Verifying the output, and turning it into something real

ACA's Part 2, *"How to Use the Results"*, is the second half of the asset and the half that gets
skipped. **It is also, read properly, a verification loop** — which is exactly what the output of
this prompt needs.

⭐ **Deliver these steps alongside the list, every time.** A list of verticals and brands with no
research plan attached will be read as findings, and half of it is not.

---

## Step 1 — Create a research plan

> "Prioritize 3-5 verticals that seem most interesting or unexpecte"

**Not all 7 to 10.** The prompt over-generates deliberately; the first act of judgement is cutting
it down.

**Prioritise on two things:**

1. **Can you name the shared audience?** If you cannot say in one sentence why that vertical's
   buyers overlap with yours, drop it however interesting it sounds.
2. **How far is it from what you already do?** The value is in distance. A vertical whose creative
   resembles yours teaches you nothing even if the audience overlap is perfect.

## Step 2 — Conduct platform-specific research

> "Check Meta Ad Library for the suggested brands · Visit their Instagram and TikTok accounts · Look
> at their YouTube channel for longer-form content · Visit their website to understand their overall
> positioning."

⭐ **This is the verification step, and it is the moment the hypothesis becomes evidence.**

**Start with the Meta Ad Library**, because it answers the question that matters first: *is this
brand actually running ads, and do they look anything like what was described?*

Three outcomes, and all three are useful:

| What you find | What it means |
|---|---|
| The brand runs ads matching the description | ⭐ Confirmed. Study it properly |
| The brand runs ads, but nothing like the description | The vertical may still be right; the *characterisation* was wrong. Look at what they actually do |
| The brand does not exist, or runs nothing | ⛔ Discard, and note it. If several in one vertical fail this way, distrust that whole row |

⚠️ **Record what failed verification, not just what passed.** A research plan that reports only its
hits looks like a perfect hit rate, and the next person to use this prompt will trust it more than
they should.

## Step 3 — Build a creative swipe file

> "Save 3-5 examples from each brand that use approaches you find compelling · Analyze what specific
> elements make them effective · Note how they trigger emotional responses that would resonate with
> your audience."

**Save the artefact, not the impression.** A screenshot or a link, with a note of what it does — not
"good hook."

⚠️ **The third instruction is the one that makes this cross-vertical rather than a mood board:** note
how the trigger would land *on your audience*, not on theirs. An ad that works on climbers because
they are climbers teaches you nothing. An ad that works on climbers because it treats gear as
identity might transfer to anyone whose purchase is identity-shaped.

## Step 4 — Integrate insights

> "Look for patterns across different verticals · Identify approaches that feel fresh for your
> category · Consider how to adapt these techniques while maintaining authenticity to your brand."

⭐ **Patterns across verticals are worth more than any single example.** One brand doing something
is a tactic. Four unrelated industries doing the same thing to the same audience is a mechanism, and
mechanisms transfer.

⚠️ *"While maintaining authenticity to your brand"* is doing real work. The technique transfers; the
voice does not. A category that is playful because its product is low-stakes will hand you a tone
that is wrong for a considered purchase, and copying it produces an ad that reads as a costume.

## Step 5 — Develop testing concepts

> "Create 2-3 test concepts inspired by cross-vertical insights · **Maintain your core value
> proposition while experimenting with new creative approaches.**"

**This is where the exercise lands.** Not a report — two or three things to make.

⭐ **The constraint is the important half.** Hold the value proposition constant and change only the
creative approach. Otherwise a test tells you nothing: if both the message and the execution moved,
a win cannot be attributed to either.

---

# What a good delivered output looks like

Structure the deliverable as five parts, in this order:

1. **The verticals, ranked, with the shared audience named for each.** State that this is the most
   reliable part of the analysis.
2. **The brands to check, explicitly labelled as unverified.** Include what to look for in each and
   the trigger you expect — as a lens for looking, not a finding.
3. **The verification plan** — which library, which handles, in what order.
4. **The techniques to adapt**, flagged as provisional until step 2 confirms the examples they rest
   on.
5. **The two or three test concepts**, with the value proposition held constant and stated.

⚠️ **Put the verification status in the document, not in the conversation around it.** A section
header line — `UNVERIFIED: brands below are research targets, not findings` — travels with the list
when someone pastes it into a deck. A caveat in chat does not.
