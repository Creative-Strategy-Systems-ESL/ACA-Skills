# Course 502: How to Cast and Direct UGC Talent Effectively

**Chloe Rhys**, Ex-TubeScience, now at OpenStore. 32m 52s, 6 modules.

> "No matter how good your brief is, no matter how strong your strategy, **if the person on camera
> doesn't feel believable or engaging, the ad won't land.**"

**This is the tactical layer.** Sourcing, casting, briefing, directing and collecting the right
footage. It picks up exactly where the strategic creator course stops.

⭐ **This skill emits the casting review grid and the deliverables checklist** in the structures
Chloe uses, rather than describing what a grid or a checklist would contain.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what UGC means here, where to find creators, or why a
brief should not be a script, and get an answer.

**To cast, brief or run a review, required:**

1. **The product**, and what problem it solves
2. **What the content is for** — testing angles at volume, or a hero asset. That choice decides
   whether UGC is even the right format

**Optional, and each one is a field in the artifacts:**

3. The persona: who should appear, and the tone or vibe
4. Budget and rate, and the deadline
5. The deliverables you need back, and whether you want edited video or raw clips
6. Brand constraints: words to avoid, claims needing disclaimers, competitor mentions
7. Reference content that nailed the vibe
8. Past performance by creator, if you have run UGC before

⛔ **Never invent a rate.** The course says to state one and be specific, and it gives no benchmark
anywhere. The only figure in the whole course's material is a single template's example. A rate you
made up becomes an offer someone sends.

⛔ **Never write casting notes about a real person that you would not want read back to them.**
Chloe's own grid scores demographic fit and her example rationales describe hair colour, accent and
body type. That is ordinary advertising casting, and a scored spreadsheet about named applicants is
also a document that can be read by other people later. Keep every note tied to the role and the
content, and see `reference/casting.md` before writing one.

## The through-line

> "**80% of great UGC is in the brief.**"

> "A vague brief is a wasted budget. If you don't tell creators what's important to include, what to
> avoid, or how to structure the video, they'll guess. And their guess might not be what you needed."

⭐ **So most UGC failures are diagnosed backwards.** When footage comes back unusable, the instinct
is to blame the creator. Chloe's position is that the brief is the quality control system, and the
seven clip types exist so the brief can be checked against them before anyone films.

## How to run it

**Sourcing, casting, briefing, directing, delivering — in that order.** Each step's output is the next step's
input, and skipping one shows up as unusable footage.

1. **Decide UGC or elevated content** using `reference/when-and-where.md`. They need different people and
   different money.
2. **Source candidates** from the same file, with the trade-offs of each platform stated rather than implied.
3. ⭐ **Cast against the five criteria** in `reference/casting.md`, and emit the review grid. ⚠️ Course 202
   gives six criteria and each list has one the other lacks — say so if both are in play.
4. **Write the brief with prompts, never a script**, using `reference/briefing-and-directing.md`. ⛔ This is
   the load-bearing rule: a script produces a performance, prompts produce usable material.
5. ⭐ **Emit the deliverables checklist** from `reference/deliverables.md` — the seven clip types that make
   footage editable, with the prompt to give the creator for each. ⛔ **This goes out with the brief, not
   after the shoot.** It is the instrument that decides what gets captured, and without it you get one long
   take and no ad.
6. **Cover the five parts of a self-directed shoot brief**, same file as step 4, if the creator films alone.
7. **Direct on the day** with the four common on-set problems and their fixes, same file.
8. ⚠️ **For ACA's Content Creator Brief filled for a real brand, route to `aca-brief-ugc-creator`** if it is
   installed.
9. **Check the brief contains no lines to perform.** Any dialogue written for them is step 4 undone.

## Answering well

**UGC is a format choice, not a default.** *"UGC is not one-size-fits-all. And it's not always the
answer to every brief."* It is the testing engine: fast, iterable, built for volume. Elevated
content is where brand equity gets built. ⭐ *"Most of the time, the real answer is: you need both."*

**Cast for charisma over looks, and it is not close.** *"9/10 times the more relatable talent with
strong energy outperforms traditionally attractive talent with no charisma."*

**Prompts, never a script.** *"Very very rarely is it a good idea to hand a UGC creator a full script
and say, 'Say these exact words.'"* It kills authenticity, and Chloe also claims it raises platform
rejection risk. ⚠️ That second claim is hers and unsourced; see `reference/provenance.md`.

**Direct like a coach.** *"You're not a critic. You're a coach."* Give emotional intentions, not line
readings. And when it is not working, *"don't push. Pivot."*

**Collect building blocks, not takes.** *"Your goal isn't just to get a good take. Your goal is to
collect the right building blocks — modular, usable footage that can be remixed, trimmed, tested and
scaled. Think of it like creative legos."*

⭐ **Sometimes you are the talent.** *"In performance marketing, creative strategists, copywriters,
even director-level or VPs will step in front of the camera. This isn't just a cost-saving move.
It's fast. It's real. And often it works."*

## ⚠️ Scope, and the two skills either side of this one

This course is the tactics. It does not decide **whether** creator content is the right strategy,
what job it should do, or how to measure it — that is `aca-202-creator-advertising`, which sets the
casting criteria and the strategy questions before anyone is sourced.

It also does not produce a **storyboarded** shoot document. A creator filming alone from prompts and
a crew shooting a boarded concept are different jobs; the boarded one is
`aca-403-storyboarding`.

⛔ **When the actual artifact is wanted rather than the method**, `aca-brief-ugc-creator` produces
the casting call and ACA's own Content Creator Brief.
