## ⛔ ACA's two creator briefs contradict each other

**Measured, not inferred.** A creator sent both would not know which to follow:

| | Content Creator Brief Template | External Creator Script Template |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture format** | *"Set your camera settings to record at **HD 1080 30 fps**"* | *"Film all content in **4K** (change your camera settings to 4K instead of HDR)"* |
| **Volume asked for** | *"At least **25-30 clips**"* | *"**20+ RAW B-Roll Shots**, **10x Scripted Testimonials**"* |
| **Head and tail** | Not specified | *"Add an extra **1-3 seconds** to the beginning and end of all raw clips"* |

⛔ **1080p and 4K is a real conflict, not a phrasing difference**, and it is the one that cannot be
fixed after the shoot. **Pick one and state it once.** 4K gives reframing and punch-in headroom,
which matters when one deliverable is cut into several aspect ratios; 1080p is smaller, faster to
upload from a phone and less likely to fail on a creator's storage. Neither is wrong. Sending both
is.

⚠️ **They agree on everything that matters most**: vertical framing, good light, no music, no
filters, no graphics, quiet room, watch for AC and fans.

## ⛔ Where this course meets the other creator courses

⭐ **This is the folder's cleanest hand-off, and it now completes.** ACA's Course 202 points at this
course three times for the tactics of sourcing, briefing, casting and managing creators, and
explicitly declines to give the brief template, sourcing tactics or rate benchmarks. Course 403 also
points here for casting and directing. Until this skill existed, both named a course with nothing
behind it.

**Checked rather than assumed: the two casting frameworks do not conflict, and each has one
criterion the other lacks.**

| Ashley Vinson's six criteria (Course 202) | Chloe's five grid criteria (this course) |
|---|---|
| Can they create interesting content? | *(Chloe gates on real UGC samples before the grid)* |
| Can they communicate clearly? | On-camera energy · Vocal style |
| Are they credible for this category? | Believability |
| Do they fit the tone and the job you need? | *(scored against the brief's persona)* |
| Do they bring something the brand cannot easily make itself? | Standout factor |
| ⭐ **Are they brand appropriate?** | ⛔ **absent** |
| ⛔ **absent** | ⭐ **Demographic fit** |

⚠️ **The two gaps run in opposite directions and both matter.** Ashley's *brand appropriate* is a
risk check on the person's public history — her worked example is only hiring people over 25 for US
alcohol brands, and screening out creators whose content shows irresponsible use. **Nothing in
Chloe's grid catches that.** Chloe's *demographic fit* is a role-match criterion that Ashley's six
never mention. **Running both is better than running either**, and neither course says so because
neither knows what the other contains.

⚠️ **They also score different objects.** Ashley's brief scores the *campaign's requirements* before
anyone applies; Chloe's grid scores *named applicants* after they do. Filling one does not fill the
other.

## ⚠️ Claims that are Chloe's and carry no source

| Claim | As given |
|---|---|
| Casting | *"**9/10 times** the more relatable talent with strong energy outperforms traditionally attractive talent with no charisma"* |
| Scripting and platform enforcement | *"Platforms know when something is scripted, and often flag that content as low-quality or **'inauthentic behavior'**"* |
| Brief quality | *"**80% of great UGC is in the brief**"* |
| Deliverables | Following the seven clip types lets you *"build **3–5 high-performing ads** from one deliverable"* |

⛔ **The platform-enforcement claim is the one to be careful with.** Neither Meta nor TikTok
documents penalising scripted creator content as such, and repeating it as fact turns a
craft argument into a compliance argument. The craft argument — scripted delivery reads as
performed and performs worse — needs no platform to back it and is what should be said.

⚠️ **The 9/10 and 80% are rhetorical figures**, not measurements, and read as such in the recording.
Quote them as her position rather than as findings.

⛔ **The course names no rate benchmark anywhere.** It requires the rate as a casting-call field and
never says what one looks like. The only figure in this course's material is the $500 example inside
the unlisted External Creator Script Template, which is one template's placeholder for one
deliverable package.

## What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Chloe's, quoted directly** — the spectrum, the four sourcing routes, the
casting-call fields, the five grid criteria, the five brief sections, the four-beat UGC structure,
the directing method and all seven clip types.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the four-file split, the routing table, rendering the
sourcing routes and the grid as tables, and turning the seven clip types into a checklist with a
`Received?` column.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The warning about what a casting grid is as a document.** Chloe's grid scores demographic fit
  on age, skin tone and hair type, and her worked rationales describe hair colour, accent and body
  type. That is ordinary advertising casting; it is also a written, scored, durable record about
  named real people, and the course never raises it. The guidance added is practical, not a
  prohibition: write what the content needs rather than a rating of the person, keep every criterion
  traceable to the brief, and assume the sheet outlives the shoot.
- ⛔ **The cross-course criteria comparison above**, and the observation that each framework is
  missing something the other has.
- ⭐ **Reading "not right for this shoot" as different from "not good"**, which is what makes the
  reuse column worth keeping.
- ⭐ **The note that beat 2 of the four-beat structure** — what else you tried that failed — is the
  one people drop and the one doing the persuasive work.
- ⭐ **The point that the still product hold is what makes an ad survive an offer change**, so one
  deliverable can carry a new price next month without a reshoot.
- ⚠️ **The note that hook variations are one line delivered five ways**, not five different hooks,
  because conflating them loses the clean comparison.
- ⚠️ **Judging creators on the metrics creative owns.** She says to track who drives high CTR, low
  CPA and strong hook rates; CPA is contaminated by the landing page, price and offer, so a creator
  marked down on it may have been let down by something they never touched.
- **The observation that every good brief example gives a relationship or a situation**, which a
  creator can act, rather than a quality like "be authentic", which they cannot.
- **The note that her four on-set fixes end correctly**: when talent does not believe in the
  product, the fix changes what you ask them to say rather than coaching them into sounding
  convinced.
- ⛔ **Opening every file before marking a clip received**, and writing why in a row rather than
  deleting it when a clip type does not apply.
- **The reading that "authentic" in this course means "reads as native"**, not "unpaid" or "a real
  customer", which the definition makes plain and which is easy to overstate to a client.

## Scope

Sourcing, casting, briefing, directing and collecting footage. It does not decide whether creator
content is the right strategy or how to measure it, and it does not produce a storyboarded shoot
document.
