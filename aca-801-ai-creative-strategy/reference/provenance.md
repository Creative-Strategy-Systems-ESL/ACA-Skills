## ⭐ Where this course fills a gap nothing else does

⛔ **It is the only place in the entire programme that addresses AI consent, disclosure and synthetic
likeness.** Three rules: read the platform's labelling guidelines, never copy a person's appearance or voice
without permission, and document creator permission against current legal frameworks.

⛔ **Courses 802 and 803 teach exactly the techniques those rules govern** — training a character model on a
person's face, cloning a brand's visual signature, generating UGC-style content with synthetic people — **and
neither mentions consent, disclosure or likeness once.** Course 802 carries a narrower disclaimer about not
cloning other brands' imagery, and nothing about people.

⭐ **So this course's ethics section should travel with the 800-Level as a whole**, not stay filed under
orientation. It is the missing half of the other two courses.

## ⛔ Where it collides with Course 105

**Both courses answer "how should a strategist adopt AI?" and they answer differently.**

| Course 105, Reza Khadjavi | Course 801, Jesse Ketonen |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Process first, then automate** | ⭐ *"**It should be your top priority to learn this**"* |
| Cautions against generating volume without a process underneath | Warns the *"productivity gap ... will be huge"* for those who *"skip the class on this"* |

⭐ **They are reconcilable and the reader should be told how:** 105 is about *sequence* (do not automate a
process you do not have), 801 is about *urgency* (do not wait). **Neither contradicts the other's actual
advice.** ⚠️ **But the tone is opposite**, and someone reading only one will get a one-sided picture.

⛔ **Neither course cites the other**, and Course 105's faculty member is the CEO of a tool named in four other
courses.

## ⚠️ How old this material is

**ACA stamps no recording date.** Received into this library in **2026-W31**, a "no later than".

⚠️ **Internal evidence suggests it is the oldest 800-Level course.** It treats AI agents as an emerging
frontier — *"We are still in very early days, where most of the use cases for agents are yet to be
discovered"* — while Courses 802 and 803 build working agentic pipelines. ⭐ **Read in curriculum order it
sounds like a preview of the two courses that follow it.**

⛔ **What dates fastest:** every tool named, the *"AI is mostly a creative assistant"* framing, the whole of
module 3, and the claim that agents are early.

⭐ **What is durable:** the five areas, the agent definition and its four verbs, the four-step workflow, the
role change, the three assignments, and ⭐ **the consent and disclosure rules**, which will outlast every tool
in the course.
