**Equipment is not the blocker and saying so is the useful part.**

## Pillar 1: Resources

### Gear, in three tiers

> "You can have the fanciest gear and make the best looking videos, but it all comes down to good
> script writing, good ideas."

| Tier | What it is | Luke's note |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Start here** | A smartphone · a ring light or softbox · a ~$50 wireless lav mic | *"all those years that I got up in the last three years, that's all shot from a smartphone."* |
| **2. Nice to have** | A tripod · a clean filming location · a teleprompter app · an upgraded camera · more reliable lighting | *"Not needed, but it is nice in some respects"* |
| **3. Scale** | A mirrorless camera · multiple lights · a dedicated studio | *"Great for scale, but it's not where you start"* |

> "I've seen brands spend ten K on a studio and post twice a month. And on the other hand, some of
> them and brands film in a closet with a ring light post daily and get millions of views.
> **Equipment is a tool, not a strategy.**"

**The challenge he sets:** set up your space against a gear checklist and film a 15-second test
clip. *"You need to get used to recording. You need to get used to being on camera."*

⭐ **Tier 1 is a genuine floor, not false modesty.** Anyone using budget as the reason they have not
started is describing a decision, not a constraint.

### The five roles

> "You need five rolls filled.... Someone can wear multiple hats, but **every hat needs to be
> worn.**"

| Role | What they own |
|---|---|
| **1. Strategist** | Decides what to post and why. Tracks trends, understands audience pain points |
| **2. Writer** | Turns concepts into scripts, on hook / body / close |
| **3. Talent** | Whoever is on camera. *"Doesn't need to be perfect. Just needs to be real"* |
| **4. Editor** | ⭐ *"Almost half the battle... you can take the best footage and make it terrible, and you can take the worst footage and make it amazing."* Pacing, captions, sound design |
| **5. Publisher** | Posting, platform, formatting, commenting, and **feeding insights back to the strategist** |

⭐ **The Publisher role closes a loop most teams leave open.** Posting is treated as the end of the
pipeline; here it is the input to the next cycle, because the person in the comments knows what the
audience actually said.

**On the strategist**, and it is the story he tells about his own turning point:

> "at clickup when I switch from just general posting to targeting project managers pain points. We
> went from okay views to a 10 million view video. **That's what the strategist does.**"

**On the editor:** *"If budget is tight, you can outsource first."* Of the five, this is the one he
says to buy before the others.

> "If you're solo, you're all five. Start posting. The concept. The team comes after the proof."

**The challenge:** map your situation to the five roles, **circle the bottleneck, and that is your
first hire.**

## Pillar 2: Experimentation — "the spaghetti season"

> "This is the module that separates brands that break through to brands that stay safe."

### What you are up against

> "how far do people scroll every day? It's a football field. 300ft of content. **Your content has
> half a second to make somebody stop.**"

| Video length | Completion rate |
|---|---|
| Under 15 seconds | **76%** |
| Over 30 seconds | **42%** |

> "The drop off is savage. People are literally scrolling on autopilot. Your content literally needs
> to physically jolt their brains awake."

He reaches for the **purple cow**: you do not remember a normal cow.

### Three kinds of pattern interrupt

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | An unexpected first frame. Expression, unusual location, sudden movement. Subverting what you expect to see. ⭐ *"half a viewers watch with the sound off, so it has to work silently."* |
| **Audio** | A record scratch, an unexpected voice, a song |
| **Verbal** | An opening line that is relatable or creates tension |

**The worked example**, a video that took a client to 70,000 followers overnight:

- A **relatable topic** — getting sick at the office
- **Timing** — posted in winter, when everyone was getting sick
- **First frame** — a girl coughing, him reacting
- **Understandable with the sound off**

> "That is a visual pattern interrupt plus perfect timing."

### Why bold beats safe

> "Safe content, polished brand videos, predictable tips, they all flatline. Bold content spikes.
> Sometimes it fails. When it hits, it explodes."

He cites **Professor Jonah Berger**, who studied 7,000 articles and found **high-arousal emotions
(excitement, anger) are significantly more viral than low-arousal ones.** Bold content triggers high
arousal.

⚠️ **"Bold" is not "risky to the brand".** Nothing here argues for controversy for its own sake, and
anger as a high-arousal emotion is an observation about virality rather than a recommendation. A
brand with claims to protect should read this as *distinctive*, not *provocative*.

## Pillar 3: Consistency

### The cadence ladder

> "How often should you post? Now I would say as much as you can as long as the video quality
> doesn't suffer."

| Level | Cadence |
|---|---|
| **Starting out** | 3 posts a week |
| **Intermediate** | 1 a day |
| **All in** | 1 to 2 a day, plus stories |
| **High professional** | 2 a day, plus 3 to 5 stories a day |

**The arithmetic he uses to argue it:** Brand A posts one great video a week, Brand B posts five good
ones. After 90 days that is **12 posts against 60** — *"five times more data, five times more shots
on goal."*

> "Gen Z toggles between apps every 44 seconds. The brands that show up in more micro sessions win.
> **Volume is not just experimentation, it's mental real estate.**"

⛔ **The 90-day rule is the part that matters:**

> "whatever you pick, try for 90 days. The compound effect takes time. **Most people quit before it
> even kicks in.** And guess what? The algorithm doesn't even look at your page until you have at
> least 100 posts usually."

⚠️ **So pick the cadence you can hold on a bad week.** A ladder rung chosen optimistically fails in
week three, and quitting at week three is the exact failure the 90-day rule exists to prevent.

### Four accountability systems — pick at least one

> "Willpower is not enough for systems."

| System | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A coach** | Weekly check-ins, number reviews, called out when you miss. *"The difference between clients with check-ins and without is staggering"* |
| **A content partner** | Someone at your stage who shares numbers weekly, say every Monday. *"It's free and it's very powerful"* |
| **Public commitment** | Post about your 90-day challenge. ⭐ *"Content about your content journey sometimes becomes the best performing content, and it can become one of your pillars"* |
| **Team cadence** | Weekly reviews, a shared calendar, daily five-minute standups |

**The challenge:** pick one and activate it today. Tell at least one person about the 90-day
commitment.

### The 60/30/10 mix

The content calendar's allocation:

| Share | Type | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| **60%** | **Planned** | Proven formats from your three content pillars, planned 1 to 2 weeks ahead |
| **30%** | **Experimental** | The spaghetti-season allocation. New hooks, new formats |
| **10%** | **Reactive** | Trends, memes, brainstorm moments. *"Leave room for lighting"* ⚠️ |

**The calendar itself:** a spreadsheet, categorised by **date, platform, type, concept and status**.
Filled in each Monday.

> "The calendar is a living document, not carved in stone. You should be planning these in 1 to 2 weeks."

⭐ **The 30% is the load-bearing number.** Without it a channel calcifies on what worked six months
ago, and with more than it the brand never builds a proven format at all.

### The weekly content review — 30 minutes, three steps

| Step | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Pull the numbers** | ~10 min | Rank posts best to worst by view rate, likes, comments, shares. **What is different about the top versus the bottom?** |
| **2. Zoom out** | ~10 min | Look at the last four weeks together. What formats consistently work? What hooks always fail? |
| **3. Apply it** | ~10 min | If it is hot, do more of it. Some people grade each video **A, B, C or D** and file it under the category it belongs to |

> "Review. Refine. Repeat. This is what turns amateur content creators into professional machines.
> **30 minutes every week. Do not skip it.**"

**The challenge:** build the calendar with the 60/30/10 rule, do the first review now, and **make it
a recurring calendar event.**

⭐ **Step 2 is the one that gets dropped, and it is where the learning is.** A single week's numbers
are noise; four weeks together is where a format proves itself.

## Hooks, bodies and stories

### Why scriptwriting is the pillar under all three pillars

> "the script writing lesson that changes everything. **Most important lesson in the course.**
> Here's why. Neuroscience. When the brain hears facts, two regions activate. When he hears a story,
> **the entire brain lights up** and it releases three chemicals **dopamine** for focus and memory.
> **Oxytocin** for empathy and trust in **endorphins** for emotional connection."

> "A well scripted story literally hacks brain chemistry. Humans are literally wired to listen to a
> story. **This is why script writing is the number one skill in content.**"

---

### The five hook types

| Type | Luke's example |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | *"This trick got us 50 million views"* |
| **Controversy** | *"Stop using Canva. Here's why"* |
| **Relatability** | *"Every marketer has done this"* — he reports 1,000 to 10 million views in one day |
| **Result** | The outcome stated up front |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Something visually or audibly unexpected |

### The body

> "Deliver on the hooks. Promise. **Every sentence delivers value or builds tension. No filler**
> structure as a list or story. Use micro hooks throughout."

**Micro-hooks** are the small re-openings mid-video that stop the drop-off: *"But here's the
thing."* · *"Watch what happens next."*

⭐ **If hooks land and people still leave, the body is the problem, not the hook.** A rising hook rate
with a falling completion rate is the specific signature of a body that does not pay off its
opening.

### The close: one action

> "**One action.** Follow. Comment. Like or save. **Pick one and be specific.** Like comment
> blueprint and I'll save you the template."

He names **ManyChat** for automating that reply.

⚠️ **Two calls to action is zero.** The specificity is the mechanism: a named keyword gives the
viewer a script and gives you a trigger to automate against.

> "The framework is the skeleton. **Your personality is the flesh.**"

---

### Advanced hook engineering

> "Research shows that prolonged short form video consumption actually **reduces prefrontal cortex
> responses.** Your audience's brains are literally less capable of keeping attention for as long as
> they used to. Literally within five years ago. So your hook needs to be that much sharper."

#### Three mechanics

| Mechanic | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Novelty** | Break the pattern. *"Our brains are wired to notice the unexpected"* |
| **Relevance** | ⭐ **Name your audience out loud.** *"Marketing managers, lean in"* · *"If you struggle with trauma, you're going to want to listen to this"* |
| **Open loops** | *"The brain has a very hard time leaving a story unfinished."* Example: *"This mistake cost us 50"* |

#### Five advanced formulas

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| **Contrast** | *"500 views to 50 million with one small change"* |
| **Challenge** | *"What's the brand name of the last food that you ate? What is the brand name of the last drink that you had? If you can't name it, they might have a problem with marketing"* |
| **Time pressure** | *"AI is going to take your job, so you better figure out how to make money now"* |
| **Secret** | *"This is the thing that nobody in content wants to talk about"* |
| **Story** | *"We filmed a video that almost got us fired"* |

#### Stacking

> "If you're really advanced, you can stack all of these triggers. Here's a hook that generated us 50
> million views, and I have never shared this publicly."

He reads that one as **relevance + contrast + secrecy**. *"Triple threat."*

#### ⛔ The rule that governs all of it

> "**Never promise what the body doesn't deliver. Clickbait with a weak payoff kills trust.** And
> honestly, it makes people angry. If there's a pin in a balloon, I'm going to need to see that
> balloon pop, or else I'm going to go nuts."

**The challenge he sets:** write ten hooks, two of each type, film yourself delivering all ten, and
mark which felt strongest.

⭐ **Filming them is the part that gets skipped and the part that teaches.** A hook that reads well
and does not survive being said out loud is a hook you would otherwise have shot.

### The three story frameworks

> "Think of it as a mini movie."

#### 1. Setup, conflict, resolution

Luke's own, about ClickUp:

> "**setup.** They told me to make Clickup go viral. **The problem** was productivity software was
> not sexy for TikTok. And **the solution** is we stop trying to be sexy. We started trying to be
> funny and calling it the pain"

#### 2. Before and after

> "Show the pain, then the transformation. **The bigger the contrast, the more powerful.** Before:
> same safe content, 500 views. After: three formats consistently over a million."

#### 3. Lesson learned

> "Start with a vulnerable admission. Walk through what changed? Like I thought you needed A5K
> camera. And that belief cost me six months."

#### What all three share

> "All three of these run on **tension** gap between what is and what could be. **If there is no
> tension, people screw up.**"

⭐ **That is the test to apply to any script**, and it is more useful than the framework names. Read
it and ask where the gap is. If you cannot point at one, no amount of editing will save it.

### On vulnerability

> "it's okay to be vulnerable on social media. In fact, it's one of the most powerful things that
> you can do."

He cites **neuroscientist Paul Zak**, who found that vulnerable stories release **oxytocin**, *"the
trust chemical."*

**And the eBay experiment:** researchers listed **$129 of ordinary trinkets** on eBay, wrote a
personal story for each one, and sold them for **over $8,000**.

> "Same objects, different stories. And that is the power of a narrative."

⚠️ **Vulnerability is a strategy with a floor.** It only works if the admission is real; a
manufactured one is the same trust failure as clickbait, arriving by a different route.

**The challenge:** pick one framework, write a complete story script, and film it this week.
