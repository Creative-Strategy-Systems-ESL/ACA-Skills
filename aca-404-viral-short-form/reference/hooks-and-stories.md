# Hooks, bodies and stories

## Why scriptwriting is the pillar under all three pillars

> "the script writing lesson that changes everything. **Most important lesson in the course.**
> Here's why. Neuroscience. When the brain hears facts, two regions activate. When he hears a story,
> **the entire brain lights up** and it releases three chemicals **dopamine** for focus and memory.
> **Oxytocin** for empathy and trust in **endorphins** for emotional connection."

> "A well scripted story literally hacks brain chemistry. Humans are literally wired to listen to a
> story. **This is why script writing is the number one skill in content.**"

---

## The five hook types

| Type | Luke's example |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | *"This trick got us 50 million views"* |
| **Controversy** | *"Stop using Canva. Here's why"* |
| **Relatability** | *"Every marketer has done this"* — he reports 1,000 to 10 million views in one day |
| **Result** | The outcome stated up front |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Something visually or audibly unexpected |

## The body

> "Deliver on the hooks. Promise. **Every sentence delivers value or builds tension. No filler**
> structure as a list or story. Use micro hooks throughout."

**Micro-hooks** are the small re-openings mid-video that stop the drop-off: *"But here's the
thing."* · *"Watch what happens next."*

⭐ **If hooks land and people still leave, the body is the problem, not the hook.** A rising hook rate
with a falling completion rate is the specific signature of a body that does not pay off its
opening.

## The close: one action

> "**One action.** Follow. Comment. Like or save. **Pick one and be specific.** Like comment
> blueprint and I'll save you the template."

He names **ManyChat** for automating that reply.

⚠️ **Two calls to action is zero.** The specificity is the mechanism: a named keyword gives the
viewer a script and gives you a trigger to automate against.

> "The framework is the skeleton. **Your personality is the flesh.**"

## Advanced hook engineering

> "Research shows that prolonged short form video consumption actually **reduces prefrontal cortex
> responses.** Your audience's brains are literally less capable of keeping attention for as long as
> they used to. Literally within five years ago. So your hook needs to be that much sharper."

### Three mechanics

| Mechanic | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Novelty** | Break the pattern. *"Our brains are wired to notice the unexpected"* |
| **Relevance** | ⭐ **Name your audience out loud.** *"Marketing managers, lean in"* · *"If you struggle with trauma, you're going to want to listen to this"* |
| **Open loops** | *"The brain has a very hard time leaving a story unfinished."* Example: *"This mistake cost us 50"* |

### Five advanced formulas

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| **Contrast** | *"500 views to 50 million with one small change"* |
| **Challenge** | *"What's the brand name of the last food that you ate? What is the brand name of the last drink that you had? If you can't name it, they might have a problem with marketing"* |
| **Time pressure** | *"AI is going to take your job, so you better figure out how to make money now"* |
| **Secret** | *"This is the thing that nobody in content wants to talk about"* |
| **Story** | *"We filmed a video that almost got us fired"* |

### Stacking

> "If you're really advanced, you can stack all of these triggers. Here's a hook that generated us 50
> million views, and I have never shared this publicly."

He reads that one as **relevance + contrast + secrecy**. *"Triple threat."*

### ⛔ The rule that governs all of it

> "**Never promise what the body doesn't deliver. Clickbait with a weak payoff kills trust.** And
> honestly, it makes people angry. If there's a pin in a balloon, I'm going to need to see that
> balloon pop, or else I'm going to go nuts."

**The challenge he sets:** write ten hooks, two of each type, film yourself delivering all ten, and
mark which felt strongest.

⭐ **Filming them is the part that gets skipped and the part that teaches.** A hook that reads well
and does not survive being said out loud is a hook you would otherwise have shot.

## The three story frameworks

> "Think of it as a mini movie."

### 1. Setup, conflict, resolution

Luke's own, about ClickUp:

> "**setup.** They told me to make Clickup go viral. **The problem** was productivity software was
> not sexy for TikTok. And **the solution** is we stop trying to be sexy. We started trying to be
> funny and calling it the pain"

### 2. Before and after

> "Show the pain, then the transformation. **The bigger the contrast, the more powerful.** Before:
> same safe content, 500 views. After: three formats consistently over a million."

### 3. Lesson learned

> "Start with a vulnerable admission. Walk through what changed? Like I thought you needed A5K
> camera. And that belief cost me six months."

### What all three share

> "All three of these run on **tension** gap between what is and what could be. **If there is no
> tension, people screw up.**"

⭐ **That is the test to apply to any script**, and it is more useful than the framework names. Read
it and ask where the gap is. If you cannot point at one, no amount of editing will save it.

## On vulnerability

> "it's okay to be vulnerable on social media. In fact, it's one of the most powerful things that
> you can do."

He cites **neuroscientist Paul Zak**, who found that vulnerable stories release **oxytocin**, *"the
trust chemical."*

**And the eBay experiment:** researchers listed **$129 of ordinary trinkets** on eBay, wrote a
personal story for each one, and sold them for **over $8,000**.

> "Same objects, different stories. And that is the power of a narrative."

⚠️ **Vulnerability is a strategy with a floor.** It only works if the admission is real; a
manufactured one is the same trust failure as clickbait, arriving by a different route.
