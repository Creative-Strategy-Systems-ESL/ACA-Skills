# Building a creative engine for your whole team

Module 3 (6m 06s). The module about the organisation rather than the work.

## The problem

> "When was the last time you had your best creative idea? I bet it wasn't in a brainstorm. And it
> wasn't in a meeting. And it wasn't sitting at your desk staring at a brief. It. It was in the
> shower or on the train, or scrolling your phone at 10 p.m. when your brain was half off."

> "Most creative teams funnel every single ad concept through one person, the creative strategist,
> the creative director, or whoever holds the title. Everyone else just waits for the brief. That's a
> massive waste of creative potential."

## The insight underneath it

The origin story, from the weekly all-hands call at Zach's DTC brand where everyone had to bring ads
they had seen that week — *"not just the marketing team, everyone: our operations manager, our
customer service person, our freelance designer."*

> "And here's what I discovered. The more we tested the better a performance was. That sounds
> obvious, but the insight underneath it isn't. It wasn't just that we tested more ads, it's that
> **we had more diverse inputs because everyone's algorithm is completely different.**"

> "Your social feed shows you different ads than mine, and your accounts feed shows them different
> ads and your creative directors, your ops managers. TikTok for you page is in a completely
> different universe than what your performance marketer sees. **That's not noise, that's signal.**"

⭐ **This is the strongest idea in the course and it is not really about ads.** Personalised feeds
mean every employee is running a free, continuously updating sample of a different slice of the
market. A company with 30 people has 30 uncorrelated research feeds and typically uses one. The
asymmetry is that the marketing team's feed is the *least* representative of the customer, because
the algorithm has learned they are a marketer.

**The bottleneck was never generation. It was capture:**

> "The problem was capturing it. We had screenshots and slack and links and email threads and
> bookmarks that nobody could find. Half the ads were already taken down by the time someone went
> back to look at them."

⚠️ **"Half the ads were already taken down" is the constraint that makes this urgent.** Ad creative
is ephemeral. A reference you did not save is usually unrecoverable, which is why a capture habit
beats a research sprint.

## Strategy 1: Get everyone contributing

> "Get your entire team looking for ways you can communicate the product or service that you sell.
> And I don't just mean your creative team. I mean everyone who sees ads, which is everyone."

What each function is uniquely positioned to see:

| Function | What their feed and their job surfaces |
|---|---|
| **Operations** | Competitors' shipping and unboxing experiences |
| **Customer service** | ⭐ *"The exact language customers use to describe your product"* |
| **Finance** | *"a completely different algorithm showing them completely different apps."* |
| **Sales** | B2B and pitch-style creative *"that you would never encounter"* |

⭐ **Customer service is the highest-value entry in this table and the least like the others.** The
rest contribute ads. Customer service contributes **the customer's own words**, which is the raw
material for hooks rather than a reference for them. Anyone building this should route that channel
separately.

**What makes it work is friction.** The whole strategy fails on capture, not on willingness:

> "The For Play mobile app plus Instagram integration makes it dead simple. Anyone on your team can
> save an ad they see on Instagram or TikTok, or anywhere else with one tap, It goes straight to
> your shared swipe file, ready to head into production."

> "Honestly, I say 80 to 90% of my best ad ideas went on a walk or on the toilet."

> "That's how you **multiply creative surface area** without adding a single person to your team."

⛔ **The tool is Zach's own product.** See `reference/provenance.md`. The mechanism that matters is
**one-tap capture from the phone into a shared place**, and any tool that clears that bar works. The
test is whether saving takes longer than the impulse lasts: a shared album, a channel with a
bookmark emoji, a note. **A process that requires opening a laptop will not survive contact with a
person on a walk**, which is where the material actually appears.

## Strategy 2: Make performance data a commodity

> "Here's a pattern I constantly see in creative teams. The strategist creates the brief, the
> designer makes the ad, the media buyer launches it, and then silence. Nobody tells the designer
> whether their ad worked or not. Nobody closes the loop. And that's insane."

> "Imagine if a basketball player took a shot but never saw if the ball went in. How would they get
> any better?"

**Feedback loops have to be fast, visible, and tied to the actual work people are doing.**

What the analytics layer is for, in his description: which hooks are converting, which formats are
driving the best CPA, and when creative is starting to fatigue — **shared with designers, creators
and the CEO, not held by the media buyer.** He names four capabilities: comparison reports, creative
fatigue alerts, gamified goal tracking, and industry benchmarks.

> "Here's the part that really matters. When people can see their impact of their work, they get
> better and they get more motivated. It turns creative production from a black box into a game with
> real scores. Your designer who never knew how their ads performed. Now they can see that their
> last concept outperformed the industry benchmark by 40%. That's not just data. That's a positive
> feedback loop that makes them want to push harder."

> "That is how you build a creative engine. Not by hiring more people, but by making the people you
> have more connected to the outcomes of their work."

⛔ **The tool here is the instructor's own product, and unlike strategy 1 the course gives no
generic version.** The mechanism that matters is **whoever made the thing finds out what it did**,
on a cadence short enough to change the next one. What that actually needs:

| The capability, as pitched | What it is generically | Cheapest honest version |
|---|---|---|
| Comparison reports | Same-metric comparison across creatives | A spreadsheet the media buyer already exports |
| Creative fatigue alerts | Noticing decay before it costs money | The 7-day versus 30-day check, run monthly |
| Gamified goal tracking | Making outcomes visible to whoever made them | ⚠️ See the caution below before building this |
| Industry benchmarks | Knowing whether good is good | ⛔ Not available free, and the course names none |

**The first two are the ones that close the loop**, and neither needs a product. A standing item in
a weekly creative review, naming which concepts won and which decayed, does the job.

⚠️ **Two cautions the course does not give:**

1. **"Gamified goal tracking" cuts both ways.** Making individual creative performance visible to
   the whole team is a motivator when results are good and a public leaderboard of failure when they
   are not. Most ads lose. A team culture that has not absorbed that will learn to make safe ads,
   which is the opposite of what the loop is for.
2. **The 40% figure is illustrative, not a result.** It is an example of what a designer might see,
   not a reported outcome. Do not repeat it as a benchmark.

⛔ **This strategy needs attribution you can defend before it is worth building.** Telling a designer
their concept won, on numbers that cannot survive scrutiny, is worse than telling them nothing.
Course 201 covers what the attribution models can and cannot prove; where that skill is installed
alongside this one, settle measurement there first.

## The close

> "The best creative teams don't depend on one person. They build systems that capture inspiration
> from everywhere, and they use data to create feedback loops that make everyone better."

> "That is what it means to be a creative cyborg **team**. Not replacing humans with AI, not copying
> competitors, but building a system where human creativity is multiplied by better tools, better
> inputs, and better feedback."

> "You plus AI is limitless. Now go build."

## Standing this up

The course describes the destination and not the transition. What it implies, in order:

1. **Pick the capture surface first, and optimise it for one-handed use on a phone.** Everything else
   depends on this and nothing else is hard.
2. **Name the contribution as part of the job**, not a favour. The all-hands call worked because it
   was a standing rule with a slot in the week.
3. **Route customer-service language separately** from ad references. Different material, different
   use.
4. **Close the loop on the way back out.** Whoever saved it should hear what happened to it,
   otherwise contribution decays within a month.
5. **Only then build the reporting layer**, and only once the numbers behind it can be defended.
