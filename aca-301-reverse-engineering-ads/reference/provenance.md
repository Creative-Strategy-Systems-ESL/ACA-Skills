# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — Course 301: Reverse Engineering Winning Ads: The Creative Research System
(300-Level, Research & Insights). 25m 51s, 3 modules.

**Zach Murray**, founder of **Foreplay**, a platform he describes as used by *"over 30,000
marketers"* to save, study and build ad creative, holding *"over 100 million ads saved by real
marketers"*. He built it four years before the recording. Before that he ran a DTC apparel brand.

## ⛔ The instructor sells the tool the course runs on. Read this before recommending anything.

This is the only course in the programme where the teaching is delivered inside the instructor's own
commercial product, and it is not disclosed as a conflict at any point.

**Foreplay features named as steps in the method:** Spyder (competitor tracking), boards (saving),
historical ad data, automatic video transcription, Creative Target Expansion (finding brands that
share an audience), the mobile app and Instagram integration (one-tap capture), and Lens (creative
analytics). Module 2 is a live screen walkthrough of the product. Module 3's two strategies are each
resolved by a Foreplay feature.

**None of that makes the teaching wrong.** He built the product because he had the problem, which is
the ordinary reason products exist, and the underlying method predates any tool: Ogilvy did it with
magazines.

⛔ **But do not present the tool as a requirement, and do not recommend it without saying who owns
it.** `reference/the-competitor-audit.md` sets out which steps survive on free public ad libraries
(roughly 70% of the workflow) and which do not (historical and seasonal depth, and automatic
grouping of same-day test cohorts).

**Also named, and not his:** Nano Banana, Higgsfield and Artlist for image generation; ChatGPT and
Claude for the messaging step; Glu, which built ACA's competitor-audit agent against the Foreplay
API. He says he personally uses Higgsfield or Artlist.

## ⚠️ Two discrepancies in ACA's own materials

**1. The sixth dimension has two names.** The Pattern Recognition Playbook calls it **Platform DNA**
throughout. The graded Ads Pattern Recognition assignment calls it **Performance DNA** in its
framework table and then **PLATFORM DNA** in its own pattern-map rows. This skill uses **Platform
DNA**: two of three appearances say it, and the question attached to it (*"what makes it feel
native?"*) is about the platform. Flagged rather than silently reconciled.

**2. The James Perse t-shirt has two prices.** *"$250 for a t-shirt"* in the case-study intro, then
*"James Perse at 125"* and *"someone who spends $125 on a t-shirt"* later. The $125 figure is used
twice and is the one the competitor comparison depends on. Both are carried where they appear.

There is also an OCR artefact in the assignment PDF (*"THEY DID:SN SNSNSN"* on one row), which is a
layout defect rather than content.

## Scope

A **research and ideation** course. It does not cover writing or designing the ad once the pattern is
chosen, media buying, budgets, testing methodology, or measurement beyond naming that feedback loops
matter.

**It names no benchmark numbers**, which is unusual in this programme and worth saying before someone
asks it for a threshold.

**Where it overlaps other ACA courses**, and where those skills are installed alongside this one:

- **Course 302** also teaches competitor work, and it is a different job. 301 reverse-engineers
  individual winning ads into reusable structures. 302 maps the competitive landscape. Route on
  whether the question is about *ads* or about *the market*.
- **Course 230's Cross-Vertical Creative Inspiration Finder** does the adjacent-brand step in more
  depth, with a verification discipline this course lacks.
- **Course 201** covers the attribution that module 3's feedback loop quietly assumes.
