# Course 202: Creator Advertising — The Strategy Before the Brief

**Ashley Vinson.** 20+ years across creative agencies, digital production, creative strategy, and
client side; former Meta and Twitter. 1h 09m, 7 lessons.

This is the **zoom-out** course. Not how to source and brief creators — how to decide what creator
content is for, before anyone is hired.

> "The goal is not just to do creators. The goal is to use creators to help brands get better
> results."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask a creator question and get an answer.

**To produce the Creator Casting Brief, required:**

1. **The client objective**, and how success will be measured
2. **The product or service**, and what makes it worth choosing
3. **Who it is for**, and what is going on in their life that the product speaks to

**Optional, and it sharpens the brief:**

4. Budget, and how many creators it realistically covers
5. Where the content will run, and whether paid media is behind it
6. Category constraints — alcohol, finance, health, gambling, anything with claims or age limits
7. What has been tried with creators before, and what happened

⚠️ **If the objective is missing, ask for it.** Every section of the brief derives from it, and a
brief built on a guessed objective casts the wrong people convincingly.

⛔ **But if the request is one-shot and has a deadline in it, ask AND deliver.** Write your best
reading of the objective into section 1, mark it `⚠️ ASSUMED, NOT SUPPLIED`, put the question at the
top of the document, and produce the rest. A reply consisting only of "what is your objective?" is
not a deliverable to someone who said "this week", and this is the situation the skill is most
likely to meet. **Ask-and-deliver wins over ask-and-wait unless the person is clearly still in
conversation with you.**

## The through-line

> "The question is not should we use creators? The better question is **what job can creator
> content help us do better than the brand could do on its own?**"

And the corrective that runs under the whole course:

> "A bad creator ad is still a bad ad. An authentic looking video with no clear brand or message is
> still unclear. A famous creator making boring content is still the wrong choice."

## How to run the brief

**Teach first, then produce.** Someone asking for a casting brief usually has not settled the
strategy, and the brief's fields are all downstream of it.

1. **Settle the terminology** if the request used UGC, influencer or organic. Ask which meaning.
2. **Work the five strategy questions** in `reference/strategy-and-casting.md`. These are the brief.
3. **Name the creator job or jobs** from the eight. Not a menu — *"You're not saying, I'll have one
   attention to trust and a side of demonstration."*
4. **Produce the brief** using `reference/the-casting-brief.md`.
5. **Say what it is for**: Ashley writes one and has the client sign it off *before anyone starts
   looking*, so "right fit" is agreed rather than argued about later.

## Answering well

**Fix the words before the plan.** *"A client says we need UGC. Someone else says we need
influencers and someone else says, can't we just find someone with like a phone... suddenly
everyone thinks they were talking about the same thing, but they are not."* Organic especially:
distribution or style? *"Those are completely different strategies."*

**Push back on follower counts with the arithmetic, not with an opinion.** Myth 1 in
`reference/myths.md` has the numbers, and they are the fastest way to win this argument.

**Do not attach creator jobs to funnel stages.** *"A testimonial is not automatically awareness. A
product demo does not automatically mean consideration."* They are creative jobs, not measurement
buckets.

**Match the metric to the job.** The most common reporting failure in the course is judging an
asset against a job it was never given.

## ⚠️ Scope: this course stops where the practical work starts

Ashley points three times at **Chloe's course** (ACA Course 502, UGC talent) for the tactics of
sourcing, briefing, casting and managing creators. This course is the strategic layer above it.

So: **it does not give a full production brief template, sourcing tactics, rate benchmarks, or
contract language.** It gives what a brief must contain and why. If someone needs the tactical
version, say so and name where it lives rather than inventing it.

⭐ **That hand-off now has skills behind it.** `aca-502-ugc-talent` carries Chloe's method — sourcing
routes, the casting review grid, briefing and directing — and `aca-brief-ugc-creator` produces the
casting call and ACA's own Content Creator Brief.

⚠️ **Route there rather than treating this course's brief as the same document.** This one scores
the *campaign's requirements* before anyone applies; hers scores *named applicants* after they do,
and the creator's brief is a third document again.

⛔ **And the two casting frameworks each catch something the other misses.** Chloe's five-criteria
grid has no equivalent of criterion 6 here, *are they brand appropriate* — the risk check on a
person's public history. Running both beats running either.

She also refers back to her own **Course 201** on Meta ads for measurement, attribution and
test-and-learn in depth, and does not repeat that material here.
