# Ad Creative Academy: the curriculum map

Ad Creative Academy (ACA) bills itself as the first professional certification for creative
strategy. **26 numbered courses, 89 modules, 13 faculty, 17h 20m of video, across 10 levels
(000 to 900), designed to be completed in 2 to 3 months.**

This skill is the front door. It answers three kinds of question, and the third is the one people
most often get wrong.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none.** Nearly everything here answers straight from the curriculum. "Which course
covers hooks" deserves an answer, not a form.

**Optional, and it sharpens the answer:**

1. **Your role** — strategist, media buyer, founder, or someone hiring a strategist
2. **What you are trying to get done, and by when** — a live client problem points to a different
   course than general upskilling
3. **How much of the programme you have already worked through**

Answer first, always. Offer these only where a tailored path genuinely changes the reply, which in
practice is "where do I start" and "what order should I do this in". Someone asking what Course 402
is should get told what Course 402 is.

## 1. "What is this programme and why does it exist?"

Read **Why the programme exists** (below) and answer in Mirella Crespi's framing, not a generic one. Her
argument is specific and worth preserving: creative strategy became a distinct role because the
targeting layer stopped being the lever. Give the actual reasoning rather than "it teaches you
about ads".

## 2. "Where do I start / what order / how long?"

Read **How to work through the programme** (below). It carries ACA's own study guidance and the four things enrollment
includes.

The levels run in dependency order, so the default answer is "start at 100 and go up". Say so
plainly, then add the one caveat that matters: the 800-level (AI) is written to be useful at any
point, and people arriving with a specific problem often want a mid-level course first. Route them
to that course rather than insisting they start at the beginning.

## 3. "Which course covers X?" and "what is course 402?"

Read **The Ad Creative Academy curriculum** (below). Every course is there with its code, title, faculty, company,
runtime and full module list. Answer from that file, not from memory.

⚠️ **The course codes are a genuine trap, and this is the most valuable thing this skill knows.**
ACA renumbered its curriculum for launch. Material produced before the renumber carries the old
codes, and a lot of it is still in circulation. So a bare "course 301" is ambiguous:

| If someone says | They may mean | Or |
|---|---|---|
| 301 | Reverse Engineering Winning Ads (Zach Murray) | the old code for Research (Mirella) |
| 302 | Research for Creative Strategy (Mirella) | the old code for Reverse Engineering |
| 401 | Brief Development | the old code covering Key Metrics too |
| 702 | Building a Growth Creative Machine (Udi Avital) | the old code for Performance Review |

**The Ad Creative Academy curriculum** (below) holds the **current shipping** numbering. When a code is ambiguous,
say so and confirm by faculty or topic rather than guessing. Naming the instructor resolves it
almost every time, because the faculty did not shuffle when the numbers did.

## How to work through it

**This skill routes; it does not teach a course.** The sequence is a routing procedure, and step 3 is the one
that most often goes wrong.

1. **Establish what they actually want** — the programme shape, a starting point, or one specific topic. The
   three questions below cover each.
2. **Resolve any course code against **The Ad Creative Academy curriculum** (below)** before answering. ⛔ ACA renumbered its
   courses, so a bare code from older material may not mean what it appears to.
3. ⛔ **Check the target skill is installed before naming it**, per **Routing: topic to skill** (below). Skills are copied
   in folder by folder and a partial copy is normal. **Sending someone to a skill that is not there is worse
   than teaching them directly**, because they get a name, try it, and nothing happens.
4. **Hand off, or teach from the map.** If the skill is present, name it. If not, answer from

5. **Use **How to work through the programme** (below)** when the question is order or duration rather than content.
6. **Check they leave with a next action**, not a list. A specific course or skill to open beats a tour of

## Why the programme exists

Mirella Crespi's framing, from the Course 100 introduction. Quoted rather than paraphrased, because
the specific argument is what makes the programme's premise coherent.

### The opening claim

> "Welcome to Ad Creative Academy. You just made one of the most important decisions of your
> career. While 90% of creative professionals are about to be blindsided by the AI revolution,
> you're positioning yourself to thrive in it."

> "Let's be honest. The creative landscape is transforming faster than at any point in history.
> What was working six months ago does not work today. Platforms change, algorithms shift, and the
> volume of creative content needed to succeed has exploded beyond what any human team can manually
> produce."

The volume argument is the load-bearing one. The claim is not that AI makes creative work easier,
it is that the required output has passed what a human team can produce by hand, so the work has to
change shape.

> "That's why you're here. Because you recognize that the next generation of creative strategists
> won't just be creative thinkers. They'll be AI powered creative forces who can generate, test,
> and scale winning campaigns with unprecedented speed and precision."

> "The future belongs to those who can harness human creativity and AI capabilities together. And
> that future starts now."

### Why creative strategy became a role at all

The deeper reason sits underneath the AI argument, and it is the one to lead with when someone asks
why this discipline exists: **the targeting layer stopped being the lever, so the creative became
the lever.**

Before the 2020-2021 privacy changes, the biggest revenue decision in paid social was audience
targeting. Rich third-party pixel data made granular lookalikes, exclusions and time-windowed
retargeting possible, and the platform algorithms did the hard work of putting an ad in front of
the right person. As Mirella puts it, you did not even need great ads.

When that data went away, targeting went broad and the algorithm started reading first-party
in-app signals instead: whether a user stops scrolling, watches, engages, shares, clicks. Those
signals are produced by the creative. So the creative became the targeting mechanism, and the
person who decides what the creative says and shows became a distinct, senior role.

That is why the discipline exists as its own job rather than a subtask of media buying, and it is
why the programme opens here rather than with tactics.

### What the programme claims to cover

The seven areas named in the introduction:

1. **Consumer Psychology & Direct Response Fundamentals** - the psychological triggers behind
   decision-making, and the direct response frameworks that hold regardless of platform
2. **Modular Ad Creative Frameworks** - the building blocks approach: analyze any ad, identify why
   it works, build high-performing variations from the parts
3. **Complete Research Methodology** - audience research for hidden pain points and desires,
   competitor analysis for gaps, account audits for quick wins, systems for tracking trends
4. **Platform-Specific Mastery** - Meta, TikTok and YouTube treated as fundamentally different
   creative problems, taught by leads and executives from each
5. **End-to-End Creative Production** - concepts, scripts, briefing creators, producers and
   editors, plus the editing techniques that move engagement and conversion
6. **Scientific Testing & Optimization** - forming hypotheses, structured testing frameworks,
   logging performance into a database of winning elements, and which metrics actually matter
7. **Advanced Analysis & Iteration** - reading performance data, isolating what works, making
   targeted improvements that beat creative fatigue and allow scale

> "In every module, we integrated AI tools and workflows that multiply your productivity and make
> you irreplaceable."

### A note on the framing

The "90% will be blindsided" line and the "irreplaceable" claim are marketing register, and they
are quoted here as what the programme says about itself. The substantive argument underneath, that
signal-based targeting made creative the lever, is the part that stands on its own and is the
better answer to give when someone asks why the discipline matters.

## How to work through the programme

ACA's own guidance on pacing and sequence, plus what enrollment includes.

### The size of it

**26 numbered courses · 89 modules · 17h 20m of video · 45 downloadable worksheets, templates and
prompt packs · a 90-minute final exam · a 5 to 7 day capstone project.** ACA designs it to be
completed in 2 to 3 months.

⚠️ **You will see 34h 40m quoted for the video, and it is a double-count.** ACA's curriculum
spreadsheet's runtime column carries a time on every course row and also on every level-summary
row. But a level *is* its own courses added up, so summing the column counts the same footage
twice: the 26 course runtimes total 17h 20m 11s, the 8 level totals come to the same 17h 20m 11s,
and 34h 40m 22s is those two added. The 89 module runtimes, in their own column, also total
17h 20m 11s.

**17h 20m is the real figure.** Mirella's own introduction says "over fifteen hours of training
videos", which is about right, and is a useful cross-check that the smaller number is the true one.

The same applies to the worksheet count. ACA's asset list has 46 rows but one is a duplicate
(the Content Creator Brief, listed once as a Google Doc and once as a PDF), so **45 distinct
downloads**. Add the assignments and per-course quizzes and the introduction's "fifty worksheets
and assignments" is also about right.

Quote 17h 20m and 45 downloads. If someone cites 34h 40m, the double-count above is the
explanation.

### ACA's study advice, in Mirella's words

> "Set aside at least an hour daily to review the materials, complete the worksheets immediately
> after watching their related modules, and take the exams while the content is still fresh in your
> mind."

> "Our data shows that students who complete the program within thirty days of starting have the
> highest success and implementation rates. Momentum really matters."

Three specific behaviours, and the reasoning behind each is worth passing on rather than just the
instruction:

1. **An hour a day.** The programme is sized so it divides into roughly one hour per working day
   over a month. Framing it that way is what makes the total tractable.
2. **Worksheets straight after the module, not batched at the end.** The worksheets are applied
   exercises against your own product, so they work while the module is still live in your head and
   decay quickly once it is not.
3. **Exams while it is fresh.** Same reasoning, applied to retention.

> "This isn't just about consuming information. It's about transforming how you approach creative
> strategy. The time that you invest now will pay dividends throughout your career."

ACA also offers a downloadable sample calendar that students can adapt to their own schedule.

### What order

The levels are ordered by dependency, so straight through from 100 is the default and is what ACA
intends:

| Level | What it is for |
|---|---|
| 000 | Orientation |
| 100 | Why the role exists, consumer psychology, the fundamentals of reading an ad |
| 200 | Platforms: Meta, TikTok, creators, cross-channel |
| 300 | Research: reverse engineering winners, audiences, competitors, trends |
| 400 | Strategy and ideation: briefs, hooks, metrics, storyboards, short-form |
| 500 | Production: getting it made, casting and directing talent |
| 600 | Launching and testing |
| 700 | Reading performance and iterating, sprint planning, the operating system |
| 800 | AI across the whole workflow |
| 900 | Exam and capstone |

Two useful caveats:

- **The 800-level is not gated.** It is written to be useful at any point, and someone whose actual
  problem is "I need to produce AI visuals this week" is better served going there directly than
  waiting until they have finished 700.
- **Someone arriving with a specific problem should go to that course first.** The dependency
  ordering is a default, not a prerequisite chain. Point them at the course that solves their
  problem and suggest the foundation courses as follow-up rather than as a gate.

### What enrollment includes

Four things, in ACA's own framing:

1. **Certification.** Become a Certified Creative Strategist. The credential is positioned as
   evidence of both timeless creative principles and current AI technique, signalling to employers
   and clients a skill set that delivers measurable results.
2. **Alumni membership.** On completion, access to ACA's community of creative professionals,
   invitations to future meetups, ongoing support, and networking with peers applying the same
   methods across other industries and markets.
3. **The toolkit.** ACA values it at over $20,000: premium tools, templates and resources,
   including the creative frameworks, worksheets, AI prompt libraries and AI agents, described as
   battle-tested in real campaigns.
4. **The job board.** A specialised board connecting certified graduates with companies looking for
   AI-powered creative strategists, available only to certified alumni.

### The certification path

The 900-level has four parts: an introduction covering how the final steps work, a **90-minute
final exam** in three sections (Knowledge Foundation, Strategic Analysis, Application Challenge),
a **5 to 7 day capstone project**, and then unlocking the certificate itself.

The exam covers Creative Strategy Fundamentals, Advertising and Platform Knowledge, Performance
Evaluation, and Strategic Decision Making. The capstone covers Creative Strategy Application,
Performance Evaluation, Testing and Iteration Planning, and Concept Development.

Individual courses also carry their own summary quizzes, and the 100-level has a combined
Consumer Psychology & Creative Fundamentals quiz covering Courses 102 and 103.

## The Ad Creative Academy curriculum

The shipping curriculum. **26 numbered courses · 89 modules · 13 faculty · 17h 20m of video ·
10 levels (000 to 900).** This file is the authority on ACA course codes, faculty, companies,

and a capstone rather than a course. So a complete walk through the programme is 28 things: the
orientation, the 26 courses, and the certification.

### Disambiguating common asks

Several topics appear in more than one course, and sending someone to the wrong one wastes their
time. The distinctions that actually matter:

- **Hooks** appear in three places. Course 401 module 3 is hook *development* (how to write them).
  Course 402 is hooks as *metrics* (thumbstop, CTR, what the numbers prove). Course 301 covers
  mining hooks from competitors. Ask which problem they have before routing.
- **Competitor work** splits by purpose. Course 301 is reverse-engineering winners into reusable
  structures. Course 302 module 3 is mapping the competitive landscape. The first is tactical, the
  second is strategic.
- **Creative testing** splits by stage. Course 601 is launching and naming. Course 602 is testing
  method. Course 703 is planning the volume that feeds the tests.
- **AI** is everywhere, but the 800-level is where it is the subject rather than a tool. If someone
  wants AI applied to research or planning, that is 801. If they want to generate images or video,
  802. If they want to automate a pipeline, 803.
- **"Content creation"** is ambiguous between Course 404 (organic short-form, Luke Kostka) and
  Course 501 (paid ad production, Mirella Crespi). These are different disciplines. Check whether
  they mean organic or paid.

### One thing to be careful about

**The total video runtime is 17h 20m, not the 34h 40m that ACA's own summary reports.** The
curriculum spreadsheet's runtime column carries a time on every course row and also on every
level-summary row, and the summary adds the whole column. Since a level is the sum of its own
courses, that counts the same footage twice. Measured: the 26 courses total 17h 20m 11s; the 8
level rows total the identical 17h 20m 11s; 34h 40m 22s is the sum of the two. The 89 module
runtimes, held in a separate column, independently total 17h 20m 11s as well.

This is a derived correction rather than something ACA states, so it is flagged here. The
per-course and per-module runtimes in `curriculum-map.md` are ACA's own and are unaltered, which is
what makes the arithmetic checkable: summing the course list in that file reproduces 17h 20m.

Mirella's introduction says "over fifteen hours of training videos", consistent with 17h 20m and a
useful independent check that the smaller figure is the right one.
