# Course 402: Key Metrics, Thumbstop & CTR Tactics

**Chloe Rhys**, Ex-TubeScience, now at OpenStore on the brand **Jack Archer**. 59m 38s, 3 modules.

> "advertising that is solely built on aesthetics or vibes isn't fulfilling the entire goal…. At the
> end of the day, we're not putting our work in museums, its job is to make people buy."

**This is the numeracy course.** It sits beside the making: it does not teach you what to make, it
teaches you to read what happened and say something defensible about it.

⭐ **This skill emits a creative read-out table and the CTR insights log** the course describes,
rather than returning a paragraph of commentary on your numbers.

⛔ **This is the only ACA course that gives metric thresholds with the advertiser attached.** That
makes it the folder's reference for benchmarks, and it also makes it the place where ACA's courses
disagree with each other. Read `reference/provenance.md` before quoting any number here.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what thumbstop is, what CTR (Link Click) means, or why
a high CTR can be bad news, and get an answer.

**To read an account or diagnose an ad, required:**

1. **The actual numbers**, per ad or per creative. At minimum impressions and 3-second views for
   thumbstop, link clicks for CTR
2. **What the ads were** — format, hook, angle. A row of numbers with no creative attached cannot be
   diagnosed, only described

**Optional, and each one sharpens the read:**

3. Spend, purchases, revenue — for CPA, ROAS and CVR
4. 10-second views, for hold rate
5. Platform and placement, because the bands are not portable across platforms
6. The account's own history, which is a better benchmark than anything in this course
7. What the landing page does, when CTR is strong and conversion is not

⛔ **Never invent a metric value, and never fill an empty column with a plausible one.** A read-out
is a document someone forwards to a media buyer. A number that was estimated and not labelled as
estimated becomes a number somebody plans against. If it was not supplied, write `not supplied`.

⛔ **Never present this course's bands as industry benchmarks.** They come from one advertiser in
one category, and the course says so. The honest form is `Chloe Rhys reports X for Jack Archer`,
not `a good CTR is X`.

## The through-line

> "You're not just bringing a creative point of view. You're bringing proof."

> "Your job is to diagnose, not just report."

⭐ **That is the standard for every output here.** A read-out that lists what happened without saying
what to change next is a report, and the course is explicit that reporting is not the job.

## How to run it

**Define, then read, then decide.** Reading numbers before defining them is how a confident wrong conclusion
gets into a client email.

1. **Ask what they actually have** — exported data, one ad's numbers, or a client question. The route differs.
2. ⛔ **Define the metrics before reading them**, from `reference/metrics.md`. ⚠️ **Hold rate especially:
   Course 701 uses a different denominator, and the two numbers are not comparable.**
3. **Separate what creative owns from what it does not**, same file. This is what keeps the read-out honest
   about attribution.
4. **Read thumbstop** with `reference/thumbstop.md` and **CTR** with `reference/ctr.md`. ⚠️ Say which of the
   five Ads Manager CTRs is meant; they are different numbers.
5. ⭐ **Produce the read-out and the insights log** from `reference/diagnose.md`. Two artifacts, emitted as
   tables that paste into a sheet.
6. **End on what to change next**, which is the only output that makes the analysis worth doing.
7. **Check the read-out says what creative did not cause.** A read-out that claims everything is a read-out
   nobody should act on.

## Answering well

**Separate what creative owns from what it does not.** Chloe's central argument:

> "Conversion metrics like CPA or CVR are affected by lots of things: the landing page, the price,
> inventory or availability, your shipping or checkout process. But engagement metrics like
> thumbstop and CTR? **That's all you. That's your script. Your image. Your format. Your hook.**"

⚠️ **And the counterweight, in her words:** *"Not every number is a creative problem. Sometimes ads
underperform because of targeting, seasonality, product-market fit, or campaign structure."*

**Stopping the wrong person is not a win.** *"It's easy to make someone stop. It's harder to make
the right person stop."* A shocking visual that spikes thumbstop and converts nobody is *"noise, not
signal"*.

**High CTR is not automatically good.** ⭐ Her most useful finding, and it is counterintuitive: at
Jack Archer *"some of our highest CTR ads had some of the lowest conversion rates"* and *"some of our
lowest CTR ads drove the highest ROAS"*. **CTR is directional, not definitive.**

**Metrics make the strategist credible, not just correct.** The course's practical argument is that
reading the numbers is what lets you defend creative decisions to media teams, leadership and
clients. *"If you're the person in the room who can both come up with the idea and interpret the
results, you have a major advantage."*

## ⚠️ Scope

Reading and diagnosing creative performance. It is **not** a media buying course: it does not cover
campaign structure, bidding, budget allocation or attribution setup, and it says several times that
those sit outside the strategist's control. It stops at "here is what to change and why"; making the
change is Course 401 and Course 403.

⚠️ **It overlaps Course 302's account audit and does not replace it.** 302 is the standing audit
process with its own worksheets; this is the metric literacy underneath it. If someone has exported
data and wants a full audit, that is 302's artifact, not this one.
