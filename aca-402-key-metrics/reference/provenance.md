## ⛔ Three numbering and structure traps in the source

**1. The files say Course 401, the course is 402.** Both transcripts are filed under a `c401` stamp,
which ACA also uses for a completely different subject. The curriculum outline is the authority and
places this course at 402.

**2. The shipped module cut is not the script's module cut.** They divide the same material
differently:

| The held script's three modules | The shipped course's three modules |
|---|---|
| Understanding Metrics — The Creative Strategist's Secret Weapon | Thumbstop Hook Frameworks |
| Thumbstop 101 — Stopping the Scroll | High-CTR Copy + Visual Tactics |
| Click-Through Rate Tactics | Motion + Design Tricks That Grab |

⚠️ **"Motion + Design Tricks That Grab" has no module of its own in the script.** That material is
archetype 6 of the seven visual hooks, *Motion, Time and Colour*, plus the presentation layer of the
thumbstop anatomy. **Checked directly rather than assumed:** the motion and editing content is
present in both transcripts. **The material is complete; only the boundaries moved.**

**3. The script's own runtime estimate does not match the shipped course.** The script header reads
*"Total Word Count: 12,486 / Estimated Total Run Time: ~78 minutes"* against a shipped 59m 38s.
⚠️ **An 18-minute gap between an estimate and a delivery is normal and is not evidence of cut
content**, but it does mean the script's per-section time budgets should not be quoted as module
runtimes.

## ⛔ A number in the delivered transcript is wrong, and this skill uses the script's

The delivered recording gives the second CTR band as *"between 0.06 and 0.08%"*. **That is
impossible inside its own passage:** it sits between a 0.5% red flag above it and a 0.9-1.3% sweet
spot below it, so the band cannot be an order of magnitude under the floor. The script reads *"between
0.6 and 0.8 percent"*.

**This skill carries 0.6% to 0.8%.** Recorded because a decimal slip in a benchmark is the kind of
error that survives being copied.

## ⛔ Where ACA's own courses disagree, and none of them say so

**This is the most important thing in this file.** Course 402 is the only ACA course that attaches
thresholds to metrics *and* names the advertiser they came from. That makes it the folder's
benchmark reference, and it also puts it in direct conflict with two other courses.

### 1. CTR: this course and Course 210 cannot both be right about the same ad

| | The number | The instruction attached |
|---|---|---|
| **Course 402**, Chloe Rhys | **0.9% to 1.3% is the sweet spot.** Over 1.4%, *"pause and ask, is this traffic qualified?"* | Treat a very high CTR as a warning |
| **Course 210**, TikTok | **Above 2% for ecommerce.** Below it, *"your CTA or landing page mismatches the promise"* | Treat anything under 2% as broken |

⛔ **An ad at 1.1% CTR is a healthy sweet-spot ad by 402 and a failing ad by 210.** Course 210's floor
sits above the point where 402 starts worrying about click quality.

**What resolves it, and what does not.** Different platforms (Meta link clicks out to a store against
TikTok), different advertisers, and near-certainly **different CTR definitions** — 402 spends a whole
section establishing that Ads Manager holds five distinct CTRs and that only CTR (Link Click) should
be used, while 210 names no variant at all. **Neither course mentions the other, and both figures are
unsourced.**

⭐ **So the usable rule is: never carry a CTR band across platforms, and always say which CTR.** Quote
each course's number only alongside its platform and its advertiser.

### 2. Thumbstop: a threshold in Course 210 that cannot be reconciled or dismissed

Course 210 gives `3-second hold rate, ≥ 70%` with the note *"if lower, your hook is ice-cold"*.
**It gives no formula for it.**

- If that metric is 3-second views ÷ impressions — the thumbstop this course defines — then 70% as a
  health floor is impossible against 402's *"50% or higher is very rare and often means you've struck
  gold"*.
- If it means something else, such as how many of those who started were still watching at 3 seconds,
  then the two are different measurements and neither number applies to the other.

⛔ **The material does not settle which.** ⭐ **The finding is the ambiguity itself: a metric name with
a hard threshold and no formula cannot be acted on**, and this course is the one that supplies the
formulas. Ask which numerator and denominator before applying anybody's 70%.

### 3. Course 302 deliberately gives no thresholds, and this one does

Course 302's account audit names no thresholds for any of these metrics, and asks you to set
targets from your own baseline. The account-audit skill built from it carries a matching prohibition
against supplying a benchmark and attributing it to that course.

⚠️ **That is not a contradiction of fact, and the prohibition is still right as written.** 302 really
does not give numbers, so quoting one as 302's would be an invention. **What changes is that ACA does
have numbers, in this course, from a named advertiser.** The correct move when someone auditing an
account asks for a benchmark: say 302 deliberately gives none, offer Chloe's bands as Chloe's, and
say the account's own baseline outranks both.

### 4. Where the courses agree, which is worth recording too

**Thumbstop's formula.** Course 302 defines hook rate as (3-second video views ÷ impressions) × 100%
and this course defines thumbstop identically. ⭐ **This course adds what 302 lacks: on TikTok the
threshold is 2 seconds, not 3.**

**Creative as targeting.** *"Now, creative is the targeting"* here, and Course 401 makes the same
argument about hooks building the audience. Two faculty, same mechanism, independently stated.

## What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Chloe's, quoted directly**, including all formulas, worked examples,
bands, archetypes and copy formulas.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the five-file split, the routing table, the metric
dictionary as a table, and separating the seven visual archetypes from the seven copy formulas into
their own tables.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The cross-course conflict analysis above.** No ACA course cross-references another; the
  collisions are only visible from outside the courses.
- ⭐ **The control panel read as a rule for deciding whose problem a bad number is**, before the
  argument between creative and media happens.
- ⛔ **The read-out table's layout**, built from her metrics and her two-line diagnostic. The
  insights log's columns are hers.
- ⛔ **The rule against inventing a metric value or filling an empty cell**, and the `not supplied`
  convention. She never addresses missing data.
- ⛔ **The note that hold rate's denominator is 3-second views, not impressions**, called out because
  mixing it with thumbstop is the easiest arithmetic error in the pair.
- ⚠️ **The observation that curiosity clicks and an over-promising hook look identical in the
  numbers.** She gives both explanations for the same pattern and does not say how to tell them
  apart; the answer is to read the ad against the page.
- ⚠️ **The point that her video-beats-static finding excludes discount statics**, which she says
  herself two paragraphs earlier but does not connect.
- ⚠️ **The note that the hook-rate-to-CTR finding is a correlation**, and that her practical advice
  survives either reading.
- ⛔ **The claims warning on copy formulas 2, 4 and 5.** Several of her own examples are health and
  results claims. The course does not raise it.
- ⭐ **Logging the losers as well as the winners**, since a log of winners cannot tell you what fails.
- **The note that a thumbstop figure without a platform is not comparable**, given the 3-second and
  2-second thresholds.
- **The point that the thumbnail is usually the media buyer's to set**, so it belongs in the hand-off
  or it does not happen. She says to reach out; this states the consequence of not doing it.
