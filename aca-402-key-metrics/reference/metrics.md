> glaze over. But here's the thing. Understanding performance data is one of the most powerful tools
> in the creative strategist's arsenal."

## The five outcome metrics

Each with ACA's formula and ACA's worked example, unchanged.

| Metric | Formula | Chloe's worked example |
|---|---|---|
| **CPA** — Cost Per Action | Spend ÷ Conversions | $200 spend, 10 purchases → **$20 CPA** |
| **ROAS** — Return on Ad Spend | Revenue ÷ Spend | $600 revenue on $200 spend → **3.0 ROAS**. *"For every $1 you spent, you made $3"* |
| **CTR** — Click-Through Rate | Link Clicks ÷ Impressions | 100,000 impressions, 2,000 clicks → **2% CTR** |
| **CVR** — Conversion Rate | Conversions ÷ Clicks | 2,000 clicks, 100 purchases → **5% CVR** |
| **CPM** — Cost Per Thousand Impressions | Spend ÷ (Impressions ÷ 1,000) | $500 spend, 100,000 impressions → **$5 CPM** |

**What she says about each, beyond the formula:**

- **CPA** *"is the end result of everything — creative, targeting, landing page, pricing, offer."*
  The primary indicator that an ad drove sales, and the one most contaminated by things you do not
  control.
- **ROAS** *"in ecommerce, this is gold. But again, like CPA it's influenced by a lot more than just
  creative."*
- **CTR** *"is a great proxy for curiosity and interest. CTR is directly tied to your copy and your
  hook."*
- **CVR** *"is influenced by your landing page, storefront, checkout experience. If your ad is great
  but your product page is confusing, or there's a bug at checkout, the CVR will drop."*
- **CPM** ⚠️ *"Low CPM can be a good thing. But sometimes a low CPM can also mean bringing in lower
  quality traffic, that might be more difficult to convert."* A cheap impression is not a good one.

⚠️ **Her key point, stated as one:** *"Not every number is a creative problem. Sometimes ads
underperform because of targeting, seasonality, product-market fit, or campaign structure. But
knowing the numbers can help you ask the right questions, and defend your creative decisions with
real data."*

## ⭐ The creative control panel

The organising argument of the whole course.

| Contaminated by things outside creative | Owned by creative |
|---|---|
| CPA · ROAS · CVR | **Thumbstop** · **CTR** |

> "Conversion metrics like CPA or CVR are affected by lots of things: the landing page, the price,
> inventory or availability, your shipping or checkout process. But engagement metrics like
> thumbstop and CTR? **That's all you. That's your script. Your image. Your format. Your hook.**"

> "That's why many creative teams use thumbstop and CTR as their main source of truth when
> evaluating ads."

**And the two-line diagnostic that follows from it, in her words:**

- *"If you're seeing strong thumbstop and CTR, but poor purchases? That might be a signal to **fix
  the landing page or review your offer**."*
- *"On the other hand, if CTR is low, that's your cue to **test a new concept**."*

⭐ **This is what makes the course worth having.** It is a rule for deciding whose problem a bad
number is, before anyone argues about it. Most disputes between creative and media teams are this
question left unasked.

## The two engagement metrics

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Thumbstop**, also called **Hook Rate** | 3-second views ÷ Impressions |
| **CTR** | Link Clicks ÷ Impressions |

> "Why 3 seconds? Because that's the minimum amount of time someone has to watch for it to count as
> a 'view' in Meta's eyes. And that moment, those first few seconds, that's your hook."

⭐ **On TikTok it is 2 seconds, not 3.** *"On TikTok, hook rate is only 2 seconds."* The same named
metric is computed from a different threshold on each platform, so a thumbstop figure without a
platform attached is not comparable to anything.

**Also worth watching, named but not defined in depth:**

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Hold rate** | 10-second views ÷ 3-second views |
| **Saves and shares** | On TikTok and Instagram Reels |
| **Outbound clicks** | Especially when sending to a blog, quiz or lead gen |

⛔ **Hold rate's denominator is 3-second views, not impressions.** It measures whether the people who
stopped kept watching, so it is a body-of-the-ad metric, not a hook metric. Mixing it with thumbstop
is the most common arithmetic error in reading these two.

## ⛔ Before you quote a threshold

This course gives real bands, in `reference/thumbstop.md` and `reference/ctr.md`. Two things have to
travel with every one of them:

1. **The advertiser.** They come from OpenStore's brand **Jack Archer**, men's apparel, and Chloe
   says so herself.
2. **ACA's other courses give different numbers for the same metrics, and none of them cross-
   reference.** See `reference/provenance.md` before you quote anything as ACA's position.

⭐ **The account's own baseline beats every band in this course**, and that is also what ACA's
account-audit course teaches: record your current average, your top and bottom performer, and set
targets from those.
