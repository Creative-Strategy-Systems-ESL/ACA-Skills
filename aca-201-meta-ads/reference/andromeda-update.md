# The Andromeda update

Module 8, recorded later than the rest as *"an extra video to give you a more current update on
meta ads in 2026, especially around Automation, Advantage Plus Tools Andromeda. And why creative
variety matters even more than it used to."*

⭐ **This module partly supersedes module 4.** Read it as the current position where the two differ.

## What Andromeda actually is

Ashley defines it carefully, and the care is the point — it is narrower than people assume.

> "Andromeda is part of Meta's newer ads delivery system. More specifically, it is part of the **ad
> retrieval stage**, which is the part where meta sorts through a massive number of possible ads and
> decides which ones are even worth considering for a specific person in a specific moment."

The mechanic: an ad slot opens, and Meta cannot deeply evaluate every possible ad in the moment
because the person is already scrolling. So it first **retrieves a smaller pool** that look like
plausible fits, and the ranking and auction then decide the winner from that pool.

> "**It is not the whole auction. It is not the whole delivery system.**"

After retrieval, everything from `the-system.md` still applies — objective, bid, estimated action
rate, whether the ad is a good experience.

## Signals

> "A signal again is basically a clue. It is a piece of information the system can use to understand
> what an ad is about, who it might be relevant for, and what kind of result it might drive."

Signals come from the campaign objective, the landing page, the pixel, the product catalog, the
copy, the format, the hook, the visuals, the person in the ad, the message — **and from how people
respond once it runs**: clicks, watches, ignores, comments, conversions, bounces.

Her examples of creative as signal:

- An ad showing a parent packing school lunches → a clue
- An ad opening *"tired of wasting money on cheap shoes"* → a clue
- A creator talking to camera about sensitive skin → a clue

> "This is why the Andromeda update matters for creative strategists. **Meta is getting better at
> reading and using the creative itself as a signal.**"

The consequence, stated plainly: *"your creative inputs matter more, not less."*

# The five things to know and do

## 1. Creative is doing more of the targeting work

The move away from narrow targeting was already true; automation makes it more so. Targeting used
to be the media team's job — they picked audience and placements, creative filled the plan. That
made sense when there were fewer algorithmic signals and everyone was guessing.

Objective, media setup, budget, pixel, CAPI, landing page and measurement all still matter. But the
creative now supplies most of what the system uses to find the match.

⭐ **So the different buyer motivations have to be visible in the creative itself.** First-time
buyers, sceptical buyers, loyal customers, gift buyers, price-sensitive buyers, quality-focused
buyers, people who need to see the product in use — those differences need to show up.

> "You cannot just make one generic ad and expect automation to magically figure it out... that is
> like giving a matchmaker one blurry dating profile and saying, yeah, hey, can you find my
> soulmate?"

## 2. ⛔ Stop thinking "more ads" means five tiny variations of the same thing

This is the correction to module 4, and Ashley is specific about it:

> "I do not mean taking one ad changing the background from blue to green. Moving the logo, slight
> delay the gift and then suddenly say, look, we made five versions."

**She marks the change as a change**, which is why this is an update and not a contradiction:

> "To be fair, some of that used to work better than it does now. There was a time when small
> variations could help refresh performance... and small variations can still have a role,
> **especially once you know a concept has potential**. But if all you are doing is making tiny
> cosmetic changes, you are probably not giving meta enough meaningful difference to work with."

> "Meta does not just need more stuff, it needs **more meaningful and distinct creative inputs**."

What meaningful difference means: different hooks · different messages · different product moments ·
different reasons to believe · different formats · different use cases · different objections ·
different proof points · different creator styles.

⭐ **The line that settles the "how many ads" question:**

> "**More ads is not automatically a strategy.** If you make ten ads that are basically the same
> idea with tiny changes, you have technically made more ads, sure, but strategically, you may not
> have given the system much more to work with."

## 3. Build creative groups around different jobs, not different formats

A single campaign might carry:

- A **product demo**
- A **creator-led use case**
- An **objection-handling** ad
- An **offer-led** ad
- A **proof or quality** ad
- A **short hook** version and a **longer consideration** version
- A **lifestyle** version, a **product-focused** version, a **brand-focused** version

⚠️ **Not a checklist.** *"You do not need every single one of those in every campaign. This is not
a grocery list."* Pick the jobs this campaign actually needs.

## 4. Build for the question you want answered

Because creative groups differ by job rather than by cosmetic, the results become interpretable —
but only if the metric matches the job.

> "If one ad gets more video views, that does not automatically mean people understand the product.
> And if one ad gets more engagement, that does not automatically mean it was good for the brand."

The upgrade in what you can learn:

> "You are not just asking which blue background one. You are asking better strategic questions.
> **Which hook worked? Which message worked? Which product moment worked? Which audience motivation
> seem to matter? Which objection needed answering? Which creative style helped people care?**"

## 5. Your job is never done

> "even the best ads fatigue, eventually people see them. Too many times the market changes,
> competitors react to the offer changes, seasonality changes. The platform keeps learning,... the
> thing that works beautifully in one week may start to get tired by **week four, week six, or week
> eight**."

⚠️ **Fatigue is not evidence the creative was bad.** *"It just means ads have a lifespan."* And in
an automated environment it compounds: when people stop responding, the system reads that as a
signal too, and delivery drops.

So the job is not one batch at launch. It is *"building a system that can keep feeding the campaign
with fresh, useful, meaningful, different creative"* — new hooks, product moments, creator angles,
proof points, objection handlers, seasonal versions, offer-led ads, and edits informed by the last
round.

> "You are not just launching creative, you are **managing creative momentum.** And yes, that sounds

## The final takeaway

> "creative variety is no longer and nice to have. It is a must have. But the keyword is not just
> variety, it is **meaningful and distinct variety.** Different creative ideas, doing different jobs
> for different audience motivations inside one clear campaign strategy."

And the argument the whole module exists to make:

> "yes, meta is definitely more automated now. Yes, Advantage Plus tools are doing more. Yes,
> Andromeda helps meta process and retrieve ads in a more advanced way, but none of that means
> creative strategy is becoming less important. It means creative strategy is becoming **more**
> important because **automation is only as good as the inputs you give it.** And if you give the
> system boring, generic, repetitive ads, you should not be shocked when the campaign struggles."

> "Meta can help find the match, but you use the creative strategist. You still need to give it
> something worth matching, and then you need to keep giving it something worth matching."
