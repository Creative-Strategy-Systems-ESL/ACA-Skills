# How Meta's system decides

Modules 1 and 2. The mechanics a creative strategist has to understand even though someone else
does the uploading.

## The scale, as ACA gives it

Meta is the parent of Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads — *"almost 4 billion
monthly users with over 3 billion active users. That's over 40% of the world."* Advertising runs
across Facebook, Instagram and the Audience Network.

Many formats exist because *"people connect in a ton of different ways depending on what time it is
and what they're doing or what mood they're in."*

## The algorithm, and the mindset it forces

Every feed is uniquely tailored. That has one consequence worth stating plainly to clients:

> "You're not the main event. You're simply a guest at someone else's curated event."

The failure it prevents is the client who reviews their ad in isolation, in a conference room,
where it looks great — and forgets it will appear between things the viewer actually chose.

⭐ **The demo:** record your own feed, splice the client's ad in, show them. *"Clients realize
pretty quickly that their ad doesn't exactly make you wanna watch."*

## The auction formula

⛔ **Bidding highest does not win.** Meta scores each candidate ad:

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Bid** | What you are willing to pay for a click or conversion. Varies with supply and demand |
| **Estimated action rate** | How likely this person is to take your desired action after seeing it |
| **User value** | Relevance and quality. Formerly called *quality score*. Covers user engagement, overall ad quality, and landing-page relevance |

> **Total value = bid + estimated action rate + user value. The highest total value wins the
> auction and is shown to that individual.**

Why a creative strategist should care: two of the three components are creative's responsibility.
You cannot raise the bid, but better creative raises both estimated action rate and user value.

Meta filters this way deliberately — the system exists to catch *"those low quality and irrelevant
ads"*, because relevant ads keep users on the platform, which keeps the revenue coming.

## Campaign setup, in the order a media specialist works

Ashley's point is that a strategist should be able to check each step and spot where it went wrong.

### 1. Route: reservation or auction

- **Reservation** — locks in price in advance for awareness or engagement. *"Very rarely used"*,
  but useful for budgeting a known big event.
- **Auction** — *"the one that people use 99.9% of the time."*

### 2. Campaign objective

⭐ **The single most consequential choice, made before any creative exists.** Choosing an objective
tells Meta which goal to pursue, and it will pursue exactly that.

> "if you tell it, you know, that you want to get traffic, it will serve your ads to only those
> people that go to websites and no one else.... Just because someone likes to click on links and go
> to a website doesn't also mean that they like to buy things. They could just be, you know, a
> digital window shopper"

The six, with Ashley's verdict on each:

| Objective | What it does | Her verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Broadest possible audience; the system still optimises for relevance | ⭐ **A favourite.** Tends to be low cost. *"You can't convert unless people know about you"* |
| **Traffic** | Clicks to a website or destination, paid per click | ⚠️ *"Metas algorithm is really good at this one and will definitely get you clicks. But like I said earlier, clicks do not equal sales."* Expensive and rarely aligned to the real outcome |
| **Engagement** | Likes, watches, reads | ⚠️ Not a favourite. *"In all my days at Twitter and Meta, we were never able to prove that there's any correlation between getting a high engagement and actual business outcomes"* |
| **Leads** | Collects information: email, newsletter, test drive | ⚠️ Expensive. **Never run alone — always pair with an awareness campaign** |
| **App promotion** | Drives downloads | ⚠️ Expensive. Same rule: always pair with awareness |
| **Sales** | Lower funnel conversions | ⭐ *"Can be magical, assuming everything is in place"* — see the prerequisites below |

### ⛔ The sales objective has prerequisites

It needs, on the client's side:

- The **Meta pixel** installed
- The **Conversions API (CAPI)**
- The **product catalog** connected, where relevant
- A **full-funnel campaign** running alongside, with awareness and consideration ads

⚠️ **And the trap worth catching before a client wastes budget:**

> "If your client hasn't already made any sales on their website yet, right, careful about using
> this objective until you've made some sales. Since meta's tool optimizes based on past sale
> history, it's critical that there's some sales or the ad system won't ever be able to run this ad
> since it will be lacking the data to optimize."

A brand-new store choosing Sales has picked an objective its own data cannot support.

### 3. Optimisation

Each objective offers optimisation choices that fine-tune what the algorithm maximises. Awareness
can optimise for impressions (possibly showing the same person repeatedly, which is fine). Sales
can optimise toward people the algorithm thinks are most likely to convert.

### 4. Audience

*"One of the biggest client debates you might have during a campaign."* See
`targeting-and-creative.md`.

### 5. Budget

Daily spend and total campaign budget. The system recommends a level, and costs fluctuate with
supply and demand — **Q4 and holiday season are always more expensive.**

## The strategist's job in all this

State the campaign objective in the strategy plan, explicitly, *"to ensure that your media person
and you are going after the same ad objectives."* Most of the failures later in this course trace
back to a mismatch that was settled, wrongly, at this step.
