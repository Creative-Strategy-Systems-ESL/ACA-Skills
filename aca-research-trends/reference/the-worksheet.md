# The Trends Research Worksheet, and the framework behind it

ACA calls it the *"Simplified Trends Research Worksheet"* — a one-pager, deliberately.

> "It's designed to be quick but thorough, helping you extract the creative formula behind trending
> content."

Six blocks: **Trend Identification · Key Elements Analysis (hooks, visuals, timing, audio, text) ·
Adaptation Planning · Brand Application.**

---

## Step 0: Place the trend

TikTok's framework, which Mirella credits to them, describing how trends become *"the language of the
platform"*.

| Level | Duration | What it is | What it justifies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moments** | 1 to 4 weeks | Viral sounds, specific transitions, challenge formats | Act within **48 to 72 hours**. ⚠️ *"I don't advise to invest too heavily in these when thinking about ads.... honestly better for your organic strategy"* |
| **Signals** | 1 to 6 months | *"Patterns that stick around long enough to become recognizable formulas"* | Campaign themes running **1 to 3 months** |
| **Forces** | 6+ months | *"The deep cultural currents... broader themes and values that shape what resonates"* | Overall creative strategy. **Significant production investment** |

**They escalate.** Moments become Signals; Signals become Forces.

> "Forces become 'the language of the platform', and creating content powered by these forces is what
> will make your content feel native."

⭐ **The framework is a budgeting tool, not a taxonomy.** It tells you how much to spend based on how
long the thing will live, and **most trend failures are a level mismatch**: a shoot booked for a
Moment, or a Force dismissed as a fad.

---

## Step 1: Find the trend — IDENTIFYING

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Doom scroll** | *"Yes, I'm actually recommending strategic scrolling."* 20 to 30 minutes a few times a week. **Save anything that catches you immediately** |
| **Search hashtags** | Not the product category. **Lifestyle hashtags, emotion-based terms, problem-focused phrases** |
| **Creative Center** | TikTok and Meta both offer free tools. Filter by industry and country |
| **Engage community** | Ask your audience, or read your comments |

**The worksheet asks for 2 to 3 linked examples.** ⭐ **That number is the point.** One example is a
video; three is a pattern, and only a pattern has a structure you can extract.

## Step 2: Establish relevance — ISOLATING

> "Not every trend will be right for your brand."

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Behavioural** | Does it align with behaviours related to your product? *"Morning routine videos would naturally fit a coffee brand"* |
| **Cultural** | Does it connect to broader values your brand stands for, like sustainability or authenticity? |
| **Psychological** | What deeper triggers does it tap? Humour, nostalgia, FOMO |
| **Emotional** | What feelings does it evoke, and do they match how you want customers to feel? |

⛔ **This is the step people skip, and skipping it is what produces brand-on-a-trend content that
everyone can see is trying too hard.** A trend that passes none of the four is a costume.

⚠️ **Passing one test weakly is not passing.** The honest output of this step is often "no", and a
"no" here has saved more money than any adaptation in the step below.

## Step 3: Choose a narrative — ITERATING

- **What is the trend hook?** A surprising statement, a visual pattern interrupt, a question format,
  a sound
- **Narrative structure** — problem/solution, before/after, Q&A
- **Product placement** — *"Some trends work better with early product reveals, others with a
  surprising reveal at the end"*

## Key Elements Analysis: the five things to break down

The worksheet's core, and what fills the adaptation.

| Element | What to capture |
|---|---|
| **Hooks** | What makes people stop in the first 3 seconds? *"A provocative statement, a sound, a visual surprise, or a certain way of framing"* |
| **Visuals** | Camera angles, lighting, locations, transitions. Highly produced or deliberately casual |
| **Timing and pacing** | How long each section runs, where the cuts and reveals land. *"The rhythm of content is often what makes it feel native."* The worksheet also asks whether there is a **specific comedic timing** that makes it work |
| **Audio strategy** | Sounds, music, voiceover. *"Often the most overlooked element but can dramatically impact performance"* |
| **Text and graphics** | Fonts, animations, placements |

## The trend template: rebuilding it

Four components:

| Component | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The opening statement that catches attention |
| **Deliver** | How it unfolds — the beat drop or transition moment, then the key point |
| **Guide** | The close and what to do next. In an ad, the CTA |
| **Sound** | The audio approach. *"Contrasting sounds that shift from slow to upbeat, matching the before and after dynamic"* |

ACA's worked example is the *"My wife has two husbands"* trend: the hook is the unexpected claim, the
structure holds, the content is replaced.

> "The power of this approach is that you're not just copying a trend — you're understanding its
> underlying structure and adapting it to your message."

⭐ **Sound being a named component is unusual and worth keeping.** Most trend breakdowns treat audio
as decoration. Here it is one of four, and the delivered session gives the cheapest possible test:
*"Oftentimes if we just change a voiceover from a human voiceover to that TikTok robot voice,
sometimes it'll make a difference in how the creative performs."*

## The simplified loop

1. **Identify the pattern** — what structure makes this work?
2. **Extract the formula** — hook style, transition approach, revelation method, closing technique
3. **Adapt to your message** — keep the structure, replace the content
4. **Test and iterate** — multiple variations

## The move worth stealing from ACA's case study

A format where creators show what they ordered online versus what arrived: show the expectation,
build anticipation, reveal what came, react with disappointment.

**Flipped for a beauty brand:** show what they had tried before (a competitor product), same
anticipation build, reveal the client's product, show genuine surprise at how much better it was.

> "This took a negative trend (disappointment) and flipped it to positive (pleasant surprise), while
> maintaining the familiar format."

⭐ **The transferable move is: invert the emotional payload, keep the structure.** A trend built on
disappointment has a ready-made reveal beat, and a brand that can credibly fill that beat with relief
gets the format's attention for free.

⚠️ **ACA reports 42% higher CTR for this campaign.** Unsourced, one unnamed client, and CTR is the
metric furthest from revenue. See `reference/provenance.md`.

## Seven organic formats that became ad standards

Not trends to chase — formats that already completed the Moment-to-Force journey and are now
permanent.

| Format | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Unboxing** | Captures authentic reactions, builds anticipation. Staple for launches |
| **ASMR product demos** | Close-up and sensory. Strong for beauty, food and tactile products. ⭐ *"specifically great for like middle to bottom of the funnel, when your customers are already aware of your brand and product and they just need some final convincing"* |
| **Street interviews** | Feels unscripted and relatable |
| **Answering questions** | Powerful for **addressing objections**. Q&A suits educational content |
| **Comedy skits** | Problem-solution scenarios. *"Humor helps messages stick and encourages sharing."* |
| **Green screen explainers** | *"The visual/verbal combination improves comprehension."* Good for education and comparison |
| **Podcast-style conversations** | Builds credibility through conversation, allows deeper dives |

⭐ **This table doubles as a coverage checklist.** Run it against an existing creative mix: any row
not represented is a format the account has never tested, and unlike a live trend, none of these
will be dead next month.

## Filling the worksheet honestly

**Date it, and name the level.** A trend analysis without a date and a Moment/Signal/Force call is
unreadable later, because the reader cannot tell whether it was ever actionable.

⚠️ **Record what you could not observe.** Whether a trend is actually working, as opposed to being
everywhere, is not visible from the outside — the same limit that applies to competitor ads. Volume
of posts is not evidence of performance.

**If the answer to step 2 is no, that is the deliverable.** Write it up as a no with the reason,
rather than forcing a template nobody should build.
