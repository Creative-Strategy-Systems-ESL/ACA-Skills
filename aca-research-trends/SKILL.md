# Research trends and turn one into an ad

**Ad Creative Academy**, Course 302 (Mirella Crespi), module 4.

## ⛔ Read this before anything else, because it inverts the usual goal

> "the research puzzle that many brands completely miss trends research.The goal here isn't to chase
> every trend **we want to create everygreen ads that can scale.** The goal is to understand the
> underlying patterns of what's working on each platform so your ads feel native and relevant **you
> want to blend in AND stand out.**"

**This is trend research in service of evergreen creative, not trend-jacking.** An answer that helps
someone move faster on a viral sound has inverted the module.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **The trend**, described or linked. 2 to 3 examples of it, because one example is a video and
   three is a pattern
2. **The brand** — what it sells, who buys it, and what it stands for. The relevance test is the
   whole decision and it cannot run without this

**Optional, and each sharpens it:**

3. The platform the ad is destined for
4. The brand's tolerance for lo-fi or unbranded creative, which is often the real constraint
5. How fast production can move, which decides whether a Moment is even reachable
6. Any trend the brand has tried before and how it landed

⚠️ **If no specific trend is supplied**, the job is monitoring rather than adaptation. Use the weekly
system in **The prompts and the weekly workflow** (below) and say that is what you are giving.

⛔ **If the brand is missing, do not skip to the template.** The relevance test in step 2 is the only
thing standing between a trend adaptation and a brand embarrassing itself, and it is the step people
want to skip.

## The decision, before the craft

**First, place the trend**, because the level sets the budget:

| Level | Life | What it justifies |
|---|---|---|
| **Moments** | 1 to 4 weeks | Act within **48 to 72 hours** or not at all. ⚠️ Mirella advises against investing heavily here for **ads** — *"honestly better for your organic strategy"* |
| **Signals** | 1 to 6 months | Campaign themes running 1 to 3 months |
| **Forces** | 6+ months | Overall creative strategy. Worth **significant production investment** |

⭐ **Most trend mistakes are a level mismatch**, not a taste failure: a production budget spent on a
Moment, or a Force treated as a passing fad. Place it first.

**Then test relevance four ways** — behavioural, cultural, psychological, emotional. A trend that
passes none of them is a costume, not a creative opportunity. **The Trends Research Worksheet, and the framework behind it** (below).

**Only then build the template.**

## How to run it

⛔ **The inversion above governs every step.** The goal is deciding whether a trend is worth using, not
finding one to use.

1. **Name the trend and where it was seen.** A trend nobody can point at is a hunch.
2. ⛔ **Place it as a Moment, a Signal or a Force** using **The Trends Research Worksheet, and the framework behind it** (below). The three have
   different shelf lives and different correct responses, and skipping this is how a Moment gets treated as a
   Force.
3. ⛔ **Run the relevance test before the craft**, per the decision section below. ⭐ **A defensible "do not
   use this" is a successful outcome of this skill**, not a failure to produce.
4. **Only if it passes, work the prompts** in **The prompts and the weekly workflow** (below).
5. **Emit the worksheet** from **The Trends Research Worksheet, and the framework behind it** (below), filled, including the verdict and its reasoning.
6. **Rebuild it as a reusable template**, not a one-off ad, which is what makes the research worth the time.
7. **Check the verdict has a reason attached.** "It is trending" is the input, never the conclusion.

## Answering well

**Structure transfers, content does not.** *"You're looking for patterns you can adapt, not specific
content to copy. The goal is to understand **why** something is resonating."*

⭐ **Audio is a first-class component here and almost nowhere else.** ACA's trend template names
**Sound** alongside Hook, Deliver and Guide. From the delivered session: *"Oftentimes if we just
change a voiceover from a human voiceover to that TikTok robot voice, sometimes it'll make a
difference in how the creative performs."* Do not leave the audio row blank.

**Check the shelf life before recommending anything.** A trend is dated the moment it is written
down. Any specific trend named in this skill's material is an example of the method, not a
recommendation — and by the time anyone reads it, most will be dead.

**Native means made for, not resized for.** The three principles Mirella gives for Reels and TikTok:
make it **entertaining**, make it **digestible**, make it **relatable**.

⚠️ **ACA's script and delivery give different triads here.** The written recap says *"Educational,
Entertaining, Aspirational"* and never defines them. The delivered session defines entertaining,
digestible, relatable. **Use the delivered set** — it is what students heard and the only one the
course explains. See **Provenance** (below).

## The prompts and the weekly workflow

ACA ships **"AI Prompts & Workflows For Trends Research"**: five prompts in two groups, plus a
monitoring system.

### Trend Identification prompts

| # | Prompt | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Platform-Specific Trend Analysis** | Reading what is currently moving on a given platform |
| **2** | **Platform-Specific Trend Analysis** (second variant) | ⚠️ ACA gives two prompts under the same heading. They are different prompts; the shared title is theirs |
| **3** | **Trend Lifecycle Assessment** | ⭐ Placing a trend as a Moment, Signal or Force |

⭐ **Prompt 3 is the one that decides the budget**, and therefore the most consequential of the five.
It is the automated version of step 0.

### Trend Analysis & Adaptation prompts

| # | Prompt | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | **Trend Structure Breakdown** | Extracting hook, pacing, audio and text structure from examples |
| **5** | **Brand Adaptation Strategist** | Rebuilding the structure for a specific brand |

---

### ⛔ The reliability problem, which ACA does not raise

**These prompts ask a model about what is trending right now.** That is the single least reliable
thing a model can be asked, for two reasons that compound:

1. **Trends are the fastest-moving information there is.** A Moment lives 1 to 4 weeks. Any model
   answering from training data is describing a dead trend with total confidence.
2. **Trend names are highly generatable.** A plausible-sounding format name, sound and structure can
   be produced for any category on demand, and it will read exactly like a real one.

⛔ **So treat every prompt output as a hypothesis to verify on the platform, never as a finding.**

| Prompt | Reliability | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1 and 2**, identifying current trends | ⚠️ **Lowest.** ⛔ Verify every named trend on the platform before acting | Claims about the world right now |
| **3**, lifecycle assessment | ⚠️ Medium. Sound if you supply real observation dates; guesswork if not | Reasoning over evidence you provide |
| **4**, structure breakdown | ✅ **Highest.** You paste the examples and it analyses them | Works on material you supply |
| **5**, brand adaptation | ✅ Good, and inherits whatever came in from 4 | Generation, not claims about the world |

⭐ **The pattern is the same one that runs through this whole course: prompts that work on material
you supply are reliable, and prompts that make claims about the world are not.** Prompts 4 and 5 are
the useful half.

**How to check a named trend, in about a minute:** search the format or sound on the platform, look at
post dates on the top results, and check whether anything is from the last two weeks. If the recent
results are thin, the trend is over or was never real.

### The weekly trend monitoring system

ACA's three-step system, and the part worth keeping even if the prompts are not used.

#### 1. Set up dedicated research accounts

Separate accounts, per platform, used only for research.

⭐ **This is the highest-value item in the entire module and it costs nothing.** Your personal feed has
been trained on you: it shows a marketer marketer-content, which is the least representative sample
of the customer available. A clean account trained by deliberate engagement on the target audience's
content is a genuine research instrument.

⚠️ **It only works if you feed it deliberately.** An account you scroll idly becomes a second personal
feed within a fortnight.

#### 2. Use AI for initial scanning

Fast, wide, unreliable. Read the reliability table above before trusting anything it names.

#### 3. Weekly analysis with AI

Structured review of what was captured — where prompt 4 does the real work, on examples you saved
yourself.

**Paired with the module's own action steps:**

> "Schedule three 30-minute 'strategic scrolling' sessions this week. Use our simplified trends
> worksheet to analyze what you find. Identify one trend that could be relevant to your brand. Draft
> a quick trend template mapping your brand story to the trend structure."

⭐ **90 minutes a week, and one trend at the end of it.** That ratio is the honest version of trend
research, and it is worth quoting to anyone expecting a pipeline of them.

---

### Running the workflow well

**Save the example, not the description.** Trend content disappears, and a note saying "that transition
one with the beat drop" is unusable in a fortnight.

**Log the date you first saw it.** Prompt 3 cannot place a trend on the Moment/Signal/Force scale
without knowing how long it has been running, and that is the input people fail to supply.

**Most weeks produce nothing.** The system's value is that it is running when something does appear,
not that it yields a trend every Friday. A week with no adaptable trend is a normal week, not a
failed one.

## The Trends Research Worksheet, and the framework behind it

ACA calls it the *"Simplified Trends Research Worksheet"* — a one-pager, deliberately.

> "It's designed to be quick but thorough, helping you extract the creative formula behind trending
> content."

Six blocks: **Trend Identification · Key Elements Analysis (hooks, visuals, timing, audio, text) ·
Adaptation Planning · Brand Application.**

---

### Step 0: Place the trend

TikTok's framework, which Mirella credits to them, describing how trends become *"the language of the
platform"*.

| Level | Duration | What it is | What it justifies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moments** | 1 to 4 weeks | Viral sounds, specific transitions, challenge formats | Act within **48 to 72 hours**. ⚠️ *"I don't advise to invest too heavily in these when thinking about ads.... honestly better for your organic strategy"* |
| **Signals** | 1 to 6 months | *"Patterns that stick around long enough to become recognizable formulas"* | Campaign themes running **1 to 3 months** |
| **Forces** | 6+ months | *"The deep cultural currents... broader themes and values that shape what resonates"* | Overall creative strategy. **Significant production investment** |

**They escalate.** Moments become Signals; Signals become Forces.

> "Forces become 'the language of the platform', and creating content powered by these forces is what
> will make your content feel native."

⭐ **The framework is a budgeting tool, not a taxonomy.** It tells you how much to spend based on how
long the thing will live, and **most trend failures are a level mismatch**: a shoot booked for a
Moment, or a Force dismissed as a fad.

---

### Step 1: Find the trend — IDENTIFYING

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Doom scroll** | *"Yes, I'm actually recommending strategic scrolling."* 20 to 30 minutes a few times a week. **Save anything that catches you immediately** |
| **Search hashtags** | Not the product category. **Lifestyle hashtags, emotion-based terms, problem-focused phrases** |
| **Creative Center** | TikTok and Meta both offer free tools. Filter by industry and country |
| **Engage community** | Ask your audience, or read your comments |

**The worksheet asks for 2 to 3 linked examples.** ⭐ **That number is the point.** One example is a
video; three is a pattern, and only a pattern has a structure you can extract.

### Step 2: Establish relevance — ISOLATING

> "Not every trend will be right for your brand."

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Behavioural** | Does it align with behaviours related to your product? *"Morning routine videos would naturally fit a coffee brand"* |
| **Cultural** | Does it connect to broader values your brand stands for, like sustainability or authenticity? |
| **Psychological** | What deeper triggers does it tap? Humour, nostalgia, FOMO |
| **Emotional** | What feelings does it evoke, and do they match how you want customers to feel? |

⛔ **This is the step people skip, and skipping it is what produces brand-on-a-trend content that
everyone can see is trying too hard.** A trend that passes none of the four is a costume.

⚠️ **Passing one test weakly is not passing.** The honest output of this step is often "no", and a
"no" here has saved more money than any adaptation in the step below.

### Step 3: Choose a narrative — ITERATING

- **What is the trend hook?** A surprising statement, a visual pattern interrupt, a question format,
  a sound
- **Narrative structure** — problem/solution, before/after, Q&A
- **Product placement** — *"Some trends work better with early product reveals, others with a
  surprising reveal at the end"*

---

### Key Elements Analysis: the five things to break down

The worksheet's core, and what fills the adaptation.

| Element | What to capture |
|---|---|
| **Hooks** | What makes people stop in the first 3 seconds? *"A provocative statement, a sound, a visual surprise, or a certain way of framing"* |
| **Visuals** | Camera angles, lighting, locations, transitions. Highly produced or deliberately casual |
| **Timing and pacing** | How long each section runs, where the cuts and reveals land. *"The rhythm of content is often what makes it feel native."* The worksheet also asks whether there is a **specific comedic timing** that makes it work |
| **Audio strategy** | Sounds, music, voiceover. *"Often the most overlooked element but can dramatically impact performance"* |
| **Text and graphics** | Fonts, animations, placements |

---

### The trend template: rebuilding it

Four components:

| Component | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The opening statement that catches attention |
| **Deliver** | How it unfolds — the beat drop or transition moment, then the key point |
| **Guide** | The close and what to do next. In an ad, the CTA |
| **Sound** | The audio approach. *"Contrasting sounds that shift from slow to upbeat, matching the before and after dynamic"* |

ACA's worked example is the *"My wife has two husbands"* trend: the hook is the unexpected claim, the
structure holds, the content is replaced.

> "The power of this approach is that you're not just copying a trend — you're understanding its
> underlying structure and adapting it to your message."

⭐ **Sound being a named component is unusual and worth keeping.** Most trend breakdowns treat audio
as decoration. Here it is one of four, and the delivered session gives the cheapest possible test:
*"Oftentimes if we just change a voiceover from a human voiceover to that TikTok robot voice,
sometimes it'll make a difference in how the creative performs."*

### The simplified loop

1. **Identify the pattern** — what structure makes this work?
2. **Extract the formula** — hook style, transition approach, revelation method, closing technique
3. **Adapt to your message** — keep the structure, replace the content
4. **Test and iterate** — multiple variations

---

### The move worth stealing from ACA's case study

A format where creators show what they ordered online versus what arrived: show the expectation,
build anticipation, reveal what came, react with disappointment.

**Flipped for a beauty brand:** show what they had tried before (a competitor product), same
anticipation build, reveal the client's product, show genuine surprise at how much better it was.

> "This took a negative trend (disappointment) and flipped it to positive (pleasant surprise), while
> maintaining the familiar format."

⭐ **The transferable move is: invert the emotional payload, keep the structure.** A trend built on
disappointment has a ready-made reveal beat, and a brand that can credibly fill that beat with relief
gets the format's attention for free.

⚠️ **ACA reports 42% higher CTR for this campaign.** Unsourced, one unnamed client, and CTR is the
metric furthest from revenue. See **Provenance** (below).

---

### Seven organic formats that became ad standards

Not trends to chase — formats that already completed the Moment-to-Force journey and are now
permanent.

| Format | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Unboxing** | Captures authentic reactions, builds anticipation. Staple for launches |
| **ASMR product demos** | Close-up and sensory. Strong for beauty, food and tactile products. ⭐ *"specifically great for like middle to bottom of the funnel, when your customers are already aware of your brand and product and they just need some final convincing"* |
| **Street interviews** | Feels unscripted and relatable |
| **Answering questions** | Powerful for **addressing objections**. Q&A suits educational content |
| **Comedy skits** | Problem-solution scenarios. *"Humor helps messages stick and encourages sharing."* |
| **Green screen explainers** | *"The visual/verbal combination improves comprehension."* Good for education and comparison |
| **Podcast-style conversations** | Builds credibility through conversation, allows deeper dives |

⭐ **This table doubles as a coverage checklist.** Run it against an existing creative mix: any row
not represented is a format the account has never tested, and unlike a live trend, none of these
will be dead next month.

### Filling the worksheet honestly

**Date it, and name the level.** A trend analysis without a date and a Moment/Signal/Force call is
unreadable later, because the reader cannot tell whether it was ever actionable.

⚠️ **Record what you could not observe.** Whether a trend is actually working, as opposed to being
everywhere, is not visible from the outside — the same limit that applies to competitor ads. Volume
of posts is not evidence of performance.

**If the answer to step 2 is no, that is the deliverable.** Write it up as a no with the reason,
rather than forcing a template nobody should build.
