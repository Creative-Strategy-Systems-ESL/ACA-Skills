# Course 704: The Growth Creative Operating System

**Mirella Crespi**, founder of Creative Milkshake. 38m 58s, 1 module.

> "I'm going to take you behind the scenes to show you how we produce **over 3000 ads every month**
> for brands like Canva, American Express, Vinted and Spoiled Child."

> "I chose the word **operating system** very intentionally, because what I'm about to share isn't a
> workflow. It's not a template. **It really is an operating system,** a complete framework for how
> creatives should function in modern performance marketing."

⚠️ **This is the most consequential course in the programme to get wrong**, because it rests on a
claim about how Meta's algorithm now works. Read `reference/provenance.md` before repeating any of
it.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what Andromeda changed, what a micro persona is, or
why diversity beats volume, and get an answer.

**To build micro personas or a research plan, required:**

1. **The product**, and the category
2. **Access to real customer language** — reviews, forum threads, comments, support tickets. ⛔ The
   whole method runs on verbatim quotes; without them there are no personas, only guesses

**Optional, and each one sharpens it:**

3. Competitors worth analysing
4. What is currently running and where it has plateaued
5. Existing personas, however broad
6. Whether creators are available to cast against personas

⛔ **Never write a customer quote you did not find.** This method's entire output is grounded in
*"the exact language your audience already uses"*, and a fabricated quote inherits that authority
while destroying it.

⛔ **Never state Andromeda's mechanics as documented fact.** Mirella says *"here's what we know so
far"* — this is a practitioner's reading of an algorithm change, not published specification.

## The through-line

> "**It's not about making 100 variations of the same ad. It's about making fundamentally different
> ads for fundamentally different people.**"

⭐ **That sentence separates this course from every other volume argument in the programme.** Volume
without diversity is what triggers the penalty she describes; volume built on distinct personas is
what escapes it.

## How to work through it

**Andromeda first.** The volume and diversity argument sounds like a preference until someone understands what
changed on the platform.

1. **Explain Andromeda** from `reference/andromeda.md` — sequence learning, the hierarchical ad index, and the
   grouping penalty that punishes ads which look alike. ⭐ This is what makes diversity a survival requirement
   rather than a nice-to-have.
2. **Give the three research pillars** in `reference/the-three-pillars.md`: AI-assisted competitor analysis,
   micro personas mined from real customer language, and platform trends read through four lenses.
3. **Cover the burner-account technique**, same file, which is the most concrete thing in the course — seeing a
   persona's actual feed rather than imagining it.
4. **Then casting**, still in that file: creators who genuinely embody each persona rather than fit a brief.
5. ⚠️ **Note what a concept means here.** `reference/the-three-pillars.md` defines it as three elements;
   Course 401 defines it in four. Say so rather than blending them.
6. ⭐ **For the artifacts, route out**: personas to `aca-build-audience-personas`, competitor work to
   `aca-analyze-competitor-creative`, volume to `aca-plan-creative-volume` or Course 703. ⚠️ Name one only if
   you can see it installed.
7. **Check they can say why two similar ads now cost more than two different ones.** That is the Andromeda
   argument, and it is the thing this course exists to land.

## Answering well

**The plateau has a named cause.** *"Your top performing ads suddenly hit a ceiling. Your CPMs are so
much higher and your ROAS started declining."* Her explanation is Meta's algorithm change, and it
reframes a creative problem as a systems one.

**Sameness is now actively penalised.** ⛔ *"If all your ads look the same, talk the same, and hook
the same, Meta groups them together. You get diminishing returns. You hit creative fatigue faster,
and your reach plateaus."*

**Macro personas are the failure.** *"Sarah, 35, works in marketing, lives in Austin and likes yoga.
**That's not really an effective persona.**"* Micro personas are defined by a pain point and a
posture, not a demographic.

**AI does the lifting, the strategist does the thinking.** ⭐ Said twice, and it is the course's
position on the whole toolchain: *"The AI scans, organizes, identifies patterns and suggests
directions, but the strategist will evaluate, prioritize, connect the dots across different data
sources, and make the final creative decision."*

⚠️ **Casting is part of the system, not a downstream task.** *"Match each persona with a content
creator that embodies that persona... **Authenticity is a non-negotiable here.**"*

## ⚠️ Scope, and the skills either side

It does not produce the personas as an artifact — `aca-build-audience-personas` runs Course 302's
copy-mining method and emits them. It does not brief or storyboard. It does not set volume, which is
Course 703 and Course 601.

⚠️ **Its definition of a concept is not Course 401's.** This course calls a concept *"the strategic
intersection of three elements"* — offer, angle, audience. Course 401's Creative Strategy Map has
**four**: persona, motivator, benefit, offer. See `reference/provenance.md`.
