# Awareness and market sophistication

The first layer of the stack, and the one that constrains everything after it. Awareness decides
what a message can attempt; sophistication decides whether the way you say it will still land.

## The 5 stages of market awareness

Mirella maps these to funnel position, which is the part people usually drop and the part that
makes them actionable.

### TOP OF FUNNEL

**1. Unaware** — they do not even realise they have a problem or a need.

Tactical approach:
- Focus on raising awareness of the problem itself
- Use pattern interrupts and curiosity triggers
- Lead with stories that illuminate an unrecognised pain point

> Example: *"The hidden reason you're exhausted even after 8 hours of sleep"*

**2. Problem Aware** — they recognise the problem but are not sure how to solve it.

Tactical approach:
- Validate their experience: "yes, this is real, and you're not alone"
- Educate about the problem's true nature and implications
- Introduce broader solution categories **without pitching your specific product yet**

### MIDDLE OF FUNNEL

**3. Solution Aware** — they know solutions exist, but not yours specifically.

Tactical approach:
- Differentiate your approach from the alternatives
- Focus on unique mechanisms or methodologies
- Highlight what makes your solution category superior

> Example: *"Why natural supplements outperform synthetic options for sustainable energy"*

**4. Product Aware** — they know your product but have not decided it is right for them.

Tactical approach:
- Address specific objections
- Provide detailed proof: studies, testimonials, demonstrations
- Compare directly to the alternatives they are considering

### BOTTOM OF FUNNEL

**5. Most Aware** — they are ready to buy with the right offer.

Tactical approach:
- Focus on urgency, scarcity, or special offers
- Reduce friction to purchase
- Reinforce decision confidence with guarantees or bonuses

## Using the stages

The worksheet's Section 1 asks you to place your audience on this spectrum and then derive the
messaging approach from that placement. The condensed version, as the worksheet states it:

| Stage | Focus on |
|---|---|
| Unaware | Problem identification, disruption, curiosity |
| Problem Aware | Problem validation, education, hope |
| Solution Aware | Differentiation, unique mechanism, comparison |
| Product Aware | Objection handling, proof, specific benefits |
| Most Aware | Offers, urgency, easy action steps |

**The most common failure this framework catches** is a mismatch: differentiation copy aimed at
someone who does not yet know they have the problem, or problem-education copy aimed at someone
already comparing two brands. Both read as competent writing and both underperform, because the
message is answering a question the reader has not asked yet or has already moved past.

**A second thing worth noticing.** A single audience is rarely at one stage. Broad targeting means
one ad set can contain people at all five. This is part of why a portfolio of creative
outperforms one perfect ad: different creatives catch people at different stages.

## Market sophistication

Awareness is about the *person*. Sophistication is about the *market*: how many times this
audience has already heard a claim like yours.

As a market matures, straightforward claims stop working, not because they became untrue but
because they became familiar. Everyone in the category is making them, so they no longer carry
information.

Mirella names two escapes:

1. **Present a unique mechanism.** *"How our proprietary technology works differently."* You are
   not making a bigger claim, you are explaining a *different reason* the claim is true. The
   mechanism is what makes a tired promise credible again.
2. **Reframe the problem entirely.** *"You've been solving the wrong problem all along."* This
   moves the conversation rather than competing inside it, and it can reset an audience's
   awareness stage in the process.

The AI prompt for this (Prompt 3, Market Sophistication Analysis) invokes **Eugene Schwartz's 5
levels of market sophistication** and asks which claims are becoming cliché, which is the
diagnostic question. If every competitor ad in the category makes the same promise, the promise
is spent, and volume will not revive it.

## How the two interact

Awareness tells you **what to say**. Sophistication tells you **how hard you have to work to be
believed when you say it**.

A problem-aware audience in an unsophisticated market can be reached with a plain statement of the
problem. The same audience in a saturated market has heard that statement a hundred times, and
needs either a mechanism or a reframe before the same content will register at all.
