# Course 102: State of Paid Social Advertising & Consumer Psychology

Mirella Crespi (Creative Milkshake), 27m 13s, 2 modules: The New Paid Social Reality, and
Data-Driven Creative Strategy.

This is the course that explains **why the strategist role exists** and then hands over the
psychology used to decide what an ad says. It ends in an artifact: a filled Consumer Psychology
Worksheet.

## What this skill needs from you

This skill teaches first and asks second. If the question is "how do awareness stages work",
answer it, no inputs needed. The inputs below are for **producing the worksheet**.

**Required, to produce the worksheet:**

1. **The product or service**, and what it actually does
2. **Who it is for**
3. **What is known about them** — research, reviews, support tickets, sales calls, or an honest
   best read

**Optional, and it sharpens the artifact:**

4. Current messaging or existing ads
5. Price point and the offer
6. What competitors are saying

⚠️ **If a required input is missing, ask for it.** Do not invent an audience or a pain point. A
plausible invented input produces a plausible wrong worksheet, and nobody downstream can tell it
was fabricated. An honest "we're guessing here" from the user is a usable input, because it travels
with a warning label. A guess the skill made silently does not.

### Three states, and the middle one is the trap

**1. They supplied the research.** Work from it. Quote it.

**2. They NAMED a source but did not supply it** — "we have 400 Amazon reviews", "there's a bunch
of Instagram comments", "sales talks to these people daily". ⚠️ **This is not the same as having
the research, and it is the easiest case to get wrong**, because the sentence sounds like evidence
was provided. Nothing has been provided. Ask for a bounded sample rather than the whole corpus:

> Paste 20 to 30 review lines, weighted towards the critical ones and the ones that repeat, plus any
> themes you have already noticed. That is enough to ground Sections 1 and 2.

Asking for all 400 gets refused; asking for a specific, achievable slice gets an answer. Skewing
the request towards negative and repeated reviews is deliberate: complaints name the pain point in
the customer's own words, which is exactly what Section 2 needs.

**3. They genuinely have no research.** Say what would be needed, then offer to proceed on
assumptions **explicitly marked as assumptions in the worksheet itself**.

### What may be reasoned and what must be supplied

The line worth holding, because it decides whether a flagged draft is useful or misleading:

| Fair to reason towards, flagged | Must come from the user |
|---|---|
| How this product category generally behaves | What **this** brand's customers actually say |
| Which awareness stage a category typically sits at | Any specific number, claim, timeframe or result |
| Which emotional drivers a category usually touches | Testimonial content, review quotes, named benefits |
| Structural advice: which angle suits which stage | Anything a competitor could sue over or a customer could disprove |

General market reasoning is a legitimate starting draft. **Brand-specific invention is not**, even
flagged, because a fabricated quote or an invented "3x faster" survives copy-paste long after the
flag falls off.

## Why this course exists

Before the 2020-2021 privacy changes, targeting was the lever. Rich third-party pixel data made
granular lookalikes, exclusions and time-windowed retargeting possible, and the algorithm put the
ad in front of the right person. In Mirella's words, you did not even need great ads.

When that data went away, targeting went broad and the algorithm began reading first-party in-app
signals instead: whether someone stops scrolling, watches, engages, shares, clicks. Those signals
are produced by the creative.

> "Creative has become your targeting."

That is the whole argument, and everything downstream follows from it. If the creative is the
targeting mechanism, then deciding what the creative *says* is the highest-leverage decision
available, which is what the rest of this course equips.

## The psychology stack

Four layers, and they are meant to be used in order. Each has its own reference file.

| Layer | Question it answers | File |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness and sophistication** | How much does this person already know, and how tired is this market of hearing about it? | `reference/awareness-and-sophistication.md` |
| **Emotional drivers** | What do they actually want, underneath the purchase? | `reference/emotional-drivers.md` |
| **Persuasion principles** | What makes them act on it? | `reference/persuasion-principles.md` |
| **Repetition and sequencing** | What happens across the second, third and fourth time they see us? | `reference/repetition-and-sequencing.md` |

Awareness comes first for a reason: it determines what the message can even attempt. A
differentiation message aimed at someone who does not yet know they have the problem is wasted,
however well written.

Those four layers establish what is **true** about the audience. Turning that into what actually
gets **said**, and then checking it, is `reference/framing-and-hooks.md`, which covers the
worksheet's remaining three sections: the message framing matrix, hook development, and the
evaluation checklist, then four ready-made formulas. Every one of the 7 fillable sections has a
methodology behind it; nothing is left to
improvisation halfway through the artifact.

⚠️ **On the persuasion principles: the course teaches 14, the worksheet lists 10.** Work from the
14. `reference/persuasion-principles.md` says which four the worksheet grid omits, so someone
working from the printed worksheet alone can see what is missing.

## Producing the worksheet

`reference/worksheet.md` holds all 8 sections with every field, plus Mirella's two worked examples
so the shape of a good answer is visible.

Work it in order, because later sections depend on earlier ones: the hooks in Section 5 are
supposed to follow from the awareness assessment in Section 1 and the emotional drivers in Section
2. Filling Section 5 first produces hooks that sound fine and are aimed at nobody.

Two habits that make the output usable rather than decorative:

- **Quote the customer's own language** wherever the research contains it. Section 2 asks what
  customers want to stop feeling and start feeling, and their words are better than your
  paraphrase of their words.
- **Mark assumptions as assumptions.** A worksheet where three sections are grounded in reviews
  and one is guessed is genuinely useful. One where the reader cannot tell which is which is not.

## The AI prompts

`reference/ai-prompts.md` carries all 20 prompts ACA ships with this course, verbatim, with their
`[BRACKETED]` placeholders intact because those are the interface. They are organised by job:
audience awareness, emotional drivers and hooks, persuasion principles, ad script development,
campaign planning and sequencing, and audit and variation.

Use them as written, or run the equivalent work directly. The prompts encode the same method as
the worksheet, so a strategist who has the frameworks does not strictly need them, but they are
what ACA hands students and they are faster.

## How to run it

**Psychology before wording.** Someone asking what an ad should say usually wants a line; the course's answer
is that the line falls out of five decisions made first.

1. **Place the buyer on the awareness spectrum** using `reference/awareness-and-sophistication.md`, and check
   market sophistication in the same file. Getting this wrong makes every later choice wrong politely.
2. **Pick the emotional driver** from `reference/emotional-drivers.md` — one, named, not a list.
3. **Choose the persuasion principles** that fit that driver, from
   `reference/persuasion-principles.md`. ⚠️ Two or three, not fourteen.
4. **Decide the framing and the hook** with `reference/framing-and-hooks.md`.
5. **Sequence it across exposures** using `reference/repetition-and-sequencing.md`, because a first-touch
   message and a fifth-touch message are not the same message.
6. ⭐ **Produce the worksheet** from `reference/worksheet.md`, all sections, using the decisions above as its
   inputs. See the producing section below.
7. **Check every section traces to research**, not to a guess. An invented audience insight is the failure
   mode here, and it survives all the way into the ad.

## Answering well

The frameworks are only worth anything applied. "Your audience is problem aware" is not an
insight; "your audience is problem aware, which is why the current ad's differentiation message
lands flat, and here is what a problem-validation hook looks like for this product" is.

When someone brings a specific ad or a specific brand, run the stack against it rather than
describing the stack. When they bring a question about the method itself, teach the method.

Where the research does not exist to answer a section honestly, say so. The worksheet's value
comes from being grounded, and a fully-filled worksheet built on invention is worse than a
half-filled one that shows its gaps.
