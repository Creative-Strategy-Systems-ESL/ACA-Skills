# Course 403: Storyboarding — Bringing Concepts to Life

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 13m 26s, 1 module.

> ""Welcome to where the rubber meets the road in our creative journey scriptwriting and
> storyboarding. This is the bridge between brilliant ideas and actual ads that perform."

The strategic work is done: audience identified, concepts developed, ad types selected, stakeholders
signed off. This course turns that into something a production team can shoot.

⭐ **This skill emits the storyboard.** ACA ships an eight-sheet working template and the point of
this skill is to fill it, in its real columns, so the output pastes straight into their workbook.
Describing what a storyboard contains is not the deliverable.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what goes in a storyboard, or which of the two
approaches suits a job, and get an answer.

**To produce a filled storyboard, required:**

1. **The concept** — what the ad is, in a sentence
2. **The product or service** being featured
3. **The brand**, and whether creative should look **branded or native**

**Optional, and each fills real columns rather than being nice to have:**

4. Talent — who is on screen, and any casting constraints
5. Platform and placement, which sets the safe space
6. Ad type, if the brand has an established library of them
7. Disclaimers or legal requirements
8. Props, outfit, set and location constraints, or the budget that bounds them
9. Reference ads, which fill the Ad References and visual-direction columns

⚠️ **A storyboard with only the first three is still shootable, and thinner.** Fill what you have,
leave ACA's own field names in place for the rest, and say which fields are open. **Do not invent
talent, props or a location** — those are production commitments with money attached, and a
plausible invention gets bought.

⛔ **If the concept has not been approved yet, say so before storyboarding it.** This course sits
*after* sign-off in ACA's sequence. Storyboarding an unapproved concept is how a team ends up
shooting something the client had not agreed to.

## First decide which document you are making

> "Neither approach is inherently better. They serve different needs. The storyboard gives you
> precision, while the production brief allows for creative collaboration."

| **Full storyboard** when | **Creative production brief** when |
|---|---|
| Shooting in-house with your own production team | Collaborating with creators or influencers who add their own style |
| Working with external creators but needing precise control | You want the creator's authentic voice and personality |
| The brand needs multiple approval layers, legal and brand teams | You care more about the strategic concept than exact script execution |
| The visual concept is complex enough to need documentation | The content benefits from spontaneity |

⭐ **Getting this wrong is the expensive mistake.** A full storyboard handed to a creator hired for
their voice buys you a stilted read of your own script. A loose brief handed to an in-house shoot
leaves the crew inventing on the day.

`reference/the-storyboard.md` builds the first. `reference/briefing-a-creator.md` builds the second.

## How to run it

**Decide which document you are making first**, per the section above — a full storyboard or a creative
production brief. They have different owners and different fields.

1. **Confirm the concept is approved.** ⛔ Storyboarding an unapproved concept is the most common way this
   work gets thrown away.
2. **Pick the document** using the section above.
3. **Work through the sheets in order** with `reference/the-storyboard.md`, starting at Overview. ⭐ The
   Overview's props and outfits column is the shoot-day shopping list and only exists if every concept row
   is filled.
4. **Write the scripts** using `reference/scripts.md`, and run each through the four questions every script
   has to pass.
5. ⭐ **Emit the sheets as tables with ACA's exact column headers**, per the instruction in
   `reference/the-storyboard.md`. ⛔ Prose describing what a storyboard contains is not the deliverable.
6. ⚠️ **Do not reproduce the `#REF!` errors** in rows C2 to C8 of ACA's shipped file. Emit clean empty rows.
7. **Hand it over.** If step 2 chose the creator route, `reference/briefing-a-creator.md` is the document —
   a different one, not a lesser one. If it chose the full storyboard, the Overview's props and outfits
   column is what production works from.
8. **Check every shot has a shot type and a reason.** A storyboard a production team has to interpret is a
   storyboard that will be interpreted wrongly.

## Answering well

**The building blocks are the spine, not decoration.** ACA's script column is built on them: Hook,
Body, CTA in one column, and the specific block in the next. A storyboard whose Strategy column is
blank has skipped the part that makes the ad testable.

**Shot type and visual direction are for the person holding the camera.** *"Camera angles,
movements, transitions, and visual references."* "Nice shot of the product" is not a direction.

**Supers are a separate column for a reason.** On-screen text is written, timed and approved
separately from what is said, and most platforms are watched muted.

⚠️ **Write for a Grade 5 reading level.** Mirella's own instruction, and she names the tool:
*"run all your scripts through [the Hemingway App] and aim for a Grade 5 reading level or lower...
The simpler your language, the better your ads will perform."*

## ⚠️ Scope

One module and about 13 minutes. It is the **document**, not the craft of writing or the shoot.

It does not cover: how to develop the concept (that is Course 401), casting or directing talent,
production logistics, editing, or media buying. It names no benchmark numbers.
