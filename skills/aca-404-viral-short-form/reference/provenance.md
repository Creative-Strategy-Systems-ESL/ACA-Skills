## ⚠️ Seven recordings, not six

The delivered file carries an **Intro** plus six numbered modules, **each restarting its own

⚠️ **The file also mixes timecode separators**: the Intro uses `HH:MM:SS:FF` with colons, all six

## ⚠️ Luke's own numbers, and every other figure

**Every number in this course is his, and none is sourced.** The two academic citations are named but
not referenced. Carry all of them as his claims.

| Claim | As given |
|---|---|
| **His own record** | 2.5 billion views · 7,000+ videos · 2 million followers |
| His client results | Alex to 50,000 followers · AD World to 276,000 · ClickUp to a 10 million view video |
| The 70k case | A client gained **70,000 followers overnight** from one video |
| Completion rates | Under 15 seconds: **76%**. Over 30 seconds: **42%** |
| Scroll volume | *"A football field. 300 feet of content"* a day, and *"half a second to make somebody stop"* |
| Attention | Gen Z toggles apps **every 44 seconds** |
| The algorithm | *"Doesn't even look at your page until you have at least 100 posts usually"* |
| Visual processing | The brain processes visuals **60,000 times faster than text**; **90%** of information transmitted to the brain is visual |
| **Jonah Berger** | Studied **7,000 articles**; high-arousal emotions are significantly more viral than low-arousal |
| **Paul Zak** | Vulnerable stories release oxytocin, *"the trust chemical"* |
| The eBay experiment | **$129** of trinkets, personal stories written for each, sold for **over $8,000** |
| Neuroscience of story | Facts activate two brain regions; a story lights the whole brain and releases dopamine, oxytocin and endorphins |
| Short-form consumption | *"Reduces prefrontal cortex responses"* |

⚠️ **The 60,000-times-faster claim is widely circulated and widely disputed**, and it has no
identifiable source in the research literature. It is quoted here because he says it; it should not
be repeated as established fact.

⛔ **The completion-rate figures come with no platform, no sample and no date**, which is the pair
most likely to be quoted as a benchmark. Use them as an argument for shorter video, not as a target.

## ⛔ This is an organic course and the system does not transfer to paid

Every framework is tuned for a feed that rewards watch time and follows. The 60/30/10 mix, the
cadence ladder and the 90-day commitment assume a posting volume nobody buys media against.

**What transfers to paid:** the hook types, the three pattern interrupts, the advanced hook formulas,
the story frameworks, and the rule against promising what the body does not deliver.

**What does not:** cadence, the content mix, the pillar structure, and the follower goals.

This skill says so up front because the surrounding programme is a paid-social certification, and
someone arriving from a paid problem will otherwise apply an organic answer to it.

## ⚠️ The three pillars here are not Course 302's

This planner's pillars are **Educational / Entertainment / Behind-the-Scenes**, each labelled with
the metric it moves (saves / shares / follows).

ACA's Course 302 script separately mentions *"the 3 pillars — Educational, Entertaining,
Aspirational"* in an on-screen recap, and **never defines them anywhere.** Different author,
different purpose, and only one word in common. **Not merged.**

## What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Luke's, quoted directly.** **Quotes are verbatim.** This is spoken delivery, so they carry it as spoken — filler, false starts, mid-sentence restarts and the transcript's own slips. Nothing is smoothed, because a tidied quotation is no longer evidence of what was taught. The artefacts below are preserved where quoted and flagged, not corrected.

**Sheet names, column headers and the pillar labels are reproduced verbatim from the workbook**,
because they are the join between this skill's output and ACA's file.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the four-file split, the routing table, grouping the six
modules under Luke's own three pillars, and rendering each planner sheet as a fillable table.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The organic-versus-paid warning**, and the split of what transfers and what does not. The
  course never addresses it, and it sits inside a paid-social certification.
- ⛔ **The instruction to pick the cadence you can hold on a bad week**, since a rung chosen
  optimistically fails in week three and quitting at week three is exactly what the 90-day rule
  exists to prevent.
- ⭐ **Reading the Publisher role as closing a loop** — posting as the input to the next cycle rather
  than the end of the pipeline.
- ⭐ **The observation that each pillar is labelled with the metric it moves**, and that a channel
  weak on one metric usually has an underfed pillar.
- ⭐ **Naming "Format to KILL" and "Surprise Winner" as the fields nobody fills**, and what each
  measures.
- ⭐ **The point that the bottleneck question maps onto the five roles**, so the answer names the
  first hire.
- **The reading that a rising hook rate with a falling completion rate means the body is the
  problem.**
- **The note that filming the ten hooks is the part that teaches**, because a hook that dies out loud
  is one you would otherwise have shot.
- **The point that "tension, the gap between what is and what could be" is a better test than the
  framework names**, and can be applied to any script.
- ⚠️ **The caution that "bold" is not "risky to the brand"**, and that anger as a high-arousal
  emotion is an observation about virality rather than a recommendation.
- ⚠️ **The caution that vulnerability only works if the admission is real**, and that a manufactured
  one is the same trust failure as clickbait.
- ⚠️ **The note that two calls to action is zero.**
- **The instruction not to invent view and follower goals**, because a number you supply becomes one
  the client is measured against.
- **The note that a thin content pillar is a finding rather than something to pad.**

## ⚠️ How old this material is
