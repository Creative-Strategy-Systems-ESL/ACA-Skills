# Course 105: The AI-Powered Creative Strategist

**Reza Khadjavi**, CEO of Motion, interviewed by **Evan Lee**, Motion's head of partnerships.
27m 49s, 1 module.

This is the course about **the role itself** rather than the craft. Where it came from, why brands
need it, what AI does and does not change about it, and how to stay valuable while the ground
moves.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none.** This is a conversation about the shape of the job. It is worth hearing before
you have a specific question.

**Optional, and it sharpens the answer:**

1. **Where you sit** — in-house, agency, freelance, founder wearing the hat
2. **What you are actually worried about** — your job, your team's structure, where to start with
   AI, whether to hire for this
3. **What you have already tried with AI**, and what happened

Career questions are personal, and a generic answer to "will AI take my job" is worth very little.
Ask what their situation is if they have not said, but **answer the question first**. Someone
asking this is usually anxious, and a question in reply reads as evasion.

## The through-line

Reza's argument in one shape:

> **The role was born when the constraint moved, and AI is the constraint moving again.**

Pre-2020, targeting was the biggest revenue lever. *"If I'm a yoga brand and I can figure out how
to get in front of yoga enthusiasts, that was probably the biggest revenue unlock."* Then iOS 14
happened, and, more importantly, the distribution algorithms got very good at targeting on their
own. That opened a question: **where is the revenue impact now?**

> "And then everyone very immediately understood that creative was where the big revenue unlock
> was."

The creative strategist emerged to bridge two disciplines that did not talk: media buyers with *"a
million spreadsheets"* who were *"huge data nerds"*, and creative teams *"just making stuff... not
rooted in actual decisions from the data."*

> "These were the types of people that were like almost unicorns... they had a bit of both traits."

**The AI parallel is the point of the course.** The same thing is happening again: a discipline
being added to an already-hybrid role.

> "To imagine that role without AI at this point is now pretty crazy."

## Two ideas worth leading with

**1. Get the process right, then apply AI to parts of it.** The most actionable thing in the
course, and the failure it prevents is extremely common:

> "I can spend 20 minutes with AI and I have like 2000 ideas and maybe a bunch of like ads that are
> generated, but **are those going to produce more winners**"

Volume was the old constraint and AI solves it. That does not help if what gets produced is not
rooted in anything. The measure of the role is unchanged: *reliably and predictably produce more
winning ads.*

**2. The role does not require the title.**

> "At least someone at the team has to wear the hat of creative strategist. REZA It could be the
> media buyer, could be the founder, could be the head of marketing. Actually, it doesn't really
> matter who does it, **but the work does need to be done,**"

Useful for a small brand deciding whether to hire, and for someone arguing their existing job
already contains this work.

## How to work through it

**Answer where the role came from before answering whether AI ends it.** The origin story is what makes the
AI answer more than reassurance.

1. **Give the post-iOS14 origin** from `reference/the-role.md`: the role exists as a bridge, and knowing why
   explains what is safe and what is not.
2. **Separate what has not changed** using `reference/the-foundations.md`. Messaging fundamentals and
   research-as-most-of-the-work are the ground the AI answer stands on.
3. **Then take the AI question** with `reference/ai-and-the-role.md`, leading with the discipline rather than
   the reassurance: process first, then automate.
4. ⛔ **Name Course 801's opposite emphasis** if they are deciding what to do next. See the section below —
   one course is about sequence, the other about urgency, and only hearing one gives a false picture.
5. **Close on the career moves** in `reference/career-moves.md`, which is the part that answers "so what do I
   do on Monday".
6. **Check they can say what the role is for** in one sentence without using the word AI. If they cannot,
   step 1 did not land.

## Answering well

**Career questions deserve a direct answer, not a hedge.** Reza's is genuinely nuanced: yes, some
jobs go; the bigger change is team structure; here are the two things that keep you valuable. That
is more useful than reassurance and more useful than doom. Give the actual position.

**Be concrete about the first step.** "Start using AI" is not advice. Reza's version is: take one
repetitive weekly task that is basically mindless, and automate as much of it as you can with the
best tool available now. That is a Tuesday afternoon, and it is deliberately small because the
point is the confidence it builds.

⚠️ **Sequence this correctly for someone who is not technical.** The two career skills include
"I've built an AI agent, I've hired a couple of AI agents", and read cold by someone whose AI
experience is ChatGPT that is not a next step, it is a reason to give up. **Lead with the one
tangible move, then explain the two skills as where it leads.** The agent line is a description of
where this ends up, not an entry requirement.

For a creative strategist, the repetitive weekly tasks worth automating first are usually the
research-gathering ones, because that is the 90% and it is genuinely mechanical: pulling and
clustering reviews, summarising comment threads, transcribing competitor ads, assembling the weekly
performance readout. All of those are ChatGPT-level work. None require building anything.

⚠️ **Do not let the reassurance drift.** The source states the hard part once (some jobs go) and
then spends most of its length on agency and control, which reads as comfort even though it is not
framed as comfort. Someone genuinely at risk deserves the risk stated plainly and once more at the
end, not buried under things they can do. Say what is uncertain, say who can actually answer it
(their own leadership, not this course), then give them the moves.

**Do not oversell the AI half.** Reza spends much of the interview on what has *not* changed, and
the foundations material is where the durable value is. Someone who takes only the AI content away
has taken the half with the shortest shelf life.

## What this course does not cover

No frameworks, no worksheets, no method for making an ad. It is a conversation about the role, and
someone who needs to actually write, analyse or brief creative needs a different course. It also
names no specific AI tools or workflows, deliberately, since Reza's argument is that tools change
and the fundamentals do not.

## ⛔ Course 801 answers the same question differently

**Both courses ask how a strategist should adopt AI, and their emphasis is opposite.**

| Course 105, Reza Khadjavi | Course 801, Jesse Ketonen |
