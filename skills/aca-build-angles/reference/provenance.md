# Provenance

## Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — Course 103: Creative Fundamentals (100-Level), taught by **Mirella
Crespi**, ACA's founder, of **Creative Milkshake**.

The artifact is **The 10 Types of Angles Worksheet**, reproduced in `reference/angle-types.md` and
`reference/strategy.md` with every angle's core message, when-to-use conditions, psychological
triggers and example applications intact, plus the Multi-Angle Strategy section that closes it.

## ⚠️ One course-code conflict in ACA's own exports

Two exports of this worksheet exist and are the same document. One page header reads **"Course 103:
Creative Fundamentals"** and the other **"Course 401: Creative Ideation & Concept Development"**.

Course 103's own material names it as a Course 103 resource — *"we've included the 10 Types of
Angles Worksheet that breaks down each angle type with examples and application guidelines"* — so
103 is treated as its home here. It may well have been refiled to 401 later. Worth knowing if you
are matching this against ACA's current curriculum and find it listed elsewhere.

## What is reproduced and what is this skill's

**Reproduced as written:** all ten angle types, their core messages (*"Because it's on sale"*,
*"Because it does this"*, and the rest), every when-to-use condition, every psychological trigger,
all thirty example applications with their `[BRACKETS]` intact, and the Multi-Angle Strategy
section's four parts and closing notes field.

**This skill's, drawn from the course rather than stated by it:**

- **The claim-risk table** — which angles cannot be written without a supplied fact. Nine of the
  ten invite a specific fabrication, and this is the most claim-generative artifact in the set: ten
  angles times three example lines is thirty pieces of copy, each a potential invented number,
  endorsement, competitor limitation or mechanism. The course does not warn about this, and the
  worksheet's brackets are the only structural defence it has.
- **The instruction to keep the brackets and mark the missing fact** rather than filling it. This
  reads as ACA's intent — their examples are all written that way — but it is nowhere stated as a
  rule, and a plausible filled bracket is a finished ad line that nobody downstream can identify
  as invented.
- **The sharper edge on three angles**: Comparison makes a claim about someone else's product,
  Influencer attaches a real person's reputation, and Education describes a mechanism that must be
  true. These fail differently from the others and worse.
- **The resolution of the combinations tension.** The course says one angle per ad in the lesson
  and then asks for angle combinations in the worksheet, without reconciling them. Resolved here as
  lead-plus-support: a combination is one angle carrying another, and if both want the headline it
  is a cluttered ad rather than a combination.
- **The confusable pairs** (Social Proof vs Influencer, Emotional vs Functional Benefit, Promotion
  vs Seasonality, Education vs Comparison), the ✅/⛔ answer-quality examples, the three-part
  definition of a testing plan, and the note that the facts-to-find list is the most useful thing
  in the closing notes field.
- **The warning against inventing audience segments** to fill the matching table.

**No worked example ships with this skill.** The course provides none for this worksheet, and
inventing a fully-filled ten-angle set for an invented product would model exactly the behaviour
the skill warns against — thirty fabricated lines presented as an exemplar. The ✅/⛔ pairs
illustrate answer quality instead and are marked as such.

## Deliberate overlap with `aca-103-creative-fundamentals`

Both skills carry the ten angle types, on purpose. Each has to run with nothing else installed, so
neither can point at the other for its content.

The split is by job:

- **`aca-103-creative-fundamentals`** teaches the framework and covers the building blocks and the
  wider creative-strategy vocabulary alongside it. Someone who wants to understand what an angle is
  wants that one.
- **This skill** generates angles for a specific offer and does not teach. Someone with a product
  who needs options wants this one.

Route on whether there is a real offer on the table.
