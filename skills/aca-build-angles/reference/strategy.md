# The Multi-Angle Strategy Worksheet

The second half of ACA's sheet, and the half that turns ten filled-in options into something
someone can act on. Four parts, plus a closing notes field.

**Do not stop at the ten.** A list of ten angles is a menu. The output someone can use is: these
three first, aimed at these segments, tested this way.

---

## Your top 3 most promising angles

> Based on your product, audience and objectives, which 3 angles seem most promising?

| Angle | Why? |
|---|---|
| | |
| | |
| | |

**The "why" column is the section.** Three angles named without reasons is a shortlist; three with
reasons is an argument someone can disagree with, which is what makes it useful in a review.

A good reason cites something specific — a when-to-use condition the offer meets, a fact that
exists, an awareness stage, or a gap in what has already been run:

✅ *"Comparison. The audience is explicitly choosing between us and two named alternatives, we have
a documented advantage on delivery time, and no ad in the account has ever made a direct
comparison."*

⛔ `Comparison. It's a competitive market.`

⚠️ **Do not pick the three that produced the best-sounding lines.** The line quality of a
fabricated Social Proof angle is not evidence; it is a symptom. Rank on fit and on whether the
supporting fact exists.

## Audience-angle matching

> Different audience segments may respond better to different angles.

| Audience segment | Primary angle | Secondary angle |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |

⭐ **The most skipped part and where the leverage usually is.** The same offer aimed at two segments
frequently wants two different angles, and running one angle at everyone is a common reason an
account plateaus with no obvious cause.

The clearest case is **Gifting**, where the buyer and the user are different people: the pain point
that converts belongs to the giver. Any offer with a gift-giving segment needs a separate row, and
its angle is rarely the one that works on the end user.

Only fill this if segments were actually given or are genuinely evident. Inventing audience
segments to fill the table produces a plan aimed at people who may not exist.

## Angle combinations

> Sometimes combining angles creates powerful messaging. List 2-3 potential combinations:

> `1. [angle] + [angle] = `
> `2. [angle] + [angle] = `
> `3. [angle] + [angle] = `

⚠️ **This sits in tension with "one angle per ad", and both are right.**

A combination is **one angle carrying a second as support**, not two arguments sharing a stage.
Social Proof + Promotion is a promotion made credible — the promotion leads, the proof makes it
believable. If both angles want to be the headline, it is not a combination, it is a cluttered ad
that communicates neither.

Write each as *lead + support*, and say which is which:

✅ *"Education (lead) + Social Proof (support) = the mechanism explained, closing on the number of
people it has worked for. The proof is there to make the mechanism credible, not to compete with
it."*

⛔ `Education + Social Proof = talk about the science and the reviews.`

Combinations that tend to work, and why:

| Combination | Why |
|---|---|
| Education + Social Proof | Proof makes an unfamiliar mechanism believable |
| Comparison + Functional Benefit | The advantage is stated, then made concrete |
| Emotional Benefit + Influencer | A person embodying the feeling beats describing it |
| Seasonality + Promotion | The need is now, and so is the deal. The standard Q4 pairing |
| Promotion + Comparison | Two hard-edged claims fighting each other. Usually reads as desperate |

## Angle testing plan

> How will you test different angles to identify the most effective options?

A list of angles is not a plan. A plan has three things:

1. **A sequence** — which first, and why. Usually the highest-fit angle against the most untested
   ground, since a winner against an exhausted angle tells you less.
2. **A way to read the result** — what is being held constant, and what metric decides it. Angle
   tests are message tests, so they want the same offer, format and audience underneath.
3. **A rule for what happens next** — what a win means (build variations inside that angle) and
   what a loss means (was the angle wrong, or the execution?).

⚠️ **Change one thing.** An angle test where the format, the audience and the offer also moved has
not tested the angle, and the result will be read as though it did.

⚠️ **Do not specify sample sizes or durations from nothing.** Those depend on the account's traffic
and baseline conversion rate. Say what needs to be calculated, or ask for the numbers.

---

## Final notes & insights

> Any additional observations or ideas about angle strategy for this specific product.

The place for what did not fit the structure: an angle that is unavailable now but would be strong
in three months, a segment worth investigating, a claim worth substantiating because it would
unlock a whole angle.

⭐ **The most useful thing that usually goes here is the list of facts to go and find.** Every angle
marked "needs your actual review count" or "needs a documented competitor advantage" is a small,
concrete errand, and gathering them converts several structures into finished angles at once.
