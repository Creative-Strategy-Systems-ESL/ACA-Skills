## ⚠️ Where the two recordings disagree

**Measured, not inferred. Both differences add an item in the delivered recording that the written
script omits.**

| | Written script | Delivered recording |
|---|---|---|
| **The visual vocabulary** | **12 shots** across four families | **13** — adds **Before & After** to the action shots, *"actions that highlights the problem or the problem being solved."* |
| **Cinematography shot types** | **9** | **10** — adds the **Level Shot**, *"looking at the subject directly"* |

⭐ **This skill carries the union, thirteen and ten, and flags which two are recording-only.**
Trimming to the script's numbers would drop shots ACA teaches; presenting the larger lists silently
would misreport what the written material says.

⚠️ **They also word the same technique differently:** *"burning paper transitions"* against *"burning
the paper hook"*, and *"screen-scrolling effects"* against *"screen-scrolling hologram effects"*. The
script's phrasings are used here.

⚠️ **One passage of the delivered recording is garbled by transcription** — a sentence about creating
a video game appears in the middle of the cinematography section and belongs to neither subject.
Nothing is quoted from it.

## What is quoted and what is organised

**Everything substantive is Mirella's, quoted directly** — the whole shot vocabulary, the
cinematography list, the four planning moves, the tagging rules, the tool list, the delivery specs,
the thumbnail elements and the four QA categories.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the three-file split, the routing table, rendering the shot
families and the tools as tables, and marking the two recording-only entries inline.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The union of the two recordings' lists**, and marking which entries appear in only one.
- ⭐ **The economic argument under the content bank** — that the marginal cost of the extra shot types
  is near zero while the creator and product are already in the room, so a shoot planned around one
  ad is the expensive way to get one ad.
- ⭐ **The arithmetic under modular production**: five hooks against three bodies is fifteen ads from
  one day, where three finished films is three.
- ⚠️ **The warning that modularity must be designed into the shoot**, since components only recombine
  if they were framed, lit and paced to. She describes the benefit and not the precondition.
- ⭐ **Reading talent-name-and-date tagging as a legal record**, not a filing convention, because
  usage rights expire and an untagged library cannot be safely reused from.
- ⭐ **The observation that three camera movements is deliberately the whole list**, small enough to
  be learned by a non-filmmaker, which is what makes it get used.
- ⭐ **Naming Frame.io as the tool that changes how a team works** rather than what it can make.
- ⚠️ **Flagging the Uplifted coupon** as a commercial relationship.
- ⚠️ **The note that platform technique lists date fastest** of anything in the course, so any of the
  seven should be checked against what is currently running before being briefed.
- **The point that a thumbnail is a decision or an accident**, and the default is an accident.
- ⛔ **The instruction never to invent a platform spec**, and to verify ratios and safe zones against
  the current platform documentation before delivery.

## Scope

Producing and delivering assets. Not concepting, not storyboarding, not casting or directing, not
measurement. It stops at a delivered, QA'd file handed to the paid media team.
