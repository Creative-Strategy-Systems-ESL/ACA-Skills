# The seven clip types, and producing the checklist

> "Even the best brief and most charismatic creator can fall flat **if the right clips don't get
> captured.**"

## ⭐ The mindset shift

> "Your goal isn't just to get 'a good take.' **Your goal is to collect the right building blocks —
> modular, usable footage that can be remixed, trimmed, tested and scaled.**"

> "Think of it like **creative legos**: the more thoughtful clips you capture, the more ad formats
> you can build."

⚠️ **These clips are what separate a deliverable from a file.** In her words, they are *"the
difference between a usable deliverable and one that ends up in the trash folder."*

---

## The seven

> "Here's the checklist I use across all the brands I work on, refined across hundreds of
> deliverables, to ensure the final ad is actually editable."

### 1. A strong face-to-camera testimonial take

**The anchor clip.** Chest-up, vertical, directly into the lens, *"delivering their story with
energy and clarity. You want natural pacing, emotional conviction and clean eye contact."*

> "This clip usually carries the hook, the product insight, or the relatable 'before' moment."

⭐ **Always ask for 2 to 3 takes with different tones** — *"one relaxed, one punchy, one a little
more exaggerated, so you have flexibility in edit."*

### 2. Clear product demo footage

> "**This one's non-negotiable.** You need to see the product being used."

*"Whether it's applying skincare, zipping up a pair of Jetsetter Pants, turning on a device —
whatever your product does, show it in action. If it solves a problem, **that problem should be
visible.** And if it has a wow moment, make sure it's captured."*

**Her pro tip:** if the creator is demonstrating with their hands, especially for beauty, *"ask for
tight close-ups and natural light. Think POV or overhead angles if relevant."*

### 3. Still product hold

> "This one's often forgotten, **but it saves the day in post.**"

Hold the product still, in good lighting, framed nicely, for **3 to 5 seconds**. Ideally smiling or
nodding while holding it.

> "This gives your editor a clean moment to **overlay pricing, promo language, or a call-to-action**
> in a way that looks natural and native."

⭐ **This is the clip that makes an ad reusable across offers.** Without it, every promotion needs a
new shoot; with it, the same deliverable carries a different price on it next month.

### 4. Reaction shots and micro-moments

> "These are small but powerful. A five-second smile. A look of surprise. A head tilt. A moment of
> laughter or playful sarcasm."

Ask creators to film a few separately, or capture them between takes.

> "They give your editor flexibility, especially for jump cuts or voiceover overlays, **and make the
> video feel human and dynamic.**"

**How to prompt them, in her words:**

- *"Give us your 'omg I can't believe this worked' face."*
- *"Now show us your skeptical face, like you're not sure this is for real."*
- *"Smile like you're texting your best friend about this product."*

### 5. Hook line in 3 to 5 variations

> "The first three seconds are everything. That's why we always ask for multiple takes of just the
> hook. **Think of it like an audition — same line, but delivered with a range of tones: bold,
> whispery, dramatic, cheeky.**"

**Her examples:**

> "I was this close to giving up on my skin…"

> "Men over 40 are not going to like this ad."

> "I would never have believed this if I hadn't tried it myself."

⚠️ **Same line, different deliveries.** This is a performance test, not five different hooks —
writing five different hooks is a separate job, and confusing the two loses the clean comparison.

### 6. B-roll lifestyle or problem footage

> "This one depends on the product, but **can elevate your ad from functional to emotional.**"

Ask for **2 to 3 short clips** of the product in its natural habitat, and/or problem shots showing
the "before" state. **Her examples:**

- A frustrated mom picking up a mess in the playroom
- A guy packing Jetsetter shorts into his suitcase for a trip
- A guy struggling with pants that are too baggy
- A CEO drinking a coffee calmly with morning light streaming in after she finished her taxes

> "This footage builds trust and relatability. **It shows that the product fits into a real
> routine.**"

### 7. Voice-only or voiceover clips ⚠️ *(she marks this one optional)*

> "If the creator is comfortable, ask for a clean voice recording of their testimonial script or the
> hook."

> "This gives you the option to **pair a strong voiceover with alternate visuals — and sometimes
> that combo outperforms face-to-camera content.**"

Also useful for TikTok-style edits and product montage formats.

⭐ **Optional in her list, but it is the cheapest one to add.** It costs the creator a minute and it
is the only clip that lets you rebuild the ad entirely if the on-camera footage disappoints.

## ⛔ Producing the checklist

> "How do you make sure creators actually give you all these clips? Simple: **you bake it into your
> brief.** Use bullet-point checklists. Include time stamps or sample videos. And if you're
> live-directing, walk them through this sequence on the call."

> "**If you treat your shoot like a checklist, not** just **a vibe, you'll walk away with everything
> you need to build three to five performing ads from one deliverable.**"

**Emit it in this shape**, filled for the actual product:

```csv
#,Clip type,Required?,What to capture for this product,How many,Prompt to give the creator,Received?
```

⚠️ **Illustrative rows.** Replace the middle columns with the real product's specifics.

| # | Clip type | Required? | What to capture | How many | Prompt to give the creator | Received? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Face-to-camera testimonial** | Required | Chest-up, vertical, to lens. Carries the hook and the before moment | 2-3 takes, different tones | "Tell me the story like you're catching up with a friend. Then one punchier, then one bigger" | ☐ |
| 2 | **Product demo** | ⛔ Required, non-negotiable | The product doing the thing, with the problem visible | 1-2 | "Show me it working. If there's a moment where it clicks, make sure we see it" | ☐ |
| 3 | **Still product hold** | Required | Held still, good light, nicely framed, smiling or nodding | 1, **3-5 seconds** | "Just hold it steady and smile for a slow count of five" | ☐ |
| 4 | **Reaction shots** | Required | Smile, surprise, head tilt, scepticism | 3-4 micro-clips | "Give us your 'omg I can't believe this worked' face" | ☐ |
| 5 | **Hook line variations** | Required | The same hook line, different tones: bold, whispery, dramatic, cheeky | **3-5 of one line** | "Same words, five ways. Treat it like an audition" | ☐ |
| 6 | **B-roll lifestyle / problem** | Required | The product in a real routine, and/or the before state | 2-3 | "Show me the annoying version of this without the product" | ☐ |
| 7 | **Voiceover** | ⚠️ Optional | Clean audio of the testimonial or the hook | 1 | "If you're comfortable, record just the audio somewhere quiet" | ☐ |

⛔ **The "Received?" column is the point.** This checklist is used twice: once inside the brief so
the creator knows what to film, and once against the returned files before the creator is paid and
released. Checking it only at the first moment is how a reshoot happens.

⚠️ **Never mark a clip received without opening it.** A file named `demo.mp4` is not a demo, and the
whole failure mode this checklist exists to prevent is discovering in the edit that something is
missing.

⛔ **If a clip type genuinely does not apply, write why in the row rather than deleting it.** A
service with nothing to hold has no still product hold; a deleted row is indistinguishable from a
forgotten one.
