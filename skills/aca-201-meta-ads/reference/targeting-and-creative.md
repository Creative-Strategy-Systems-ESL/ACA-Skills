# Targeting, placements, formats and creative

Modules 3 and 4. Where the strategist's decisions actually live.

## Creative is the targeting

The history, as Ashley tells it: first advertising bought **space** (a billboard, and hope). Then it
bought **audiences** (TV and magazines promising a gender, age, income, interest). Both were proxies.

> "everyone was using proxies, right? Assumptions about who might buy your product. For example,
> targeting like a 20 to 30-year-old women that earn $50,000 a year and likes kittens, you know,
> thinking that those are the people who would buy your product. **We don't know if that's true.**"

Meta's algorithm removes the need for the proxy. Directed by the objective, it finds the people
most likely to produce the outcome.

### ⛔ Go broad. Two reasons, and the second is the one that convinces clients

1. **The demographic usually does not matter.** *"Whether the person is a 20-something guy or a
   50-something woman doesn't matter as long as it leads to the desired outcome."*
2. **Narrow targeting costs more and excludes buyers.** Narrowing shrinks the audience → raises
   competition → and in an auction, **costs go up**. It also cuts out people who would have bought.

⭐ **The bra example.** A client wanted women 22 to 49, certain that was the sweet spot.

> "Don't women before 22 buy a bra? And I hope they're buying a bra after 49. Because what they
> were doing by narrowing down like that, they were missing out on potential customers."

**The rule:** *"target as broadly as your budget and logic will allow."* Use Meta's **Advantage
Plus audience** where budget permits. Not every budget allows full breadth, so narrow intelligently
when you must — and note that a genuinely product-linked cut (women, for women's bras) is a
reasonable one.

### And then the consequence

> "When you're casting that wide net with your audience, your creative becomes the key to
> attracting the most relevant people."

That is the whole argument of the course: broad targeting does not make creative less important, it
makes creative *the* targeting mechanism.

## Choosing which ads to make

The strategist should drive this — you know the objective, the audience insight and the success
measure by now.

⚠️ **The old way is dead.** Agencies used to take the media plan and fill each placement to spec,
reusing one asset everywhere it fit. *"Look, we can make 172 by 90 banner ad and it will fit into
94 placements. Score! Less work."* Now the requirement is **content fit for placement** — an ad
that makes sense where it is seen.

Build a **joint media plan** with the client and media team, so placements bought and formats made
are decided together rather than creative being stuffed into a plan that already exists.

For deciding what *type* of content goes into each format, Ashley points at Mirella's five stages
of market awareness (ACA Course 102).

## Placements and formats

**Formats** are the ad itself: video, carousel, image, and more. **Placements** are where it is
seen: Reels, Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Stories, Search Results, Messenger.

### Variety is not optional

> "Meta's algorithm works best when it has a range of creative formats and placements to optimize.
> More ad formats and more placements, including variations within those, **won't drive up your
> costs.** In fact, having more ads and placements is likely to reduce costs and improve
> performance"

**Meta recommends at least six placements.** The temptation is to back only Reels and Feed because
that is where the eyeballs are; more placements give the algorithm more room to optimise.

⛔ **Do not lean on auto-placement.** Uploading one asset and letting Meta resize it into everything
is convenient and *"not always ideal... often the resized ads don't look their best. Not really
great at all."* A 4:5 image does not become a Reel, and a 9:16 looks wrong in Instagram search.

Build per-placement versions where you can. In a pinch, supply 9:16 **and** 4:5 — and preview every
ad in every placement regardless.

### Format guidance

- **Video, in multiple lengths.** From a few seconds up to about 15. Longer is a risk: *"anything
  longer and well, you're clearly a risk taker... Videos can be longer, but it better be damn
  interesting."* Hedge long cuts with much shorter versions.
- **Aspect ratios:** everything in at least **9:16, 1:1 or 4:5**.
- **Still images**, especially multi-image ads.
- **Cinemagraphs** — one part of a still animated. Ashley's own favourite, *"really eye catching."*

Every campaign should carry video (even a fast-moving slideshow of stills) **and** compelling
stills. Lower-funnel work needs conversion-driving content too.

## Variations within a format

⚠️ **Read `andromeda-update.md` alongside this section.** The later module revises it: cosmetic
variation is no longer sufficient on its own.

As taught in module 4 — colour variations (backgrounds, designs), copy variations (headlines,
primary text, calls to action), and small edits like a different thumbnail or a reworked headline,
which *"can refresh your ad and reduce ad fatigue."*

Ashley's tooling note: if a creative team is building each variant by hand in Photoshop, *"stop
right now. You are wasting a ton of time and budget."* AI-driven variation tools exist for this.

### ⛔ The Meta Advantage Creative tool, and why she warns against it

It auto-generates variations inside Ads Manager. Her verdict, explicitly time-stamped:

> "As of this date, it's not the greatest... it can seriously go off brand in terms of visuals and
> messaging. If you just let the AI tool build out your ads, you don't know necessarily what you're
> gonna get because you aren't able to preview all the versions."

**You always need to see every version in its placement.** Treat this as a snapshot judgement, not
a permanent one — she adds *"hopefully they'll improve the tool soon."*

## Creative best practices

⭐ **Why this matters more than anything else in the course**, with ACA's figures:

- **~70% of a campaign's ROI** traces back to the quality of the ad
- Following creative best practices can give **up to 4× the sales impact**
- Campaigns using them typically see **50% higher ROAS**

> "You can have the best strategy, a great product, and a solid media buy. But if your ad creative
> doesn't stop the thumb, your campaign is going to tank."

Ashley's realism about the strategist's role: you are usually not designing it, you are directing
it — *"and trust me, most creatives can't nail a meta ad without a lot of direction. It's really a
niche skill."*

### The seven

1. **Clear message.** The viewer spends an average of **1.7 seconds** on a piece of content. *"No
   more than 10 words on any frame. Heck, five words would even be better."*
2. **Never assume audio is on.** Subtitles on all video. If audio *is* on, use voiceover or music.
   Subtitle font large enough to read on a phone.
3. **Fit for placement.** Build per-placement versions; preview them; do not trust auto-resize.
4. **Short, but not too short.** Aim **under about six seconds**. *"Yes, you might have a story to
   tell, but if you can't hook them quickly, that story won't even get started."* Longer is fine
   with a real reason — test different edit lengths.
5. **Brand and message up front — in the first two seconds.** *"If you're relying on your audience
   to wait until the end to remember your brand and message, you're doing it wrong. People rarely
   watch ads till the end."* Critical for awareness campaigns.
6. **Variety.** More uploaded creative gives the algorithm more to find a winner among. Five product
   benefits → try an ad per benefit.
7. ⭐ **Every ad must stand on its own.** *"Never assume that your audience has seen your previous
   ads."* You do not control the order Meta serves them in. **Even in retargeting, assume this is
   the first time.**

## The copy that sits outside the creative

Written by the strategist and handed to the media specialist. Not every placement renders all of
it, and Meta accepts multiple versions of each for testing.

| Element | Length |
|---|---|
| **Primary text** | ~125 characters, can run longer. Position varies by ad |
| **Headline** | Up to 255 characters, *"but I would keep it short"* |
| **Description** | ~125 characters or less. Does not appear in every format |
| **Destination URL** | The landing page |

## The line to end on

> "**There is no perfect ad.** Every brand is going to need to experiment and find out what works
> for their brand."
