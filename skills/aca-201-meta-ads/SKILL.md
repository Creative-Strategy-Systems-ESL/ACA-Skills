# Course 201: Mastering the Essentials of Meta Ads

**Ashley Vinson**, 20+ years in digital, ex-Meta and ex-Twitter, now consulting through Outcome
Company in Amsterdam. 1h 16m, 8 modules.

The platform course. Not how to make a good ad in general — how **Meta's system decides**, and
what a creative strategist must therefore do differently.

> "Most creative strategists will never upload the ads into the meta ad system. But knowing each of
> these steps and where it can go horribly wrong is critical so you can discuss and check it later."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none.** Ask a Meta question and get an answer.

**Optional, and it makes the answer specific:**

1. **The campaign objective** already chosen, or the business outcome wanted
2. **What is being sold**, and whether the account has conversion history
3. **What is set up** — pixel, Conversions API, product catalog
4. **Current targeting**, especially if it is narrow
5. **What has been run and what happened**

## The through-line

> **You are a guest at someone else's curated event.**

Every feed is uniquely tailored, so an ad enters a space built around someone else's interests. If
it does not add value it gets swiped away.

⭐ **Ashley's demo for clients who do not believe it:** record your own Instagram or Facebook feed,
full of the content you actually love, then splice the client's ad into it. *"The contrast is often
stark and well, a bit depressing."* It converts the argument from opinion into something they can
see.

## ⚠️ Two things to get right when answering

**1. The course contradicts itself on variation, deliberately, and the later answer wins.**

Module 4 teaches variation as colour swaps, background changes and headline tweaks. Module 8,
recorded later as a 2026 update, says that is no longer enough:

> "I do not mean taking one ad changing the background from blue to green... Meta does not just
> need more stuff, it needs more meaningful and distinct creative inputs."

Ashley marks the change herself — *"some of that used to work better than it does now"* — so this
is an update, not an inconsistency. **Answer "make meaningfully different ads, not cosmetic
variants", and mention that small tweaks still have a role once a concept is proven.**

**2. This is the most time-bound skill in the set.** Platform mechanics move. Anything naming a
tool, a feature, a threshold or a spec is true as of the recording and must be given that way.
`reference/watchouts.md` lists what dates fastest. Never invent a current Meta spec, limit or
feature — if it is not in these files, say so and point at Meta's own ad guide and business help
centre (Meta's advertising documentation, published at business.facebook.com and
developers.facebook.com; ACA gives no deep links and neither should you).

⚠️ **The course carries no recording date**, so "true as of the recording" is a hedge with an
empty variable in it. `reference/provenance.md` sets out what can and cannot be established about
how old this is. Read it before defending any platform specific to a sceptical agency.

## How to work through it

**System first, then the levers.** Almost every wrong instinct about Meta comes from not knowing what the
auction is actually optimising.

1. **Explain the auction and the objectives** from `reference/the-system.md`, including which objectives are
   traps. Nothing later makes sense without this.
2. **Do targeting and creative together** in `reference/targeting-and-creative.md`, because the argument for
   broad targeting only holds once creative is the variable.
3. **Add the Andromeda update** from `reference/andromeda-update.md`. It changes what "enough ads" means, so
   it belongs before any volume advice.
4. **Then testing and measurement** in `reference/testing-and-measurement.md` — hypothesis-driven tests, and
   ⚠️ what each of the six attribution models can and cannot prove.
5. **Run the watchouts** in `reference/watchouts.md` against their account, and use
   `reference/account-audit.md` if they have one to look at.
6. **Check they can say why broad beats narrow here**, and which objective they would pick and why. Both
   answers are wrong in a specific, common way if step 1 was skipped.

## Answering well

**Objectives are where campaigns are won or lost before creative exists.** *"Ensuring that you
choose the right one can make the difference between a campaign going well or well, not so well."*
If someone describes a goal that does not match their objective, say so first.

**Push back on narrow targeting, with the reason.** Narrow shrinks the audience, which raises
competition, which raises cost in an auction, and it excludes buyers. Ashley's client wanted women
22 to 49 for bras: *"don't women before 22 buy a bra? And I hope they're buying a bra after 49."*

**Be honest about attribution.** The course's position is that you cannot prove a single channel
drove a sale, and that saying so is the professional answer rather than a weak one. Give the
contribution case, not a false certainty.

**Do not oversell engagement.** *"In all my days at Twitter and Meta, we were never able to prove
that there's any correlation between getting a high engagement and actual business outcomes."*

## What this course does not cover

It is Meta only — no TikTok, no Google, no other platform. It does not teach how to write or design
an ad, only what Meta rewards; the craft is elsewhere in the programme. It gives no current rate
cards, benchmarks by vertical, or step-by-step Ads Manager clicks.
