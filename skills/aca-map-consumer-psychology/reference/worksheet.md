# The worksheet

Seven sections to fill, in order, plus a Section 8 of ready-made formulas that is reference rather
than a section to complete. Every field, plus what a good answer looks like.

**Open each filled section with its provenance**, so it survives being copy-pasted into a deck:

```
GROUNDED IN: [what was supplied — e.g. 34 Amazon review quotes, Oct 2025]
```
```
⚠️ ASSUMPTION, NOT VERIFIED: [what was reasoned about the category, and what would confirm it]
```
```
⚠️ CLIENT-ASSERTED, UNVERIFIED: [what the client stated that nobody has checked]
```

A flag living in the conversation around the worksheet is gone the moment someone copies the
worksheet. A flag on line one of the section travels with it.

⚠️ **And a section flag does not travel with a single line, which is what actually gets copied.**
That is what the Claims Register at the end is for. Any line in Sections 4, 5 or 6 carrying a
number, a superlative or a claim about what the product does to a body gets a register row and an
inline tag.

---

## Section 1 — Audience Awareness Assessment

Place the audience on the spectrum: Unaware · Problem Aware · Solution Aware · Product Aware ·
Most Aware. Table of what each means and its message focus is in `frameworks.md`.

**Fill:** `Based on this assessment, my primary messaging approach should be: ______`

✅ **Good:** *"Problem aware. Of 28 supplied lines, 19 describe the symptom without naming any
product category ('I just assumed I was tired because of work'), and only 3 mention having tried
anything. They have not started shopping for a solution. Messaging should validate the problem
before differentiating anything."*

⛔ **Not good:** *"Problem aware."* That is a guess wearing a label. State the evidence, so someone
can disagree with it.

⚠️ **Use pre-purchase language for this section, not post-purchase.** A review is written by
someone who already bought; they are past the stage you are placing. *"I've tried every collagen
powder going"* is a prospect describing their history and can carry Section 1. *"I bought it for
the collagen but the sleep was the surprise"* is a customer describing an outcome and cannot — it
tells you about buyers, not about the people you are trying to reach. Sort before you use, and say
how many lines the placement actually rests on.

Note a significant secondary stage if there is one, but commit to a primary.

## Section 2 — Emotional Drivers Analysis

**Maslow's Hierarchy** — check all that apply:
- [ ] Physiological (basic survival needs)
- [ ] Safety (security, stability, protection)
- [ ] Belonging (connection, relationships, community)
- [ ] Esteem (recognition, status, respect, achievement)
- [ ] Self-actualization (growth, creativity, purpose)

**Life-Force 8** — check all that apply:
- [ ] Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension
- [ ] Enjoyment of food and beverages
- [ ] Freedom from fear, pain, and danger
- [ ] Sexual companionship
- [ ] Comfortable living conditions
- [ ] To be superior; winning
- [ ] Care and protection of loved ones
- [ ] Social approval

**Then narrow to one transformation:**

> **FROM:** [current negative emotion] → **TO:** [desired positive emotion]

> **Helps customers avoid feeling:** [list]
> **Helps customers feel:** [list]

**The narrowing is the point.** Checking four drivers and stopping is not a decision; the writer
downstream will make it for you, arbitrarily.

⚠️ **Emotions, not transactions.** "From tired to energised" is an emotional transformation. "From
not owning it to owning it" is a purchase, and it will not generate a hook. If the FROM state reads
like a description of not having bought yet, redo it.

Use the customer's own words. `I'm tired of waking up tired` beats `combats fatigue`.

## Section 3 — Persuasion Principles Selection

Pick **2 to 3** and say specifically how each will be used. A decision with the execution named,
not a shortlist.

| Principle | Is | How I'll use it |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Giving something valuable first | |
| Unity | Creating tribal identity | |
| Commitment | Small yeses leading to bigger ones | |
| Loss aversion | What they'll lose by not acting | |
| Social proof | Others like them are using it | |
| Anchoring | Setting value reference points | |
| Authority | Expert endorsement or credentials | |
| Pratfall effect | Strategic vulnerability for credibility | |
| Liking | Rapport and relatability | |
| Scarcity | Limited availability or time | |

⚠️ The course teaches four more this grid omits: **MAYA, distinctiveness, self-centeredness,
primacy and recency**. Consider them; `frameworks.md` has all fourteen.

✅ **Good:** *"Social proof — lead with the 4,200 five-star reviews specifically from parents,
since the research shows buyers checking whether other parents rated it."*
⛔ **Not good:** `Social proof — use testimonials.`

The **mechanism** frame is the one people skip and the one that matters most in a crowded category,
because it is the escape from sophistication.

## Section 5 — Hook Development

- **For unaware / problem aware:** [creates curiosity or names the problem]
- **For solution / product aware:** [differentiates your approach]
- **For most aware:** [urgency or offer]

Each hook must be traceable back to Sections 1 and 2. A hook that does not follow from the
awareness stage and the emotional driver may be a good line, but it is not the output of this
process and should not be presented as though the research produced it.

Both hook menus are in `frameworks.md` if nothing comes.

## Section 7 — Creative Evaluation Checklist

**Deliver this as a gate to run later, against finished creative.** Ticking it against the
worksheet proves nothing; it exists to catch the drift between what was planned and what got made.

- [ ] Does it clearly address the right awareness level?
- [ ] Does it tap into at least one powerful emotional driver?
- [ ] Does it emphasise transformation, not just features?
- [ ] Does it leverage 2 to 3 persuasion principles effectively?
- [ ] Is the hook strong enough to stop the scroll?
- [ ] Is the primary benefit immediately clear?
- [ ] Would I be excited to see this ad if I were the target customer?
- [ ] Have I created pattern interrupts or distinctive elements?
- [ ] Does it address objections the audience might have?
- [ ] Is the call-to-action clear and compelling?

**Areas for improvement:** ______

Question 7 is the one that catches what the others miss. The other nine can all pass on an ad that
is technically correct and lifeless. Answer it as the customer, not as the person who made it.

## Section 8 — Winning Creative Formulas

**Nothing to fill.** Four ready-made structures printed at the bottom of the sheet, in ACA's words
*"quick reference formulas that have proven effective across countless campaigns"*, there for when
a section stalls.

| Formula | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Problem → Agitation → Solution** | Identify a painful problem · Intensify the emotion around it · Present your solution as the relief |
| **Feature → Advantage → Benefit** | What it is · Why that matters · How it improves their life |
| **Before → After → Bridge** | Show the painful "before" state · Show the desirable "after" state · Position your product as the bridge |
| **Objection → Counter → Reframe** | Acknowledge their concern · Counter with new information · Reframe the perspective |

Use them to unstick a section, not to replace one:

- **Section 4's benefit frame reads as a spec sheet** → *Feature → Advantage → Benefit*, and take
  the third step. Most feature-frame copy stops at the second.
- **Section 4's pain-avoidance frame will not come** → *Problem → Agitation → Solution*.
- **Section 2's transformation needs to become a script** → *Before → After → Bridge*. It is the
  same FROM/TO, staged.
- **Checklist item 9, objections** → *Objection → Counter → Reframe*.

**Carry them into the delivered worksheet.** They are what the writer receiving it reaches for, and
the artifact is less useful without them.

### ⭐ How the worksheet ends, and it belongs in the delivered version

ACA closes the sheet on this:

> Great creative strategy balances psychological insight with authentic value. Your goal is not
> manipulation, but connecting people who have real problems with solutions that genuinely help
> them.

Not decoration. It is the frame for everything above it, and the reason the grounding rules in
`SKILL.md` are strict: every technique on this sheet works just as well on a product that does not
deliver, which is exactly what makes inventing the research dangerous rather than merely sloppy.

## The Claims Register

**Not part of ACA's sheet. Added here, and required.** The section flags above are per-section;
copy is written per-line, and a line is what gets pasted into the ad.

Every line in Sections 4, 5 and 6 carrying a **number**, a **superlative**, or a **claim about what
the product does to a body** gets a row. Tag it inline where it appears — `[C2]` — so the fragment
carries its own pointer when someone copies it.

| # | Claim | Where it appears | Status | Needs before it ships |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | | | | |
| C2 | | | | |

**Status is one of four:**

- **Supplied fact** — the brand's own knowledge of its own product. Usable.
- **⚠️ Client-asserted, unverified** — an estimate, comparison or superlative about the outside
  world. Record it; do not build a line on it.
- **⚠️ Outcome claim, needs substantiation** — what it does to a person.
- **Attributed quote** — one named customer's report. Must stay visibly a quote in the artifact.

**Record the quarantined claims too, with "nowhere" in the where-it-appears column.** A claim that
is simply missing looks overlooked. A claim recorded as deliberately excluded is a decision, and it
stops someone quietly reinstating it three weeks later.

⭐ **The hedged sentence in the brief is usually the most quotable one.** *"Our nutritionist reckons
it's about 3x"* is twice-hedged, unsubstantiated, and born wanting to be a headline. That
combination is exactly what the register exists to catch.

# A worked example

ACA's own, for a supplement brand, carried through the full sheet. **Every line is illustrative** —
a real version needs the brand's actual research, and the mechanism claim in particular would need
to be true of the actual formulation.

⚠️ **This example shows the reasoning, not the delivery format.** ACA gives the layer choices
without the provenance headers, and it is reproduced that way rather than being dressed up. A real
delivered sheet carries a marker on every section and a register at the end. The markers each
section *would* need are noted below, so the gap does not read as permission.

**Sections 1 to 3** (ACA's original example):

> `⚠️ ASSUMPTION, NOT VERIFIED: no research behind these choices — they are a teaching example.`

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Life-Force drive | Survival and life extension |
| Awareness level | Problem aware |
| Emotional trigger | Fear of decline, hope for vitality |
| Persuasion principles | Authority, scarcity, social proof |

Note the coherence: a problem-aware audience gets validation rather than differentiation, the
trigger is a pair (a fear and its matching hope), and all three principles serve credibility, which
is what a health claim needs most.

**Section 4** — core message: *the tiredness you have accepted as normal is not normal.*

| Frame | Line |
|---|---|
| Benefit | "Wake up before your alarm, because you actually slept" |
| Pain avoidance | "Stop planning your day around the 3pm crash" |
| Status / identity | "The people who seem to have endless energy are not lucky" |
| Process / mechanism | "Why magnesium timing matters more than magnesium dose" |
| Testimonial / story | "I thought I needed more coffee. I needed the opposite." |

**Section 5** — hooks by awareness:

- *Unaware / problem aware:* "You are not tired because you are busy."
- *Solution / product aware:* "Most magnesium supplements use the form your body absorbs worst."
  `[C2]`
- *Most aware:* "Your first month, and if it does nothing, keep it." `[C3]`

Each traces back: the first names a problem someone has normalised, the second differentiates on
mechanism, the third removes friction for someone already convinced.

**The Claims Register for this example**, which is the point of showing it — three of these lines
cannot ship as written, and reading the sheet without the register would not tell you which:

| # | Claim | Where | Status | Needs before it ships |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | "magnesium timing matters more than dose" | S4 mechanism | ⚠️ Outcome claim, needs substantiation | Evidence that timing changes the result for this formulation |
| C2 | "most magnesium supplements use the form your body absorbs worst" | S5 hook 2 | ⚠️ Client-asserted, unverified | A survey of what competitors actually use, and absorption data |
| C3 | "if it does nothing, keep it" | S5 hook 3 | Supplied fact, if true | Confirm the refund terms actually say this |
| C4 | "wake up before your alarm" | S4 benefit | ⚠️ Outcome claim | Substantiation, or soften to a customer quote |

Note that **C2 is the sharpest hook in the set and the one on the weakest ground.** That is the
normal case, not a coincidence: the line that differentiates hardest is usually the line making the
biggest claim about someone else.

**Section 7** — the question that would catch this one is #3, transformation not features. A cut
opening on capsule shots and absorption percentages fails it even though the strategy was sound,
which is exactly the drift the gate exists to find.

**A second ACA example**, for contrast, showing a different shape:

| Layer | Productivity app |
|---|---|
| Maslow level | Esteem needs (achievement, confidence) |
| Life-Force drive | To be superior; winning |
| Awareness level | Solution aware |
| Emotional trigger | Pride, ambition, fear of falling behind |
| Persuasion principles | Social proof, loss aversion, distinctiveness |

Here the audience already knows the category, so the work is differentiation. Maslow and the
Life-Force drive agree with each other, which is a good sign the driver was identified rather than
picked from a list.
