# Course 703: Sprint Planning & Creative Volume

**Chloe Rhys**, Ex-TubeScience, now at OpenStore. 55m 41s, 3 modules — the largest course in the
700-Level.

> "You can't scale a performance campaign with just a handful of ads. **If you're not launching
> enough concepts, you're not learning. And if you're not learning, you're not winning.**"

⭐ **This skill emits the sprint plan.** For the board itself, `aca-plan-creative-sprint` produces
ACA's planning template.

⚠️ **ACA's second asset for this course is not a worksheet.** The *Sprint Planning Operation System
Worksheet* is engineering documentation for the stack OpenStore built around the board. There is
nothing to fill in, so it is carried as `reference/automating-the-system.md` rather than as a
producible artifact.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what a MORE request is, how to define a win, or when a
result is readable, and get an answer.

**To plan a sprint, required:**

1. **Daily or monthly ad spend.** ⛔ Say which — this course indexes on **daily** spend and Course
   601 indexes on **monthly**, and confusing them is a 30× error
2. **Your team's actual weekly output capacity**, honestly stated

**Optional, and each one changes the plan:**

3. Brand maturity — new to paid social, or with a library of learnings
4. How campaigns are structured: dedicated testing campaigns, or new ads fed into evergreen ones
5. The sprint goal: growth, efficiency or stabilisation
6. The CPA target, which sets the win threshold
7. What is currently winning, which sets the MORE pipeline

⛔ **Never invent a volume number.** This course gives five variables and one worked account, not a
lookup table. A number produced without the five is a guess wearing a framework.

⛔ **Never plan past the team's stated capacity.** ⭐ Chloe's own rule: *"Launching 6 Concepts a week,
every week, will outperform 12 one week and none for the next month."*

## The through-line

> "You don't scale by finding one winner. **You scale by turning that winner into a series.**"

⭐ **That is what a sprint is for.** The four request types exist so that a week of creative is a
portfolio — mostly building on what works, some rescuing near-misses, some genuinely new — rather
than a pile of fresh ideas competing for the same budget.

## How to run it

**Volume comes from the account, not from a benchmark.** The five variables exist so the number is derived
rather than borrowed.

1. **Work the five variables** in `reference/volume-and-cadence.md` to get this account's volume. ⛔ Indexed
   on **daily** spend — Course 601's ladder is monthly, and mixing them is a 30× error.
2. **Set launch cadence and turnaround times**, same file.
3. ⛔ **Establish when a result is readable** and when it is still noise, before anything launches. This is
   what stops ads being killed early, which is the failure the course names.
4. **Define a win** with the four criteria in `reference/the-sprint.md`, including the 20× CPA scale rule.
   ⭐ Note that Course 601's $2,000-per-test at a $100 CPA is the same threshold, reached independently.
5. **Set the request mix** — MORE, FIX, ADAPT, NEW — to the target proportions in the same file.
6. **Cover the ego-less factory-line mindset**, which is what makes the cadence survivable rather than a
   burnout schedule.
7. ⭐ **To fill the board, route to `aca-plan-creative-sprint`** if installed. ⛔ It emits structure only,
   never ACA's client data.
8. **Check the volume number came from their variables.** A number quoted from another course is the mistake
   this sequence exists to prevent.

## Answering well

**Volume is derived, not looked up.** Five variables set it: spend level, brand maturity, team
bandwidth, testing structure and goal type. ⭐ *"There's no universal number that works across the
board."*

**Consistency beats peaks.** The single most actionable line in the course is about cadence, not
volume: a steady six a week beats twelve then nothing.

**A win has four parts, and three of them are about durability.** Beating CPA is only the first;
holding at scale, across multiple variants, and over time are what separate a winner from a lucky
edit.

**Not every dead ad is bad creative.** ⚠️ *"Sometimes ads just don't get spend, or media buyers turn
off your ads before they get a fair shot... This happens more often than you'd think — and it's not
always the creative's fault."* There is a protocol for it rather than a shrug.

⚠️ **This course competes with Course 601 on creative volume and neither mentions the other.** They
use different units and different time bases. `reference/provenance.md` sets out both;
`aca-plan-creative-volume` carries the comparison.

## ⚠️ Scope

Planning and running the creative sprint. It does not set up campaigns or budgets — that is the media
buyer's, and the course is clear about the boundary. It does not define the metrics it uses, which is
Course 402. It does not design the individual tests, which is Course 602.
