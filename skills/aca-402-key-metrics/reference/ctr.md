# Click-through rate

> "CTR stands for Click-Through Rate, and it's one of the most referenced metrics for creative
> strategists. And personally, I feel like it's also **one of the most misunderstood.**"

**Link Clicks ÷ Impressions.** 1,000 impressions, 10 clicks, 1% CTR.

## ⭐ There are five CTRs in Ads Manager, and only one is the answer

> "Inside Ads Manager, there are actually multiple versions of CTR, and it's important to know which
> one you're looking at."

| CTR | What it counts | Use it? |
|---|---|---|
| **CTR (All)** | *"Any kind of click"* — expanding the caption, tapping your profile picture, pausing the video, the See More button | ⛔ *"Don't use it to evaluate creative effectiveness, because not all of those clicks are meaningful."* Fine for a general sense of engagement |
| **CTR (Link Click)** | Impressions that led to a click on the actual link — Shop Now, the headline link | ⭐ **This is the one.** *"This is your go-to CTR... This is what we looked at most closely at OpenStore for Jack Archer"* |
| **Unique CTR (Link Click)** | One click per person, however many times they clicked | Useful for how many *people* clicked. ⚠️ *"It can slightly understate performance if your ad is compelling enough to get multiple clicks from the same person"* |
| **Outbound CTR** | Clicks that take someone off Meta entirely, to your own site | Useful for measuring who left the platform. *"For most e-comm campaigns, CTR (Link) and Outbound CTR will be nearly identical"* |
| **Unique Outbound CTR** | One outbound click per person | How many individual people reached your site |

⚠️ **Where Link and Outbound diverge:** *"if your ad opens a lead form first."* The click happened
on-platform, so it is a link click and not an outbound one.

⛔ **Agree the metric before you argue about the number.** *"Make sure you're aligned with other
stakeholders on which metric you'll be tracking. I generally recommend always using CTR (Link),
which is the cleanest signal of whether your creative inspired someone to take action."* Two people
comparing CTR (All) against CTR (Link) are not disagreeing, they are measuring different things.

## The bands

> "From what we've seen at OpenStore, specifically on Jack Archer, and across thousands of paid
> social ads, here's a general rule of thumb."

| CTR (Link) | Her reading |
|---|---|
| **Below 0.5%** | *"Usually a red flag. That's when you want to go back and ask: is this ad even making sense to the viewer? Are we saying anything worth clicking?"* |
| **0.6% to 0.8%** | *"Better, but still below average. It might be worth testing a stronger hook or changing the lead visual"* |
| **0.9% to 1.3%** | ⭐ *"The sweet spot we found on Jack Archer. Ads in that range consistently pulled their weight. They drew real interest without driving low-quality clicks"* |
| **Over 1.4%**, into the 2s and 3s | *"That can be super exciting. But it's also a moment to pause and ask: **is this traffic qualified?** Sometimes really high CTRs come from curiosity clicks"* |

⛔ **These are one advertiser's numbers in men's apparel and she says so.** They are not an ACA
benchmark and not an industry one, and another ACA course gives a CTR floor that sits above this
course's "start worrying" line. See `reference/provenance.md` before quoting either.

⚠️ **Aggressive CTR goals are a known failure mode, from inside her own team.** *"At OpenStore,
we're often given aggressive CTR goals. And I get it. But what we learned over time is that **CTR is
only part of the story.**"*

## How CTR interacts with everything else

> "If your ad is driving tons of clicks, but those clicks aren't converting, **you're essentially
> lighting money on fire.**"

### CPC — cost per click

**Spend ÷ Link Clicks.** $200 and 50 link clicks is a $4 CPC; another ad with 50 clicks that cost
$100 is a $2 CPC, *"twice as efficient"*.

⚠️ *"Sometimes you'll see a high CTR but also a high CPC. That might mean the ad is working, but
it's being shown to a more expensive audience."*

### CPM — cost per thousand impressions

⚠️ *"A high CTR on a high-CPM ad might look impressive, but it can still be inefficient. Even if
your creative is performing well, if you're paying $50+ just to show the ad, it's going to be harder
to hit CPA targets."*

### ⭐ CVR — and the finding she calls controversial

> "At OpenStore for Jack Archer, we found that **high CTR didn't always mean high CVR.** In fact,
> **some of our highest CTR ads had some of the lowest conversion rates.** Why? Because they were
> curiosity clicks. People were intrigued by the ad, but didn't end up buying."

> "We also found that **some of our lowest CTR ads drove the highest ROAS**, because the few people
> who did click were actually ready to convert."

> "That's why you'll often hear me say: **CTR is directional, not definitive.**"

⚠️ **The other explanation she gives for the same pattern:** *"Sometimes the hook is so strong, it
actually over-promises, and the landing page underwhelms."* ⛔ **That is a creative fault, not a
traffic-quality one**, and the two look identical in the numbers. Distinguishing them means reading
the ad against the page, not reading the metrics harder.

⚠️ *"Over time, that creates friction between creative and performance teams. And as a creative
strategist, you risk chasing performance signals that don't actually move the business."*

**The questions this turns into:**

- *"If CTR is high but CPA is terrible, is the landing page failing?"*
- *"If CTR is average but CVR is high, should we scale this more?"*

> "**Your job is to diagnose, not just report.**"

## ⭐ What drove CTR up at Jack Archer

> "We ran multiple deep-dive studies across thousands of Jack Archer ads and campaigns, and a few
> clear trends emerged."

| Finding | The figure | Her explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Video beats static** | **12% higher CTR** | *"This was a really surprising learning. The traditional assumption is that it's easier to get someone to click on an image then on a video."* Motion catches attention, and they layered in voiceover, visual proof and story formats that *"primed them better for converting"* |
| **More creative variants** | **60% boost in CTR** | Running **6 to 12 variants** instead of their standard 4. *"Showing different angles to different segments helps more people find the 'in' that resonates with them"* |
| **Strong hooks** | **55% higher CTR** | Ads with **hook rates above 40%**. *"That correlation was consistent. So if your thumbstop is strong, and your hook rate is strong, CTR usually follows"* |

⛔ **All three figures are one advertiser's, uncontrolled, and reported without a sample size or a
time window.** They are the strongest-sounding numbers in the course and the easiest to misquote as
general laws. `Video gets 12% more clicks` is not what the material supports; `Chloe Rhys reports
video drove 12% higher CTR across Jack Archer's tests"* is.

⚠️ **She gives the exception to her own first finding.** *"Many DTC brands rely heavily on static
promo ads, and yes, **promos typically do drive a higher click-through rate** than many other types
of ads. Showing a large 'percent off' offer static image ad will likely get you a high click-through
rate."* The video advantage she measured is against statics that *"just showcase the product"*, not
against a discount static.

⚠️ **The third finding is a correlation and she calls it one.** Hook rate above 40% and CTR moving
together does not establish that lifting the hook lifts the CTR; both plausibly follow from the ad
being good. Her practical reading — check the hook first — survives either way.

## Applying it

**Her four takeaways, in order:**

1. ⭐ **Use CTR as a tool, not a trophy.** *"A high CTR with a low CVR? That's not a win. That's a
   leaky funnel."*
2. **Watch how CTR interacts with CPM, CPC and CVR.** *"Sometimes the creative is great, but the
   campaign targeting is off. Sometimes CTR tanks because the audience is tired of seeing the same
   concept."*
3. ⭐ **Test broadly and log the patterns.** *"When you find a high-CTR ad, don't just rerun it,
   study it."* What was the angle? What was the first line of copy? Was the voiceover doing the heavy
   lifting? Was it UGC or polished?
4. **Read it in context.** *"It's a clue, not a conclusion."*

> "At Jack Archer, we maintained **a running doc of CTR insights across quarters**, and over time we
> started predicting which creative would outperform, just based on hook + angle."

⭐ **That running doc is the artifact of this course.** `reference/diagnose.md` produces it.

### Isolating what worked inside a test

> "When you're testing a group of ad variants, say 6 versions of the same core concept, but with
> different opening visuals or headline copy, **CTR can help you isolate what's working.**"

**3 headers × 3 visual openers** will produce wildly different CTRs across the combinations.

⭐ **And this is the part worth keeping:** *"Even if none of them are hitting your CPA or conversion
rate goals yet, CTR can still be a directional signal. It helps you identify what's pulling people
in. From there, you can iterate toward conversion. **You keep the parts that are hooking the viewer**
— the image that gets the pause, the line that gets the click — **but you adjust the rest of the
sequence** to better prime someone for the site experience."*

**Then check the seam between ad and page:**

- Is the value prop clear enough?
- Is the product benefit tied to what they'll see next on the landing page?
- Is there a smoother connection between the creative and the page they land on?

> "Sometimes, the issue isn't the ad. It's that **the ad and the landing page feel like two separate
> conversations.** And that disconnect can kill your conversion rate, even if the ad itself is
> strong."

## Her closing position

> "here's my hot take. CTR is a helpful metric, but it is **not the ultimate measure of creative
> success.** Yes, it's one of the few metrics that creative can truly fully own. But if your team
> starts treating CTR as the only and sole goal, that is a mistake. Just because someone clicks
> doesn't mean that they're going to buy. **Engagement** is important, but engagement **without
> conversion doesn't grow your business.**"

> "CTR is a creative strategist's flashlight. It illuminates part of the path, but not the whole
> picture."
