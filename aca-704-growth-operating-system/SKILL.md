## Answering well

**The plateau has a named cause.** *"Your top performing ads suddenly hit a ceiling. Your CPMs are so
much higher and your ROAS started declining."* Her explanation is Meta's algorithm change, and it
reframes a creative problem as a systems one.

**Sameness is now actively penalised.** ⛔ *"If all your ads look the same, talk the same, and hook
the same, Meta groups them together. You get diminishing returns. You hit creative fatigue faster,
and your reach plateaus."*

**Macro personas are the failure.** *"Sarah, 35, works in marketing, lives in Austin and likes yoga.
**That's not really an effective persona.**"* Micro personas are defined by a pain point and a
posture, not a demographic.

**AI does the lifting, the strategist does the thinking.** ⭐ Said twice, and it is the course's
position on the whole toolchain: *"The AI scans, organizes, identifies patterns and suggests
directions, but the strategist will evaluate, prioritize, connect the dots across different data
sources, and make the final creative decision."*

⚠️ **Casting is part of the system, not a downstream task.** *"Match each persona with a content
creator that embodies that persona... **Authenticity is a non-negotiable here.**"*

## What changed, and why diversity became survival

### The symptom

> "If you've been running ads recently, you've probably noticed something changed. **Your top
> performing ads suddenly hit a ceiling. Your CPMs are so much higher and your ROAS started
> declining.** So what happened?"

⭐ **This is the course's hook and it is worth keeping**, because it is the exact complaint a team
brings to a strategist. Her answer is that the account did not get worse — the system it runs inside
changed.

### ⚠️ Her explanation, with her own hedge

> "Meta rolled out a massive algorithm update that fundamentally changed how the platform evaluates
> and delivers creative, and most advertisers still don't understand what's actually happening.
> **Here's what we know so far.**"

⛔ **"What we know so far" is doing real work in that sentence.** Everything below is a
practitioner's reading of an algorithm change, assembled from Meta's communications and from what her
team observes in accounts. **Repeat it as her reading, not as documented behaviour.**

#### Old system against new

| | The old system | ⭐ **Sequence learning** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it read** | *"Just pulled signals like clicks or page links, but it completely ignored the order and timing of those actions"* | *"analyzes events, streams with timestamps so it can understand exactly where a user is in their journey."* |
| **The consequence** | *"It treated every click the same, whether someone was just as covering your brand or about to buy."* | It can tell discovery from consideration from purchase |

> "Think about it when someone is in the discovery phase, they need a **completely different
> creative** than someone that's in the consideration phase or the purchase phase. **The old system
> couldn't tell the difference. The new one can.**"

#### The hierarchical ad index

> "They've built a **hierarchical ad index.** Think of it as a sophisticated filing system that
> organizes ads based on multiple dimensions."

**The dimensions she names:** format · messaging · persona · visual style · hook structure.

> "They're building **a multi dimensional map of your creative universe.** Then they're using AI to
> match the right creative to where each user is in their journey. The goal? More personalization,
> faster matching, and **less reliance on targeting.**"

⭐ **This is the "creative is your targeting" argument taken one step further.** In the earlier
version, creative selected the audience by who engaged. Here, the platform is explicitly indexing
creative by its attributes and matching on them — so the attributes themselves are the targeting
parameters.

---

### ⛔ What it means for advertisers

#### Budget no longer concentrates

| Before | Now |
|---|---|
| Upload a few ads. Meta would pick the winner. **Budgets would concentrate on that ad** | *"**No single creative dominates budget.** It's spread more evenly across all ads"* |
| The strategy: find that one-size-fits-all creative that works for everyone when targeting broad | *"Meta actively **penalizes generic, broad creative** and rewards personas. Specific ads that match users to their journey stage."* |

⭐ **That first row changes the economics of a test.** If budget no longer piles onto one winner, a
single brilliant ad cannot carry an account — which is the mechanical reason the rest of this course
is about production systems rather than about making better single ads.

#### ⛔ The grouping penalty

> "**If all your ads look the same, talk the same, and hook the same, Meta groups them together.**
> You get diminishing returns. You hit creative fatigue faster, and your reach plateaus."

> "But if you have **true creative diversity** — different formats, different personas, different
> hooks, different motivators — **Meta sees each as a distinct entity.** You unlock new audiences,
> you extend your reach, and you avoid the grouping penalty."

> "This is why creative volume and diversity aren't just nice to have anymore. **They're literally
> survival.**"

⚠️ **"Grouping penalty" is her term.** It describes an observed effect, not a documented Meta
mechanism with that name. The observation — near-identical ads returning less than their count
suggests — is one many practitioners report; the causal story is hers.

⭐ **The load-bearing distinction, and the whole course in one line:**

> "**It's not about making 100 variations of the same ad. It's about making fundamentally different
> ads for fundamentally different people.**"

⛔ **This is what separates diversity from volume**, and it is the thing most teams get wrong when
they are told to ship more. Fifty variations of one concept is exactly the input the grouping penalty
punishes. The four dimensions she names as real diversity are **format, persona, hook and
motivator** — and persona is the one that generates the other three.

### Her closing position

> "The brands that win in 2026 and beyond **won't be the ones with the best single ads. They'll be
> the ones with the best creative system** designed for how Andromeda actually works."

⚠️ **A dated claim, deliberately.** It names a year and a specific algorithm regime. Check whether
the regime still holds before building a plan on it — the argument for diversity survives most
platform changes, but the mechanism she attributes it to may not.

> "It all starts with research. I'm not talking about surface level research. I'm talking about
> **becoming obsessed with understanding your audience better than they understand themselves. The
> brands that do the most research when full stop,**"

---

### Pillar 1 — Competitor research, AI-assisted

> "We don't just look at what competitors are running. We use AI to analyze their **entire creative
> strategy**, identifying hooks, emotional triggers, messaging patterns and proof elements."

**What she looks for:**

| Looking at what they do | ⭐ Looking at what they don't |
|---|---|
| What hooks are they using repeatedly? | **Where are the gaps?** |
| What pain points are they agitating? | **What aren't they saying?** |
| What proof elements build trust? | **What audiences are they ignoring?** |
| What calls to action are they testing? | **What angles are they missing?** |

> "**That's where we find our competitive advantage.**"

⭐ **The right-hand column is the point.** Cataloguing what competitors run produces a swipe file;
cataloguing what they avoid produces a position.

#### Her workflow

Custom **Gumloop** workflows connected to the **Foreplay API**:

1. Input a competitor brand name
2. Scan their entire ad library and identify ⭐ **the top 20 ads that have been running the longest** —
   *"usually, these tend to be the winners"*
3. Watch and analyse each systematically
4. Generate an analysis sheet breaking down: **format** (UGC, founder-led, testimonial) · **hook
   category and the exact opening** · **visual style and production approach** · **persona targeted**
   · **angle and key messaging** · **proof elements** · **calls to action**

> "What used to take a creative strategist? Hours of manual work now can be done in just a few
> minutes."

⭐ **Longevity as the winner signal is the transferable idea here**, and it needs no tooling: an ad
still running after months is one somebody keeps paying for.

⛔ **Her stated boundary, and she repeats it later:** *"**AI isn't replacing the strategist, it's
doing the heavy lifting.** The AI will give us the data, the strategist interprets it, identifies the
gaps, and develops the counter-strategy."*

### Pillar 2 — Micro personas

#### ⛔ Why macro personas fail

> "This is where most brands get it wrong. They create these broad macro personas. **'Sarah, 35,
> works in marketing, lives in Austin and likes yoga.' That's not really an effective persona.**"

**Her worked example, a footwear brand.** The macro persona is *"young women interested in
comfortable shoes."* The micro personas are:

| Micro persona |
|---|
| **The skeptical shopper** who has been burned by false comfort claims before |
| **The frequent traveler** who needs shoes that work for 12-hour days |
| **The fashion enthusiast** who won't compromise style for comfort |
| **The pregnant mom** desperately seeking relief for swollen feet |
| **The dog mom** who needs durable shoes for daily walks |

> "Each of these micro personas has **completely different pain points emotional motivators,
> objections, proof needs, and language patterns.** And here's the critical part. **Each micro
> persona requires different creative.**"

⭐ **Notice what defines each one: a situation and a posture, never a demographic.** "Pregnant mom"
is a life stage with a specific physical problem; "the skeptical shopper" is a history of being
disappointed. Neither is an age bracket, and both can be written to.

#### What a persona must answer

| Question | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Who are they?** | Demographics and psychographics |
| **What do they care about?** | Motivators and values |
| **Why do they buy?** | Pain points and desires |
| ⭐ **What language do they use?** | *"These are literally the verbatim customer quotes"* |

#### Step 1 — find the verbatim quotes

> "The best creative strategists go deep. They systematically mined forums, reviews, comments
> sections to uncover **the emotional language, objections, and desires** that shape high performing
> creative."

| Source | Her note |
|---|---|
| **Reddit threads and Reddit Answers** | Search the subreddits for your product category |
| **Giga Brain** | An AI tool that surfaces the most useful Reddit discussions |
| **Amazon reviews** | ⭐ *"Especially those **three-star and four-star** reviews. They're the most honest"* |
| **YouTube comments** | On competitor videos |
| **Facebook group discussions** | |
| **Customer support** | Your own tickets |

> "You're looking for **the raw, unfiltered language** — real people describing real problems in
> their own words."

⭐ **The three-and-four-star tip is the sharpest thing in this section.** Five-star reviews are
enthusiasm and one-star reviews are usually a delivery complaint; the middle is where people say what
is genuinely good and genuinely not.

#### Step 2 — sort by emotion, and the angle appears

> "Once you've gathered dozens of quotes, **sort them by the underlying emotion. This will reveal
> your creative angles.**"

**Her two worked examples**, quoted as the recording renders them. ⚠️ The second opens *"by our H"*, which is the transcript's rendering of **by hour four**:

| Quote | Emotion | Persona | ⭐ Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| *"Every pair of comfortable shoes I've bought ends up hurting after a week. I don't believe the marketing anymore"* | **Skepticism** | The skeptical shopper | **Honest proof over empty promises** |
| *"by our H of my travel day. I'm limping through the airport. I have tried everything"* | **Exhaustion / frustration** | The frequent traveler | **Comfort that actually lasts 12+ hours** |

> "When you map emotions to micro personas, **you get a matrix of angles to test, and you're not
> guessing. You're using the exact language your audience already uses.**"

⛔ **This is the step that must never be faked.** The output's whole authority comes from the quotes
being real. **Never write a quote you did not find** — if no research was supplied, say what would be
searched and where, and produce nothing else.

#### Her AI workflow for it

Raw data in → **AI clusters by underlying emotion** (frustration, fear, excitement, guilt, overwhelm,
desire) → **suggests angles and hooks using the actual language from the quotes** → **identifies and
maps the micro personas**, filling a persona sheet for each.

⛔ **And the boundary again, stated more fully:** *"**AI is doing the heavy lifting, but the
strategist is doing the strategic thinking.** The AI scans, organizes, identifies patterns and
suggests directions, but the strategist will evaluate, prioritize, connect the dots across different
data sources, and make the final creative decision that actually moves the business."*

#### ⭐ The burner account

> "create a **burner Instagram or** a **TikTok account** and behave exactly like your micro persona.
> If you're targeting the pregnant mom, for example, you create a new account and you start
> following pregnancy accounts. You like content about third trimester struggles. You watch videos
> about maternity fashion. You engage with posts about swollen feet and discomfort, and **within 48
> hours, your feeds will transform to show exactly what that person sees**"

**What it gives you:** what content patterns they are used to · what hooks stop their scroll · what
tone and style feels native · what your ads need to look like to blend in.

> "**Your ad shouldn't stand out as an ad.** It should blend seamlessly into their natural content
> consumption, and then pattern-interrupt with your message."

⭐ **This is the cheapest research method in the entire programme** — no tool, no budget, 48 hours.
And it produces the one thing a quote cannot: what the persona's feed actually looks like.

#### Casting against the persona

> "Once you have your micro personas mapped, the next step is crucial. **Match each persona with a
> content creator that embodies that persona.** You're not just hiring random creators — **you're
> casting people who authentically represent your audience.**"

*"The skeptical shopper needs to be played by someone who genuinely comes across as skeptical... the
pregnant mom needs to be a creator who's actually been through that experience. **Authenticity is a
non-negotiable here. Audiences can spot a fake from a mile away.**"*

### Pillar 3 — Platform trends, through four lenses

> "most people get wrong. They just scroll TikTok and reels looking for viral videos **without
> actually understanding what they're** actually **looking for.** You need a systematic framework"

| # | Lens | What to document |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trends** | Recurring content formats. *Get Ready With Me · Day in the Life · What I Eat in a Day · Things I Wish I Knew Before · POV* |
| 2 | **Themes** | The narrative arcs and story structures. *What emotions do they evoke? What problems do they address? How do they structure the story?* |
| 3 | **Sound** | Trending audio, viral sound bites, voiceover styles, music choices. Original against trending against ASMR, and how sound drives pacing |
| 4 | **Visual effects** | Editing techniques, transitions, camera angles, filters, pattern interrupts |

⭐ **The instruction attached to lens 1 is the one that makes this more than a swipe file:**

> "**Don't just note the format. Note how it connects the target persona** and **their pain points
> and solutions.** I get ready with me for a busy mom, solving a morning chaos problem is very
> different than I get ready with me. For a fashion enthusiast, that's showcasing her style. **Same
> format, different persona, different pain points. Different**"

**On themes:** *"The key is documenting **the why** behind what makes this content resonate."*

**On sound:** *"**Sound is an emotional amplifier.** It works as an ear-stopper in your hook. Don't
just save the sound — note **why** it works emotionally and how you could translate it to your
brand."*

**On visual effects**, the specifics she names: the paper burn · the zoom · clone effects · snap
transitions · camera angles (POV, overhead, mirror selfie, walking POV) · text animations, on-screen
effects, green screens · pattern interrupts like quick zooms, sudden zooms and split screens.

⭐ **Every lens ends with the same instruction: record the why, not the what.** A saved reference you
cannot explain is a screenshot; one you can explain is a technique.

### What a concept is, in this course

> Why we are selling it, why people should care — the angle. And who we are talking to — the
