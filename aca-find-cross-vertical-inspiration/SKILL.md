# Find cross-vertical creative inspiration

Ad Creative Academy's Course 230 artifact, run for a specific brand. This skill runs the research.
It does not teach cross-channel strategy.

> "This prompt helps you **break out of your industry bubble** and discover fresh creative
> approaches that will make your ads stand out while still resonating with your target audience."

**The output is a research plan, not a set of findings.** That distinction is the whole skill; see
below.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **The product or service**, described specifically
2. **The target audience** — and ⭐ **psychographics, not just demographics.** ACA is explicit that
   this is what decides whether the output is any good: *"Mentioning interests, values, and
   lifestyle factors will yield better matches than demographics alone."*
3. **Your current creative approach** — what your messaging and creative actually look like now

**Optional, and it sharpens the output:**

4. What you have already tried and found stale
5. Which markets you sell in, since vertical overlap is geographic
6. Any category you cannot be seen next to

⚠️ **All three required inputs do real work and none is a formality.** Without the current approach,
the output cannot tell you what is *different* from what you already do — which is the entire point.
Ask for it if it is missing.

## ⛔ The failure this skill exists to prevent

**This prompt asks a model to name specific brands in industries it has not researched, and it will
name them, confidently.**

Some will be real brands running exactly the creative described. Some will be real brands that have
never run anything like it. Some will be plausible-sounding names attached to nothing. **All three
arrive in the same format, in the same tone, in one list.**

So the output is never a finding. It is a **hypothesis about where to look.**

| ⛔ Never say | ✅ Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Liquid Death uses shock humour in their ads" | "Liquid Death is worth checking for shock humour — verify in the Meta Ad Library" |
| "These five brands all lead with founder stories" | "Check whether these five lead with founder stories; that is the pattern to test for" |
| "Here are 8 verticals and what they do" | "Here are 8 verticals to research, and what to look for in each" |

**Every brand named is a research target.** Write the output so that survives being skim-read — the
verification step is where the value actually is, and a reader who skips it has a document of
confident fiction.

⚠️ **The verticals are more reliable than the brands.** Reasoning about which industries chase the
same audience is a genuine inference from the audience description. Naming which specific companies
run which specific creative is a claim about the world. Weight your confidence accordingly, and say
so in the output.

## How to run it

1. **Confirm the three required inputs.** Ask for whichever is missing, and push for psychographics
   if the audience is described demographically.
2. **Run the prompt** in **The prompt** (below), or produce its four outputs directly.
3. **Frame every named brand as a target to verify**, per the rule above.
4. **Deliver the verification plan**, not just the list — **Verifying the output, and turning it into something real** (below) has ACA's
   four-step workflow, which is exactly a verification loop.
5. **End with 2 to 3 test concepts**, which is where ACA's own workflow lands.

## Getting the output right

**Unexpected is the requirement, not a flavour.** The prompt asks for verticals *"that likely target
the same core audience but sell completely different products/services."* If the list comes back
full of adjacent categories — skincare suggesting haircare — it has failed, and the fix is a sharper
audience description rather than a rerun.

**Say what the shared audience actually is.** A vertical is only worth studying if you can name why
its buyers overlap with yours. *"Both sell to time-poor professionals who treat spending as
self-permission"* is a reason. `Both are premium` is not.

**Emotional triggers are the transferable part.** Products do not transfer across verticals; the
trigger being pulled does. That is what makes step 4 of the prompt — techniques to adapt — the
output that matters.

**Do not let it become a competitor audit.** The moment a direct competitor appears in the list, the
prompt has drifted back into the bubble it exists to escape.

## The prompt

ACA's Cross-Vertical Creative Inspiration Finder, reproduced as written. The `[BRACKETED]`
placeholders are the interface — fill them, do not rewrite them.

```
I'm looking for cross-industry creative inspiration for my [PRODUCT/SERVICE]. I want to find brands
in completely different verticals that target a similar audience so I can study their creative
approaches.

My product/service: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT OR SERVICE]

My target audience: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRIMARY AUDIENCE - include demographics, psychographics, pain
points, desires]

My current creative approach: [BRIEFLY DESCRIBE YOUR CURRENT MESSAGING AND CREATIVE STYLE]

Based on this information, please:

1. Identify 7-10 unexpected verticals/industries that likely target the same core audience but sell
   completely different products/services

2. For each vertical, suggest:
   - 2-3 specific brands to study
   - What creative elements to pay attention to (hooks, storytelling approaches, etc.)
   - Why their creative might resonate with my audience
   - What emotional triggers they likely leverage

3. Recommend which specific platforms/channels would be best for researching these brands' creative
   approaches

4. Suggest 3-5 specific creative techniques or approaches from these other verticals that I could
   adapt to my industry in a fresh way
```

### ⚠️ What each of the four outputs is worth

They are not equally reliable, and the document treats them as if they were.

| Output | How much to trust it |
|---|---|
| **1. The verticals** | ⭐ **Most reliable.** A genuine inference from the audience description — reasoning about who else sells to these people |
| **2a. The named brands** | ⛔ **Least reliable.** A claim about which real companies exist and what creative they run. **Verify every one** |
| **2b-d. What to look at, why it resonates, the triggers** | Useful as a *lens* for the research, not as a description of what you will find |
| **3. Where to research** | Reliable, and largely generic — the ad libraries and the brands' own channels |
| **4. Techniques to adapt** | ⭐ **The actual deliverable**, but only after step 2 has been verified. Techniques derived from ads nobody checked are fiction |

⭐ **Note that output 2 asks for what triggers a brand "likely" leverages.** That hedge is ACA's own,
and it is honest — the prompt is asking for a hypothesis. Keep the hedge in the output; do not
upgrade "likely" to "does".

## ACA's worked example

Reproduced because it shows the level of audience detail the prompt needs to work.

```
My product/service: Premium organic skincare subscription box targeting women 30-45

My target audience: Professional women, ages 30-45, household income $100k+, health-conscious,
environmentally aware, time-starved but prioritize self-care, willing to pay premium for quality
and convenience, concerned about aging but want natural solutions

My current creative approach: Mostly before/after transformations, dermatologist testimonials, and
ingredient education focused on clean beauty and natural results
```

⭐ **Look at what the audience line actually contains.** Two demographic facts (age, income) and six
psychographic ones — health-conscious, environmentally aware, time-starved, prioritises self-care,
pays for convenience, wants natural rather than clinical solutions.

**Those six are what generate unexpected verticals.** "Women 30-45, $100k+" could describe half the
market. "Time-starved but prioritises self-care, pays premium for convenience" points somewhere
specific — meal kits, boutique fitness, high-end domestic services, premium travel.

⚠️ **And note the third field is doing work too.** *"Before/after transformations, dermatologist
testimonials, ingredient education"* tells the prompt what is already exhausted, so it can propose
something that is not more of it.

## ACA's four tips

Reproduced as written, with what each is actually guarding against.

**1. Be specific about your audience.** *"The more detailed your audience description, the more
relevant the cross-vertical suggestions will be."*

**2. Include psychographics.** *"Mentioning interests, values, and lifestyle factors will yield
better matches than demographics alone."*

⭐ **This is the one that decides whether the output is useful.** A demographic-only description
produces adjacent categories, because demographics are shared by everyone; values and behaviours are
what create genuine cross-vertical overlap.

**3. Provide context.** *"Briefly explaining your current approach helps identify truly different
perspectives."*

Without it, the prompt cannot tell what would be *new* to you, and will confidently propose the
thing you have been doing for a year.

**4. Stay open-minded.** *"The most valuable inspiration often comes from the most unexpected
sources."*

⚠️ The practical form of this: when the list contains a vertical that seems absurd, that is the one
to check first, provided you can name the shared audience. Absurd-with-a-reason is the target;
absurd-without-one is noise.

## Verifying the output, and turning it into something real

ACA's Part 2, *"How to Use the Results"*, is the second half of the asset and the half that gets
skipped. **It is also, read properly, a verification loop** — which is exactly what the output of
this prompt needs.

⭐ **Deliver these steps alongside the list, every time.** A list of verticals and brands with no
research plan attached will be read as findings, and half of it is not.

---

### Step 1 — Create a research plan

> "Prioritize 3-5 verticals that seem most interesting or unexpecte"

**Not all 7 to 10.** The prompt over-generates deliberately; the first act of judgement is cutting
it down.

**Prioritise on two things:**

1. **Can you name the shared audience?** If you cannot say in one sentence why that vertical's
   buyers overlap with yours, drop it however interesting it sounds.
2. **How far is it from what you already do?** The value is in distance. A vertical whose creative
   resembles yours teaches you nothing even if the audience overlap is perfect.

### Step 2 — Conduct platform-specific research

> "Check Meta Ad Library for the suggested brands · Visit their Instagram and TikTok accounts · Look
> at their YouTube channel for longer-form content · Visit their website to understand their overall
> positioning."

⭐ **This is the verification step, and it is the moment the hypothesis becomes evidence.**

**Start with the Meta Ad Library**, because it answers the question that matters first: *is this
brand actually running ads, and do they look anything like what was described?*

Three outcomes, and all three are useful:

| What you find | What it means |
|---|---|
| The brand runs ads matching the description | ⭐ Confirmed. Study it properly |
| The brand runs ads, but nothing like the description | The vertical may still be right; the *characterisation* was wrong. Look at what they actually do |
| The brand does not exist, or runs nothing | ⛔ Discard, and note it. If several in one vertical fail this way, distrust that whole row |

⚠️ **Record what failed verification, not just what passed.** A research plan that reports only its
hits looks like a perfect hit rate, and the next person to use this prompt will trust it more than
they should.

### Step 3 — Build a creative swipe file

> "Save 3-5 examples from each brand that use approaches you find compelling · Analyze what specific
> elements make them effective · Note how they trigger emotional responses that would resonate with
> your audience."

**Save the artefact, not the impression.** A screenshot or a link, with a note of what it does — not
"good hook."

⚠️ **The third instruction is the one that makes this cross-vertical rather than a mood board:** note
how the trigger would land *on your audience*, not on theirs. An ad that works on climbers because
they are climbers teaches you nothing. An ad that works on climbers because it treats gear as
identity might transfer to anyone whose purchase is identity-shaped.

### Step 4 — Integrate insights

> "Look for patterns across different verticals · Identify approaches that feel fresh for your
> category · Consider how to adapt these techniques while maintaining authenticity to your brand."

⭐ **Patterns across verticals are worth more than any single example.** One brand doing something
is a tactic. Four unrelated industries doing the same thing to the same audience is a mechanism, and
mechanisms transfer.

⚠️ *"While maintaining authenticity to your brand"* is doing real work. The technique transfers; the
voice does not. A category that is playful because its product is low-stakes will hand you a tone
that is wrong for a considered purchase, and copying it produces an ad that reads as a costume.

### Step 5 — Develop testing concepts

> "Create 2-3 test concepts inspired by cross-vertical insights · **Maintain your core value
> proposition while experimenting with new creative approaches.**"

**This is where the exercise lands.** Not a report — two or three things to make.

⭐ **The constraint is the important half.** Hold the value proposition constant and change only the
creative approach. Otherwise a test tells you nothing: if both the message and the execution moved,
a win cannot be attributed to either.

## What a good delivered output looks like

Structure the deliverable as five parts, in this order:

1. **The verticals, ranked, with the shared audience named for each.** State that this is the most
   reliable part of the analysis.
2. **The brands to check, explicitly labelled as unverified.** Include what to look for in each and
   the trigger you expect — as a lens for looking, not a finding.
3. **The verification plan** — which library, which handles, in what order.
4. **The techniques to adapt**, flagged as provisional until step 2 confirms the examples they rest
   on.
5. **The two or three test concepts**, with the value proposition held constant and stated.

⚠️ **Put the verification status in the document, not in the conversation around it.** A section
header line — `UNVERIFIED: brands below are research targets, not findings` — travels with the list
when someone pastes it into a deck. A caveat in chat does not.
