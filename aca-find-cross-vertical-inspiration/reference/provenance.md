**Ad Creative Academy** — *AI Prompt: Cross-Vertical Creative Inspiration Finder*, shipped with

## What is reproduced and what is this skill's

**Reproduced as written:** the prompt in full with its `[BRACKETED]` placeholders, ACA's complete
worked example (the premium organic skincare subscription box), all five steps of Part 2, and all
four tips from Part 3.

**This skill's, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The verification discipline.** The prompt asks a model to name specific brands in industries
  it has not researched. It will do so confidently, and some of those brands will not run the
  creative described — or will not exist. ACA does not warn about this anywhere. The framing of
  every named brand as a **research target rather than a finding**, the ⛔/✅ phrasing table, and
  the instruction to put the unverified status inside the document are all this skill's.
- **The reliability ranking of the prompt's four outputs.** The verticals are an inference from the
  audience description and are relatively sound; the named brands are claims about the world and are
  not. ACA presents all four outputs as equivalent.
- **The reading of Part 2 as a verification loop.** ACA presents it as "how to use the results";
  functionally, step 2 is where a hypothesis becomes evidence, and that is how it is framed here.
- **The three outcomes of checking a brand in the ad library**, including the useful middle case
  where the vertical is right and the characterisation was wrong.
- **The instruction to record what failed verification**, not only what passed.
- **The note that psychographics are what generate unexpected verticals**, worked through ACA's own
  example to show which six phrases in it are doing the work.
- The structure of the delivered output, and the guidance on prioritising verticals by whether the
  shared audience can be named in one sentence.

## ⚠️ Two errors in ACA's own document

Neither changes the method; both are visible to anyone reading the PDF.

1. **Part 2's steps are misnumbered.** They run **1, 2, 3, 3, 4** — there are five steps and two of
   them are numbered 3. This skill numbers them 1 to 5 in `verify-and-use.md`, which is a
   renumbering rather than a change to the content.
2. **A truncated word.** Step 1 reads *"Prioritize 3-5 verticals that seem most interesting or
   unexpecte"*. The intended word is *unexpected*, and it is completed silently where quoted.

There is also a stray character on the final content page (*"aCross Channel Strategies"* in the
footer), which is a layout artefact rather than anything meaningful.

## ⚠️ How old this material is

ACA stamps no date on the asset. It was received into this library in **2026-W31** — a "no later
than", not a publication date.

**What dates here is thinner than in the platform courses**, which is a point in this asset's
favour: the prompt itself is method rather than platform mechanics. The one time-bound element is
Part 2's list of research surfaces — the Meta Ad Library, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube — which are
named as places to look and may change availability or access.

⛔ **The example brands a run of this prompt produces are always current-as-of-nothing.** They are
generated at run time by whatever model is running it, not carried in this asset, so their accuracy
is a property of the run and not of ACA's document. This is the reason the verification step exists.

## Deliberate overlap with `aca-230-cross-channel`

Both skills relate to Course 230, on purpose, and each must run with nothing else installed.

The split is by job:

- **`aca-230-cross-channel`** teaches the course — multi-channel strategy, push versus pull, the
  platform personalities, funnel mapping, and the four in-course AI prompts. It names this asset
  and routes here rather than paraphrasing the prompt.
- **This skill** runs this one prompt for a specific brand and does not teach cross-channel
  strategy.

Route on whether there is a real brand and a stale-creative problem on the table.
