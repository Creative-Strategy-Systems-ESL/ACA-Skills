# Run the Creative QA Checklist

Produces **Ad Creative Academy's Course 501 artifact** — Mirella Crespi's eight-page Creative QA
Checklist, filled.

> "Use this checklist to ensure quality and performance optimization for all creative assets
> **before launching them in your ad accounts.**"

⭐ **ACA states two purposes, and the second is the one people miss:**

> "This tool serves two purposes: **catching technical errors** and **optimizing for performance.**
> The performance sections are particularly valuable for **training team members to develop their
> creative intuition.**"

⚠️ **So a filled checklist is a teaching document as well as a gate.** Write the "needs attention"
notes so somebody learns from them, not just so the row is filled.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **The asset**, or a detailed enough description to judge it — what happens on screen and when,
   what is said, what is on screen as text
2. **The platform and placement** it is going to run on. Half the technical rows cannot be scored
   without it

**Optional, and each one turns rows from unscoreable into scoreable:**

3. The storyboard or brief it was made from. ⛔ Without it, the whole Creative Integrity section is
   unscoreable — it asks whether anything was missed
4. Brand guidelines: colours, logo, fonts, required disclaimers
5. The other hooks in the batch, for the Hook Variation section
6. The conversion objective, for the CTA rows
7. Whether it is a branded, native or expert ad

⛔ **Never mark a row from a description that could not settle it.** *"Clean audio with no background
noise"* cannot be judged from a script. **An unscoreable row is marked `Cannot assess`**, with what
would be needed. A checklist of confident ticks that nobody actually verified is worse than no
checklist, because it certifies the ad.

⛔ **Never invent a brand guideline.** If the brand's fonts, colours and disclaimers were not
supplied, the Brand Compliance rows are `Cannot assess` — not a pass.

## How to run it

**Both halves, in order.** Technical Review first, because a failed spec makes the performance
questions academic.

1. ⛔ **Confirm the asset is actually here.** This skill cannot run on a description of an ad; it needs the
   file or a frame-by-frame account of it.
2. **Establish the placement and platform**, because the spec rows are judged against them.
3. **Run the Technical Review** from `reference/the-checklist.md`, in the order the checklist gives.
4. **Then the performance half**, same file, including the runtime bands and the heartbeat method.
5. **Mark every row** with one of the four marks below. ⛔ Leaving a row blank reads as a pass.
6. **Emit the verdict from coverage, not just failures.** ⚠️ A checklist with six *Cannot assess* rows and no
   failures is not a pass — say what could not be judged and what is needed to judge it.
7. **Name who else should review it**, per ACA's two process rules below.

**Every row gets one of four marks:**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Good to Go** | Checked and passes |
| **Needs attention** | Checked and fails, with what to change |
| **Cannot assess** | ⛔ Not judgeable from what was supplied, with what is missing |
| **N/A** | Genuinely does not apply, with why in the cell |

⭐ **ACA's own two process rules, and they pull against each other on purpose:**

> "**Have at least two team members review each asset** but keep the QA process efficient and
> focused."

> "The QA process should be **thorough but not endless.** Establish clear guidelines and timeframes
> to prevent perfectionism from delaying your testing and learning cycles."

⚠️ **This skill is one set of eyes, not two.** Say so when handing the filled checklist over: ACA
asks for a second human reviewer, and an automated pass does not satisfy that.

## ⚠️ What this skill is not

**It is not a performance prediction.** Every row is a craft check on the asset in front of you.
Whether it works is decided by the numbers after it runs, which is `aca-402-key-metrics`.

**It is not a strategy review.** Whether the concept was right is Course 401's question, and whether
the storyboard was right is Course 403's.

**It is not a legal or compliance sign-off.** It flags missing disclaimers and copyright risk as
craft items; it does not replace whoever actually approves claims.
