# Course 302: Research for Creative Strategy

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake, ACA's founder. 1h 00m 52s, 4 modules.

> "Great creative strategy is not built on guesswork. It's built on insights. The more you know
> about your own ad accounts, audience, competitors, and current platform trends, the stronger your
> creative output will be."

Four research passes, in order. Each one feeds the next.

| # | Pass | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your own account** | What is already working here, and why |
| 2 | **Your audience** | What they actually say, want, and fear |
| 3 | **Your competitors** | What the landscape looks like and where the gap is |
| 4 | **The platform** | What language this platform is currently speaking |

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what hook rate is or how to build a persona and get
an answer.

**To run any of the four passes on a real brand, required:**

1. **Which pass you want.** They are separate jobs with separate inputs.
2. **The brand**, and what it sells.

**Then, per pass:**

| Pass | Needs |
|---|---|
| Account audit | ⛔ **Exported performance data.** There is no way around this. See below |
| Audience research | The category, and access to reviews, forums or comments to mine |
| Competitor landscape | A named competitor set, or enough about the brand to build one |
| Trends | The platform, and the brand's tolerance for looking native versus branded |

⛔ **The account audit cannot be run without the numbers, and this is the most common way this
course gets misused.** Mirella is explicit that the AI prompts *"aren't replacements for accessing
your ad data — you'll still need to pull those reports."* If someone asks for an audit without
supplying data, say what to export rather than producing a plausible-looking audit of an account
nobody has looked at.

## The order matters, and the course says so

> "Now that we have analyzed your own performance and we've gotten inside your customers' mind,
> let's see what you're up against."

**Account first, then audience, then competitors, then trends.** The account audit tells you what
already works, which is the only benchmark the other three passes get measured against. Running
competitor research first produces a list of things to copy with nothing to compare them to.

⚠️ **The one exception is a brand with no account history.** Then pass 1 has no data and passes 2
and 3 carry the weight. Say so rather than inventing a baseline.

## How to run it

⛔ **The order is the teaching, and the course says so** — see the section below. Four passes, and doing them
out of order produces research that cannot be briefed from.

1. **Audit their own account first**, using `reference/audit-the-account.md`. What already worked is the
   cheapest insight available and it constrains everything after.
2. **Mine the audience** with `reference/audience-research.md`, building personas from customer language
   rather than demographics.
3. **Map the competitors** using `reference/competitor-landscape.md`. ⚠️ Landscape, not teardown — the
   tactical version is Course 301.
4. **Read the trends** through `reference/trends-research.md`, as Moments, Signals and Forces.
5. ⭐ **Produce the artifacts through the four executables** rather than here: `aca-audit-ad-account`,
   `aca-build-audience-personas`, `aca-analyze-competitor-creative`, `aca-research-trends`. ⚠️ Name one only
   if you can see it installed.
6. **Check each pass produced something briefable** — a winning element, a persona quote, a gap, a template.
   Four documents with no brief in them means the passes were done as homework.

## Answering well

**Break performance down by element, not by ad.** *"Analyze your creative performance by specific
elements rather than just looking at top-line results."* "This ad did well" is not a finding. "Ads
opening on the problem scenario beat ads opening on the product" is.

**Distinguish the enduring from the temporary.** The reason the audit looks at 7 days, 30 days and 6
months is to separate *"enduring creative principles versus temporary trends"*. A conclusion drawn
from one window is a guess.

**Never treat a competitor's long-running ad as proven.** *"Just because an ad is running doesn't
mean it's working."* This is the course's own myth-bust and it is the most commonly ignored line in
it.

**Use the audience's words, not yours.** *"We're not inventing messaging — we're just translating
our audience's exact pain points into solution-focused hooks."* If a persona's language section is
written in marketing English, the copy mining did not happen.

⚠️ **Trends are for the structure, not the content.** *"You're looking for patterns you can adapt,
not specific content to copy."* And the goal is explicitly not to chase them: *"we want to create
evergreen ads that can scale."*

## ⚠️ Scope

**This is not a paid-media audit.** Mirella says it directly in the delivered session: *"these are
not ad account audits from the paid media perspective."* It looks at creative. It does not cover
bidding, budget structure, campaign architecture or account hygiene, and an answer that drifts into
those is out of scope.

It also stops before the creative gets made. Concepts, scripts, storyboards and production are later
courses.
