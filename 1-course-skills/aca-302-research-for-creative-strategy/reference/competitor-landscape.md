# Pass 3: The competitor landscape

Module 3 (14m 54s).

> "Paid social advertising is a competitive sport. Who else is fighting for your audience's
> attention and wallet? How are they positioning themselves? What can we learn from them?"

> "But here's the thing — and I want to be super clear about this — **we're not looking at
> competitors to copy them. We're investigating to get inspired and find gaps in the market that we
> can own.** Big difference."

## ⛔ The myth this module exists to kill

> "Let's bust a myth right away: **just because an ad is running doesn't mean it's working.** I can't
> tell you how many times I've seen brands dump money into campaigns that are absolute duds simply
> because they had the budget to keep them going."

> "Those 'top ads' you see featured in showcases? Sometimes they're just the ones with the biggest
> spend behind them, not necessarily the best performers."

And from the delivered session, the reason you can never be sure:

> "You never know what their conversion objective is or what their CPA goal is."

⭐ **This is the most important caution in the whole course and it is routinely ignored.** A
competitor ad's longevity is evidence of budget and attention, not of profitability. You cannot see
their target, their margin, or whether anyone is watching the account. **Long-running is a reason to
look harder, never a reason to copy.**

⚠️ **ACA's own Course 301 leans the other way**, treating run length as *"that's your winner, that's
the pattern to study."* Both are defensible: 301 is reading run length as the best signal available
from outside, and 302 is warning that the signal is weak. Where both skills are installed, **carry
the tension rather than picking a side**: run length is the best outside signal *and* it is not
proof.

## What you are actually looking for

> "What we want is strategic insight. When you look at what others are doing, you start to notice
> patterns:
> - Which angles keep showing up across multiple brands?
> - What hooks seem to be working in your space?
> - Are there messaging approaches everyone's using — or maybe an obvious angle everyone's missing?"

> "From there, you can make smarter decisions about whether to differentiate with something
> completely fresh or put your unique spin on a proven approach."

**And beyond the direct set:**

> "Some of my biggest creative breakthroughs came from studying brands in completely different
> industries that happened to target similar audiences. A skincare brand can learn a ton from how a
> fitness app hooks their audience, because at the end of the day, they might be speaking to the
> exact same person."

## The tools

### Meta Ad Library

*"It's free, it's comprehensive, and it's incredibly revealing."*

- Search competitors by name, or by keywords for the category
- **How many active ads** each brand is running — hints at budget and testing velocity
- **How long ads have been active** — *"something running for months might be a winner"*
- Which **formats** they use
- ⭐ **How their hooks evolve.** *"If you see a competitor using the same hook structure but with
  different visuals or products, they've probably found a winner they're scaling."*

That last one is the strongest read available, because it is a *behavioural* signal: repetition with
variation is what a brand does when it has something it does not want to lose.

### TikTok Creative Center

> "Similar idea, but with an important caveat — their 'Top Ads' section can be misleading. These are
> often sorted by engagement, not by actual business results. **Great for seeing what stops the
> scroll, not always great for seeing what drives conversion.**"

- Filter to your industry and objective
- Look at **hook styles** — text reveals, shocking statements, before-and-afters
- Look at **talent** — founders, everyday users, professional creators

> "One pro tip: when an ad has tons of likes but few substantive comments, be skeptical. But when you
> see comments like 'Just ordered!' or 'This solved my exact problem!' — that's a much stronger
> signal."

### Google Ads Transparency Center

*"Especially if your product is more consideration-based or has a strong educational component."*

- Start with searches on category keywords, study headlines and copy
- The Transparency Center shows **any brand's entire YouTube ad history** — what is running now, when
  it started, in which regions

> "YouTube ads often use completely different creative approaches than social. They tend to be
> longer, more story-driven, and sometimes more educational."

Watch: how they structure the **first 5 seconds** (before the skip button), whether they use
testimonials, demos or storytelling, and how they handle the CTA.

⭐ **And the inference worth stealing:** *"If a competitor is investing heavily in YouTube, they're
usually playing a longer game with higher customer values. This can reveal a lot about their
business model and where they see the highest ROI."*

### Ad intelligence platforms

**aduniverse.ai** for spend levels: *"If a competitor is dumping serious cash into one specific
campaign, it might be a winner — or they might be a VC-funded startup with more money than sense.
Either way, it's worth investigating."*

**Foreplay or Motion** to organise, tag and track competitor ads over time, which is how testing
strategy becomes visible:

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Small tweaks of the same core creative | Optimization |
| Constantly different approaches | Exploration |
| Certain angles returning in new formats | Scalable winners |

> "When I'm analyzing competitors, I tag everything by creative approach: UGC testimonial, founder
> story, product demo, before/after transformation, etc. This makes patterns jump out immediately.""

## The Competitor Analysis Framework

Five dimensions, one pass per competitor.

**1. Creative volume and budget indicators**
- How many unique creatives are running right now?
- How frequently do they test new concepts?
- One platform or several?

> "A brand running 50+ variations is probably doing systematic testing and has significant budget."

**2. Format strategy**
- Ratio of video to static to carousel
- For video, which lengths
- UGC, studio-produced, or a mix
- How they adapt to platform-specific trends, including placement-specific cuts for Reels and Stories

**3. Talent approach**
- Who delivers the message? Founders, paid creators, customers?
- What demographic characteristics does the on-screen talent have?
- Consistent talent, or testing presenter types?
- *"Really try to pay attention to what personas they seem to be speaking to"*

**4. Creative execution**
- Which hook styles appear most
- Editing styles and pacing
- Visual aesthetic
- Text overlays and captions
- *"What messaging themes or value propositions you see repeated over and over again?"*

> "This is where you'll find their creative 'formula'."

**5. Temporal patterns**
- How have their creatives evolved?
- Sticking with what works, or constantly innovating?
- *"Do you notice that during holiday season, Black Friday, Q1, they completely roll out a new set of
  creatives with new messaging?"*
- Have they recently pivoted?

> "They'll usually double down on winners and move away from what's not performing."

**Document it** in a spreadsheet or Notion doc per competitor, and revisit **at least quarterly**.

## From research to strategy

The five moves. Only the first two are about differentiation; the rest are about borrowing well.

**1. Identify white space.** *"Look for angles, hooks, or claims that nobody in your space is making.
Sometimes the most powerful position is the one everyone else has overlooked."*

**2. Differentiate your positioning.** *"If all your competitors are pushing 'affordable', maybe you
emphasize 'premium quality'. If they're all about 'quick results', perhaps your angle is
'sustainable, long-term success'."*

**3. Elevate proven approaches.** *"When you see an angle that's clearly working, don't just copy it
— elevate it."*

⭐ **The delivered session gives the four concrete moves the script leaves abstract:** *"We make it
more authentic, we add stronger social proof, we connect it more with a unique value proposition, we
agitate the problem more."* That is what "elevate" means operationally.

**4. Import cross-category inspiration.** *"A B2B SaaS company might learn from how DTC fashion
brands create urgency."*

**5. Track creative evolution.** *"If everyone suddenly shifts to a new hook style or claim, there's
probably a reason — either the market is changing or they've all discovered something that works."*

> "The goal isn't to become a carbon copy of your competitors. It's to understand the landscape so
> you can carve out your own distinctive place within it — or disrupt it entirely."

## Cadence

> "Competitor research should be an ongoing part of your creative process, not a one-time exercise.
> **Set a calendar reminder to spend an hour every month** refreshing your competitive analysis.
> Markets shift quickly, and yesterday's winning approach might be today's overused cliché."

⚠️ **An hour a month is the whole ask**, which is worth quoting to anyone who has decided competitor
research is a quarterly project they never start.

## Key points, as ACA gives them

- **Research to inspire, not copy**
- **Long-running ≠ success** — verify with multiple signals
- **Platform-specific analysis** across Meta, TikTok, YouTube
- **Structured framework**: volume, formats, talent, execution, evolution
- **Look beyond your category**
- **Regular updates** — a monthly habit
