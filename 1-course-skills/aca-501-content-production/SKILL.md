# Course 501: Content Creation & Production

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 17m 28s, 1 module.

> "**Production quality does not always mean high production value or big budgets.** It means the
> right style for your audience and platform."

**This is the craft layer.** It sits between an approved concept and a delivered ad, and it exists
because the strategist has to describe a shoot they will not personally run.

> "As a creative strategist, **you are in charge of overseeing the production of your creative
> assets** and passing them on to the paid media team."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what a canted angle is, which aspect ratios to deliver,
or how to name files, and get an answer.

**To plan a shoot or a delivery, required:**

1. **The concepts or storyboards** to be produced
2. **Where the ads will run** — the platforms and placements decide the ratios, the safe zones and
   half the framing decisions

**Optional, and each one sharpens the plan:**

3. Who is shooting: a creator, an agency, a studio, or you
4. Budget and timeline
5. What already exists in the content bank
6. Brand constraints: fonts, colour, disclaimers
7. The tools the team already has

⛔ **Never invent a platform specification.** Aspect ratios, safe zones and placement lists change,
and the ones here are current-as-of-recording. Give them as ACA's starting point and say to check
the platform's current spec before delivery.

## The through-line

> "The most important part of effective collaboration between creative strategy and creative
> production is **a shared common language.**"

⭐ **That is what the whole course is for.** Every list in it exists so a strategist can say exactly
what they want to a creator, an agency or a studio, and be understood. A brief that says "make it
feel premium" and a brief that says "extreme close-up, low angle, track left" cost the same to write.

## How to run it

**Vocabulary first.** Most production waste in this course's telling comes from a brief the shooter had to
guess at.

1. **Give the shot vocabulary** from `reference/shot-vocabulary.md` — thirteen native shot types across four
   families, nine cinematography types, three camera movements. ⭐ This is the course's real gift: a
   strategist who can name the shot gets the shot.
2. **Plan the shoot** with `reference/production-planning.md` — batching, modular shooting, and what makes a
   day efficient rather than long.
3. **Decide the platform adaptations and delivery specs**, same file, including aspect ratios and safe zones.
   ⛔ Decide these before the shoot, not in the edit.
4. **Set up asset management and tagging** so the footage can be found again. ⚠️ This is the step teams skip
   and pay for six months later.
5. **Cover thumbnails**, which the course treats as part of production rather than an afterthought.
6. ⭐ **For the pre-launch QA pass, route to `aca-run-creative-qa`** — it holds ACA's Creative QA Checklist
   and needs the finished asset. ⚠️ Confirm it is installed before naming it.
7. **Check the brief names shot types, not moods.** "Make it feel premium" is the brief this course exists to
   replace.

## Answering well

**Polish is not the goal.** *"The best performing ads aren't always the most polished. They're the
ones that speak the native language of the platform and connect authentically with your audience."*

**Shoot the bank, not the ad.** ⭐ *"Having these shots in your content bank allows you to create
endless variations of successful ads."* And: *"When shooting content, **B-roll is your best friend.**
Always try to capture as much B-roll as possible."*

**Plan for production before you plan the concept.** *"Great concepts mean nothing if they're not
production-friendly."* Batching, modular components and platform adaptations are decisions made
before a camera is booked, not after.

**Tag as you go, or lose it.** *"How you organize and tag your assets is absolutely critical"* — and
tagging talent name and date is how usage rights stay traceable.

## ⚠️ Scope

Producing and delivering the assets. It does not generate concepts (Course 401), build the
storyboard (Course 403), cast or direct the creator (Course 502), or read the results (Course 402).
It stops at a delivered, QA'd file handed to the paid media team.

⛔ **The pre-ship quality check has its own skill.** ACA ships an eight-page Creative QA Checklist
for this course, and `aca-run-creative-qa` runs it against a real finished asset.
