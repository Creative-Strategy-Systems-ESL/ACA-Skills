# Planning, managing and delivering

> "**Great concepts mean nothing if they're not production-friendly.**"

---

## The four planning moves

### 1. Batching similar concepts

Group what can be shot together:

- All UGC concepts with **the same creator**
- All product-focused videos that need **the same setup**
- All testimonial videos that need **the same location**

> "This approach **dramatically reduces setup time and logistics costs.**"

### 2. ⭐ The modular production approach

> "Think of your creative elements as **modular components**."

- Shoot **multiple hooks** that can be mixed with different bodies
- Film **various product demonstrations** that can be used across concepts
- Capture **a range of CTAs** that can be attached to different videos

> "This modular approach lets you **produce many more variations without proportionally increasing
> production time or budget.** It's how you scale creative testing efficiently."

⭐ **This is the highest-leverage idea in the course.** Hooks, bodies and CTAs shot as separate
components multiply: five hooks against three bodies is fifteen ads from one shoot day. Shot as three
finished films, it is three.

⚠️ **Modularity has to be designed into the shoot, not discovered in the edit.** Components only
recombine if they were framed, lit and paced to. A hook filmed in a different room from the body
cannot be cut against it.

### 3. Platform-specific adaptations

> "Plan for how each concept will be adapted across platforms: **Meta feed vs. stories vs. reels.
> TikTok vs. YouTube Shorts.** Each requires slight modifications in framing, pacing and text
> treatment."

> "Building these adaptations into your production plan **prevents scrambling later.**"

### 4. Asset management system

> "Create a clear system for organizing all these assets."

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Naming conventions** | That reflect the concept and the variation |
| **Folder structure** | That makes it easy to find specific elements |
| **Tracking document** | That maps which assets have been used in which ads |

> "Production-friendly planning is how you turn great creative concepts into reality **without
> blowing your budget or timeline.**"

## Digital asset management

> "In order to streamline your creative production, post-production, and most importantly,
> **iteration**, how you organize and tag your assets is absolutely critical."

**What to tag:**

| Tag | Why |
|---|---|
| **Shot type** | Talking head, product shot, and the rest of the vocabulary |
| ⭐ **Talent name and date** | *"To track usage rights"* |
| **Everything, into one searchable database** | Raw assets, working files and final ads together |

⭐ **Tagging talent name and date is the one with consequences.** Usage rights expire, and an asset
library that cannot answer "who is in this and when did we license them" is a library you cannot
safely reuse from. It is a legal record disguised as a filing convention.

**Tools she names:** *"**Air** is the simplest version of this, but there are many Digital Asset
Management tools in the market."* And **Uplifted**, *"a particularly great tool to organize and tag
your assets. They have AI tagging capabilities that automatically identify your content type and make
your entire asset library searchable."*

⚠️ **The Uplifted recommendation comes with a coupon in ACA's resources**, which is a commercial
relationship the course does not name as one. The capability is real; treat the endorsement as an
endorsement.

## Post-production tools

⚠️ **Her own framing of the tooling question:** simplified tools like **CapCut** *"are more than
enough to create ads for social."* But for cross-team collaboration and faster iteration, *"using
more pro tools like **Adobe Premiere** is the best approach."*

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Frame.io** | Share creatives for review · capture feedback **directly on specific frames** · syncs with Adobe Creative Cloud |
| **Moises.io** | Extract background music from existing assets. *"Also available in CapCut Pro"* |
| **Adobe Podcast (Enhance Speech)** | One-click audio clean-up, removing background noise and static. *"Also available in CapCut Pro"* |
| **Mister Horse** | After Effects and Premiere plug-in for transitions and animation presets |
| **CapCut** | Text-to-speech, background removal, AV sync |
| **Premiere Pro plugins** | **Fire Cut** — silence cutting, captions, B-roll integration · **Social Media Text Tools** — presets for native styles and colours |

⭐ **Frame.io is the one that changes how a team works rather than what it can make.** Feedback
attached to a frame instead of a paragraph is the difference between one revision round and three.

## Platform-optimised delivery

> "When editing your videos, you want to make sure they're optimized for **every placement.**"

**Deliver every ad in multiple aspect ratios:**

| Platform | Ratios |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | **9:16 · 4:5 · 1:1** |
| **TikTok** | **9:16** |
| **YouTube** | **9:16 · 16:9** |

### ⛔ Safe zones

> "Following safe zones is extremely important because **every platform and placement has UI
> elements that could cover your ad.** Every key title and visual should always be within the safe
> space."

**ACA's own margins**, from the Safe Spaces & Ad Specs reference:

| Ratio | Keep text, logos and CTAs |
|---|---|
| **1:1 · 4:5** | ⭐ Out of the **top and bottom 14%** of the frame |
| **9:16** | ⭐ Inside the **central 80% vertically and 90% horizontally** |

**Why the margin exists at all**, in their words: *"Every platform (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, etc.) adds
interface overlays (like buttons, captions, or progress bars) on top of your ad."* A safety zone is
*"the central area of your video or image where all critical content will always remain visible, no
matter the placement."*

**Their four instructions:**

1. *"Use platform templates that already account for safety zones."*
2. *"Preview ads across placements before launch (feed, Stories/Reels, Tiktok, etc.)."*
3. *"Keep subtitles, logos, and CTAs inside safe areas."*
4. *"Always export multiple aspect ratios (1:1, 4:5, 9:16) so the ad looks good everywhere."*

⛔ **The three crop-safe templates ACA links here — Meta, TikTok, and a combined Meta × TikTok — are
not in this folder.** The margins above are the whole specification we hold. Build your own guides
from them, or ask ACA for the templates.

⚠️ **Platform-native fonts are a separate ACA reference and are also not held**, so a font question
has no answer here.

⛔ **These ratios and placements are current-as-of-recording.** Platform specs change. Give them as
the starting point and verify before delivery.

## Thumbnail design

> "This is something that often gets overlooked in the production process, but it's **critically
> important.** While it shouldn't be the first thing you focus on, you definitely shouldn't ignore
> it either."

**Why it matters:** *"In Meta, **not all placements are autoplay.** In many cases, users will see
your thumbnail before they decide whether to watch your video. That initial frame needs to work as a
standalone **'advertisement for your advertisement.'**"*

| Element | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Visual impact** | A high-contrast, attention-grabbing image that communicates your value instantly |
| **Clear subject** | Make sure your product or key visual is immediately identifiable |
| **Text overlay** | If using text, keep it short, large enough to read, and **placed in safe zones** |

> "Remember to **intentionally design that first frame** for maximum impact. Don't just let it be
> whatever happens to be at the beginning of your video."

⭐ **"Don't just let it be whatever happens to be at the beginning" is the whole instruction.** A
thumbnail is a decision or it is an accident, and the default is an accident.

## Quality assurance

> "Before any asset goes live, it needs to pass a comprehensive quality assurance process. **This
> isn't just about catching errors. It's about ensuring every asset reaches its full potential.**"

**Her four categories:**

| Category | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Technical specifications** | Time length for the platform · correct aspect ratios per placement · safe space compliance · proper rendering and export quality |
| **Brand consistency** | Brand guidelines where they apply · font usage, branded or platform-native · consistent colour and visual identity |
| **Content quality** | Spelling and grammar · sound quality and audio levelling · hook effectiveness, all five elements present · sufficient variation between hook versions · a clear CTA aligned to the conversion objective |
| **Engagement optimisation** | Sound pattern interrupts and "ear stoppers" · strategic sound effects at key moments · pacing without dead spots · visual hierarchy · message congruency throughout |

⭐ **Two rules she attaches, and they pull in opposite directions on purpose:**

> "I recommend getting **at least two sets of eyes on every asset** before it goes live."

> "The QA process should be **thorough but not endless.** Establish clear guidelines and timeframes
> to prevent perfectionism from delaying your testing and learning cycles."

⛔ **ACA ships an eight-page Creative QA Checklist for this**, and it is held. When there is a real
finished asset to check rather than a process to explain, `aca-run-creative-qa` runs it.
