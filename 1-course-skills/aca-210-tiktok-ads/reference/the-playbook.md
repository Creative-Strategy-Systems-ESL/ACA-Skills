# Why TikTok needs its own playbook

Sections 1 to 3 of the course.

## The core argument

> "TikTok is an entertainment platform disguised as a social network. People arrive in discovery
> mode, sound-on, thumb primed for the next dopamine hit. That means ads can't act like
> interruptions — **they must behave like the very content the audience came to consume.**"

⭐ **ACA's supporting figure:** a global study of 3,500 ads found that when brands swapped TV-style
spots for "TikTok-first" videos, **watch time nearly doubled and purchase intent rose 37%.**

> "Everything that follows hangs on one mantra: **ads must entertain first and sell as a natural
> consequence.**"

## Why it is worth learning even if TikTok is not your platform

Instagram built Reels to keep pace. YouTube launched Shorts. Facebook, Snapchat and Pinterest all
serve vertical, sound-on scrollers now.

> "They're all morphing into the same thing: infinite discovery feeds where entertainment decides
> distribution. TikTok simply got there first, **and it still writes the rules the others copy six
> months later.**"

So the course frames itself as *"a master-class in the future of social advertising"* rather than a
platform tutorial: hooks that grab attention in three quarters of a second, the language of
micro-communities, and refresh rhythms that stop fatigue *"before your CPA starts bleeding."*

# Campaign architecture

⚠️ **Choose the objective first and carefully.** *"Choose wrong and every setting downstream —
bidding, optimisation event, even the templates you'll see — will sabotage you."*

Three objectives, each with the formats that suit it:

| Objective | Formats | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | TopView (full-screen at first app-open) · Brand Takeover | Big-canvas placements |
| **Consideration** | In-Feed · Spark Ads · Search Ads | Clicks, app installs, lead forms |
| **Conversion** | Video Shopping · Collection Ads · Dynamic Showcase | Catalog feeds with swipe-to-buy immediacy |

## Placements: leave them automatic

> "**Ninety-five percent of advertisers can leave placements on 'automatic'.** Only change them if
> legal guidelines or brand-safety clauses demand it."

The reason, and it matches the argument in ACA's Meta course: *"TikTok's auction is
placement-agnostic; narrow targeting only makes CPMs climb."*

# Audience Insights, and sub-culture mining

> "**Stop guessing who your buyer is; ask TikTok directly.**"

Open Analytics → Audience Insights.

⭐ **The distinctive idea in this course**, and the part people skip:

> "Demographics are the appetizer; **the main course is sub-culture mining.**"

## What to actually look at

**Interest and hashtag clouds.** Export the list and highlight *"not only the obvious (#running,
#skincare) but the weirdly specific tags — #GoblinCore, #SlowLiving, #TinyDeskDIY. These are
self-organising micro-communities that the algorithm loves to serve niche content."*

**Community overlap graph.** TikTok shows what else your buyers binge.

> "A plant-based snack brand may discover its audience also lives inside #BookTok; that's a hint to
> pair recipe content with cosy reading vibes."

## ⭐ Insights give you language, not just targeting

The move that makes this operational rather than interesting:

> "Insights don't just point to targeting; **they give you language.** If #copperhair is trending
> inside your cohort, open your next video by literally stating it: *'Copper hair lovers, this one's
> for you…'*"

## The drill

Mirella sets it as homework, and it is the cheapest useful thing in the course:

> "Right after this session, pick one unexpected hashtag from your Insights, click through to its
> feed, **watch ten videos, and write down the inside jokes.** Your next ad starts there."

⚠️ **This is research you have to actually do.** The hashtags above are ACA's examples, not a
starter list for your brand — and a sub-community you have not watched cannot supply you with its
language. Do not invent the inside jokes of a community you have not observed; that is precisely
the failure that makes brand content on TikTok read as tourism.
