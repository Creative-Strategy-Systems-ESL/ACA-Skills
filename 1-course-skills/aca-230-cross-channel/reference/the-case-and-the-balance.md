# The case for multi-channel, and the push/pull balance

## Why more than one channel

⭐ **ACA's supporting evidence:** a **Harvard Business Review study of 46,000 shoppers** found that
omnichannel customers spend significantly more than single-channel ones — **9% more in-store and
10% more online.**

Four arguments, and the third is the one people underrate:

1. **Visibility and memorability.** Being present in several places makes the message stick.
2. **Engagement and trust.** *"When people see your brand in different places, they're more likely
   to trust you and take action."*
3. ⭐ **Resilience and risk diversification.** *"If one channel's performance drops, algorithm
   changes, or costs spike, your other channels keep your brand in front of your audience."* This
   is an argument for multi-channel even when a single channel is currently working well.
4. **Richer data.** Seeing which messages and platforms work where enables faster, better decisions.

⚠️ **The cost of opening channels, named honestly:** *"when you open up new channels, you also enter
the world of multi-channel attribution — the beauty and the complexity. Understanding which channel
truly drove a conversion can be challenging."*

> "A multi-channel strategy isn't just about being everywhere — it's about **being everywhere that
> matters**."

# Push versus pull

The distinction that decides how budget splits.

| | **Pull** | **Push** |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Captures demand that already exists | Creates demand that does not yet exist |
| The moment | Someone is actively searching | Someone is scrolling, not looking for you |
| Typical form | Search, Shopping | Video in feed, display, notifications |
| Intent | Highest | None yet |

**Pull** *"meet existing demand and help customers find you when their intent is highest."*
**Push** is *"proactively putting your message in front of people who may not be looking for your
product or service yet... creating demand and sparking interest."*

## The two examples, and the correction that follows them

**Booking.com — pull.** You type *"best hotels in Paris"* into Google and Booking.com is the
sponsored result at the top. Classic demand capture.

**Dr. Squatch — push.** You are scrolling TikTok, Instagram or YouTube and a funny video about
natural soap for men appears. *"Maybe you weren't even thinking about soap, but the ad hooks you
with humor and storytelling."*

⭐ **Then the course immediately corrects the obvious inference:**

> "Does this mean Booking.com only uses pull strategies, and Dr. Squatch only uses push? **Of course
> not!** Why? Most successful brands blend both approaches."

- **Booking.com** also runs demand-gen ads, targets broad audiences, and retargets on social —
  *"classic push tactics."*
- **Dr. Squatch** invests in search ads and content marketing to capture people actively looking for
  natural soap — *"so they're not just creating demand, but also capturing it when it appears."*

> "Push and pull aren't either/or—they **work best together.** As a brand, your goal is to find the
> right balance and allocate your budget in a way that guarantees the best return."

That balance depends on brand, product, audience and objectives. There is no percentage in the
course, and inventing one would be worse than saying so.

## ⭐ The seasonal rhythm, which is the actionable part

The balance **is not static**, and this is the most immediately usable idea in the section:

> "Brand seasonality and key sales moments — like Black Friday — can significantly influence your
> approach. During peak sales periods or seasonal spikes, brands often **ramp up push marketing** to
> maximize reach and create urgency among broader audiences. This push builds awareness and
> stimulates demand ahead of or during these critical moments."
>
> "After this push phase, **pull strategies become especially important** to capture high-intent
> audiences who are now actively searching and ready to convert, **often at higher efficiency and
> lower cost.**"

**The sequence, plainly:** push creates the demand, then pull harvests it — and the harvest is
cheaper than the creation because intent has already been manufactured.

> "A dynamic, data-driven approach to balancing push and pull — adjusted for seasonality and sales
> events — is essential for sustainable growth."

⚠️ **The failure this prevents:** a brand that only runs pull during a peak is competing for a
demand pool it did nothing to fill, at the moment that pool is most contested. A brand that only
runs push has manufactured intent and then left it uncaptured.
