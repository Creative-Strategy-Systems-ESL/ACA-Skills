# What changed, and why diversity became survival

## The symptom

> "If you've been running ads recently, you've probably noticed something changed. **Your top
> performing ads suddenly hit a ceiling. Your CPMs are so much higher and your ROAS started
> declining.** So what happened?"

⭐ **This is the course's hook and it is worth keeping**, because it is the exact complaint a team
brings to a strategist. Her answer is that the account did not get worse — the system it runs inside
changed.

## ⚠️ Her explanation, with her own hedge

> "Meta rolled out a massive algorithm update that fundamentally changed how the platform evaluates
> and delivers creative, and most advertisers still don't understand what's actually happening.
> **Here's what we know so far.**"

⛔ **"What we know so far" is doing real work in that sentence.** Everything below is a
practitioner's reading of an algorithm change, assembled from Meta's communications and from what her
team observes in accounts. **Repeat it as her reading, not as documented behaviour.**

### Old system against new

| | The old system | ⭐ **Sequence learning** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it read** | *"Just pulled signals like clicks or page links, but it completely ignored the order and timing of those actions"* | *"analyzes events, streams with timestamps so it can understand exactly where a user is in their journey."* |
| **The consequence** | *"It treated every click the same, whether someone was just as covering your brand or about to buy."* | It can tell discovery from consideration from purchase |

> "Think about it when someone is in the discovery phase, they need a **completely different
> creative** than someone that's in the consideration phase or the purchase phase. **The old system
> couldn't tell the difference. The new one can.**"

### The hierarchical ad index

> "They've built a **hierarchical ad index.** Think of it as a sophisticated filing system that
> organizes ads based on multiple dimensions."

**The dimensions she names:** format · messaging · persona · visual style · hook structure.

> "They're building **a multi dimensional map of your creative universe.** Then they're using AI to
> match the right creative to where each user is in their journey. The goal? More personalization,
> faster matching, and **less reliance on targeting.**"

⭐ **This is the "creative is your targeting" argument taken one step further.** In the earlier
version, creative selected the audience by who engaged. Here, the platform is explicitly indexing
creative by its attributes and matching on them — so the attributes themselves are the targeting
parameters.

---

## ⛔ What it means for advertisers

### Budget no longer concentrates

| Before | Now |
|---|---|
| Upload a few ads. Meta would pick the winner. **Budgets would concentrate on that ad** | *"**No single creative dominates budget.** It's spread more evenly across all ads"* |
| The strategy: find that one-size-fits-all creative that works for everyone when targeting broad | *"Meta actively **penalizes generic, broad creative** and rewards personas. Specific ads that match users to their journey stage."* |

⭐ **That first row changes the economics of a test.** If budget no longer piles onto one winner, a
single brilliant ad cannot carry an account — which is the mechanical reason the rest of this course
is about production systems rather than about making better single ads.

### ⛔ The grouping penalty

> "**If all your ads look the same, talk the same, and hook the same, Meta groups them together.**
> You get diminishing returns. You hit creative fatigue faster, and your reach plateaus."

> "But if you have **true creative diversity** — different formats, different personas, different
> hooks, different motivators — **Meta sees each as a distinct entity.** You unlock new audiences,
> you extend your reach, and you avoid the grouping penalty."

> "This is why creative volume and diversity aren't just nice to have anymore. **They're literally
> survival.**"

⚠️ **"Grouping penalty" is her term.** It describes an observed effect, not a documented Meta
mechanism with that name. The observation — near-identical ads returning less than their count
suggests — is one many practitioners report; the causal story is hers.

⭐ **The load-bearing distinction, and the whole course in one line:**

> "**It's not about making 100 variations of the same ad. It's about making fundamentally different
> ads for fundamentally different people.**"

⛔ **This is what separates diversity from volume**, and it is the thing most teams get wrong when
they are told to ship more. Fifty variations of one concept is exactly the input the grouping penalty
punishes. The four dimensions she names as real diversity are **format, persona, hook and
motivator** — and persona is the one that generates the other three.

## Her closing position

> "The brands that win in 2026 and beyond **won't be the ones with the best single ads. They'll be
> the ones with the best creative system** designed for how Andromeda actually works."

⚠️ **A dated claim, deliberately.** It names a year and a specific algorithm regime. Check whether
the regime still holds before building a plan on it — the argument for diversity survives most
platform changes, but the mechanism she attributes it to may not.
