# Jobs, teams and what keeps you valuable

The part people actually came for. Reza's answer is neither reassurance nor doom, and the nuance is
the useful bit.

## "Will AI take my job?"

His answer, unhedged:

> "**I think it'll take some jobs,** but I think it's more a question of all of our jobs just
> evolving and changing. If you if you even just think about the job titles that exist today, if you
> were to go back 15, 20 years, how many of those job titles would even exist then? Right? So I
> think **I think it will take some jobs, it'll create some new**"

But that is not where he puts the emphasis:

> "**the biggest change is just going to be in the structure of teams.** I don't know how long it
> takes for this to play out. What's very likely to happen is that you won't need to hire as many
> people as you as you had in the past. So like a much leaner, smaller team can get a lot more"

The concrete shape of it:

> "what's more likely is that **a 20 person company could probably stay 20 people and do the work of
> 40** or a 40 person company. You could probably stay 40 and do the do the work of 80."

⚠️ **Read what that implies.** The risk is less "you are fired" and more "the role above you is
never opened, and the team you expected to build does not get hired." The threat lands on growth
and progression before it lands on employment, which changes what you do about it.

## ⭐ The two skills

> "If you want to be valuable to your organization, it's probably two really core important skill
> sets."

**1. Rising to a higher level of abstraction.**

> "if you think about a junior role, if you want to move up in your career, how do you rise to a
> higher level of abstraction? So you're not just staying at like junior execution roles?"

**2. Scaling that.**

Historically, scaling meant people:

> "if you go to a company and you're doing really well and you're creating a lot of value,
> oftentimes what happens is you can create more value by saying, now if I grow my team to ten
> people... I've risen to this level of abstraction. REZA And then now I've scaled my team."

Now the second half can be done without headcount:

> "instead of hiring all of these people, what you can do instead is you've risen up to this level
> of abstraction. You're able to add strategic, high leverage value. You're able to help the
> leadership of your team get more done with less people. So you say that, **hey, instead of hiring
> two more people, I've built an AI agent, I've hired a couple of AI agents, and I'm doing a bunch
> of stuff myself.** REZA **And guess what? We don't need those five other people that** that **we
> thought we might**"

His summary:

> "**If you can learn how to help your organization stay lean and scale without necessarily adding
> headcount, then you'll be very, very valued at that organization.**"

He is also optimistic about the aggregate effect:

> "Hopefully what ends up happening is that a lot more companies get created. **So if you're ever at
> all thinking about starting a company, this is probably a good time to do that.** But my guess of
> what happens is that there's a lot more companies, but those companies are smaller."

## ⭐ How to actually rise a level

The concrete method, and it is refreshingly unmystical:

> "having an understanding of **the 1 or 2 levels above you, what do they care about? What are they
> thinking about? What are their priorities** becomes really important. Often when we're at an
> organization, we're kind of just thinking about our level and down what is in front of me, what do
> I need to get done?"

Why it works:

> "**If you as an individual in an organization can contribute to one of the most important
> problems of the organization, then you will have created value for that organization, and they
> will want to make sure to keep you there.**"

> "if you know it, then in your day to day work, you can think about how to contribute to that.
> Often the big issue is that we just don't know, like we're not paying attention to that."

And the failure it prevents:

> "that's how you can wake up one day being surprised and be like, oh my God, what? REZA Everything
> is so different now and my job is at stake... and then we can get surprised when priorities shift,
> and we're not adding as much value as we thought we were."

**Note how this connects to the CEO whisperer idea in `the-foundations.md`.** A creative strategist
doing real market research already holds information leadership wants. Routing it upward is
simultaneously the research practice and the career move; they are the same action.

## Use AI in your personal life

His first piece of closing advice, and it is not filler:

> "everybody in their own personal life should be using AI a ton, right? And I think actually
> getting into the habit of outside of work, just starting to get immersed in it as a way of just
> how you operate, I think is really important because then it's not just related to work."

> "**if we are the type of individuals that are just using AI in so many different aspects of our
> day to day, then it'll start to come very naturally.** So I'd say one of the, one of the key areas
> is just if you find yourself using AI more and more, just generally in your life, and that's
> probably a good indication that you're in, you're"

The bar he sets, via analogy:

> "imagine, for example, somebody asks in the modern era, if you're a knowledge worker, **how do you
> use Google?** REZA It's like, what do you mean? What kind of question is that? It's just it's so,
> so much a part of what I do. **I think that is the right feeling for AI.**"

That is a genuinely useful self-test. If using AI still feels like a decision, it has not become
infrastructure yet.

## ⭐ The one tangible starting move

If someone wants a single action, this is it:

> "a very tangible idea is **take one part of your work that is pretty repetitive, that you seem to
> be going back to every single week. That is kind of a mindless thing for you,** and it's just it's
> just repetitive and try to take just that one thing. And with the best tools that you can find at
> the time that you're trying to do this, what's the best way that you can automate as much of that
> thing as possible?"

And why it is deliberately small:

> "**Once you do that, then it becomes pretty addictive** and you're like, oh, now what else can I
> do? And I think that basically starts the trend of, how do I look at everything that I do and find
> more and more leverage with AI. So solving that one specific use case now gives you the confidence
> to say, okay, I can start to use AI more and more in my in my day to day and **find ways to
> automate the things that are really repetitive so that I can rise up and do things that are more
> strategic and** more **higher value.**"

Note the loop closing: automating repetitive work is not the goal, it is how you buy the time to
operate at the higher level of abstraction that the two skills call for.

## How he closes

> "Over the next several months and years, it's getting to the point where **all of humanity
> basically has superintelligence available on tap**... And what's interesting about it is that
> **everybody has access to it in the exact same way.** REZA So it's it's just like great equalizer.
> So it's not like some individuals or teams are going to have access to this advanced technology
> that the rest of us won't."

> "And then the question becomes, what do you do with it? Right. And I think, that is a real
> differentiator **in a world where everybody has access to the same capability.** REZA **It's a
> really great opportunity for people to differentiate and create a ton of value by being really,
> really good at creating value using the super**"

> "It's a really chaotic time right now. So much is happening, so many things are changing really
> fast, but it's also a time of tremendous opportunity."

⚠️ This is the most speculative passage in the interview and reads as a founder's closing note.
Quote it as his outlook rather than as a forecast. The argument underneath it, that equal access
makes application the differentiator, stands on its own regardless of what one thinks of
"superintelligence on tap".
