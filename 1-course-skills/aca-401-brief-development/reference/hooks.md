# Hooks

> "those critical first 1-3 seconds that determine whether someone keeps watching your ad or scrolls
> right past it."

## Why the hook is the highest-leverage element

Three reasons, and the first is the one people miss:

1. ⭐ **It is your real targeting mechanism.** *"When someone stops on your ad, the algorithm serves
   it to similar users, effectively building your audience based on who finds your hook
   compelling."*
2. **Different hooks reach different segments.** *"By testing multiple approaches for the same
   creative, you can tap into entirely new customer groups."*
3. **Refreshing hooks prevents fatigue.** *"Even when your core message is working, a new hook can
   revitalize a campaign that's starting to see diminishing returns."*

⭐ **Reason 3 is the cheapest performance fix in the programme.** A fatiguing ad with a working body
needs a new opening, not a new concept. Check whether that has been tried before briefing anything
new.

## Five pattern interrupt techniques

| Technique | ACA's example |
|---|---|
| **Trigger curiosity** | *"The one mistake costing most advertisers thousands..."* |
| **Trigger emotions** | *"We were shocked when we saw these results..."* |
| **Be relatable** | *"That moment when your ad account performance suddenly tanks..."* |
| **Be shocking or surprising** | Unusual visuals, surprising statements, counterintuitive claims |
| **Showcase an opportunity** | *"The three-step process that cut our CPAs by 60%..."* |

## The five elements of an effective hook

Not just the words. **All five, working together.**

| Element | What it carries |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | Who appears. *"leverage actors who resemble your target audience, and who display emotions in the way that your target audience would when faced with their problem."* |
| **Message** | The bold problem statement. *"In a few words, your audience should clearly be able to connect this problem to a personal experience"* |
| **Visual** | *"The engaging, shocking or satisfying visual will tell your audience what emotions they should feel"* |
| **Sound** | *"Pattern-interrupting sounds, voice tonality, or even strategic silence"* |
| **Thumbnail** | ⚠️ *"The static image that appears before a video plays... This single frame needs to generate enough interest to earn that initial click"* |

> "When these five elements work together, you create a hook that's much harder to scroll past."

⭐ **Thumbnail is the element nobody briefs**, and it is the only one that does work before the video
plays at all.

## Meta's hook categories

> "A powerful framework developed by **Meta's Creative Shop in Israel**. Led by our faculty member
> **Udi Avital**, this team identified... hook categories that consistently drive engagement."

⚠️ **ACA says "twelve distinct hook categories" and then lists seventeen.** Counted from the course
itself. All seventeen are below; the count in the narration is wrong, not the list.

| # | Category | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dramatize the Problem** | Amplifies the pain point. *"Makes the problem feel impossible to ignore"* |
| 2 | **Motion Tricks** | Eye-catching transitions and visual effects that stop the scroll |
| 3 | **Podcast** | Mimics a podcast format. *"The “expert voice” introduction in the first seconds establishes authority and signals valuable information ahead"* |
| 4 | **Reminder** | Time-sensitive. Connects the product to an upcoming event, sale or season |
| 5 | **The Absurd Alternative** | An exaggerated or ridiculous alternative, making your solution obvious |
| 6 | **In Real Life** | *"Perfect for gaming and tech."* Bridges virtual experiences with real situations |
| 7 | **Reaction in Action** | Authentic reactions, often split-screen or green-screen |
| 8 | **Evolution** | Visualises transformation over time, problem to solution |
| 9 | **Question** | Opens on a question targeting the exact pain point |
| 10 | **On Trend (FOMO)** | Taps a current trend, or triggers the desire to participate |
| 11 | **Teaser Hook** | *"The classic don't-show-everything approach."* Withholds key details |
| 12 | **Make Me Laugh** | Humour disarms and builds positive association |
| 13 | **Emphasize the Solution** | A *"magic moment"* visualising the product's impact |
| 14 | **Negative Hook** | A negative statement or warning, creating urgency |
| 15 | **Satisfying Intro** | Sensory-rich visuals that are inherently pleasing to watch |
| 16 | **Destroy, Toss & Burn** | *"Destruction is a powerful pattern interrupt."* Breaking or smashing |
| 17 | **Skeptical Voice** | Acknowledges hesitation upfront, *"lowering resistance"* |

⭐ **Use the list as a coverage check.** Ten hooks that are all Question and Dramatize the Problem is
two hooks written five times. The point of a categorised list is to force range.

⚠️ **Categories 5, 15 and 16 carry brand risk** and the course does not flag it. Absurdity,
destruction and sensory content each work by being unlike the category norm, which is also what makes
them the ones a cautious brand rejects. Propose them knowing that.

## Five hook copy formulas

Templates, with ACA's own examples across three verticals.

| Formula | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Start with the problem** | *"Stop using makeup to hide breakouts."* · *"If you're struggling to breathe, you need to see this!"* · *"Guys, stop wearing uncomfortable jeans"* |
| **Ask a question about the problem** | *"Are you tired of breakouts and skin bumps?"* · *"Did you know your snoring can lead to serious health risks?"* · *"Do your jeans look like this?"* |
| **Make a list** | *"3 Ways This Serum Fights Acne"* · *"Here are 3 reasons why you need to try this anti-snoring device!"* · *"5 things your dermatologist won't tell you"* |
| **Start with the product** | *"This acne serum sold out 5x!"* · *"This anti-snoring device can end your snoring overnight"* · *"Jeans that feel like sweatpants"* |
| **Solve the problem** | *"This serum cleared my acne in 2 weeks."* · *"This simple anti-snoring trick can add years to your life!"* · *"How to dress like a real man"* |

⛔ **Several of ACA's own examples make claims that would need substantiation.** *"Cleared my acne in
2 weeks"*, *"can add years to your life"*, *"sold out 5x"* are results, health and sales claims. They
are reproduced here because they are ACA's, and **a hook you write must not invent one.** If the
brand has not made the claim, the hook cannot.

## The Hook Development Worksheet, and producing it

ACA's artifact. Four sections.

⛔ **Section 1 is research output, not a field to fill from the product description.** A filled
demographics and pain-point table reads as findings, and it travels: the audience written here is
the audience every hook below it is aimed at. **Mark each of its four fields `supplied`, `inferred`
or `not known`** and put the marker in the cell, not in a note underneath. If nothing was supplied,
the worksheet still gets produced, with Section 1 marked inferred throughout and a line saying the
hooks are aimed at a guess.

### 1. Target Audience

| Field |
|---|
| Primary audience |
| Key demographics |
| Main pain points |
| Current solutions they use |

### 2. Core Problem & Solution

| Field |
|---|
| Problem your product solves |
| Unique solution you offer |

### 3. Hook Brainstorming

Five buckets, and the buckets are the diversity mechanism:

| Bucket |
|---|
| **Problem-Focused Hooks** |
| **Results-Focused Hooks** |
| **Curiosity Hooks** |
| **Social Proof Hooks** |
| **Humor Hooks** |

### 4. Hook Testing Plan

⛔ **Emit the worksheet with these sections and fields**, filled. Not a description of what a hook
worksheet contains.

## Writing into the Comprehensive Hook Database structure

ACA's database is organised **by category, then sub-category, then bracketed templates**:

> **Problem/Pain Hooks → Direct Problem Statements**
> - *"Stop scrolling if you suffer from [PROBLEM]"*
> - *"[TARGET AUDIENCE], we gotta stop doing [BAD HABIT]"*
> - *"Don't do [ACTIVITY] without doing [SOLUTION] first"*
> - *"[PROBLEM] making it hard to go anywhere?"*
> - *"Break up with your [PROBLEM]"*
> - *"If you have [PROBLEM], you need to hear this"*

⭐ **Keep the `[BRACKETS]` when the value is unknown.** ACA's own database is bracketed, and a
template with the placeholder intact is reusable; one with a guessed value filled in reads as a
finished hook and gets shipped.

**So when producing hooks, deliver both:**

1. **Filled hooks** for this brand, ready to test
2. **The bracketed template** each came from, so the brand keeps a reusable pattern

## Delivering hooks

- **Tag every hook with its category** from the seventeen, and **check the spread** before delivering
- **Ten hooks minimum for a test batch**, spread across at least four categories
- **Flag any hook carrying a claim** the brand has not supplied evidence for
- **Note which of the five elements each hook depends on** — a hook that only works with a specific
  visual or sound is a production requirement, not just a line
