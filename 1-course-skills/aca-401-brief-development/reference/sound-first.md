# Sound-first ads

> "Sound is arguably one of the most underutilized elements in ad creative development."

## Why it deserves attention

Three arguments, in ACA's order:

1. **Platform behaviour.** *"Over 90% of TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts users engage with sound
   on."*
2. **Performance.** *"Our creative tests with clients running ads on Meta and TikTok show that videos
   with bespoke sound can **lower CPAs by up to 50%** compared to generic background tracks or silent
   videos."*
3. **It matches organic behaviour.** *"Songs and sounds go viral because creators dance to them,
   lip-sync to them, and build their own content around audio trends."*

> "Think of the hook as not just your 'scroll stopper' but your **'ear stopper'** as well."

⚠️ **Both figures are ACA's and unsourced**, and the second is their own client data with an "up to"
attached. See `reference/provenance.md`.

⛔ **The 90%-sound-on claim is platform-specific and does not generalise.** ACA's own Meta course
warns never to assume audio, and both are right about their own platform. Carrying the TikTok figure
into a Meta feed brief is a real error, and the courses do not reconcile it.

## Reversing the process

| Traditional | Sound-first |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify the target persona | 1. **Start with the sound concept** |
| 2. Develop the visual hook | 2. Build the visual elements around the audio |
| 3. Create the messaging | 3. Ensure the messaging complements the sound-driven narrative |
| 4. Add sound as the final element | 4. Optimise the whole package for the platform |

> "This reversal mimics how organic content often develops on social platforms, where creators
> frequently start with a trending sound and build their creative around"

## The three worked examples

| Brand | The sound concept |
|---|---|
| **Wise**, *"Spend Like a Local"* | *"What does it sound like to use Wise?"* The beeps, chimes and confirmations of payment machines, cut against quick location changes. ⭐ *"The sound drives the pace of the edits"* |
| **Roman**, telehealth | The contrast between **a cat's meow and a lion's roar**, representing hesitance to confidence. *"Communicates the emotional benefit without explicitly stating it"* |
| **Spoiled Child** | Took the trending *"man in finance"* sound, but **worked with a music producer on their own remix** rather than using the original |

⭐ **The Roman example is the clearest argument for the whole approach.** An ED brand cannot state
the emotional benefit plainly; a meow becoming a roar says it in a second and clears compliance,
because a metaphor is not a claim.

⚠️ **The Spoiled Child example is the practical one.** Remixing rather than lifting a trending sound
gets the familiarity while sidestepping the rights problem and the expiry date on the original.

## The four-step framework

⛔ **ACA ships a fillable version of this and it is not in this folder.** The *Sound-First Ads:
Creative Development Worksheet* is named on their download list for this course and is not held. What
follows is the method as taught in the recording, complete; what is missing is their form for it.

⚠️ **It is a different asset from the two sound-first items that ARE here** — *AI Tools for
Sound-First Creative Development* and *AI Prompts for Sound-First Creative Development*. The titles
are close enough to be read as the same thing, and they are not. If ACA sends the worksheet later, it
belongs here rather than in place of either of those.

### Step 1: Sound concept exploration

Three prompts:

| Prompt | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Emotional soundscapes** | What emotions are associated with using the product? Calm, confident, relieved, powerful, sexy? **What transformation occurs before to after?** Stressed → calm · exhausted → energized · insecure → confident |
| **Product sound reality** | What does it literally sound like to receive and use? Packaging sound, unboxing, unwrapping? Does it make a sound when used (**ASMR potential**)? For digital, the app or game sound effects |
| **Cultural sound connections** | Seasonal themes? Trending sounds you can adapt or remix? **Sound metaphors** that relate to your benefits? |

### Step 2: Sound production approaches

| Approach | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Original production** | Custom sound effects · original music embodying the brand emotion · voice with distinctive delivery or catchphrases |
| **Trend adaptation** | Remix or reinterpret a trending sound · a brand version of a popular format · the structure of a viral sound with new content |
| **Sound design** | Layer elements · use contrast for surprise · ⭐ **build audio patterns that create recognition across multiple ads** |

⭐ **That last one is the strategic option.** A recurring audio signature makes a whole account
recognisable in a feed, which no single ad can do.

### Step 3: Visual integration

- Match visual cuts to audio beats
- Create visual metaphors that reinforce sound metaphors
- **Show reactions to sounds within the ad itself**
- Use on-screen text to highlight key sound moments
- Consider how the visuals explain or enhance the sound

### Step 4: Testing variations

| What to vary |
|---|
| Different sound intensity levels |
| Various pacing of the same sound concept |
| **Male vs. female voices**, if using voiceover |
| Subtle vs. obvious sound effects |
| **Original sound vs. trending sound adaptations** |

⭐ **These are variations on one concept, not five concepts.** That is what makes it a clean test:
the sound is the variable and everything else holds.

## The checklist

**Audio hook: does the sound grab attention in the first 1 to 2 seconds?**

⚠️ **The transcript's checklist is cut off after this first item** in the filed material, so the rest
of ACA's criteria are not held. The first one is the load-bearing one and is reproduced as given.

## Briefing sound-first

**Name the sound concept before anything else in the brief**, and say which of the three production
approaches it needs — original, adaptation or design. Those have very different costs and lead times,
and a brief that says "great sound" without choosing gets a stock track.

⛔ **A sound-first ad needs a silent fallback, and the course does not mention one.** Some placements
autoplay muted regardless of platform norms. Say what the ad does with the sound off: if the answer
is nothing, that is a placement restriction the media buyer needs before launch, not after.
