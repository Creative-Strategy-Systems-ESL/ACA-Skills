# Course 301: Reverse Engineering Winning Ads — The Creative Research System

**Zach Murray**, founder of **Foreplay**. 25m 51s, 3 modules.

The course argues one thing hard, and everything else follows from it:

> "You were not original. That concept you're so proud of, someone did it before you... And here's
> the thing. That's not a problem. That's actually how creativity works."

Creativity is **pattern recognition**, not divine inspiration. The job is not to invent from nothing;
it is to collect better inputs than everyone else and recombine them with taste.

> "Don't waste time trying to be original. Use your time to do something better."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask why a competitor's ad works, or whether studying
competitors counts as copying, and get an answer.

**To produce the Pattern Map and Remix brief, required:**

1. **Three ads** you think are genuinely good, described or linked. Zach's instruction is *"pick
   three ads you genuinely think are great"* — admiration is the selection criterion, not
   performance data.
2. **The brand you are remixing for** — what it sells, roughly what it costs, who buys it.

**Optional, and it sharpens the output:**

3. What the brand's current creative looks like, so the gap analysis is real
4. The platform the remix is destined for
5. Price point relative to the competitors you named, which is what makes the audit useful

⚠️ **Two ads still work. One does not, and this is a hard floor.** The Remix draws one dimension
from each of several ads, so with two it degrades honestly and you say the map is partial. **With a
single ad every dimension comes from that ad, which is not a remix, it is a copy of it** — the
failure `reference/the-pattern-map.md` calls legally and creatively bad. On one ad, produce the
pattern map, which is useful on its own, and say plainly that the Remix needs a second source.

⛔ **If the brand is missing, ask for it, but deliver the pattern maps anyway.** Breaking down the
three ads does not require knowing the destination. Only the Remix does. Produce the maps, mark the
Remix `⚠️ AWAITING BRAND`, and ask.

## ⚠️ Triaging a swipe file, which the course does not cover

ACA's selection rule is *"pick three ads you genuinely think are great."* **That does not
discriminate at the volume real swipe files reach** — everything in the file is already there
because someone thought it was great. At thirty ads the rule returns thirty.

Three cuts that do discriminate, in order:

1. **Drop anything from your own category first.** The Remix takes one dimension from each of three
   ads, and three direct competitors produce a fourth version of the same ad. ⛔ **If the whole file
   is direct competitors, that is your first finding** — the swipe file is the mechanism producing
   the sameness, and it should be said out loud before anything is mapped.
2. **Keep the ones you cannot immediately explain.** An ad whose trick is obvious has already been
   absorbed. The ones worth mapping are the ones that worked on you without your noticing why.
3. **Take three from three different categories** if the file allows it. Diversity of source is what
   makes the Remix produce something new rather than a blend of neighbours.

## How to run the artifact

**Teach the frame first, then produce.** Someone asking for an ad teardown usually has not accepted
the premise, and the premise is what stops the output being a copy.

1. **Settle whether they are remixing or copying.** The distinction is in
   `reference/the-competitor-audit.md` and it is the difference between the course working and
   producing a lawsuit.
2. **Map each ad across the six dimensions** using `reference/the-pattern-map.md`. Each dimension
   gets three entries: what they did, what the pattern is, and where else it has been seen.
3. **Do the Remix.** Pick one pattern per dimension, from any of the three ads, and combine.
4. **Close with the one-sentence form**, which is ACA's own template and the test that the remix is
   coherent rather than a pile of parts.

## Answering well

**The pattern is the abstraction, not the execution.** This is the single most common failure. *"You're
not stealing the price point. You're stealing the structure."* If someone writes "they used a
green background", that is what they did, not the pattern. Push for the level that transfers.

**"Seen this in" is the proof the pattern is real.** A pattern observed once is a coincidence. The
column exists to force a second sighting, and an honest "nowhere else yet" is a useful answer.

**Do not confuse this course with Course 302's competitor work.** 301 reverse-engineers individual
winning ads into reusable structures. 302 maps the competitive landscape as a whole. If the question
is "who are we up against and how are they positioned", that is 302's job, not this one's.

⛔ **Do not present the tooling as neutral.** The walkthrough runs inside Foreplay, which Zach
founded, and Foreplay features are named as the method throughout. `reference/provenance.md` sets
out exactly which steps depend on the product and
`reference/the-competitor-audit.md` gives the version that runs without it. **Say which you are
giving.**

## ⚠️ Scope

Three modules and about 25 minutes. It is a **research and ideation** course.

It does not cover: how to write or design the ad once the pattern is chosen, media buying, budgets,
testing methodology, or measurement beyond naming that feedback loops matter. It names no benchmark
numbers at all, which is unusual for this programme and worth knowing before someone asks it for
one.
