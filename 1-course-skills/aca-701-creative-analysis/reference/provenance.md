## ⛔ Its metric definitions contradict other ACA courses

**This is the most important thing in this file.** Three ACA courses define or benchmark the same
metrics differently, and none of them mentions the others.

### Hold rate — two incompatible formulas

| Course | Formula |
|---|---|
| **This course (701)**, Evan Lee | ⛔ **15-second views ÷ impressions** |
| **Course 402**, Chloe Rhys | ⛔ **10-second views ÷ 3-second views** |

⛔ **Different numerator and different denominator.** They are not two ways of expressing one number;
they measure different things. 402's is a *retention* rate among people who already stopped; 701's is
a *reach-weighted* rate across everyone served. **The same account will produce wildly different
values, and only 701 attaches a benchmark** — 8-12% typical, 20-30% for top performers, which is
meaningful **only** against 701's formula.

⚠️ **Evan flags the ambiguity himself:** *"There are a bunch of ways to calculate this metric, but one
of the most popular ways is..."* ⭐ **So the honest handling is: always state the formula beside the
number.** A hold rate quoted without its definition is unusable across teams.

### CTR — three benchmarks across three courses

| Course | The figure |
|---|---|
| **This course (701)** | *"The average click-through rate for a Meta ad is around **1 to 2%**"* |
| **Course 402**, Chloe Rhys | Sweet spot **0.9-1.3%**; above 1.4%, question whether the traffic is qualified |
| **Course 210**, TikTok | **Above 2%** for ecommerce |

⛔ **An ad at 1.5% CTR is above average by 701, a qualification risk by 402, and failing by 210.**
Three courses, three positions, no cross-references. Platform and CTR variant explain some of it —
210 is TikTok, 402 is explicitly CTR (Link Click) — but **701 does not say which CTR its 1-2% refers
to**, and it discusses both CTR (all) and outbound CTR immediately beforehand.

### First frame retention

⚠️ **Attributed inside the course to Mirella** — *"According to Morella, this always should be above 90%."* (the recording renders her name that way) **It appears in none of Mirella's own held courses.** It is a real and checkable metric
(1-second views ÷ impressions), but the 90% floor is a second-hand attribution with no source in this

## ⭐ The safe handling, and it is Evan's own

> "I would suggest you look at the hold rates among **your top performing creatives**, and figure out
> a benchmark that makes sense for your brand."

> "It's better to measure against **your own internal benchmarks.**"

⭐ **Said twice, about two different metrics.** The account's own distribution is the benchmark; every
number above is a starting reference. That instruction resolves all three collisions above, and it
came from the course that gave the most numbers.
