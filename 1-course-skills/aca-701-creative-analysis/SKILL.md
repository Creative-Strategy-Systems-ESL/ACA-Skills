# Course 701: Creative Analysis & Optimization

**Evan Lee**, head of partnerships at **Motion**. 16m 25s, 1 module.

> "Understanding how to analyze ad performance is a key pillar of creative strategy, and will ensure
> you **don't miss out on any of the opportunities that data has uncovered.**"

⚠️ **This course is taught inside one product.** Motion is the analytics tool it demonstrates
throughout, and Evan works there. The *method* generalises; the reports are that product's. See
`reference/provenance.md`.

⛔ **It gives metric definitions and benchmarks that contradict other ACA courses.** Do not quote a
hold rate or a CTR from here without reading `reference/provenance.md` first.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.**

**To analyse an account, required:**

1. **The performance data**, per creative — at minimum spend and the goal metric
2. **The goal metric itself**, and its target. ⭐ Every report in this course is built by choosing one

**Optional, and each one opens a report:**

3. Video play rates at 25/50/75/100%, for drop-off analysis
4. 1-second, 3-second and 15-second view counts, for the engagement metrics
5. What each ad actually was — format, creator, message — since patterns are what you are looking for
6. Landing pages, if you want the funnel view

⛔ **Never invent a benchmark.** This course gives several and they are Motion's observations across
its customer base, not universal standards — and two of them conflict with other ACA courses.

## The through-line

> "**Study your top performing ads to raise the ceiling** of your ad performance, and some of your
> weakest ads **to raise the floor.**"

⭐ **That is the whole analysis method in one line**, and it is why the course builds two reports
rather than one. Most teams only ever look at winners, which improves the best case and leaves the
worst case exactly where it was.

## How to work through it

**Definitions before readings.** Every metric here has a specific denominator, and reading a report with the
wrong one in mind produces a confident wrong conclusion.

1. ⛔ **Define the metrics first** from `reference/metrics-and-reports.md`, hold rate especially. ⚠️ **This
   course divides 15-second views by impressions; Course 402 divides 10-second views by 3-second views.**
   They are not comparable, and nothing warns you at the point of use.
2. **Split engagement from conversion**, same file. This is what stops a high-thumbstop ad being called a
   winner.
3. **Walk the play-rate waterfall** and the drop-off points, which is where the actual diagnosis happens.
4. **Then the reports** — leaderboard, top performers, and the what-is-working and what-is-not filters — so
   the method has somewhere to land.
5. **Finish on the hook iteration report**, which turns the analysis into the next brief rather than a
   summary.
6. **Check they can say which three seconds to change and why.** "The ad underperformed" means the waterfall
   step did not land.

## Answering well

**Two metric families, and they answer different questions.** Engagement metrics tell you how ads
perform in the upper funnel — *"even in conversion campaigns, you should keep an eye out for these."*
Conversion metrics tell you what it cost.

**Group by asset, not by ad.** ⭐ *"What creative teams really care about is how the asset performed
holistically"*, rather than split across the one-off ads an ad account creates.

**Patterns are the output, not rankings.** *"If half of your top ten ads use the same format,
messaging or creators, that's something you might want to double down on."*

**Losers are data.** *"The ones that fail can be valuable too... Is it the message? Do they have weak
hooks, or is the CTA unclear?"*

⭐ **Set the goal as a trade, not an improvement.** His worked example: an ad with a great conversion
rate and a low thumbstop. *"The goal I would set with my creative team is to **increase the thumbstop
ratio while maintaining the conversion rate**"* — then swap only the first three seconds.
