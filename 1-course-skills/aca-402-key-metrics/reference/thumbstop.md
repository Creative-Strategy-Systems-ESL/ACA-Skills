# Thumbstop

> "Thumbstop rate, also known as Hook Rate, is the percentage of people who pause to watch your ad
> for at least three seconds."

**3-second video views ÷ impressions.** So 100 people see the ad, 35 pause for 3 seconds, thumbstop
is 35%. ⭐ **On TikTok it is 2-second views**, and the same name means a different calculation there.

## ⭐ Not all thumbstops are created equal

> "It's easy to make someone stop. **It's harder to make the right person stop.** A chaotic or
> shocking visual might spike thumbstop, but if those viewers don't go on to watch more, click, or
> convert, it's **noise, not signal**."

> "Our job isn't to just grab attention. It's to **earn the right to tell the rest of the story.**"

**Thumbstop is targeting.** *"In the pre-iOS14 world, we relied on data signals to serve the right
ad to the right person. Now, creative is the targeting."* When someone pauses for 3 seconds, Meta
reads that as *"this person found this interesting"* and starts building a profile of people like
them.

> "The stronger your thumbstop, the faster and more efficiently your creative can train the
> algorithm."

⚠️ **Which is why a high thumbstop that leads nowhere is worse than a low one.** *"A high thumbstop
that doesn't lead to clicks or purchases isn't just ineffective, it's misleading."* It trains the
algorithm on the wrong people.

> "The key question isn't just: did they stop scrolling? It's: **did the creative stop the right
> people, for the right reasons, at the right time?**"

## The bands

| Thumbstop | Chloe's reading |
|---|---|
| **Below 20%**, consistently | *"Time to iterate and revisit your hook approach"* |
| **25-30% and up** | *"Generally considered solid"* |
| **40%+** | *"Very strong"* |
| **50% or higher** | *"Very rare and often means you've struck gold"* |

⛔ **Do not present these as industry benchmarks.** They are ACA's, given without a source or a
vertical, and another ACA course gives a number for a similarly named metric that cannot be
reconciled with these. See `reference/provenance.md`.

⚠️ **Modest thumbstop is not automatically a failure.** *"You'll sometimes have ads with a modest
thumbstop but excellent conversion rates. That's usually because they stop fewer people, but the
ones they do stop are very qualified."*

⚠️ **And the reverse.** *"Other times, you'll have ads with incredible thumbstop and CTR, but they
never convert. That's often a sign that the hook was strong, but the angle didn't attract the right
audience. Or worse, it misled them."*

> "📌 **Thumbstop is not a number. It's a signal.** It tells us what's grabbing attention, and more
> importantly, **who** it's grabbing."

## The anatomy of a thumbstop: three layers

> "We're talking about a moment, one charged second when a person decides whether to lean in or keep
> scrolling. And that decision is influenced by three elements that work together: **Visual.
> Language. Presentation.**"

### 1. The hook visual — shot selection and imagery

**The visual should do one of two things:** deliver an emotional or sensory jolt, **or** create a
question the brain wants to answer.

| What works | Why |
|---|---|
| **Close-ups** | Skin, texture, hands, faces, motion. *"Intimacy stops people"* |
| **Incomplete actions** | A knife slicing almost through a cake. A hand reaching. *"The viewer wants to see the resolution, and that curiosity buys you seconds"* |
| **Sensory visuals** | Cream being applied, leggings being pulled, hair being brushed. *"These simulate real-life texture and help eCommerce brands overcome the 'I can't touch this' barrier"* |
| **Colour** | High contrast, bright tones, or super clean minimalism. Vibrant nail polish, powder sparkles, bold packaging |

> "Your visual doesn't need to be wild. It just needs to make someone feel something: delight,
> tension, satisfaction, curiosity."

⭐ **This is a shot list instruction, not a taste note.** *"When you're concepting or briefing, don't
just think about coverage. Think about thumbstop potential. **What is the moment someone will want
to see paused?** That's your opener, that's your hook."*

### 2. The hook copy / header — messaging

The words on screen in the first second or two. **The header does one or both of:**

- **Frames the visual** — helps the viewer instantly understand what they are seeing
- **Creates tension or desire** — raises a question, suggests a payoff, taps an emotion

> "You're not writing a paragraph. You're writing **a billboard that disappears in 3 seconds.**"

| Header style | Chloe's example |
|---|---|
| Bold problem/solution line | *"Hate your jeans? Try these."* |
| A hooky question | *"Still using 2008 deodorant?"* |
| A POV or first-person setup | *"POV: You finally find a bra that fits."* |
| A conversational testimonial pull | *"I'll never wear wire bras again."* |

**The best ones are: specific** (not vague), **emotional** (fear, relief, pride, curiosity), and
**relatable** (*"that's me"* or *"I want that"*).

⭐ *"Great hooks often already exist in your buyer's words."* Study customer reviews and the patterns
in your own performance data.

### 3. The presentation — layout, style and timing

| Rule | What she says |
|---|---|
| **Static headers almost always win** | *"If the header animates in, slides around, or competes with motion in the visual, you're splitting the viewer's attention"* |
| **Legibility is everything** | Black or red on a white box, or white on black. Familiar social-native fonts, meme-style layouts. Usually sans serif. *"Anything overly decorative, complex, or hard to read? Pass"* |
| **Limit the noise** | *"If the copy's animating, the talent is moving, and product is spinning, all at once, it becomes overwhelming. **Anchor one thing. Let the eye rest.**"* |
| **Motion should serve the story** | Gentle upward motion mimics scroll behaviour and feels native. Zoom-ins add energy. Reversals trigger curiosity. *"But all motion should feel purposeful"* |
| **Thumbnails still matter** | *"The still frame before autoplay can influence who watches."* If it includes your header and a recognisable image, you have an edge |

⭐ **On brand fonts, and it is a client-handling note as much as a design one:** *"I've been in
situations where clients request to use their specific brand font in all paid advertising. If this
is a calligraphy font, gently remind them that the style with the platform it's running on is always
optimal for performance. Social native style graphics and text will typically always outperform a
heavily branded approach in thumbstop."*

⚠️ **The thumbnail is usually not yours to set.** *"Since media buyers are typically setting the
thumbnail, feel free to reach out to them with a screenshot or recommendation."* Put it in the
hand-off or it does not happen.

**Her four pre-ship questions:**

1. Does this make sense right away to someone with **zero context**?
2. Is my hook tailored to the audience or persona I'm trying to capture?
3. Is there **one clear focal point**?
4. Would I stop to watch?

> "If you're unsure, chances are the algorithm will be too. And it won't deliver your ad the way you
> want."

## The 7 visual hook archetypes

> "seven visual hook patterns that consistently stop the scroll. These are time-tested formats used
> across categories and platforms—and they work. **You can use these patterns to select the right
> first shot for your video ad.**"

| # | Archetype | What it is, and her example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Incomplete Action — the cliffhanger shot** | Begin an action, don't finish it. *"People crave resolution."* Cutting into a dish, cut away right before the bite. Works in food, skincare, fitness, home |
| 2 | **Tactile Close-Ups** | *"Think visual ASMR."* Hands rubbing in lotion, fingers stretching fabric, a razor gliding over skin. *"If the viewer can almost feel it through the screen, you're doing it right"* |
| 3 | **Relatable Problems or Desirable Outcomes** | Emotional targeting. A tangled cord, leggings adjusted at the office; or shiny hair, calm skin. Creates recognition (*"that's so me"*) or aspiration (*"I want that"*) |
| 4 | **Split Screens — before vs after** | Transformation communicated instantly. One leg shaved, one not. One side of the surface spotless |
| 5 | **Unexpected Visuals — the pattern interrupt** | *"I also call these **WTF openers**."* A bird's-eye view, legs rotated upside down, something poured in reverse. *"Just enough confusion to create curiosity, without veering into randomness"* |
| 6 | **Motion, Time and Colour — editing tactics** | `Not about what was filmed, but how it's edited.` Push-ins, zooms, speed ramps, boomerangs, colour grading |
| 7 | **Layered Storytelling — visual + headline harmony** | The image and the line as one unit. A black scrub on a face is odd; paired with *"I had no idea how oily my skin was"* it lands |

⭐ **Her own ranking: if you nail one, nail number 7.** *"The best creative teams think of the visual
and the copy as one unit: a mini-story. **If you only nail one thing, it should be that combo of
visual and headline.**"*

**Three notes she attaches, which are the parts people skip:**

- ⭐ **On split screens (4), exaggerate the "before" deliberately.** *"If your header says 'Stop
  Wearing Sloppy Sweatpants' and you show a split screen, make sure that your shot shows the
  ugliest, baggiest sweatpants shot possible, so that your product clearly looks like an incredible
  alternative in comparison."* This is why an **exaggerated-problem shot list** is an asset worth
  keeping.
- ⚠️ **On split screens and platform policy.** *"For quite a while, it seemed that Meta had strict
  restrictions on showing before-afters when it came to the personal attributes space: weight-loss
  transformations or skincare results, resulting in ads being flagged. Be cautious of this with
  weight-loss and skincare brands. However, I have noticed in recent years they seem to have
  loosened their policies."* ⛔ **This is her observation of a policy at a moment, not the policy.**
  Check the current rules before briefing one.
- ⛔ **On pattern interrupts (5), the clickbait line.** *"These can often garner really high
  thumbstop, but watch out because **you can quickly fall into clickbait territory** if the opener
  visual has no clear connection to the product."* This is the archetype that produces the
  high-thumbstop, no-conversion ad described above.

**On colour grading, one concrete technique:** *"On before/after split screens, we often colour the
before visual to greyscale in order to make the solution stand out even more."*

## The 7 copy hook formulas

> "strong hooks tend to follow predictable copywriting formulas. And the best ones feel like they
> were written by a real person—because **often, they were.** In fact, some of the best copy I've
> used has been pulled straight from customer reviews."

| # | Formula | Structures | Her examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem / Solution** | *Hate That? Try This* · *Still doing That?* · *Can your [X] do this?* · *I'll never do X again* · *I've been doing it wrong my whole life* | *"Hate paying extra for pink razors?"* · *"I'll never wear a wire bra again."* |
| 2 | **Desirable Claims** | *The #1 secret to [result]* · *Experts have reinvented [category]* · *This [product] is helping [group] do [thing]* | *"The #1 secret to looking good in every dress."* · *"Psychologists have reinvented weight loss."* |
| 3 | **Reasons to Switch** | *X reasons why [persona] are switching to Y* · *Why [group] is quitting Z* · *Why everyone's buying [number] of this* | *"4 reasons why women with thick hair are switching razors"* · *"Why men are buying 5 pairs of these pants"* |
| 4 | **How-To and Informational** | *How to [result]* · *How to get X without Y* · *What to do when [problem]* | *"How to get rid of dark circles"* · *"How to get a flat stomach without giving up pasta"* |
| 5 | **Easy Trick / Hack Language** | *Easy trick to [result]* · *The lazy way to [result]* · *Hack your [problem]* | *"Easy hack to fix frizzy hair"* · *"The lazy way to whiten your teeth"* |
| 6 | **Myths, Mistakes, Misconceptions** | *Everything you know about X is wrong* · *You've been doing X wrong your whole life* · *X myths about Y* | *"Everything you know about shampoo is wrong"* · *"3 myths about plus-size fashion"* |
| 7 | **Conversational & Social Native** | *PSA to all [group]* · *My friend said I HAD to try this* · *Not me finally trying [product]* · text-bubble opens · POV headers | *"POV: you finally find a bra that doesn't dig in"* |

⛔ **Formulas 2, 4 and 5 generate claims, and several of her examples are claims a brand would have
to substantiate.** *"Psychologists have reinvented weight loss"*, *"how to get a flat stomach"*,
*"the lazy way to whiten your teeth"* are health and results claims. They are reproduced because
they are hers. **A hook written for a real brand must not invent a claim the brand has not made.**
The course does not raise this.

⭐ **Her actual instruction is to steal from reviews, not to fill in the templates.** *"When in
doubt, try stealing directly from the review section. If a customer said 'I'll never wear jeans
again after these', great, that's your headline."* And: *"Think of the formulas we covered **not as
templates, but as tools.** The best hooks sound like they came from your audience, not from a
marketing team."*

**Presentation note:** this copy is *"typically presented as an on-screen header, sometimes static,
sometimes styled like a text bubble or tweet, or even karaoke-style read-along text."*

## ⭐ Persona-callout hooks

Her own most-quoted tactic, from Jack Archer.

> "One of the most effective tactics we've discovered at Jack Archer is **calling out your target
> persona directly in the hook copy.** This means explicitly mentioning the audience you're trying
> to reach, be it their age, gender, or a specific life stage."

**Her example, and its result:** *"One of our recent top-performing hooks, coming in at **almost 50%
thumbstop**, is: 'Why do men over 30 waste money on mediocre pants?'"*

> "The specificity made the message feel personal, almost as if we were speaking directly to them."

**How to run it:**

1. **Start with specificity.** Name a demographic or persona. Either a new persona you want to reach
   or your existing most reliable customer base
2. **Test variations.** *"'Why busy millennials are switching to microwavable meals for one' could be
   adapted to 'Why seniors are switching to microwavable meals for one.'"*
3. **Monitor performance** in Ads Manager, including the demographic breakdowns

### ⛔ The algorithm claim, and how she qualifies it

She argues the callout may also steer delivery, and she is careful about it. **Quote her hedge or do
not quote the claim:**

> "**Meta doesn't explicitly confirm** that mentioning a specific audience in your copy will
> influence targeting, the platform does use machine learning to predict which users are most likely
> to engage with your ad based on its content. So when your ad copy specifies 'men over 30', the
> algorithm **may** interpret this as a signal to prioritize delivery to users who fit that
> description, especially if initial engagement metrics are strong."

⛔ **Reported as fact, this becomes "writing your audience into the copy targets them", which is not
what she said and not something Meta documents.** The defensible half is the first one: naming the
persona makes the persona feel addressed, and that lifts engagement. The delivery effect is her
inference, and she labels it as one.
