# Course 601: Launching Ad Campaigns

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 9m 57s, 1 module.

> "Congratulations! You've created your ads, and they're ready to go live. **Now comes the critical
> phase where creative strategy meets media buying.**"

**This is the handover course.** It is short, and it is about the seam where most creative work
quietly loses its ability to teach anyone anything.

⚠️ **It opens by naming its own boundary**, which is unusually honest for a curriculum:

> "While as a creative strategist, **determining ad account structure isn't technically your specific
> role**, understanding how creative testing works is essential. And if you happen to be a media
> buyer taking this course — kudos to you! You're building a powerful skillset that combines both
> worlds."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what a naming convention should track, why it matters,
or how much creative an account needs, and get an answer.

**To design a naming convention, required:**

1. **What you want to be able to compare later.** ⭐ This is the whole design input and it is the
   step people skip — the convention is built backwards from the questions you want answered
2. **Who else has to use it.** *"This is a combined effort between creative strategists and media
   buyers. There's simply no way around it"*

**To plan creative volume, required:**

3. **Monthly ad spend.** ⛔ `aca-plan-creative-volume` runs the ladder properly, including where
   ACA's two recordings disagree

**Optional:**

4. Whether the account uses a tagging tool like Motion, which changes how much the convention matters
5. Current fatigue signals, ROAS goals, audience breadth

⛔ **Never invent a volume figure for a spend level.** ACA's two recordings of this course give
different numbers at four points on the ladder, and averaging them produces a figure neither one
gives.

## The through-line

> "This collaboration creates **the feedback loop** that drives continuous improvement."

⭐ **Everything in this course exists to close that loop.** Naming conventions, the tracker and the
volume ladder are three answers to one question: how does what happened last month change what you
make next month? An account that cannot answer it is producing creative, not learning from it.

## How to run it

**Naming is designed backwards from the comparisons you want to run**, so the comparisons come first.

1. **Establish why a strategist needs the account** from `reference/naming-and-tracking.md`, even without
   running it. This is the argument the rest depends on.
2. ⛔ **Ask what they want to compare later.** The four comparative questions in that file are the starting
   set; the real ones are whatever this account argues about.
3. **Give the eleven elements a convention must track**, same file. ⚠️ ACA's own sheet implements five of
   them, and three of the four questions cannot be answered without the missing six.
4. **Frame the tracker as institutional memory**, not a report, from the same file.
5. **Give the volume ladder** from `reference/the-ladder-in-brief.md`. ⛔ It is indexed on **monthly** spend;
   Course 703's five variables are on **daily** spend, and confusing them is a 30× error.
6. ⭐ **Produce the artifacts through their own skills**: `aca-name-creative-assets` for the convention,
   `aca-plan-creative-volume` for the number. ⚠️ Name either only if installed.
7. **Check the convention answers their actual comparison question.** A convention that cannot is a naming
   scheme, not an instrument.

## Answering well

**Three reasons the strategist needs the account**, and they are all forward-looking:

1. *"To plan creative production efficiently"*
2. *"To schedule regular performance check-ins"*
3. *"To gather data for your next round of iterations"*

**Naming is designed backwards from the comparison.** ⭐ The delivered recording gives the four
questions the convention exists to answer, and they are the best argument in the course:

> "Are image ads working better than video ads? Is UGC working better than studio production? Are ads
> with the VO better than ads with just music? And are funny hooks working better than hooks that
> show the problem first? **You can't answer any of these questions if your creatives are not named
> or tagged effectively.**"

**The tracker is memory, not reporting.** *"This isn't meant to replace your reporting tools — it's a
strategic document that builds **institutional knowledge** about what works and why."* And the reason
it pays: *"When new team members join or you're developing new concepts, **this historical record
becomes invaluable.**"*

**Volume guidance is a ceiling, not a quota.** ⛔ *"If you see no signs of fatigue, **don't produce
endless ads you won't test.**"*

⚠️ **Tagging tools are a real alternative to naming discipline.** *"The beauty of tools like Motion is
that you can tag assets directly if these naming conventions weren't implemented properly."* She
still recommends starting with the structure, but a team that will not maintain a convention is
better served by a tagging tool than by a convention nobody follows.

## ⚠️ Scope

The handover, the documentation and the volume question. It does not set up campaigns, choose
objectives, structure ad sets or manage budgets — the course says outright that account structure is
not the strategist's role. It does not define the metrics used to spot fatigue, which is Course 402.
It does not design the tests, which is Course 602.

⛔ **The two artifacts have their own skills.** `aca-name-creative-assets` emits ACA's naming
generator; `aca-track-creative-tests` fills the testing tracker; `aca-plan-creative-volume` runs the
ladder.
