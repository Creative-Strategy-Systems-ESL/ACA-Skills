# When UGC is right, and where to find the people

## What UGC means here

> "UGC technically stands for user-generated content—but in performance marketing, we use that term
> a little differently. We're not just talking about organic content, **UGC typically means native-
> looking, creator-made video content—often scripted, directed, and edited** but still giving al
> native, personal, and real to the platform"

> "Someone holding the product up to the camera. Talking straight to lens. Explaining their
> experience. Showing a transformation. Using the kind of casual, unscripted energy that feels like
> **a friend giving you a rec, not an ad pushing you to buy.**"

⭐ **Note what that definition does not require: that the creator is a real customer, or that the
content is unpaid, or that it is unscripted.** It is a production style, not a provenance claim. The
word "authentic" in this course means "reads as native", not "unpaid testimonial".

## ⭐ The spectrum, not the binary

> "For the brands I oversee at OpenStore, I often think about the creative mix along a spectrum."

| | UGC / native | Elevated / studio |
|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Low-lift, lo-fi, fast turnaround | Polished, brand-forward, production-heavy |
| **What it is for** | *"Your scrappy, high-velocity testing engine"* | *"Where you build brand equity"* |
| **Because it is** | Fast to produce · easy to iterate or redo · built for volume · blends into the feed | Premium positioning · cinematic storytelling · reinforces product quality and lifestyle aspiration |
| **Use it for** | Testing value props, pain points or personas · collecting quick signal data on hooks and CTAs | Evergreen hero ads · website banners and PDP imagery and video · seasonal campaigns · establishing trust through aesthetics |

⚠️ **Each end is itself a spectrum**, and this is the part people flatten: *"A studio e-commerce
shoot versus a full-on TV commercial production, or a more elevated UGC product shoot versus a quick
and dirty in-car testimonial."* "Let's do UGC" does not specify a budget or a look.

**The choice, in her words:**

- **UGC over elevated** *"when speed, signal and relatability matter most"*
- **Elevated over UGC** *"when you need brand lift, premium perception, or a strong visual identity"*

> "Most of the time, the real answer is: **you need both.**"

⭐ **This is a budgeting argument as much as a creative one.** A system that scales native UGC while
leaving room for hero moments is the goal; treating UGC as the cheap default gets you volume with no
brand, and treating studio as the standard gets you a beautiful account with no test data.

## The four routes to talent

> "Let's start with where to look."

| Route | What you get | The trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Backstage** | *"A massive platform used by actors, models, voiceover artists, dancers."* High volume | ⚠️ *"A very wide mix of experience levels. A lot of people submit to every listing that pops up."* Expect dozens or hundreds of responses, many *"completely unfamiliar with short-form, social-first UGC"* |
| **UGC platforms** — Trend.io, Billo, Cohely | *"Built for brands looking for social content."* Creators experienced at vertical, to-camera, prompt-following work. You pay per deliverable and the platform handles some logistics | ⭐ *"If you're in a time crunch or working with a limited budget, they can be a great shortcut"* |
| **Organic outreach, especially TikTok** | ⭐ *"The third and often most underrated option."* Creators who already know how to speak to camera and hook attention. Search within your product niche | Micro-creators with small followings but *"very tapped into what works on short-form video. And they're often excited to do UGC work if you reach out directly"* |
| ⭐ **You** | See below | |

⚠️ **TikTok sourcing works even if you do not advertise there.** *"Even if you're not running ads on
TikTok, it's one of the best places to find creators."* The skill you are buying is on-camera
delivery, and it ports.

### ⭐ Sometimes you are the talent

> "here's one more thing no one tells you: sometimes, **you are the talent.** In performance
> marketing—especially in fast-moving sprints—creative strategists, copywriters, even directors-
> level or VPs will step in front of the camera. We shoot testimonials. We use our hands in POV
> product demos. We test new hooks in selfie format."

> "This isn't just a cost-saving move. It's fast. It's real. And often, it works. **You know your
> product. You know your angle. You know the voice and delivery style that performs.**"

⚠️ **She frames this as a career asset, not a fallback:** *"If you're comfortable filming content
with your phone and can deliver with energy and clarity, it's a superpower. And it's something
hiring managers in this space increasingly look for when building creative teams."*

## The casting call

> "Your casting posting should clearly include:"

| # | What to state |
|---|---|
| 1 | **What the product is** |
| 2 | **Who the brand is** — *"ok if you don't want to disclose the actual brand, but it helps"* |
| 3 | **The tone or vibe you're going for** |
| 4 | **Who you're looking for** in terms of energy, look and/or persona |
| 5 | **Exactly what you need submitted** — a final edited video? Raw clips? Both? |
| 6 | **The rate** |

⛔ **The rate is a required field and the course gives no benchmark for it.** Chloe says to be
specific and never says what specific looks like. Ask for the budget rather than filling it in.

> "Be specific, but not rigid. **If you're too broad, you'll waste time reviewing people who aren't
> a fit. But if you're too narrow, you'll limit the number of submissions** — especially if you're
> on a tight timeline."

### ⭐ The 30-second sample, and its cost

> "You can also explicitly request a quick **30-second video sample** — ideally speaking unscripted
> about a product they like. This gives you a fast read on camera presence, vocal tone, energy level
> and authenticity. **You'll learn more in 30 seconds of off-the-cuff talking than in an entire
> polished reel.**"

⚠️ **And she names the trade-off rather than selling it:** *"Requiring a video sample will no doubt
reduce the overall number of submissions you'll receive, and often if they have a solid UGC reel
this step can be avoided."*

⭐ **So the rule is conditional, not universal:** ask for the sample when the applicant pool is broad
or the reels are not UGC. Skip it when someone already has a real UGC reel and the timeline is tight.

## Matching the route to the situation

> "Use the right platform for your needs. **Backstage gives you volume. Trend, Billo and Cohely give
> you speed and relevance. TikTok gives you nuance and potential long-term collaborators.**"

⭐ **That last phrase is the one worth acting on.** The first two routes are transactional and the
third builds a bench, which matters because the course later tells you to track and reuse the
creators who perform.
