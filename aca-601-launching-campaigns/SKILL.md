# Course 601: Launching Ad Campaigns

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 9m 57s, 1 module.

> "Congratulations! You've created your ads, and they're ready to go live. **Now comes the critical
> phase where creative strategy meets media buying.**"

**This is the handover course.** It is short, and it is about the seam where most creative work
quietly loses its ability to teach anyone anything.

⚠️ **It opens by naming its own boundary**, which is unusually honest for a curriculum:

> "While as a creative strategist, **determining ad account structure isn't technically your specific
> role**, understanding how creative testing works is essential. And if you happen to be a media
> buyer taking this course — kudos to you! You're building a powerful skillset that combines both
> worlds."

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what a naming convention should track, why it matters,
or how much creative an account needs, and get an answer.

**To design a naming convention, required:**

1. **What you want to be able to compare later.** ⭐ This is the whole design input and it is the
   step people skip — the convention is built backwards from the questions you want answered
2. **Who else has to use it.** *"This is a combined effort between creative strategists and media
   buyers. There's simply no way around it"*

**To plan creative volume, required:**

3. **Monthly ad spend.** ⛔ `aca-plan-creative-volume` runs the ladder properly, including where
   ACA's two recordings disagree

**Optional:**

4. Whether the account uses a tagging tool like Motion, which changes how much the convention matters
5. Current fatigue signals, ROAS goals, audience breadth

⛔ **Never invent a volume figure for a spend level.** ACA's two recordings of this course give
different numbers at four points on the ladder, and averaging them produces a figure neither one
gives.

## The through-line

> "This collaboration creates **the feedback loop** that drives continuous improvement."

⭐ **Everything in this course exists to close that loop.** Naming conventions, the tracker and the
volume ladder are three answers to one question: how does what happened last month change what you
make next month? An account that cannot answer it is producing creative, not learning from it.

## How to run it

**Naming is designed backwards from the comparisons you want to run**, so the comparisons come first.

1. **Establish why a strategist needs the account** from **Naming conventions and the tracker** (below), even without
   running it. This is the argument the rest depends on.
2. ⛔ **Ask what they want to compare later.** The four comparative questions in that file are the starting
   set; the real ones are whatever this account argues about.
3. **Give the eleven elements a convention must track**, same file. ⚠️ ACA's own sheet implements five of
   them, and three of the four questions cannot be answered without the missing six.
4. **Frame the tracker as institutional memory**, not a report, from the same file.
5. **Give the volume ladder** from **How much creative an account needs** (below). ⛔ It is indexed on **monthly** spend;
   Course 703's five variables are on **daily** spend, and confusing them is a 30× error.
6. ⭐ **Produce the artifacts through their own skills**: `aca-name-creative-assets` for the convention,
   `aca-plan-creative-volume` for the number. ⚠️ Name either only if installed.
7. **Check the convention answers their actual comparison question.** A convention that cannot is a naming
   scheme, not an instrument.

## Answering well

**Three reasons the strategist needs the account**, and they are all forward-looking:

1. *"To plan creative production efficiently"*
2. *"To schedule regular performance check-ins"*
3. *"To gather data for your next round of iterations"*

**Naming is designed backwards from the comparison.** ⭐ The delivered recording gives the four
questions the convention exists to answer, and they are the best argument in the course:

> "Are image ads working better than video ads? Is UGC working better than studio production? Are ads
> with the VO better than ads with just music? And are funny hooks working better than hooks that
> show the problem first? **You can't answer any of these questions if your creatives are not named
> or tagged effectively.**"

**The tracker is memory, not reporting.** *"This isn't meant to replace your reporting tools — it's a
strategic document that builds **institutional knowledge** about what works and why."* And the reason
it pays: *"When new team members join or you're developing new concepts, **this historical record
becomes invaluable.**"*

**Volume guidance is a ceiling, not a quota.** ⛔ *"If you see no signs of fatigue, **don't produce
endless ads you won't test.**"*

⚠️ **Tagging tools are a real alternative to naming discipline.** *"The beauty of tools like Motion is
that you can tag assets directly if these naming conventions weren't implemented properly."* She
still recommends starting with the structure, but a team that will not maintain a convention is
better served by a tagging tool than by a convention nobody follows.

## ⚠️ Scope

The handover, the documentation and the volume question. It does not set up campaigns, choose
objectives, structure ad sets or manage budgets — the course says outright that account structure is
not the strategist's role. It does not define the metrics used to spot fatigue, which is Course 402.
It does not design the tests, which is Course 602.

⛔ **The two artifacts have their own skills.** `aca-name-creative-assets` emits ACA's naming
generator; `aca-track-creative-tests` fills the testing tracker; `aca-plan-creative-volume` runs the
ladder.

### Naming conventions

> "Let's start with something that **seems simple but is incredibly powerful**: naming conventions."

> "This is **a combined effort between creative strategists and media buyers — there's simply no way
> around it.** Both teams need to collaborate on naming conventions to maintain an effective feedback
> loop."

#### ⭐ The four questions naming exists to answer

The delivered recording gives the argument the written script leaves out, and it is the part worth
quoting to anyone who thinks this is admin:

> "Your naming structure should track key elements so you can **create comparative analysis reports**
> that will answer super important questions like:"

| Question |
|---|
| **Are image ads working better than video ads?** |
| **Is UGC working better than studio production?** |
| **Are ads with the VO better than ads with just music?** |
| **Are funny hooks working better than hooks that show the problem first?** |

> "**You can't answer any of these questions if your creatives are not named or tagged
> effectively.**"

⛔ **Design the convention backwards from the questions.** Each of those four requires a specific
field to exist: format, producer, VO type, hook category. A convention built without asking what you
want to compare will be missing exactly the field you need.

#### The eleven elements

> "Your naming structure should track these key elements to enable meaningful analysis."

| # | Element | What it records |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Producer** | Who made the ad? Agency, in-house, or influencer? |
| 2 | **Date** | When was it created? |
| 3 | **File Name** | Unique identifier |
| 4 | **Concept Stage** | Net new, or an iteration? |
| 5 | **Format** | Video, image, carousel, GIF |
| 6 | **Ad Type** | Listicle, before/after, skit |
| 7 | **Concept ID** | Which concept does this execute? |
| 8 | **Hook Identifier** | A, B, C |
| 9 | **Hook Category** | Problem-focused, benefit-focused |
| 10 | **Talent Name** | Who appears in the ad? |
| 11 | **VO Type** | No VO, text-to-speech, human VO |

⚠️ **The delivered recording ends this list with *"just to name a few important ones"***, which the
script does not. Treat eleven as ACA's starting set rather than a closed specification.

⛔ **ACA's own generator sheet implements only five of these eleven.** Date, File Name, Concept
Stage, Format, Hook Category and VO Type have no column in it. That is not a small gap: **four of
those six are exactly what the four comparative questions need.** See **Provenance** (below), and
`aca-name-creative-assets` for the sheet with the gap flagged.

#### The generator

> "In your resources section, I've included **a handy ad naming generator sheet** that you can use and
> share with your media buying team. **On the first tab, you'll build out all the parameters you want
> to track. The second tab includes dropdowns that automatically generate URL parameters** when you
> make selections."

⭐ **`aca-name-creative-assets` emits that sheet**, all three tabs, with its defects flagged.

#### ⚠️ The alternative she names

> "The beauty of tools like **Motion** is that you can **tag assets directly** if these naming
> conventions weren't implemented properly. But **starting with a solid naming structure makes
> analysis far more efficient.**"

The delivered version is more permissive: *"if you're using a tool like Motion, it makes life so much
easier because **you don't have to actually be using these naming conventions**, you can tag the
assets directly."*

⭐ **This matters for real teams.** A naming convention only works if every person naming a file
follows it every time, and most teams do not. Tagging after the fact is worse in principle and better
in practice than a convention that is half-followed, because a half-followed convention produces
comparisons that are silently wrong rather than obviously missing.

### The Creative Testing Tracker

> "Beyond naming conventions, you need **a system to document test results and learnings over time.**"

**What it does, in her list:**

| Function |
|---|
| Document what was tested |
| Record your hypothesis and goals for each test |
| Track spend and key performance metrics |
| Link to creative assets and detailed reports |
| Summarise learnings and next steps |

#### ⛔ What it is not

> "**This isn't meant to replace your reporting tools** — it's a strategic document that builds
> **institutional knowledge** about what works and why."

The delivered recording is blunter: *"**don't go crazy adding a million metrics in there**, stick to
Motion for that."*

⭐ **That instruction is the one that keeps the tracker alive.** A tracker that duplicates the
dashboard becomes a second thing to maintain and is abandoned within a quarter. The columns that
justify its existence are the ones no dashboard holds: the hypothesis, and what you learned.

#### Why it pays

> "**When new team members join** or you're developing new concepts, **this historical record becomes
> invaluable.**"

⚠️ **The delivered version adds a case the script does not:** *"or if you're working with a new
agency."* Agency turnover is where undocumented learning disappears fastest, and it is a stronger
argument than onboarding.

⭐ **`aca-track-creative-tests` fills ACA's actual sheet**, and flags the three fields Course 602 says
to capture that the sheet has no column for.

⚠️ **Course 602 calls the same artifact by a different name** — *"the Creative Test Tracker I use with
most of my clients"* — and Mirella calls it the *"Creative Testing Tracker & Pipeline template"* and
the *"creative pipeline document"*. One sheet, three names, two courses.

## How much creative an account needs

> "One of the most common questions I hear is: **'How much creative does my ad account actually
> need?'**"

> "**The hard truth is that every account is different.** Some find a winning ad that runs profitably
> for months, while others burn through creative weekly. However, we can establish some guidelines
> based on monthly ad spend."

### ⛔ Read this before quoting any number below

**ACA's two recordings of this course disagree at four points on the ladder**, including on what a
"creative" is: the script counts finished creatives, the delivered recording counts **concepts, each
with three to five variations**. At $10k-25k that is 3-4 assets on one reading and 9-20 on the other.

⭐ **The table below is the script's, which is the more conservative reading.** For a real account,
use `aca-plan-creative-volume`, which carries both recordings side by side, runs ACA's arithmetic,
and never averages the two.

### The six tiers, at a glance

| Monthly spend | New creative | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **$0-10k** | **~1 per month**, or as needed | *"Creative fatigue isn't a major concern"* |
| **$10k-25k** | **3-4 per month** | *"You're spending enough to gather data faster, which means your current winners may tire more quickly"* |
| **$25k-50k** | **~1 per week** (4-5 per month) | Higher if ROAS goals are aggressive or the audience is narrow |
| **$50k-100k** | **2-4 per week** (6-20 per month) | ⭐ **40-50% iterations of proven winners**, the rest net-new angles |
| **$100k-500k** | **10-50% of spend allocated to testing** | **30-40% new concepts · 60-70% iterations.** Volume is derived from budget, not counted |
| **$500k-$1M+** | **25-50% of spend to testing** | Big swings plus heavy iteration. *"Multiple creative teams or agencies working side by side"* |

⭐ **Tier 5 is where the ladder changes kind.** Below it, volume is a count. At $100k+ it becomes a
budget divided by a cost per test, which is why ACA gives a worked calculation there and nowhere
else. `aca-plan-creative-volume` runs it.

### ⛔ The three rules that override the tier

> "Always let your data guide your decisions."

| Signal | What to do |
|---|---|
| **No signs of fatigue** | ⛔ *"Don't produce endless ads you won't test"* |
| **Performance drops** | *"You may need more new concepts or iterations"* |
| **Scaling quickly** | *"Ramp up creative production to avoid hitting a performance plateau"* |

⭐ **Rule 1 is the one that saves money and the one that gets ignored**, because it contradicts the
six tiers of numbers printed above it. **The ladder is a ceiling on what is worth making, not a quota
to hit.** An account with a stable winner and no fatigue does not need its tier's volume.

### What to measure the ladder against

> "Test, iterate, and closely monitor **how many winners you're producing.** Never stop feeding the
> algorithm new angles to explore — that's how you stay ahead of creative fatigue and continue to
> scale profitably."

⭐ **Hit rate is the number that tells you whether your volume is right.** Volume is the input; how
many winners come out of it is the answer. Doubling production and producing the same number of
winners means the problem was never volume.

### ⚠️ The framework ACA ships for this is not in this folder

> "The **'Assess Your Current Creative Needs' framework** in your resources can help you calculate
> **how many new campaigns or ads you'll need to hit a specific revenue goal.** Just adapt it to your
> metrics and historical success rate."

⛔ **It is named in both recordings, it is on ACA's download list, and it is not held.** It also runs
the calculation in the opposite direction to the ladder: *revenue goal → volume*, using the account's
own historical hit rate. Nothing here can do that, because nothing in the recordings supplies a hit
rate. Say so rather than approximating it.
