# Naming conventions and the tracker

## Bridging creative and media

> "The relationship between creative strategists and media buyers is **crucial for campaign
> success.** Even if you're not the one setting up the campaigns, you need to understand how the ad
> account is structured for **three key reasons.**"

| # | Reason |
|---|---|
| 1 | **To plan creative production efficiently** |
| 2 | **To schedule regular performance check-ins** |
| 3 | **To gather data for your next round of iterations** |

> "This collaboration creates **the feedback loop** that drives continuous improvement."

⭐ **All three are forward-looking.** None is about reporting on what happened; each is about what
gets made next. That is the test for whether a handover is working.

## Naming conventions

> "Let's start with something that **seems simple but is incredibly powerful**: naming conventions."

> "This is **a combined effort between creative strategists and media buyers — there's simply no way
> around it.** Both teams need to collaborate on naming conventions to maintain an effective feedback
> loop."

### ⭐ The four questions naming exists to answer

The delivered recording gives the argument the written script leaves out, and it is the part worth
quoting to anyone who thinks this is admin:

> "Your naming structure should track key elements so you can **create comparative analysis reports**
> that will answer super important questions like:"

| Question |
|---|
| **Are image ads working better than video ads?** |
| **Is UGC working better than studio production?** |
| **Are ads with the VO better than ads with just music?** |
| **Are funny hooks working better than hooks that show the problem first?** |

> "**You can't answer any of these questions if your creatives are not named or tagged
> effectively.**"

⛔ **Design the convention backwards from the questions.** Each of those four requires a specific
field to exist: format, producer, VO type, hook category. A convention built without asking what you
want to compare will be missing exactly the field you need.

### The eleven elements

> "Your naming structure should track these key elements to enable meaningful analysis."

| # | Element | What it records |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Producer** | Who made the ad? Agency, in-house, or influencer? |
| 2 | **Date** | When was it created? |
| 3 | **File Name** | Unique identifier |
| 4 | **Concept Stage** | Net new, or an iteration? |
| 5 | **Format** | Video, image, carousel, GIF |
| 6 | **Ad Type** | Listicle, before/after, skit |
| 7 | **Concept ID** | Which concept does this execute? |
| 8 | **Hook Identifier** | A, B, C |
| 9 | **Hook Category** | Problem-focused, benefit-focused |
| 10 | **Talent Name** | Who appears in the ad? |
| 11 | **VO Type** | No VO, text-to-speech, human VO |

⚠️ **The delivered recording ends this list with *"just to name a few important ones"***, which the
script does not. Treat eleven as ACA's starting set rather than a closed specification.

⛔ **ACA's own generator sheet implements only five of these eleven.** Date, File Name, Concept
Stage, Format, Hook Category and VO Type have no column in it. That is not a small gap: **four of
those six are exactly what the four comparative questions need.** See `reference/provenance.md`, and
`aca-name-creative-assets` for the sheet with the gap flagged.

### The generator

> "In your resources section, I've included **a handy ad naming generator sheet** that you can use and
> share with your media buying team. **On the first tab, you'll build out all the parameters you want
> to track. The second tab includes dropdowns that automatically generate URL parameters** when you
> make selections."

⭐ **`aca-name-creative-assets` emits that sheet**, all three tabs, with its defects flagged.

### ⚠️ The alternative she names

> "The beauty of tools like **Motion** is that you can **tag assets directly** if these naming
> conventions weren't implemented properly. But **starting with a solid naming structure makes
> analysis far more efficient.**"

The delivered version is more permissive: *"if you're using a tool like Motion, it makes life so much
easier because **you don't have to actually be using these naming conventions**, you can tag the
assets directly."*

⭐ **This matters for real teams.** A naming convention only works if every person naming a file
follows it every time, and most teams do not. Tagging after the fact is worse in principle and better
in practice than a convention that is half-followed, because a half-followed convention produces
comparisons that are silently wrong rather than obviously missing.

## The Creative Testing Tracker

> "Beyond naming conventions, you need **a system to document test results and learnings over time.**"

**What it does, in her list:**

| Function |
|---|
| Document what was tested |
| Record your hypothesis and goals for each test |
| Track spend and key performance metrics |
| Link to creative assets and detailed reports |
| Summarise learnings and next steps |

### ⛔ What it is not

> "**This isn't meant to replace your reporting tools** — it's a strategic document that builds
> **institutional knowledge** about what works and why."

The delivered recording is blunter: *"**don't go crazy adding a million metrics in there**, stick to
Motion for that."*

⭐ **That instruction is the one that keeps the tracker alive.** A tracker that duplicates the
dashboard becomes a second thing to maintain and is abandoned within a quarter. The columns that
justify its existence are the ones no dashboard holds: the hypothesis, and what you learned.

### Why it pays

> "**When new team members join** or you're developing new concepts, **this historical record becomes
> invaluable.**"

⚠️ **The delivered version adds a case the script does not:** *"or if you're working with a new
agency."* Agency turnover is where undocumented learning disappears fastest, and it is a stronger
argument than onboarding.

⭐ **`aca-track-creative-tests` fills ACA's actual sheet**, and flags the three fields Course 602 says
to capture that the sheet has no column for.

⚠️ **Course 602 calls the same artifact by a different name** — *"the Creative Test Tracker I use with
most of my clients"* — and Mirella calls it the *"Creative Testing Tracker & Pipeline template"* and
the *"creative pipeline document"*. One sheet, three names, two courses.
