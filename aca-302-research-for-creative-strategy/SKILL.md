# Course 302: Research for Creative Strategy

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake, ACA's founder. 1h 00m 52s, 4 modules.

> "Great creative strategy is not built on guesswork. It's built on insights. The more you know
> about your own ad accounts, audience, competitors, and current platform trends, the stronger your
> creative output will be."

Four research passes, in order. Each one feeds the next.

| # | Pass | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Your own account** | What is already working here, and why |
| 2 | **Your audience** | What they actually say, want, and fear |
| 3 | **Your competitors** | What the landscape looks like and where the gap is |
| 4 | **The platform** | What language this platform is currently speaking |

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what hook rate is or how to build a persona and get
an answer.

**To run any of the four passes on a real brand, required:**

1. **Which pass you want.** They are separate jobs with separate inputs.
2. **The brand**, and what it sells.

**Then, per pass:**

| Pass | Needs |
|---|---|
| Account audit | ⛔ **Exported performance data.** There is no way around this. See below |
| Audience research | The category, and access to reviews, forums or comments to mine |
| Competitor landscape | A named competitor set, or enough about the brand to build one |
| Trends | The platform, and the brand's tolerance for looking native versus branded |

⛔ **The account audit cannot be run without the numbers, and this is the most common way this
course gets misused.** Mirella is explicit that the AI prompts *"aren't replacements for accessing
your ad data — you'll still need to pull those reports."* If someone asks for an audit without
supplying data, say what to export rather than producing a plausible-looking audit of an account
nobody has looked at.

## The order matters, and the course says so

> "Now that we have analyzed your own performance and we've gotten inside your customers' mind,
> let's see what you're up against."

**Account first, then audience, then competitors, then trends.** The account audit tells you what
already works, which is the only benchmark the other three passes get measured against. Running
competitor research first produces a list of things to copy with nothing to compare them to.

⚠️ **The one exception is a brand with no account history.** Then pass 1 has no data and passes 2
and 3 carry the weight. Say so rather than inventing a baseline.

## How to run it

⛔ **The order is the teaching, and the course says so** — see the section below. Four passes, and doing them
out of order produces research that cannot be briefed from.

1. **Audit their own account first**, using **Pass 1: Audit the account** (below). What already worked is the
   cheapest insight available and it constrains everything after.
2. **Mine the audience** with **Pass 2: Audience research and personas** (below), building personas from customer language
   rather than demographics.
3. **Map the competitors** using **Pass 3: The competitor landscape** (below). ⚠️ Landscape, not teardown — the
   tactical version is Course 301.
4. **Read the trends** through **Pass 4: Trends research** (below), as Moments, Signals and Forces.
5. ⭐ **Produce the artifacts through the four executables** rather than here: `aca-audit-ad-account`,
   `aca-build-audience-personas`, `aca-analyze-competitor-creative`, `aca-research-trends`. ⚠️ Name one only
   if you can see it installed.
6. **Check each pass produced something briefable** — a winning element, a persona quote, a gap, a template.
   Four documents with no brief in them means the passes were done as homework.

## Answering well

**Break performance down by element, not by ad.** *"Analyze your creative performance by specific
elements rather than just looking at top-line results."* "This ad did well" is not a finding. "Ads
opening on the problem scenario beat ads opening on the product" is.

**Distinguish the enduring from the temporary.** The reason the audit looks at 7 days, 30 days and 6
months is to separate *"enduring creative principles versus temporary trends"*. A conclusion drawn
from one window is a guess.

**Never treat a competitor's long-running ad as proven.** *"Just because an ad is running doesn't
mean it's working."* This is the course's own myth-bust and it is the most commonly ignored line in
it.

**Use the audience's words, not yours.** *"We're not inventing messaging — we're just translating
our audience's exact pain points into solution-focused hooks."* If a persona's language section is
written in marketing English, the copy mining did not happen.

⚠️ **Trends are for the structure, not the content.** *"You're looking for patterns you can adapt,
not specific content to copy."* And the goal is explicitly not to chase them: *"we want to create
evergreen ads that can scale."*

## ⚠️ Scope

**This is not a paid-media audit.** Mirella says it directly in the delivered session: *"these are
not ad account audits from the paid media perspective."* It looks at creative. It does not cover
bidding, budget structure, campaign architecture or account hygiene, and an answer that drifts into
those is out of scope.

It also stops before the creative gets made. Concepts, scripts, storyboards and production are later
courses.

### The Quick Performance Analysis, seven steps

#### 1. Find the unicorn ad

> "One of the most valuable insights from any performance analysis is discovering your 'unicorn ad'
> — that rare creative that consistently outperforms everything else. Almost every successful ad
> account has one."

Three markers:

- Takes up a **disproportionate amount of spend**
- **Maintains performance** over extended periods
- **Consistently delivers** results

> "It doesn't matter how many times you launch it into new campaigns, new audiences, Meta always
> seems to send budget to that creative."

**Find it with historical data**, not last week's report: *"which creative has maintained the
highest spend with strong performance over the longest time period. This isn't just your top
performer from last week — it's the creative that's shown staying power."*

**Then dissect it** with the building-blocks framework:

- Which **structure** does it use to capture attention? What is the hook, the body, the CTA, and
  **when is the product introduced**?
- What specific **pain points or desires** does it address?
- Which **persona or creator** delivers the message?
- What **format and production style**?
- Which **demonstration or proof** elements?
- How does it structure the **call to action**?

> "This analysis reveals your creative 'success formula' — the specific combination of elements that
> resonates most deeply with your audience. This is a gold mine for creative iteration."

⭐ **And then the reversal, which is the most important thing in this module and appears only in the
delivered session, not the written script:**

> "We don't really want that. You don't want to have that single creative that consistently
> outperforms everything else in the ad account. Our goal is to strive for some balance."

**The unicorn is a concentration risk as much as an asset.** An account whose performance rests on
one creative is one fatigue cycle away from a cliff. So the unicorn is two things at once: the best
available description of what works here, and a warning about how narrow the account's base is.

⚠️ **Treat "we have a unicorn" as a finding to act on in both directions**: mine it for the formula,
*and* ask why nothing else has come close. The step-7 question *"are new creatives approaching
unicorn status, or is there still a significant performance gap?"* is where that gets answered.

#### 2. Recent performance snapshot, last 30 days

- Which **formats** drive the best results? Video, image, carousel
- Which specific creatives have the highest **conversion rate or ROAS**
- What **messaging angles** appear in the top performers
- Which **creative styles** are represented? UGC, lifestyle, product-focused

> "This gives you a clear picture of what's working right now. It provides immediate direction for
> your next batch of creative without reinventing the wheel."

#### 3. Short-term stability, last 7 days against the 30

- Are the 30-day winners still performing in the last 7?
- Have any new creatives risen to the top?
- Is there rapid performance decay in previously strong ads?

> "If your 30-day winners aren't in your 7-day top performers, you may have scalability issues or
> accelerated creative fatigue."

⭐ **This comparison is the cheapest diagnostic in the course.** Two reports, one subtraction, and it
distinguishes a creative problem from a scaling problem.

#### 4. Hook rate

> **Hook rate = (3-second video views ÷ impressions) × 100%**

- Which visuals and **opening frames** generate the highest hook rates?
- Is there a pattern in the **messaging approaches** that stop the scroll?
- Do certain elements recur? **Text overlays, talent, personas being spoken to, settings** (indoor
  versus outdoor)

> "Without a strong hook, your message never gets a chance to convert."

#### 5. Hold rate

> **Hold rate = (ThruPlays ÷ impressions) × 100%**

- Which **storytelling approaches** maintain attention longest?
- Do certain **pacing, editing styles or demonstration methods** correlate with higher hold rates?
  Does this audience prefer fast pacing, demos, or storytelling?
- Is there a relationship between hold rate and **conversion rate** in this account? *"Usually that
  tends to be the case."*

> "Hold rate demonstrates engagement quality better than average watch time, as it shows the
> percentage of impressions that resulted in significant viewing."

⭐ **The mechanism, given in the delivered session:** *"Meta and TikTok tend to reward creatives that
can retain users inside the app longer. So if you can have high average watch times, usually you get
better placements and costs."* That is why hold rate is worth optimising for its own sake and not
only as a conversion proxy.

#### 6. Medium-term trends, 6 months

- How have the winning approaches **changed**?
- Which **evergreen** concepts have maintained performance?
- Have formats or styles **grown or declined**?
- Are there **seasonal** patterns? Summer versus winter, start of year, holiday season

> "This historical view helps you identify enduring creative principles versus temporary trends."

#### 7. Conclusions and quick wins

Four questions:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Immediate iteration opportunities** | Which top performers should you vary right away? |
| **Creative gaps** | Which formats or angles are underrepresented but showing promise? |
| **Red flags** | What is consistently underperforming and should be avoided? |
| **Low-hanging fruit** | What simple changes would improve the mix quickly? |

Then compare everything against the unicorn: does it share characteristics with recent top
performers, or is it an outlier? Is anything approaching it?

### The In-Depth Creative Audit

#### Set the objectives first

- What are the brand's overall goals for digital marketing and paid social?
- Which metrics matter most to the brand?
- **What should be your North Star metric for creative success?**
- Do the current campaign objectives and optimization settings align with those goals?

⭐ **The concrete version, from the delivered session:** *"There is no point in looking at ROAS or CPA
if there is another metric like contribution margin that you should be paying attention to."*

**This alignment check comes first for a reason:** an audit that measures creative against the wrong
metric produces confident, wrong conclusions, and every step after it inherits them.

#### Break performance down by element

**1. Format performance**

- Video versus image versus carousel
- **Video by length range.** ⚠️ The written script gives *0-15s, 15-30s, 30-60s, 60s+*; the delivered
  session says *10-15s* for the first band. Use the script's 0-15s, which has no gap in it
- Images: lifestyle versus product-focused versus graphic-heavy
- Carousels: by card count and structure. *"Does your audience prefer those long carousels where
  they can browse a lot of products, or is a three-slide structure usually the best?"*

**2. Creative elements**

- Text on screen versus none
- Voice-over versus creator talking to camera
- **Branded styling versus native platform aesthetic**
- Music versus no music, and music style
- Opening shot type: person, product, or problem scenario

⚠️ **Mirella states a strong claim here in the delivered session:** *"A lot of brands tend to insist
on following their brand guidelines and fonts and colours, when really the data shows that the
creatives that follow native platform aesthetic, meaning they don't look like ads and don't look
branded, tend to perform better."* **The data is not named.** Carry it as her position and settle it
from the account's own numbers, which is exactly what this comparison is for.

**3. Content structure**

- Problem-first versus solution-first
- Feature-focused versus benefit-focused
- Emotional versus rational
- Before/after demonstrations versus standalone product showcases
- Direct testimonials versus implied social proof. *"One person telling their own story versus
  creatives that show a lot of different people"*

#### Audience response patterns

**Demographic:** how do age groups differ? Gender-based patterns? Geographic preferences?

> "super-native content that mimics real style or TikTok style ads will resonate and convert better
> with a younger audience. Versus slower, longer videos, more information-heavy might resonate more
> with an older"

**Funnel stage:** which approaches work for cold audiences, which for warm, and are there
significant differences between prospecting and retargeting?

#### Cost-effectiveness

**Production investment versus return.** Compare high-production creative against simpler, lower-cost
approaches. Compare creator and influencer content against studio content.

> "Is investing in studio production actually worth it? Or is it better to collaborate with a lot of
> influencers and UGC creators to source lower production value content, because that's what
> performs best?"

**Creative lifespan.** Calculate the average effective lifespan by creative type. Identify what
correlates with longer-lasting performance. Determine optimal refresh rates by format and audience.

> "How long does it take to actually start hitting that creative fatigue? How long do the creatives
> averagely last on your ad account? Do static images last longer than video ads? That usually tends
> to be the case."

⭐ **Lifespan is the number most accounts never calculate and the one that makes production planning
possible.** Refresh cadence is guesswork until you know how long a format actually lasts here.

#### Turn insights into action

**1. Creative strategy refinement**
- Which **3 to 5 creative principles** should guide the approach?
- What is the optimal creative mix by format and style?
- Which elements should be standardised across creative? Hooks, CTAs

**2. Creative testing plan**
- Identify **3 to 5 specific elements** to test next cycle
- Prioritise by potential impact and resource requirement
- **Develop a clear hypothesis for each**, based on audit findings

**3. Creative optimization roadmap**
- For each top performer, **2 to 3 iteration opportunities**
- A refresh schedule based on observed fatigue patterns
- Specific improvement targets for hook rate, hold rate and conversion rate

> "The purpose of this research isn't just to gather information — it's to develop a clear creative
> direction based on evidence rather than assumptions."

---

### Key takeaways, as ACA gives them

- Start with a quick performance analysis to identify immediate patterns and opportunities
- **Hook rate and hold rate are critical diagnostic metrics** that reveal attention quality
- Look for patterns across time periods to identify stable principles versus temporary trends
- **Break down performance by specific creative elements** rather than just overall results
- Translate findings into concrete strategy, testing plans and optimization roadmaps

⚠️ **The course names no thresholds for any of these metrics.** There is no "a good hook rate is X".
Step 3 of the roadmap asks you to set your own targets from your own baseline, which is the honest
answer, and it means **any threshold quoted here would be invented.**

⚠️ **ACA's numbers live in Course 402 instead.** `aca-402-key-metrics` gives thumbstop and CTR bands
with the advertiser attached. Offer those as that faculty member's figures for that one brand if a
reference point is genuinely needed; never as this course's, and never above the account's own
baseline.

## Pass 2: Audience research and personas

Module 2 (12m 29s).

> "Understanding your audience goes way beyond basic demographics like age and location. It's about
> uncovering what really drives people — their emotional triggers, pain points, and those specific
> moments that make someone think, 'I need this product right now.'"

> "When we nail this part, our ads don't feel like ads anymore. They feel like someone read your
> mind and created the perfect solution just for you."

### The premise: stop guessing, start mining

Mirella calls the goal *"developing your customer empathy muscle"* — the ability to think, feel and
talk like your customers.

> "Most marketers make the mistake of guessing what their audience cares about. But here's the
> secret — you don't have to guess! There's a goldmine of information already out there just waiting
> to be discovered. We call this process **'Copy Mining'** — literally digging up the exact words,
> phrases, and emotions your ideal customers are already using."

**Zero-party data**, her term for what you are collecting: *"direct insights straight from your
audience's mouths — their actual words, feelings, and complaints."*

---

### Where to mine

#### Customer surveys and reviews

> "There's nothing more valuable than direct feedback from people who've already bought from you or
> your competitors."

- **Ask open questions.** *"What made you finally decide to buy?"* · *"What's your biggest
  frustration with products like this?"* Nothing answerable with yes or no
- ⭐ **"Don't just look at 5-star reviews! The 3-star ones are where you'll find the most honest
  feedback about what's working and what's not."**
- **Pay special attention to emotional words** — *"frustrated", "finally", "game-changer",
  "disappointed"*. These are the clues about what actually matters

> "I once found a review where someone said they were 'literally in tears' when our product solved
> their problem. That exact phrasing became the foundation for an entire campaign that crushed it."

#### Amazon

> "Even if you don't sell on Amazon, it's a treasure trove of research."

- Search your product category, look at the **top 3 to 5 bestsellers**
- **Filter by 2-3 stars for pain points, 4-5 stars for the benefits that matter**
- Build a swipe file of phrases like *"I love how it..."* and *"I wish it would..."*

#### GigaBrain and Reddit

> "This tool searches across Reddit and other forums where people talk candidly about their
> problems. **Unlike reviews, these conversations happen before someone's bought anything, so you see
> raw pain points.**"

- Search your category, competitor names, or common problems
- Look for threads with lots of comments and emotional language
- Save comments where people vent frustrations or describe ideal solutions

> "Find the subreddits where your audience hangs out and just observe. **Don't post or sell — just
> listen.** You'll learn how they talk, what they value, and what solutions they've already tried."

⭐ **The pre-purchase versus post-purchase distinction is the sharpest idea in this module.** Reviews
tell you what people say once they own the thing and have to justify the decision. Forums tell you
what they said while the problem was still unsolved, which is the state your ad has to meet them in.

#### Ad comments

> "Sometimes the best research is hiding in plain sight — right in the comments section of your ads
> or your competitors' ads."

- Look at your **highest and lowest** performing ads. What are people saying?
- Check competitors' ads in the Facebook Ad Library and scan their comments
- Notice **objections, questions, and enthusiastic responses**

#### Pinterest, Answer the Public, search trends

*"For certain niches, especially lifestyle, home and DIY products."*

- **Pinterest:** search the topic, look at the most saved pins and their descriptions
- **Answer the Public:** shows exactly what questions people are asking
- **Google Trends:** seasonal interest and related search terms that reveal angles you had not
  considered

#### Social platform search

- Search relevant hashtags and keywords on TikTok and Instagram
- Watch top-performing content, note **style, pacing, language**
- Read comments: what are people asking for more information about?

---

### The four questions to hold the whole time

| | Question | The point |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Who are they really?** | Psychographics, not demographics. What do they value? What communities do they belong to? |
| **2** | **What do they genuinely want?** | Not the surface problem, the deeper desire. *"They don't just want a vacuum cleaner; they want a clean home without back pain or wasted time"* |
| **3** | **What triggered them to search or buy?** | A move, a recurring frustration, a comparison with friends. *"These trigger moments are when people are most receptive to solutions"* |
| **4** | **How do they naturally speak?** | Technical or casual? *"The closer your ad language matches their natural speech, the more they'll trust you"* |

---

### Turning it into personas

> "Start by grouping similar patterns in your research. You might notice clusters forming around
> specific desires, frustrations, or life situations."

Each persona carries five things:

1. **A memorable name** — *"Fitness Enthusiast Ethan"*, *"Busy Mom Brenda"*
2. **Primary emotional drivers** — security, belonging, achievement
3. **Key pain points and objections**
4. **The specific language they use**
5. **Their trigger moments for purchasing**

> "These personas aren't just theoretical exercises — they become your creative North Star. When
> you're writing a script or designing creative, you should be able to ask, 'Would Busy Mom Brenda
> connect with this message?' and know the answer instantly."

⭐ **That test is also the quality check on the persona itself.** If the answer is not instant, the
persona is too vague to be worth having. A persona that cannot decide anything is a description, not
a tool.

#### The worked exercise

A meal delivery service, persona **"Overwhelmed Working Parent Olivia"**.

**Step 1, pull exact phrases** from reviews and forums:

> - "I feel so guilty ordering takeout again, but who has time to cook after a full day?"
> - "I want my family to eat healthy, but meal planning is just one more thing on my endless to-do
>   list."
> - "The 5pm 'what's for dinner' panic is the worst part of my day."

**Step 2, convert each into a hook:**

> - "End the 5pm dinner panic. Healthy meals delivered with zero prep time."
> - "Stop feeling guilty about takeout. Start feeling proud of what you're feeding your family."
> - "What if meal planning never appeared on your to-do list again?"

> "See how we're not inventing messaging — we're just translating our audience's exact pain points
> into solution-focused hooks? That's the magic of audience research done right."

⭐ **Read the three pairs closely, because the transformation is consistent and copyable:** each hook
keeps the customer's own noun phrase (`the 5pm panic`, `guilty about takeout`, `to-do list`)
and changes only the tense or the outcome. Nothing new is introduced. **If a hook contains a phrase
that appears nowhere in the research, it was invented rather than mined.**

### ⚠️ The AI caution, in her own words

Mirella is unusually firm about this, and it is the opposite of how these tools are usually sold:

> "I want to be super clear about something — **mastering the manual process first is absolutely
> essential.** I've seen too many marketers jump straight to AI without understanding what they're
> actually looking for, and the results are... well, let's just say not great."

> "But once you've done a few deep dives yourself? That's when these tools become incredibly
> powerful."

> "**AI is your research partner, not a replacement for developing genuine customer empathy.** Use
> these tools to scale what you've learned, not to skip learning the fundamental skills."

> "The best creative strategists I know use AI to handle the heavy lifting so they can focus on the
> insights and creative direction that machines simply can't provide."

**What the AI prompts do**, per her description: extract patterns from dozens of reviews at once,
analyse Reddit threads and social comments for authentic language, and map the question journey a
customer goes through, *"perfect for identifying those trigger moments when someone moves from
casual browsing to actively looking for a solution."*

⛔ **The prompts summarise material you supply. They do not find it.** That distinction is the whole
of her caution: a model asked to invent what an audience says will do it fluently, and the output
will be marketing English rather than customer English, which is exactly the failure copy mining
exists to prevent.

### Key points, as ACA gives them

- **Copy mining:** gather authentic language from where your audience already shares its thoughts
- **Zero-party data:** direct insights reveal motivations behaviour alone cannot
- **Emotional drivers:** rarely features or price
- **Persona development:** group findings into personas with distinct pain points, language and
  trigger moments
- **Use their words:** *"The most powerful copy comes directly from your customers' mouths, not your
  marketing team's brainstorms"*

## Pass 3: The competitor landscape

Module 3 (14m 54s).

> "Paid social advertising is a competitive sport. Who else is fighting for your audience's
> attention and wallet? How are they positioning themselves? What can we learn from them?"

> "But here's the thing — and I want to be super clear about this — **we're not looking at
> competitors to copy them. We're investigating to get inspired and find gaps in the market that we
> can own.** Big difference."

### ⛔ The myth this module exists to kill

> "Let's bust a myth right away: **just because an ad is running doesn't mean it's working.** I can't
> tell you how many times I've seen brands dump money into campaigns that are absolute duds simply
> because they had the budget to keep them going."

> "Those 'top ads' you see featured in showcases? Sometimes they're just the ones with the biggest
> spend behind them, not necessarily the best performers."

And from the delivered session, the reason you can never be sure:

> "You never know what their conversion objective is or what their CPA goal is."

⭐ **This is the most important caution in the whole course and it is routinely ignored.** A
competitor ad's longevity is evidence of budget and attention, not of profitability. You cannot see
their target, their margin, or whether anyone is watching the account. **Long-running is a reason to
look harder, never a reason to copy.**

⚠️ **ACA's own Course 301 leans the other way**, treating run length as *"that's your winner, that's
the pattern to study."* Both are defensible: 301 is reading run length as the best signal available
from outside, and 302 is warning that the signal is weak. Where both skills are installed, **carry
the tension rather than picking a side**: run length is the best outside signal *and* it is not
proof.

### What you are actually looking for

> "What we want is strategic insight. When you look at what others are doing, you start to notice
> patterns:
> - Which angles keep showing up across multiple brands?
> - What hooks seem to be working in your space?
> - Are there messaging approaches everyone's using — or maybe an obvious angle everyone's missing?"

> "From there, you can make smarter decisions about whether to differentiate with something
> completely fresh or put your unique spin on a proven approach."

**And beyond the direct set:**

> "Some of my biggest creative breakthroughs came from studying brands in completely different
> industries that happened to target similar audiences. A skincare brand can learn a ton from how a
> fitness app hooks their audience, because at the end of the day, they might be speaking to the
> exact same person."

### The tools

#### Meta Ad Library

*"It's free, it's comprehensive, and it's incredibly revealing."*

- Search competitors by name, or by keywords for the category
- **How many active ads** each brand is running — hints at budget and testing velocity
- **How long ads have been active** — *"something running for months might be a winner"*
- Which **formats** they use
- ⭐ **How their hooks evolve.** *"If you see a competitor using the same hook structure but with
  different visuals or products, they've probably found a winner they're scaling."*

That last one is the strongest read available, because it is a *behavioural* signal: repetition with
variation is what a brand does when it has something it does not want to lose.

#### TikTok Creative Center

> "Similar idea, but with an important caveat — their 'Top Ads' section can be misleading. These are
> often sorted by engagement, not by actual business results. **Great for seeing what stops the
> scroll, not always great for seeing what drives conversion.**"

- Filter to your industry and objective
- Look at **hook styles** — text reveals, shocking statements, before-and-afters
- Look at **talent** — founders, everyday users, professional creators

> "One pro tip: when an ad has tons of likes but few substantive comments, be skeptical. But when you
> see comments like 'Just ordered!' or 'This solved my exact problem!' — that's a much stronger
> signal."

#### Google Ads Transparency Center

*"Especially if your product is more consideration-based or has a strong educational component."*

- Start with searches on category keywords, study headlines and copy
- The Transparency Center shows **any brand's entire YouTube ad history** — what is running now, when
  it started, in which regions

> "YouTube ads often use completely different creative approaches than social. They tend to be
> longer, more story-driven, and sometimes more educational."

Watch: how they structure the **first 5 seconds** (before the skip button), whether they use
testimonials, demos or storytelling, and how they handle the CTA.

⭐ **And the inference worth stealing:** *"If a competitor is investing heavily in YouTube, they're
usually playing a longer game with higher customer values. This can reveal a lot about their
business model and where they see the highest ROI."*

#### Ad intelligence platforms

**aduniverse.ai** for spend levels: *"If a competitor is dumping serious cash into one specific
campaign, it might be a winner — or they might be a VC-funded startup with more money than sense.
Either way, it's worth investigating."*

**Foreplay or Motion** to organise, tag and track competitor ads over time, which is how testing
strategy becomes visible:

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Small tweaks of the same core creative | Optimization |
| Constantly different approaches | Exploration |
| Certain angles returning in new formats | Scalable winners |

> "When I'm analyzing competitors, I tag everything by creative approach: UGC testimonial, founder
> story, product demo, before/after transformation, etc. This makes patterns jump out immediately.""

### The Competitor Analysis Framework

Five dimensions, one pass per competitor.

**1. Creative volume and budget indicators**
- How many unique creatives are running right now?
- How frequently do they test new concepts?
- One platform or several?

> "A brand running 50+ variations is probably doing systematic testing and has significant budget."

**2. Format strategy**
- Ratio of video to static to carousel
- For video, which lengths
- UGC, studio-produced, or a mix
- How they adapt to platform-specific trends, including placement-specific cuts for Reels and Stories

**3. Talent approach**
- Who delivers the message? Founders, paid creators, customers?
- What demographic characteristics does the on-screen talent have?
- Consistent talent, or testing presenter types?
- *"Really try to pay attention to what personas they seem to be speaking to"*

**4. Creative execution**
- Which hook styles appear most
- Editing styles and pacing
- Visual aesthetic
- Text overlays and captions
- *"What messaging themes or value propositions you see repeated over and over again?"*

> "This is where you'll find their creative 'formula'."

**5. Temporal patterns**
- How have their creatives evolved?
- Sticking with what works, or constantly innovating?
- *"Do you notice that during holiday season, Black Friday, Q1, they completely roll out a new set of
  creatives with new messaging?"*
- Have they recently pivoted?

> "They'll usually double down on winners and move away from what's not performing."

**Document it** in a spreadsheet or Notion doc per competitor, and revisit **at least quarterly**.

### From research to strategy

The five moves. Only the first two are about differentiation; the rest are about borrowing well.

**1. Identify white space.** *"Look for angles, hooks, or claims that nobody in your space is making.
Sometimes the most powerful position is the one everyone else has overlooked."*

**2. Differentiate your positioning.** *"If all your competitors are pushing 'affordable', maybe you
emphasize 'premium quality'. If they're all about 'quick results', perhaps your angle is
'sustainable, long-term success'."*

**3. Elevate proven approaches.** *"When you see an angle that's clearly working, don't just copy it
— elevate it."*

⭐ **The delivered session gives the four concrete moves the script leaves abstract:** *"We make it
more authentic, we add stronger social proof, we connect it more with a unique value proposition, we
agitate the problem more."* That is what "elevate" means operationally.

**4. Import cross-category inspiration.** *"A B2B SaaS company might learn from how DTC fashion
brands create urgency."*

**5. Track creative evolution.** *"If everyone suddenly shifts to a new hook style or claim, there's
probably a reason — either the market is changing or they've all discovered something that works."*

> "The goal isn't to become a carbon copy of your competitors. It's to understand the landscape so
> you can carve out your own distinctive place within it — or disrupt it entirely."

### Cadence

> "Competitor research should be an ongoing part of your creative process, not a one-time exercise.
> **Set a calendar reminder to spend an hour every month** refreshing your competitive analysis.
> Markets shift quickly, and yesterday's winning approach might be today's overused cliché."

⚠️ **An hour a month is the whole ask**, which is worth quoting to anyone who has decided competitor
research is a quarterly project they never start.

## Pass 4: Trends research

Module 4 (12m 38s).

### ⛔ The goal is not to chase trends

> "the research puzzle that many brands completely miss trends research.The goal here isn't to chase
> every trend we want to create everygreen ads that can scale. The goal is to understand the
> underlying patterns of what's working on each platform so your ads feel native and relevant **you
> want to blend in AND stand out.**"

That sentence is the whole module. Everything below serves it, and an answer that treats this as
"jump on trends faster" has inverted it.

---

### The three levels: Moments, Signals, Forces

A framework Mirella credits to **TikTok**, describing how trends become *"the language of the
platform"*.

| Level | Duration | What it is | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moments** | 1 to 4 weeks | Viral sounds, specific transitions, challenge formats | Act within **48 to 72 hours** or not at all. ⚠️ *"I don't advise to invest too heavily in these when thinking about ads.... These are honestly better for your organic strategy"* |
| **Signals** | 1 to 6 months | *"Patterns that stick around long enough to become recognizable formulas"* | Deserve real attention. Can carry **campaign themes running 1 to 3 months** |
| **Forces** | 6+ months | *"The deep cultural currents... broader themes and values that shape what resonates"* | Should influence overall creative strategy. **Worth significant production investment** |

**They escalate.** Moments evolve into Signals; Signals evolve into Forces.

> "Forces become 'the language of the platform', and creating content powered by these forces is what
> will make your content feel native."

⭐ **The practical value is budget allocation, not taxonomy.** The framework tells you how much to
spend on a thing based on how long it will live. A Moment gets a same-week organic post; a Force gets
a production budget. Most trend mistakes are a level mismatch.

⚠️ **This same Moments / Signals / Forces framework is taught in ACA's TikTok course.** Where that
skill is installed alongside this one, the frameworks agree and either can carry it.

**Formats named as usable references:** ASMR unboxing, "what I ordered vs. what I got", "we listen
and we don't judge", stitching videos, specific audios.

---

### The three-step framework

#### Step 1: Find a trend — IDENTIFYING

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Doom scroll** | *"Yes, I'm actually recommending strategic scrolling."* 20 to 30 minutes a few times a week on the For You page. **Save anything that catches your attention immediately** |
| **Search hashtags** | Not just the product category. **Lifestyle hashtags, emotion-based terms, problem-focused phrases** your audience would use |
| **Use Creative Center** | TikTok and Meta both offer free tools showing trending content, sounds and formats. Filter by industry and country |
| **Engage community** | Ask your audience what they are enjoying, or check comments for trend mentions |

#### Step 2: Establish brand and product relevance — ISOLATING

> "Not every trend will be right for your brand."

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Behavioural** | Does it align with behaviours related to your product? *"Morning routine videos would naturally fit a coffee brand"* |
| **Cultural** | Does it connect to values the brand stands for, like sustainability or authenticity? |
| **Psychological** | What deeper triggers does it tap? Humour, nostalgia, FOMO |
| **Emotional** | What feelings does it evoke, and do they match how you want customers to feel? |

⭐ **This step is the one that gets skipped**, and skipping it is what produces the brand-on-a-trend
content everyone recognises as trying too hard. A trend that passes none of the four is not a
creative opportunity, it is a costume.

#### Step 3: Choose a narrative — ITERATING

- **What is the trend hook?** What makes people stop — a surprising statement, a visual pattern
  interrupt, a specific question format, a sound
- **Choose the narrative structure** — problem/solution, before/after, Q&A
- **Decide on product placement** — *"Some trends work better with early product reveals, others with
  a surprising reveal at the end"*

---

### The trend template

Four components, for breaking a trend down and rebuilding it:

| Component | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The opening statement that catches attention |
| **Deliver** | How the trend unfolds — the beat drop or transition moment, then the key point |
| **Guide** | The close, and what to do next. In an ad adaptation, the CTA |
| **Sound** | The audio approach. *"Contrasting sounds that shift from slow to upbeat, matching the before and after dynamic"* |

ACA's worked example is the **"My wife has two husbands"** trend: the hook is the unexpected claim,
and the structure stays while the content is replaced.

> "The power of this approach is that you're not just copying a trend — you're understanding its
> underlying structure and adapting it to your message."

⭐ **Sound as a named component is unusual and worth keeping.** Most trend breakdowns treat audio as
decoration. From the delivered session: *"Oftentimes if we just change a voiceover from a human
voiceover to that TikTok robot voice, sometimes it'll make a difference in how the creative
performs."*

---

### Seven organic trends that became ad standards

> "None of these were originally created as ad formats — they evolved from what was already working
> organically."

| Format | Origin | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Unboxing** | Genuine consumer excitement opening products | Captures authentic reactions, builds anticipation. Staple for launches and reveals |
| **ASMR product demos** | A niche sensory content category | Close-up, sensory-rich, creates desire. Strong for beauty, food and tactile products. ⭐ *"specifically great for like middle to bottom of the funnel, when your customers are already aware of your brand and product and they just need some final convincing"* |
| **Street interviews** | Man-on-the-street creator content | Feels unscripted and relatable |
| **Answering questions** | Creators responding to comments and FAQs | Powerful for **addressing objections** and showing expertise. Q&A suits educational content |
| **Comedy skits** | Creator comedy | Effective for problem-solution scenarios. *"Humor helps messages stick and encourages sharing."* |
| **Green screen explainers** | Creators sharing information over images and video | *"The visual/verbal combination improves comprehension."* Good for product education and comparison |
| **Podcast-style conversations** | Actual podcast clips | Builds credibility through conversation. Allows deeper dives into benefits |

⭐ **This table is the most immediately usable thing in the module**, because each row is a format
with a known job attached. It doubles as a checklist against an existing creative mix: any row not
represented is a format the account has never tested.

---

### What to analyse in any trend

| | What to look at |
|---|---|
| **Hooks** | What makes people stop in the first 3 seconds? Provocative statement, sound, visual surprise, framing |
| **Visuals** | Camera angles, lighting, locations, transitions. Highly produced or deliberately casual |
| **Timing and pacing** | How long each section runs, where transitions land, when the main point is revealed. *"The rhythm of content is often what makes it feel native"* |
| **Audio strategy** | Sounds, music, voiceover. *"Often the most overlooked element but can dramatically impact performance"* |
| **Text style** | Fonts, animations, placements |

> "You're looking for patterns you can adapt, not specific content to copy. The goal is to understand
> **why** something is resonating."

### The simplified loop

1. **Identify the pattern** — what underlying structure makes this work?
2. **Extract the formula** — hook style, transition approach, revelation method, closing technique
3. **Adapt to your message** — keep the structure, replace the content
4. **Test and iterate** — multiple variations

> "The most successful brands don't view trends as one-off opportunities — they see them as formulas
> to be adapted and refined over time."

---

### The case study

A trending format where creators show what they ordered online versus what arrived:

1. Show the expectation, usually a screenshot
2. Build anticipation with a transition
3. Reveal what actually arrived
4. React, usually disappointment

**Flipped for a beauty brand:** show what they had tried before (a competitor product), same
anticipation build, reveal the client's product, show genuine surprise at how much better it was.

> "This took a negative trend (disappointment) and flipped it to positive (pleasant surprise), while
> maintaining the familiar format that was getting high engagement. The campaign saw a **42% higher
> CTR** than their previous creative."

⚠️ **The 42% is ACA's unsourced claim** about one unnamed client, and CTR is the metric furthest from
revenue. See **Provenance** (below).

⭐ **The move is more transferable than the number: invert the emotional payload, keep the
structure.** A trend built on disappointment has a ready-made reveal beat, and a brand that can
credibly fill that beat with relief gets the format's attention for free.

---

### Why any of this works: the platform changed

From the delivered session, and not in the written script:

> "what social networks were just a few years ago, and what they are now, they have completely
> changed. We used to go on Facebook and Instagram and see life updates. This person got engaged.
> This one is traveling. This one had a baby. It was all about who you followed, whether it was your
> family, friends,"

> "The Reels and TikTok algorithms are completely different. **They're all about discovery. People
> open up Reels because they want to be entertained.**"

**Three principles for ads on Reels and TikTok**, as delivered:

1. **Make it entertaining.** Use music, visuals and storytelling to provoke an emotional response
2. **Make it digestible.** *"Reels and TikTok are fast. You have to keep it short and to the point."*
   Use emojis and text to help convey the message
3. **Make it relatable.** Use real people and content creators, authentic, in a lo-fi tone

> "By mastering these three key principles and the language of the platform, you can really make ads
> then blend in and stand out."

⛔ **ACA's script and ACA's delivery give different triads, and this skill carries the delivered
one.** The written script's on-screen recap says *"deploy the 3 pillars — Educational, Entertaining,
Aspirational"*, and those three are never defined anywhere in the module. The delivered session
defines **entertaining, digestible, relatable**. Only *entertaining* appears in both.

**Use the delivered set**, because it is the one students heard and the only one the course actually
explains. Flagged rather than silently reconciled.

### Key takeaways, as ACA gives them

- **Trend hierarchy:** Moments (1-4 weeks), Signals (1-6 months), Forces (6+ months), to allocate
  resources
- **Practical framework:** find a trend, establish relevance, choose your narrative
- **Template method:** Hook, Deliver, Guide
- **Organic origins:** many of today's ad formats began as organic content trends
- **Strategic adaptation:** replicate the feel and structure, not the exact content
