## ⛔ The goal is not to chase trends

> "the research puzzle that many brands completely miss trends research.The goal here isn't to chase
> every trend we want to create everygreen ads that can scale. The goal is to understand the
> underlying patterns of what's working on each platform so your ads feel native and relevant **you

That sentence is the whole module. Everything below serves it, and an answer that treats this as

## The three levels: Moments, Signals, Forces

A framework Mirella credits to **TikTok**, describing how trends become *"the language of the
platform"*.

| Level | Duration | What it is | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moments** | 1 to 4 weeks | Viral sounds, specific transitions, challenge formats | Act within **48 to 72 hours** or not at all. ⚠️ *"I don't advise to invest too heavily in these when thinking about ads.... These are honestly better for your organic strategy"* |
| **Signals** | 1 to 6 months | *"Patterns that stick around long enough to become recognizable formulas"* | Deserve real attention. Can carry **campaign themes running 1 to 3 months** |
| **Forces** | 6+ months | *"The deep cultural currents... broader themes and values that shape what resonates"* | Should influence overall creative strategy. **Worth significant production investment** |

**They escalate.** Moments evolve into Signals; Signals evolve into Forces.

> "Forces become 'the language of the platform', and creating content powered by these forces is what
> will make your content feel native."

⭐ **The practical value is budget allocation, not taxonomy.** The framework tells you how much to
spend on a thing based on how long it will live. A Moment gets a same-week organic post; a Force gets
a production budget. Most trend mistakes are a level mismatch.

⚠️ **This same Moments / Signals / Forces framework is taught in ACA's TikTok course.** Where that
skill is installed alongside this one, the frameworks agree and either can carry it.

**Formats named as usable references:** ASMR unboxing, "what I ordered vs. what I got", "we listen
and we don't judge", stitching videos, specific audios.

## The three-step framework

### Step 1: Find a trend — IDENTIFYING

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Doom scroll** | *"Yes, I'm actually recommending strategic scrolling."* 20 to 30 minutes a few times a week on the For You page. **Save anything that catches your attention immediately** |
| **Search hashtags** | Not just the product category. **Lifestyle hashtags, emotion-based terms, problem-focused phrases** your audience would use |
| **Use Creative Center** | TikTok and Meta both offer free tools showing trending content, sounds and formats. Filter by industry and country |
| **Engage community** | Ask your audience what they are enjoying, or check comments for trend mentions |

### Step 2: Establish brand and product relevance — ISOLATING

> "Not every trend will be right for your brand."

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Behavioural** | Does it align with behaviours related to your product? *"Morning routine videos would naturally fit a coffee brand"* |
| **Cultural** | Does it connect to values the brand stands for, like sustainability or authenticity? |
| **Psychological** | What deeper triggers does it tap? Humour, nostalgia, FOMO |
| **Emotional** | What feelings does it evoke, and do they match how you want customers to feel? |

⭐ **This step is the one that gets skipped**, and skipping it is what produces the brand-on-a-trend
content everyone recognises as trying too hard. A trend that passes none of the four is not a
creative opportunity, it is a costume.

### Step 3: Choose a narrative — ITERATING

- **What is the trend hook?** What makes people stop — a surprising statement, a visual pattern
  interrupt, a specific question format, a sound
- **Choose the narrative structure** — problem/solution, before/after, Q&A
- **Decide on product placement** — *"Some trends work better with early product reveals, others with
  a surprising reveal at the end"*

## The trend template

Four components, for breaking a trend down and rebuilding it:

| Component | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The opening statement that catches attention |
| **Deliver** | How the trend unfolds — the beat drop or transition moment, then the key point |
| **Guide** | The close, and what to do next. In an ad adaptation, the CTA |
| **Sound** | The audio approach. *"Contrasting sounds that shift from slow to upbeat, matching the before and after dynamic"* |

ACA's worked example is the **"My wife has two husbands"** trend: the hook is the unexpected claim,
and the structure stays while the content is replaced.

> "The power of this approach is that you're not just copying a trend — you're understanding its
> underlying structure and adapting it to your message."

⭐ **Sound as a named component is unusual and worth keeping.** Most trend breakdowns treat audio as
decoration. From the delivered session: *"Oftentimes if we just change a voiceover from a human
voiceover to that TikTok robot voice, sometimes it'll make a difference in how the creative
performs."*

## Seven organic trends that became ad standards

> "None of these were originally created as ad formats — they evolved from what was already working
> organically."

| Format | Origin | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Unboxing** | Genuine consumer excitement opening products | Captures authentic reactions, builds anticipation. Staple for launches and reveals |
| **ASMR product demos** | A niche sensory content category | Close-up, sensory-rich, creates desire. Strong for beauty, food and tactile products. ⭐ *"specifically great for like middle to bottom of the funnel, when your customers are already aware of your brand and product and they just need some final convincing"* |
| **Street interviews** | Man-on-the-street creator content | Feels unscripted and relatable |
| **Answering questions** | Creators responding to comments and FAQs | Powerful for **addressing objections** and showing expertise. Q&A suits educational content |
| **Comedy skits** | Creator comedy | Effective for problem-solution scenarios. *"Humor helps messages stick and encourages sharing."* |
| **Green screen explainers** | Creators sharing information over images and video | *"The visual/verbal combination improves comprehension."* Good for product education and comparison |
| **Podcast-style conversations** | Actual podcast clips | Builds credibility through conversation. Allows deeper dives into benefits |

⭐ **This table is the most immediately usable thing in the module**, because each row is a format
with a known job attached. It doubles as a checklist against an existing creative mix: any row not
represented is a format the account has never tested.

## What to analyse in any trend

| | What to look at |
|---|---|
| **Hooks** | What makes people stop in the first 3 seconds? Provocative statement, sound, visual surprise, framing |
| **Visuals** | Camera angles, lighting, locations, transitions. Highly produced or deliberately casual |
| **Timing and pacing** | How long each section runs, where transitions land, when the main point is revealed. *"The rhythm of content is often what makes it feel native"* |
| **Audio strategy** | Sounds, music, voiceover. *"Often the most overlooked element but can dramatically impact performance"* |
| **Text style** | Fonts, animations, placements |

> "You're looking for patterns you can adapt, not specific content to copy. The goal is to understand
> **why** something is resonating."

## The simplified loop

1. **Identify the pattern** — what underlying structure makes this work?
2. **Extract the formula** — hook style, transition approach, revelation method, closing technique
3. **Adapt to your message** — keep the structure, replace the content
4. **Test and iterate** — multiple variations

> "The most successful brands don't view trends as one-off opportunities — they see them as formulas
> to be adapted and refined over time."

## The case study

A trending format where creators show what they ordered online versus what arrived:

1. Show the expectation, usually a screenshot
2. Build anticipation with a transition
3. Reveal what actually arrived
4. React, usually disappointment

**Flipped for a beauty brand:** show what they had tried before (a competitor product), same
anticipation build, reveal the client's product, show genuine surprise at how much better it was.

> "This took a negative trend (disappointment) and flipped it to positive (pleasant surprise), while
> maintaining the familiar format that was getting high engagement. The campaign saw a **42% higher
> CTR** than their previous creative."

⚠️ **The 42% is ACA's unsourced claim** about one unnamed client, and CTR is the metric furthest from
revenue. See `reference/provenance.md`.

⭐ **The move is more transferable than the number: invert the emotional payload, keep the
structure.** A trend built on disappointment has a ready-made reveal beat, and a brand that can
credibly fill that beat with relief gets the format's attention for free.

## Why any of this works: the platform changed

From the delivered session, and not in the written script:

> "what social networks were just a few years ago, and what they are now, they have completely
> changed. We used to go on Facebook and Instagram and see life updates. This person got engaged.
> This one is traveling. This one had a baby. It was all about who you followed, whether it was your
> family, friends,"

> "The Reels and TikTok algorithms are completely different. **They're all about discovery. People
> open up Reels because they want to be entertained.**"

**Three principles for ads on Reels and TikTok**, as delivered:

1. **Make it entertaining.** Use music, visuals and storytelling to provoke an emotional response
2. **Make it digestible.** *"Reels and TikTok are fast. You have to keep it short and to the point."*
   Use emojis and text to help convey the message
3. **Make it relatable.** Use real people and content creators, authentic, in a lo-fi tone

> "By mastering these three key principles and the language of the platform, you can really make ads
> then blend in and stand out."

⛔ **ACA's script and ACA's delivery give different triads, and this skill carries the delivered
one.** The written script's on-screen recap says *"deploy the 3 pillars — Educational, Entertaining,
Aspirational"*, and those three are never defined anywhere in the module. The delivered session
defines **entertaining, digestible, relatable**. Only *entertaining* appears in both.

**Use the delivered set**, because it is the one students heard and the only one the course actually
explains. Flagged rather than silently reconciled.

## Key takeaways, as ACA gives them

- **Trend hierarchy:** Moments (1-4 weeks), Signals (1-6 months), Forces (6+ months), to allocate
  resources
- **Practical framework:** find a trend, establish relevance, choose your narrative
- **Template method:** Hook, Deliver, Guide
- **Organic origins:** many of today's ad formats began as organic content trends
- **Strategic adaptation:** replicate the feel and structure, not the exact content
