# Pass 1: Audit the account

Module 1 (20m 51s). Two audits, for two different situations.

| | **Quick Performance Analysis** | **In-Depth Creative Audit** |
|---|---|---|
| When | *"A regular health check you should conduct monthly or quarterly"* | *"Ideal when first working with a new account"* |
| Purpose | Immediate patterns and opportunities | The drivers of performance, for long-term strategy |
| Output | Quick wins for the next production cycle | The creative principles, testing plan and roadmap |

⛔ **Scope, stated in the delivered session and easy to miss:** *"these are not ad account audits
from the paid media perspective."* This looks at creative. Not bidding, not budget structure, not
campaign architecture.

---

## The Quick Performance Analysis, seven steps

### 1. Find the unicorn ad

> "One of the most valuable insights from any performance analysis is discovering your 'unicorn ad'
> — that rare creative that consistently outperforms everything else. Almost every successful ad
> account has one."

Three markers:

- Takes up a **disproportionate amount of spend**
- **Maintains performance** over extended periods
- **Consistently delivers** results

> "It doesn't matter how many times you launch it into new campaigns, new audiences, Meta always
> seems to send budget to that creative."

**Find it with historical data**, not last week's report: *"which creative has maintained the
highest spend with strong performance over the longest time period. This isn't just your top
performer from last week — it's the creative that's shown staying power."*

**Then dissect it** with the building-blocks framework:

- Which **structure** does it use to capture attention? What is the hook, the body, the CTA, and
  **when is the product introduced**?
- What specific **pain points or desires** does it address?
- Which **persona or creator** delivers the message?
- What **format and production style**?
- Which **demonstration or proof** elements?
- How does it structure the **call to action**?

> "This analysis reveals your creative 'success formula' — the specific combination of elements that
> resonates most deeply with your audience. This is a gold mine for creative iteration."

⭐ **And then the reversal, which is the most important thing in this module and appears only in the
delivered session, not the written script:**

> "We don't really want that. You don't want to have that single creative that consistently
> outperforms everything else in the ad account. Our goal is to strive for some balance."

**The unicorn is a concentration risk as much as an asset.** An account whose performance rests on
one creative is one fatigue cycle away from a cliff. So the unicorn is two things at once: the best
available description of what works here, and a warning about how narrow the account's base is.

⚠️ **Treat "we have a unicorn" as a finding to act on in both directions**: mine it for the formula,
*and* ask why nothing else has come close. The step-7 question *"are new creatives approaching
unicorn status, or is there still a significant performance gap?"* is where that gets answered.

### 2. Recent performance snapshot, last 30 days

- Which **formats** drive the best results? Video, image, carousel
- Which specific creatives have the highest **conversion rate or ROAS**
- What **messaging angles** appear in the top performers
- Which **creative styles** are represented? UGC, lifestyle, product-focused

> "This gives you a clear picture of what's working right now. It provides immediate direction for
> your next batch of creative without reinventing the wheel."

### 3. Short-term stability, last 7 days against the 30

- Are the 30-day winners still performing in the last 7?
- Have any new creatives risen to the top?
- Is there rapid performance decay in previously strong ads?

> "If your 30-day winners aren't in your 7-day top performers, you may have scalability issues or
> accelerated creative fatigue."

⭐ **This comparison is the cheapest diagnostic in the course.** Two reports, one subtraction, and it
distinguishes a creative problem from a scaling problem.

### 4. Hook rate

> **Hook rate = (3-second video views ÷ impressions) × 100%**

- Which visuals and **opening frames** generate the highest hook rates?
- Is there a pattern in the **messaging approaches** that stop the scroll?
- Do certain elements recur? **Text overlays, talent, personas being spoken to, settings** (indoor
  versus outdoor)

> "Without a strong hook, your message never gets a chance to convert."

### 5. Hold rate

> **Hold rate = (ThruPlays ÷ impressions) × 100%**

- Which **storytelling approaches** maintain attention longest?
- Do certain **pacing, editing styles or demonstration methods** correlate with higher hold rates?
  Does this audience prefer fast pacing, demos, or storytelling?
- Is there a relationship between hold rate and **conversion rate** in this account? *"Usually that
  tends to be the case."*

> "Hold rate demonstrates engagement quality better than average watch time, as it shows the
> percentage of impressions that resulted in significant viewing."

⭐ **The mechanism, given in the delivered session:** *"Meta and TikTok tend to reward creatives that
can retain users inside the app longer. So if you can have high average watch times, usually you get
better placements and costs."* That is why hold rate is worth optimising for its own sake and not
only as a conversion proxy.

### 6. Medium-term trends, 6 months

- How have the winning approaches **changed**?
- Which **evergreen** concepts have maintained performance?
- Have formats or styles **grown or declined**?
- Are there **seasonal** patterns? Summer versus winter, start of year, holiday season

> "This historical view helps you identify enduring creative principles versus temporary trends."

### 7. Conclusions and quick wins

Four questions:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Immediate iteration opportunities** | Which top performers should you vary right away? |
| **Creative gaps** | Which formats or angles are underrepresented but showing promise? |
| **Red flags** | What is consistently underperforming and should be avoided? |
| **Low-hanging fruit** | What simple changes would improve the mix quickly? |

Then compare everything against the unicorn: does it share characteristics with recent top
performers, or is it an outlier? Is anything approaching it?

## The In-Depth Creative Audit

### Set the objectives first

- What are the brand's overall goals for digital marketing and paid social?
- Which metrics matter most to the brand?
- **What should be your North Star metric for creative success?**
- Do the current campaign objectives and optimization settings align with those goals?

⭐ **The concrete version, from the delivered session:** *"There is no point in looking at ROAS or CPA
if there is another metric like contribution margin that you should be paying attention to."*

**This alignment check comes first for a reason:** an audit that measures creative against the wrong
metric produces confident, wrong conclusions, and every step after it inherits them.

### Break performance down by element

**1. Format performance**

- Video versus image versus carousel
- **Video by length range.** ⚠️ The written script gives *0-15s, 15-30s, 30-60s, 60s+*; the delivered
  session says *10-15s* for the first band. Use the script's 0-15s, which has no gap in it
- Images: lifestyle versus product-focused versus graphic-heavy
- Carousels: by card count and structure. *"Does your audience prefer those long carousels where
  they can browse a lot of products, or is a three-slide structure usually the best?"*

**2. Creative elements**

- Text on screen versus none
- Voice-over versus creator talking to camera
- **Branded styling versus native platform aesthetic**
- Music versus no music, and music style
- Opening shot type: person, product, or problem scenario

⚠️ **Mirella states a strong claim here in the delivered session:** *"A lot of brands tend to insist
on following their brand guidelines and fonts and colours, when really the data shows that the
creatives that follow native platform aesthetic, meaning they don't look like ads and don't look
branded, tend to perform better."* **The data is not named.** Carry it as her position and settle it
from the account's own numbers, which is exactly what this comparison is for.

**3. Content structure**

- Problem-first versus solution-first
- Feature-focused versus benefit-focused
- Emotional versus rational
- Before/after demonstrations versus standalone product showcases
- Direct testimonials versus implied social proof. *"One person telling their own story versus
  creatives that show a lot of different people"*

### Audience response patterns

**Demographic:** how do age groups differ? Gender-based patterns? Geographic preferences?

> "super-native content that mimics real style or TikTok style ads will resonate and convert better
> with a younger audience. Versus slower, longer videos, more information-heavy might resonate more
> with an older"

**Funnel stage:** which approaches work for cold audiences, which for warm, and are there
significant differences between prospecting and retargeting?

### Cost-effectiveness

**Production investment versus return.** Compare high-production creative against simpler, lower-cost
approaches. Compare creator and influencer content against studio content.

> "Is investing in studio production actually worth it? Or is it better to collaborate with a lot of
> influencers and UGC creators to source lower production value content, because that's what
> performs best?"

**Creative lifespan.** Calculate the average effective lifespan by creative type. Identify what
correlates with longer-lasting performance. Determine optimal refresh rates by format and audience.

> "How long does it take to actually start hitting that creative fatigue? How long do the creatives
> averagely last on your ad account? Do static images last longer than video ads? That usually tends
> to be the case."

⭐ **Lifespan is the number most accounts never calculate and the one that makes production planning
possible.** Refresh cadence is guesswork until you know how long a format actually lasts here.

### Turn insights into action

**1. Creative strategy refinement**
- Which **3 to 5 creative principles** should guide the approach?
- What is the optimal creative mix by format and style?
- Which elements should be standardised across creative? Hooks, CTAs

**2. Creative testing plan**
- Identify **3 to 5 specific elements** to test next cycle
- Prioritise by potential impact and resource requirement
- **Develop a clear hypothesis for each**, based on audit findings

**3. Creative optimization roadmap**
- For each top performer, **2 to 3 iteration opportunities**
- A refresh schedule based on observed fatigue patterns
- Specific improvement targets for hook rate, hold rate and conversion rate

> "The purpose of this research isn't just to gather information — it's to develop a clear creative
> direction based on evidence rather than assumptions."

---

## Key takeaways, as ACA gives them

- Start with a quick performance analysis to identify immediate patterns and opportunities
- **Hook rate and hold rate are critical diagnostic metrics** that reveal attention quality
- Look for patterns across time periods to identify stable principles versus temporary trends
- **Break down performance by specific creative elements** rather than just overall results
- Translate findings into concrete strategy, testing plans and optimization roadmaps

⚠️ **The course names no thresholds for any of these metrics.** There is no "a good hook rate is X".
Step 3 of the roadmap asks you to set your own targets from your own baseline, which is the honest
answer, and it means **any threshold quoted here would be invented.**

⚠️ **ACA's numbers live in Course 402 instead.** `aca-402-key-metrics` gives thumbstop and CTR bands
with the advertiser attached. Offer those as that faculty member's figures for that one brand if a
reference point is genuinely needed; never as this course's, and never above the account's own
baseline.
