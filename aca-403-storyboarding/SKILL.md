# Course 403: Storyboarding — Bringing Concepts to Life

**Mirella Crespi**, Creative Milkshake. 13m 26s, 1 module.

> ""Welcome to where the rubber meets the road in our creative journey scriptwriting and
> storyboarding. This is the bridge between brilliant ideas and actual ads that perform."

The strategic work is done: audience identified, concepts developed, ad types selected, stakeholders
signed off. This course turns that into something a production team can shoot.

⭐ **This skill emits the storyboard.** ACA ships an eight-sheet working template and the point of
this skill is to fill it, in its real columns, so the output pastes straight into their workbook.
Describing what a storyboard contains is not the deliverable.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required: none, to explain any of it.** Ask what goes in a storyboard, or which of the two
approaches suits a job, and get an answer.

**To produce a filled storyboard, required:**

1. **The concept** — what the ad is, in a sentence
2. **The product or service** being featured
3. **The brand**, and whether creative should look **branded or native**

**Optional, and each fills real columns rather than being nice to have:**

4. Talent — who is on screen, and any casting constraints
5. Platform and placement, which sets the safe space
6. Ad type, if the brand has an established library of them
7. Disclaimers or legal requirements
8. Props, outfit, set and location constraints, or the budget that bounds them
9. Reference ads, which fill the Ad References and visual-direction columns

⚠️ **A storyboard with only the first three is still shootable, and thinner.** Fill what you have,
leave ACA's own field names in place for the rest, and say which fields are open. **Do not invent
talent, props or a location** — those are production commitments with money attached, and a
plausible invention gets bought.

⛔ **If the concept has not been approved yet, say so before storyboarding it.** This course sits
*after* sign-off in ACA's sequence. Storyboarding an unapproved concept is how a team ends up
shooting something the client had not agreed to.

## First decide which document you are making

> "Neither approach is inherently better. They serve different needs. The storyboard gives you
> precision, while the production brief allows for creative collaboration."

| **Full storyboard** when | **Creative production brief** when |
|---|---|
| Shooting in-house with your own production team | Collaborating with creators or influencers who add their own style |
| Working with external creators but needing precise control | You want the creator's authentic voice and personality |
| The brand needs multiple approval layers, legal and brand teams | You care more about the strategic concept than exact script execution |
| The visual concept is complex enough to need documentation | The content benefits from spontaneity |

⭐ **Getting this wrong is the expensive mistake.** A full storyboard handed to a creator hired for
their voice buys you a stilted read of your own script. A loose brief handed to an in-house shoot
leaves the crew inventing on the day.

**The storyboard, sheet by sheet** (below) builds the first. **The creative production brief** (below) builds the second.

## How to run it

**Decide which document you are making first**, per the section above — a full storyboard or a creative
production brief. They have different owners and different fields.

1. **Confirm the concept is approved.** ⛔ Storyboarding an unapproved concept is the most common way this
   work gets thrown away.
2. **Pick the document** using the section above.
3. **Work through the sheets in order** with **The storyboard, sheet by sheet** (below), starting at Overview. ⭐ The
   Overview's props and outfits column is the shoot-day shopping list and only exists if every concept row
   is filled.
4. **Write the scripts** using **Writing the script** (below), and run each through the four questions every script
   has to pass.
5. ⭐ **Emit the sheets as tables with ACA's exact column headers**, per the instruction in
   **The storyboard, sheet by sheet** (below). ⛔ Prose describing what a storyboard contains is not the deliverable.
6. ⚠️ **Do not reproduce the `#REF!` errors** in rows C2 to C8 of ACA's shipped file. Emit clean empty rows.
7. **Hand it over.** If step 2 chose the creator route, **The creative production brief** (below) is the document —
   a different one, not a lesser one. If it chose the full storyboard, the Overview's props and outfits
   column is what production works from.
8. **Check every shot has a shot type and a reason.** A storyboard a production team has to interpret is a
   storyboard that will be interpreted wrongly.

## Answering well

**The building blocks are the spine, not decoration.** ACA's script column is built on them: Hook,
Body, CTA in one column, and the specific block in the next. A storyboard whose Strategy column is
blank has skipped the part that makes the ad testable.

**Shot type and visual direction are for the person holding the camera.** *"Camera angles,
movements, transitions, and visual references."* "Nice shot of the product" is not a direction.

**Supers are a separate column for a reason.** On-screen text is written, timed and approved
separately from what is said, and most platforms are watched muted.

⚠️ **Write for a Grade 5 reading level.** Mirella's own instruction, and she names the tool:
*"run all your scripts through [the Hemingway App] and aim for a Grade 5 reading level or lower...
The simpler your language, the better your ads will perform."*

⭐ **The SCENE and STRATEGY columns are the structure the whole course rests on.** SCENE names the

dropdowns:

> "For hooks, we use a dropdown menu with all the hook categories... **This ensures we're
> intentionally testing different hook approaches.** For the body, another dropdown lists the
> various building blocks like problem agitation, solution introduction, benefits, and proof."

**Five hook rows is deliberate.** One concept ships with five hook options against one body, which
is what makes the shoot produce five testable ads instead of one.

Values seen in ACA's own file: hook categories *Skeptical Voice*, *Dramatize Problem*; shot types

shoot. Leave them empty in a delivered storyboard rather than inventing filenames.

### 7. Building Blocks

ACA's reference sheet, reproduced with their own descriptions. **Fifteen blocks.**

| Building Block | Description |
|---|---|
| **Attention** | Grab the viewer's attention with eye-catching/bizarre visuals and an opening statement that creates a need to learn more |
| **Interest / Intro Product** | Build curiosity in our product/service by describing it in a succinct way that maintains attention |
| **Desire** | Point at the reasons and benefits that will make the viewer want what we're offering |
| **Proof** | Testimonials, reviews, demos, endorsements, and more to convince the viewer they can trust us |
| **Action** | Directly inviting the viewer to take the action to get the solution |
| **Problem** | Call out a hot burning issue that viewers need to solve. Can we personify the root cause as a villain? |
| **Agitate** | Going into detail on the problem. Make it feel personal, visceral, emotional, and necessary to solve now |
| **Discredit** | Describe how alternative solutions to our product are unideal or cause further issues |
| **Solution** | Clearly describing or depicting how the product/service is solving a common user pain point |
| **Benefits** | List out and describe the various features and benefits from a customer-focused angle |
| **Barriers** | Addressing worries, concerns, and any reasons for reluctance holding the viewer back |
| **Scarcity** | Urging the viewer to act now by arguing the product is scarce and the opportunity may not last |
| **Offer** | Making a clear, hard to resist offer that summarizes what you get and why to get it now |
| **Credibility** | Offering reasons and evidence as to why audiences can believe what we're saying |
| **Before** | Describe how the problem affects the customer's life in a storytelling way |

⛔ **These fifteen are NOT the fourteen taught in Course 103.** That course groups its blocks
Structure / Product / Person and names them differently. **Two taxonomies, from the same programme,
and they must not be merged.** Use this list when filling this template, because the template's
dropdown is built from it. If someone is working from Course 103's list, say the names differ and
map by meaning rather than assuming a shared vocabulary.

### 8. Sales Copy Formulas

Two direct-response formulas, given side by side. Each is a sequence of the blocks above.

| **AIDPA** | **PADSA** |
|---|---|
| **A**ttention — grab attention with an opening that creates a need to learn more | **P**roblem — present the problem your viewer feels |
| **I**nterest — build curiosity in the product, succinctly | **A**gitate — detail the problem, make it personal and urgent |
| **D**esire — the reasons and benefits that make them want it | **D**iscredit — how alternative solutions are unideal |
| **P**roof — testimonials, reviews, demos, endorsements | **S**olution — how the product solves the pain point |
| **A**ction — invite them to take the action | **A**ction — invite them to take the action |

⭐ **Both end on Action, and they differ in where they start.** AIDPA opens on the product's appeal;
PADSA opens on the viewer's pain. Choosing between them is a real creative decision and it belongs
in the STRATEGY column, not left implicit.

### Producing a filled storyboard

Deliver, in this order:

1. **A header line** naming the concept, the brand, and the date
2. **The Overview row** for this concept, in the Ad Breakdown columns
3. **The Storyboard sheet** — header block, INTERNAL INFO, then the scene table with five hook
   options, the body rows and the CTA
4. **The Graphic Ads rows**, if statics are in scope
5. **The Extra Hooks rows**, if this is a Hook Factory job
6. ⛔ **An open-fields line** — every column left blank and why

⛔ **Never invent a production commitment.** Talent, props, outfit, set and location cost money and
get bought from this document. If they were not supplied, leave ACA's field name with
`⚠️ NOT SUPPLIED` beside it rather than filling it plausibly.

⚠️ **Keep ACA's column headers exactly, emoji included.** They are the join between this output and
their workbook. Renaming `🧠 STRATEGY` to "Strategy" means a paste no longer lines up.

**Offer CSV if the output is going into the spreadsheet**, one block per sheet, headers in row one.
That is the form that actually drops in.

## The creative production brief

The other path. Use it when the creator's own voice is the point.

> "This works better when: you're collaborating with content creators or influencers who add their
> own style · you want to harness the authentic voice and personality of creators · you're more
> focused on the strategic concept than exact script execution · you're creating content that
> benefits from spontaneity and authenticity."

### The eight parts

Mirella's list, in her order:

| # | What goes in |
|---|---|
| **1** | The **core concept** and strategic objective |
| **2** | **Target persona** description, with emotional drivers |
| **3** | **Key message points** that must be included |
| **4** | ⭐ **Hook suggestions — at least 3 different approaches** |
| **5** | **Brand guidelines** or style references |
| **6** | ⛔ **Creative freedom boundaries** — what is flexible versus fixed |
| **7** | **Examples of the creator's previous content** that has the right tone |
| **8** | **Clear deliverable specifications** |

### The two that carry the weight

**Hook suggestions, and Mirella singles them out:**

> "Including hook suggestions is particularly important. While you want to leverage their
> creativity, providing hook options ensures they start strong and aligned with your strategy."

⭐ **Three different *approaches*, not three phrasings of one.** The point is to give the creator a
choice of angle while guaranteeing the first three seconds are deliberate. Pull the three from
different hook categories, the same discipline the storyboard's dropdown enforces.

**Creative freedom boundaries** is the item that prevents the argument:

⛔ **Every brief has fixed elements and flexible ones, and an unstated boundary is discovered by
being crossed.** Say explicitly which of these are non-negotiable and which are the creator's call:

| Usually fixed | Usually flexible |
|---|---|
| Claims, and any regulated language | Wording and phrasing |
| The offer and the CTA action | Pacing and structure |
| Product presentation and how it is used | Setting and wardrobe |
| Legal disclaimers | Humour and tone within brand |
| Deliverable specs — length, ratio, format | The hook chosen from your three |

**Item 7 is a research task, not a formality.** Naming the creator's own previous posts that hit the
right tone tells them what you mean far faster than an adjective does. It also proves you watched
their work before hiring them, which changes the relationship.

### Why this is not a lesser document

> "This approach gives creators the foundation they need while allowing their authentic voice to
> shine through."

A brief is not a storyboard with fields missing. It is a different instrument: it specifies the
**strategy and the boundaries** and deliberately leaves the **execution** open, because the
execution is what the creator was hired for.

⚠️ **The failure mode in each direction:**

- **Brief too tight** → you have paid creator rates for a stilted reading of your script, and lost
  the authenticity you were buying
- **Brief too loose** → on-brand-adjacent content that misses the message, with no basis to ask for
  a reshoot because nothing was specified

**The boundaries item is what keeps it between the two.**

### Producing one

Deliver the eight parts in ACA's order, with:

- **Three hook options**, each from a different category, each written as the creator would say it
- **A two-column fixed / flexible table**, filled for this specific job rather than the generic one
  above
- **Named reference posts** from that creator, or ⚠️ a note that none were supplied and the tone
  reference is therefore missing
- **Deliverable specs stated as numbers** — length, aspect ratio, file format, how many variants,
  by when

⛔ **If the creator has not been chosen yet, the brief cannot be finished.** Items 7 and the tone
half of 5 depend on knowing whose feed you are writing for. Produce the rest, mark those two
`⚠️ AWAITING CREATOR`, and say so.

⚠️ **Casting and directing the creator is a different ACA course** (502, Chloe Rhys). This course
covers the document, not choosing the person or running the shoot. Where that skill is installed
alongside this one, route casting there: `aca-502-ugc-talent` for the method, and
`aca-brief-ugc-creator` for the casting call and ACA's Content Creator Brief.

⛔ **Her brief and this one are different documents, not two versions of one.** This is a *boarded*
shoot: shots are specified and the creator executes them. Hers is *self-directed*: the creator gets
prompts and finds their own words, because a word-for-word script kills the delivery. Sending a
self-directed creator this document over-specifies them, and handing a boarded crew hers leaves them
without shots.

## Writing the script

### The four questions every script has to pass

Mirella's review checklist, and it is the most portable thing in the course:

> "When reviewing your scripts, always ask:
> - Is a specific persona clearly defined?
> - Are the main problem and benefit clearly shown?
> - Would a first-time viewer immediately understand what's being offered?
> - Is the language simple enough that a 10-year-old could understand it?"

> "**If you answer 'no' to any of these questions, your concept or body needs further refinement.**"

⭐ **Note where she sends you when a script fails: back to the concept or the body, not to the
wording.** A script that fails question 2 does not have a copy problem. Rewriting the sentences on a
concept that never had a clear benefit produces a better-written unclear ad.

**Question 3 is the one to run cold.** A first-time viewer has no context, has not read the brief,
and is not paying attention. Read the script as that person, or hand it to someone who has not seen
the concept.

### Match the formula to the format

> "Remember to reference direct response copywriting formulas for structured ads, but **adjust your
> approach for more organic formats** like comedy skits, trends, and street interviews. These should
> feel conversational with room for improvisation during shoots."

| | Structured ads | Organic formats |
|---|---|---|
| Write | To a copywriting formula, AIDPA or PADSA | Conversationally, with room to improvise |
| Examples | Problem-solution, demo, testimonial | Comedy skits, trends, street interviews |
| On the day | The script is the script | The script is the shape, the talent finds the words |

⚠️ **A word-perfect script handed to a comedy skit kills it**, and an improvised brief handed to a
structured direct-response ad loses the argument the ad was supposed to make. The format decides.

### Grade 5, and the tool that checks it

> "**The Hemingway App** for testing readability. Run all your scripts through it and aim for a
> **Grade 5 reading level or lower.** It also gives you reading time, which is invaluable for timing
> your ads. **The simpler your language, the better your ads will perform.**"

⭐ **The reading-time output is the underrated half.** A script is a duration before it is words, and
reading time is the cheapest way to know whether a script fits a six-second slot before anyone books
a shoot.

**What usually pushes a script above Grade 5:** subordinate clauses, brand vocabulary the customer
does not use, and feature language where benefit language belongs. Cutting those three fixes most of
it.

### The tools she names for scaling

> "Here are the tools that help my team turn one ad script into ten."

| Tool | For |
|---|---|
| A **database of hook formulas** | Quick inspiration, and the reason the Hook Category column is a dropdown |
| **AI writing assistants** — Copy.ai, Claude, ChatGPT | Generating variations |
| **TikTok Symphony's script generator** | *"Specifically trained on platform best practices"* |
| **The Hemingway App** | Readability and reading time |

> "These tools don't replace strategic thinking, but they dramatically accelerate the execution
> process while ensuring your scripts remain easy to understand."

⚠️ **Named tools date.** Availability, pricing and access move, and TikTok Symphony in particular is
a platform feature rather than a product. The instruction that survives is the *test* — Grade 5 or
lower, and check the reading time.

### Writing into the template

The script does not live on its own. It lives in the scene table, one row per beat, and every row
carries a **STRATEGY** value naming its building block.

**So write the structure first, then the words.** Choose the blocks in sequence — AIDPA, PADSA, or
your own order — then write the line for each. A script written as prose and then chopped into rows
usually has two blocks doing the same job and one missing entirely.

**Supers are written, not extracted.** The Hook Supers and supers columns are separate from the
script because on-screen text is its own craft: shorter than the spoken line, readable in a glance,
and carrying the message when the sound is off. **Do not just copy the script into the supers
column.**

**Five hooks against one body.** The template's five hook rows exist so one shoot produces five
testable openings on one proven middle. Write all five, vary the category, and keep the body fixed.
