# Writing the script

## The four questions every script has to pass

Mirella's review checklist, and it is the most portable thing in the course:

> "When reviewing your scripts, always ask:
> - Is a specific persona clearly defined?
> - Are the main problem and benefit clearly shown?
> - Would a first-time viewer immediately understand what's being offered?
> - Is the language simple enough that a 10-year-old could understand it?"

> "**If you answer 'no' to any of these questions, your concept or body needs further refinement.**"

⭐ **Note where she sends you when a script fails: back to the concept or the body, not to the
wording.** A script that fails question 2 does not have a copy problem. Rewriting the sentences on a
concept that never had a clear benefit produces a better-written unclear ad.

**Question 3 is the one to run cold.** A first-time viewer has no context, has not read the brief,
and is not paying attention. Read the script as that person, or hand it to someone who has not seen
the concept.

## Match the formula to the format

> "Remember to reference direct response copywriting formulas for structured ads, but **adjust your
> approach for more organic formats** like comedy skits, trends, and street interviews. These should
> feel conversational with room for improvisation during shoots."

| | Structured ads | Organic formats |
|---|---|---|
| Write | To a copywriting formula, AIDPA or PADSA | Conversationally, with room to improvise |
| Examples | Problem-solution, demo, testimonial | Comedy skits, trends, street interviews |
| On the day | The script is the script | The script is the shape, the talent finds the words |

⚠️ **A word-perfect script handed to a comedy skit kills it**, and an improvised brief handed to a
structured direct-response ad loses the argument the ad was supposed to make. The format decides.

## Grade 5, and the tool that checks it

> "**The Hemingway App** for testing readability. Run all your scripts through it and aim for a
> **Grade 5 reading level or lower.** It also gives you reading time, which is invaluable for timing
> your ads. **The simpler your language, the better your ads will perform.**"

⭐ **The reading-time output is the underrated half.** A script is a duration before it is words, and
reading time is the cheapest way to know whether a script fits a six-second slot before anyone books
a shoot.

**What usually pushes a script above Grade 5:** subordinate clauses, brand vocabulary the customer
does not use, and feature language where benefit language belongs. Cutting those three fixes most of
it.

## The tools she names for scaling

> "Here are the tools that help my team turn one ad script into ten."

| Tool | For |
|---|---|
| A **database of hook formulas** | Quick inspiration, and the reason the Hook Category column is a dropdown |
| **AI writing assistants** — Copy.ai, Claude, ChatGPT | Generating variations |
| **TikTok Symphony's script generator** | *"Specifically trained on platform best practices"* |
| **The Hemingway App** | Readability and reading time |

> "These tools don't replace strategic thinking, but they dramatically accelerate the execution
> process while ensuring your scripts remain easy to understand."

⚠️ **Named tools date.** Availability, pricing and access move, and TikTok Symphony in particular is
a platform feature rather than a product. The instruction that survives is the *test* — Grade 5 or
lower, and check the reading time.

## Writing into the template

The script does not live on its own. It lives in the scene table, one row per beat, and every row
carries a **STRATEGY** value naming its building block.

**So write the structure first, then the words.** Choose the blocks in sequence — AIDPA, PADSA, or
your own order — then write the line for each. A script written as prose and then chopped into rows
usually has two blocks doing the same job and one missing entirely.

**Supers are written, not extracted.** The Hook Supers and supers columns are separate from the
script because on-screen text is its own craft: shorter than the spoken line, readable in a glance,
and carrying the message when the sound is off. **Do not just copy the script into the supers
column.**

**Five hooks against one body.** The template's five hook rows exist so one shoot produces five
testable openings on one proven middle. Write all five, vary the category, and keep the body fixed.
