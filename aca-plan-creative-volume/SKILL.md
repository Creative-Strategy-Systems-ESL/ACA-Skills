# Plan creative volume

Produces the creative pipeline for an account, from **Ad Creative Academy's Course 601** — Mirella
Crespi's answer to the question she says she is asked most:

> "One of the most common questions I hear is: **'How much creative does my ad account actually
> need?'**"

> "**The hard truth is that every account is different.** Some find a winning ad that runs profitably
> for months, while others burn through creative weekly."

⛔ **So everything here is a starting point and ACA says so repeatedly.** *"Remember, these are
starting points. Always analyze your data and adjust accordingly."* Output that reads as a
prescription has misrepresented the course.

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **Monthly ad spend.** The entire ladder is indexed on it and nothing here works without it

**Strongly wanted, because they change the answer:**

2. **CPA**, and **what a test costs to run** — the two inputs to ACA's own worked calculation. ⛔ Never
   supply either from memory; ACA's example uses $100 and ~$2,000 and those are *her example's*
   numbers, not defaults
3. **Whether performance is currently fatiguing**, since ACA's first balancing rule is to *not*
   produce creative you will not test

**Optional, and each one sharpens it:**

4. How many winners the account currently produces, and how fast they fatigue
5. Video or static, which changes what a "variation" is
6. Whether ROAS goals are aggressive or the audience is narrow — ACA says both push volume up
7. Who produces the creative, and their throughput

⛔ **Never invent a spend, a CPA or a cost per test.** This skill turns numbers into a production
commitment that someone staffs and budgets against. A guessed input produces a confident hiring plan.

## How to run it

1. **Place the spend on the ladder** and give the tier's numbers
2. ⛔ **Give both readings where ACA's two recordings disagree**, and they disagree in four places.
   Never average them, never pick one silently
3. **Say what the unit is** — concepts or finished assets. ⭐ This is the single biggest source of
   error in the whole course; see **The six tiers** (below)
4. **Run the arithmetic** if a CPA and a cost per test were supplied
5. **Apply the three balancing rules**, which can override the tier
6. **State the assumptions and what would change the answer**

## ⚠️ What this skill is not

**It is not the framework ACA ships for this.** Their *Assess Your Current Creative Needs* framework
is named in the course and **is not in this folder** — see **Provenance** (below). This is built
from the ladder and the worked example in the recordings.

**It does not design the tests**, which is `aca-602-creative-testing`, or log them, which is
`aca-track-creative-tests`.

**It does not tell you whether creative is fatiguing.** That is read from the account's numbers, and
the metrics are `aca-402-key-metrics`.

## The six tiers

> "We can establish some guidelines based on monthly ad spend to help you estimate your creative
> production needs. **Remember, these are starting points.**"

### ⛔ First: what is a "creative"?

**ACA's two recordings of this course use different units, and the difference is not small.**

| | Unit |
|---|---|
| **The production script** | *"3-4 new **creatives** per month"* |
| **The delivered recording** | *"3–4 new **creatives** per month"* |

⭐ **At $10k-25k that is 3-4 assets on one reading and 9-20 on the other.** Every tier below carries
both. **Always say which unit you are quoting**, and if the account has to commit to a number, the
concepts-plus-variations reading is the one that matches how the rest of the programme uses the word
*concept*.

⚠️ **What a variation is depends on the format**, and only the delivered recording says so: *"These
can be **hook variations** if they're videos or **ad type variations** if they're statics."*

---

### The ladder

#### Tier 1 — the smallest accounts

⛔ **The two recordings disagree on the band itself**, and by an order of magnitude:

| | Band | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| **Script** | **$0-10k/month** | *"Just one new creative per month, or simply produce as needed"* |
| **Delivered** | **"zero to a thousand"** ($0-1k) | *"one or two new creative concepts per month with three to five hook variations"* |

**Both agree on the reasoning:** *"At this spending level, **creative fatigue isn't a major
concern.**"* And: *"Sometimes you'll discover a **'big swing' concept that carries your account for
months.** At these lower budgets you're not forced to constantly refresh — just monitor performance
and be ready to pivot."*

⚠️ **The gap between $1k and $10k is unaddressed by the delivered recording.** Using the script's
band is the safer reading, since it covers the whole range.

#### Tier 2 — $10k-25k/month

> "You need a more regular system."

| | Volume |
|---|---|
| **Script** | **3-4 new creatives per month**, or new ads as older ones show fatigue |
| **Delivered** | **3–4 new creatives per month** each |

> "This is where **ongoing iterations become increasingly important.** You're spending enough to
> gather data faster, which means **your current winners may tire more quickly.**"

#### Tier 3 — $25k-50k/month

| | Volume |
|---|---|
| **Script** | *"One new creative per week — about **4-5 each month**"* |
| **Delivered** | *"new concepts every week, about **five to six each month**"* |

⚠️ **The two give different monthly totals for the same weekly cadence.** Neither is wrong; a month
is 4.3 weeks.

**Both flag the same escalation:** *"For some accounts this might need to be higher, especially if you
have **aggressive ROAS goals or a narrow audience.** Keep a watchful eye on performance data and
scale up if winning ads are few and far between."*

#### Tier 4 — $50k-100k/month

> "Creative production must be **one of your top priorities.**"

| | Volume |
|---|---|
| **Script** | **2-4 new creatives per week**, about **6-20 per month** |
| **Delivered** | **2-4 concepts per week**, about 6-20 per month, ⭐ **plus the 3-5 variations for each concept** |

**The split, from both:** ⭐ *"Approximately **40-50% of your new ads should be iterations of proven
winners**, with the other half focused on net-new angles to discover fresh audiences."*

**Why the range is so wide:** *"Different accounts see varied results depending on **product category
and average order value.**"*

#### Tier 5 — $100k-500k/month

> "Think about creative volume as **a percentage of your total ad spend allocated to testing.**"

**Allocate 10-50% of spend specifically to creative testing.**

**The split:** **30-40% completely new concepts · 60-70% iterations** of near-winners or proven hits.

⭐ **This is the tier where the ladder stops giving you a number and gives you a method instead.**
Below it, volume is a count; here it is a budget divided by a cost.

##### ⭐ ACA's worked calculation

> "If you're spending **$250k monthly** with **40% for testing**, that's **$100k**. With a **CPA of
> $100** and roughly **$2,000 per test** to identify a winner, you could test **50 new creatives
> monthly** (about **10-15 per week**)."

**The arithmetic:**

```
testing budget = monthly spend × testing allocation
tests per month = testing budget ÷ cost per test
```

⛔ **The $100 CPA does not appear in the calculation.** $100k ÷ $2,000 = 50 tests, with or without it.
She states the CPA because it is what determines the ~$2,000 cost per test in the first place — you
need enough conversions to judge a winner — but **the arithmetic that produces 50 runs on the budget
and the cost per test alone.** Anyone reproducing this needs their own cost per test, and that is the
number to ask for.

⚠️ **"About 10-15 per week" is ACA's own rounding of 50 a month**, which is closer to 11-12. Quote the
monthly figure.

#### Tier 6 — $500k-$1M+/month

> "**Both quantity and variety become even more crucial.** At this scale you're reaching a massive
> audience, so you need **'big-swing' creatives** that test entirely new angles, target new audience
> niches and explore out-of-the-box ideas. **Simultaneously**, you should constantly refine winners
> through multiple iterations."

⛔ **The two recordings give different allocations:**

| | Share of total spend on testing |
|---|---|
| **Script** | **25-50%** |
| **Delivered** | **20-50%** |

**Both note the same structure:** *"It's common to have **multiple creative teams or agencies working
side by side**, each bringing fresh perspectives to maintain both quantity and quality at scale."*

---

### ⛔ The three balancing rules, which can override the tier

> "Always let your data guide your decisions."

| Signal | What to do |
|---|---|
| **No signs of fatigue** | ⛔ *"Don't produce endless ads you won't test."* The delivered version is blunter: *"there's no point in investing in creative production and spreading your creative testing budget so thin"* |
| **Performance drops** | *"You may need more new concepts or iterations"* |
| **Scaling quickly** | *"Ramp up creative production to avoid hitting a performance plateau"* |

⭐ **Rule 1 is the one that gets ignored, and it is the one that saves money.** The ladder is a
ceiling on what is worth making, not a quota to hit. An account with a stable winner and no fatigue
does not need its tier's volume.

### The closing position

> "Whether you're spending thousands or millions, the goal remains the same: **keep your ad account
> healthy and growing with a steady flow of fresh, strategic ideas.** Test, iterate, and closely
> monitor **how many winners you're producing.** Never stop feeding the algorithm new angles to
> explore."

⭐ **"How many winners you're producing" is the metric this whole ladder should be tuned against.**
Volume is the input; hit rate is what tells you whether the input is right.

---

### Producing the plan

**Deliver:**

1. **The tier**, named with its band
2. **Concepts per month and per week**, and **variations per concept** — ⛔ with the unit stated
3. ⛔ **Both recordings' numbers wherever they differ**, labelled. Never averaged
4. **The net-new / iteration split**, where the tier gives one
5. **The testing budget**, where the tier is expressed as a percentage
6. **The test-count arithmetic**, only if a cost per test was supplied. ⛔ Otherwise say it is the
   missing input and ask for it
7. **Which balancing rule applies**, if the account's fatigue state was described
8. ⛔ **A line naming every input that was assumed rather than given**
9. ⛔ **Ask whether the current creative is actually fatiguing, before emitting any volume at all.**
   The rules above guard the *inputs*; this one guards the *premise*. Balancing rule 1 says not to
   produce creative you will not test, and it is the only rule that can zero the whole plan — but it
   only fires if somebody asked. **A pipeline produced without that question tells an account with a
   stable winner to go and spend money it does not need to spend**, which is the exact failure the
   rule exists to prevent. If the fatigue state is unknown, say so at the top of the output and state
   plainly that the numbers below are a ceiling that rule 1 may reduce to nothing.

⚠️ **If the spend sits on a boundary, give both tiers.** $50k is the top of one band and the bottom of
another, and the honest answer is a range.

⛔ **Never present the output as a commitment.** It is what ACA suggests as a starting point for an
account of this size, to be adjusted against the account's own fatigue and hit rate.

## Provenance

### Source

**Ad Creative Academy** — Course 601: Launching Ad Campaigns (600-Level). **Mirella Crespi**, Creative
Milkshake. The volume ladder is the closing section of the course.

**Two recordings back it:** a production script and the studio delivery. ⛔ **They disagree in four
places and both are carried.**

### ⛔ What ACA ships for this and is not here

**The *Assess Your Current Creative Needs* framework.** Named in both recordings:

> "The 'Assess Your Current Creative Needs' framework in your resources can help you **calculate how
> many new campaigns or ads you'll need to hit a specific revenue goal.** Just adapt it to your
> metrics and historical success rate."

⛔ **It is on ACA's tracked-download list and it is not in this library.** It is the only outstanding
gap for this course.

⚠️ **And it does something the ladder does not.** The ladder runs *spend → volume*. That framework
runs *revenue goal → volume*, using the account's own historical success rate. **This skill cannot do
that**, and should say so rather than approximating it: the missing input is the hit rate, and
nothing in the recordings supplies one.

⭐ **What is held is enough to be useful:** all six tiers, both splits, the worked calculation and the
three balancing rules. What is missing is ACA's fillable version and the goal-first direction.

### ⛔ The four places the recordings disagree

**Measured directly from both, not inferred.**

| # | | Script | Delivered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⭐ **The unit** | *"new **creatives**"* | *"new **concepts** per month with three to five variations"* |
| 2 | **Tier 1's band** | **$0-10k** | *"zero to a thousand"* ($0-1k) |
| 3 | **Tier 3's monthly total** | **4-5** per month | **5-6** per month |
| 4 | **Tier 6's testing share** | **25-50%** of spend | **20-50%** |

⭐ **Number 1 is the load-bearing one.** At $10k-25k it is the difference between 3-4 assets a month
and 9-20. Everywhere else in the ACA programme *concept* means a distinct idea that spawns several
executions, so the delivered recording's usage is the consistent one — but the script is what ACA
published as the written course, and averaging them would produce a number neither recording gives.

⚠️ **Number 2 leaves a real hole.** The delivered recording's tier 1 stops at $1k and its tier 2
starts at $10k, so accounts between them have no tier. The script's $0-10k covers the range.

⚠️ **Number 3 is not really a contradiction.** One new concept per week is 4-5 or 5-6 a month
depending on how you round 4.3 weeks.

### ⚠️ About the worked calculation

> "$250k monthly with 40% for testing, that's $100k. With a CPA of $100 and roughly $2,000 per test
> to identify a winner, you could test 50 new creatives monthly (about 10-15 per week)."

- ⛔ **The CPA is not in the arithmetic.** $100,000 ÷ $2,000 = 50. The CPA explains *why* a test costs
  about $2,000 — you need enough conversions to call a winner — but it is not an operand. Anyone
  reproducing this needs their own **cost per test**, and that is the number to ask for.
- ⚠️ **"About 10-15 per week" is loose.** 50 a month is nearer 11-12 a week.
- ⚠️ **$2,000 per test and $100 CPA are one example's figures**, given without a category or a
  platform. They are not defaults and this skill never supplies them.

### ⚠️ The scale of the source

The whole ladder rests on *"the hard truth is that every account is different"* plus ACA's experience.
**No sample size, no dataset, no vertical breakdown, and no source for any band.** The splits
(40-50%, 30-40%/60-70%, 10-50%, 20-50%) are all given as observed practice.

⭐ **The course is honest about this and repeats it three times** — *"these are starting points"*,
*"always analyze your data and adjust accordingly"*, *"always let your data guide your decisions"*.
Anything produced here should carry the same caveat, because a table of numbers reads as more
authoritative than the prose it came from.

### ⚠️ Where this meets the other courses

⛔ **Course 703 answers the same question with an incompatible framework, and neither course cites
the other.**

| | **This course (601)**, Mirella | **Course 703**, Chloe Rhys |
|---|---|---|
| **Indexed on** | ⛔ **Monthly** spend | ⛔ **Daily** spend |
| **Shape** | A six-tier lookup ladder | **Five variables** you derive from: spend level, brand maturity, team bandwidth, testing structure, goal type |
| **Unit** | Creatives, or concepts with 3-5 variations | **Concepts**, each in its own ad set, × 3-6 Variants |
| **Worked case** | $250k/mo × 40% ÷ $2,000 per test = 50 tests/month | One brand at **9-12 Concepts/week**, **50-70 ads/week** |

⛔ **The time base is the trap.** $10,000/day is roughly $300,000/month — this course's tier 5. That
tier derives ~50 tests a month; Course 703 puts a comparable account at 50-70 **ads per week**. They
are counting different objects, and nothing in either course says so.

⭐ **Where they independently agree, and it is striking.** This course says *"roughly $2,000 per test
to identify a winner"* alongside a $100 CPA. Course 703 says a win must hold through *"at least 20×
your CPA"*. **20 × $100 = $2,000.** Two faculty, no cross-reference, the same threshold — and 703's
form is the more useful one because it scales with the account.

⚠️ **When both are available, prefer 703's five variables.** They include team capacity and campaign
structure, which are what actually cap output and which a spend-indexed ladder cannot see. Use this
ladder as the sanity check, not the instrument. `aca-703-sprint-planning` carries it.

**Course 602 argues the same thing from the other end.** Carlotta reports a correlation between spend
and the number of creatives launched, and cites Meta research on tests per year. This course turns
that into an operating table. ⚠️ **Neither cites the other**, and 602's *15 tests a year* floor is far
below anything on this ladder — because it counts formal experiments and this ladder counts creative
produced. **Do not compare the two numbers directly.**

**Fatigue detection is not here.** The ladder tells you what to make; whether the current creative is
fatiguing is read from the account, and the metrics live in Course 402.
