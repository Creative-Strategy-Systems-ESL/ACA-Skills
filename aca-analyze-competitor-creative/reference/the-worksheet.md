# The Competitor Creative Analysis Worksheet

One per competitor. ACA's guidance is a set of **5 to 6**, chosen across price points rather than
only direct rivals.

⚠️ **ACA's own section numbering is broken.** Two consecutive sections are both labelled "SECTION 1"
(Competitor Overview, then Creative Volume & Budget Indicators) before it continues to SECTION 2.
This file numbers them 1 to 8, which is a renumbering rather than a change to the content.

---

## 1. Competitor overview

Competitor name · analysis date · brand positioning · target audience (if known) · primary platforms.

⭐ **"Analysis date" matters more than it looks.** Ad libraries show what is live now. A worksheet
without a date is unreadable in three months, and this is a document explicitly meant to be revisited
quarterly.

## 2. Creative volume and budget indicators

**Active creative count** — number of active ads observed, and the platforms they run on: Meta
(Facebook/Instagram) · TikTok · YouTube · Google Search/Display · other.

**Testing velocity**, as a four-way choice:

| Launch frequency | ACA's paired spend read |
|---|---|
| Multiple new ads weekly | **Very high** — extensive testing, premium placements |
| Weekly new creative | **High** — regular testing across multiple platforms |
| Bi-weekly new creative | **Moderate** — focused testing on 1-2 platforms |
| Monthly or less frequent | **Low** — minimal testing, infrequent new creative |

Plus free-text notes on volume and budget strategy.

⚠️ **This table infers spend from creative volume, and that inference is loose.** A brand can run few
ads on a large budget, or many ads cheaply. Volume tells you about **testing philosophy** reliably
and about **budget** only weakly. Report it as the former.

> "A brand running 50+ variations is probably doing systematic testing and has significant budget."

## 3. Format strategy

Percentage distribution, broken out per platform:

| Platform | Fields |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | Video % (and typical length range) · static image % · carousel % (and typical card count) · collection/instant experience % |
| **TikTok** | Native-style TikTok % (typical length) · repurposed content % (typical length) · other formats |
| **YouTube** | Skippable in-stream % · non-skippable % · bumper ads % |

⭐ **The TikTok split is the most diagnostic line in the whole worksheet.** *Native-style* versus
*repurposed* separates brands that make for the platform from brands that resize for it. A competitor
running mostly repurposed content on TikTok has left an opening that costs nothing to take.

## 4. Creative execution

Which hook styles appear most · editing styles and pacing · visual aesthetic · text overlays and
captions · **messaging themes and value propositions that repeat**.

> "This is where you'll find their creative 'formula'."

## 5. Talent approach

Who delivers the message — founders, paid creators, customers? What demographics? Consistent talent
or testing presenter types?

> "Really try to pay attention to what personas they seem to be speaking to."

## 6. Temporal patterns

How the creative has evolved · sticking with what works or constantly innovating · seasonal shifts ·
recent pivots.

> "Do you notice that during holiday season, Black Friday, Q1, they completely roll out a new set of
> creatives with new messaging?"

⭐ **Two timing signals do most of the work here**, and both are free to read:

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| **Ads launched the same day** | A creative test. They were putting these against each other |
| **Repetition with variation** | The strongest available signal. Same hook structure, different visuals or products, means they are scaling something they trust |

⚠️ **Long run length alone is weaker than either**, because an ad can run long through neglect,
contract, or an evergreen brand slot nobody reviews.

## 7. Creative swipe file

Where the actual examples live. **Capture the ad, not a description of it** — screenshots or links,
because ads come down and half of them will be gone when you revisit this.

## 8. Strategic insights and action items

The output section. See `reference/the-presentation.md` for the five moves that fill it.

## Where to pull the ads

| Source | What it gives | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta Ad Library** | Free and comprehensive. Active ad counts, how long each has run, formats, how hooks evolve | The best single source for this worksheet |
| **TikTok Creative Center** | Trending formats, hook styles, talent use. Filter by industry and objective | ⚠️ Top Ads are *"often sorted by engagement, not by actual business results"* |
| **Google Ads Transparency Center** | Any brand's full YouTube ad history: what is running, when it started, which regions | ⭐ *"If a competitor is investing heavily in YouTube, they're usually playing a longer game with higher customer values"* |
| **Ad intelligence platforms** | Spend levels (aduniverse.ai), or organising and tagging over time (Foreplay, Motion) | Paid. The worksheet fills without them |

**What tagging over time reveals**, if you use a platform for it:

| Pattern | Means |
|---|---|
| Small tweaks of the same core creative | Optimization |
| Constantly different approaches | Exploration |
| Certain angles returning in new formats | Scalable winners |

## Filling it honestly

⛔ **Separate observed from inferred, in the document.** Roughly:

| Observed | Inferred |
|---|---|
| Number of active ads, start dates, formats, who is on screen, what the copy says | Budget level, testing philosophy, what is working, why they pivoted |

**Everything in the right-hand column is a hypothesis about a business you cannot see inside.** Mark
it `⚠️ inferred` in the cell. A competitor analysis gets excerpted into a pitch deck, and that is
where an inference quietly becomes a fact.

⚠️ **Percentages need a denominator.** "60% video" from a sample of five ads is not a format strategy.
Record how many ads the estimate is based on, in the section.

**A note on what you cannot see at all:** landing pages, offers, retargeting sequences, email, and
every ad that was turned off before you looked. The worksheet describes the visible surface of the
creative strategy, which is not the same as the strategy.
