# Analyse competitor creative

**Ad Creative Academy**, Course 302 (Mirella Crespi), module 3.

> "We're not looking at competitors to copy them. We're investigating to get inspired and find gaps
> in the market that we can own. **Big difference.**"

## What this skill needs from you

**Required:**

1. **The brand** this analysis is for, and what it sells
2. **A competitor set**, or enough about the brand to build one. ACA's own guidance is 5 to 6
3. **The ads**, or access to them. Public ad libraries are free and are where this runs

**Optional, and each sharpens it:**

4. Your own brand's current creative, so the output is a comparison rather than a description
5. Which platforms matter, if not all of them
6. Whether this is for internal use or a client presentation, which decides whether slide 5 matters
7. Any known positioning or price differences across the set

⚠️ **Analysis without seeing the ads is not analysis.** If no ads or library links are supplied, say
which libraries to pull from (Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, Google Ads Transparency
Center) and what to capture. A worksheet filled from general knowledge of a brand is a description of
its reputation, not of its creative.

## ⛔ The rule that governs every reading here

> "**Just because an ad is running doesn't mean it's working.** I can't tell you how many times I've
> seen brands dump money into campaigns that are absolute duds simply because they had the budget to
> keep them going."

> "You never know what their conversion objective is or what their CPA goal is."

**Everything visible from outside is a signal about budget and attention, never about
profitability.** You cannot see their target, their margin, or whether anyone is watching the
account.

⛔ **So every inference in the output has to be labelled as one.** "They are testing heavily" is
observable. "This is working for them" is not, and stating it is the single most common way this
analysis misleads the person who commissioned it.

## How to run it

1. **One worksheet per competitor.** **The Competitor Creative Analysis Worksheet** (below). Do not merge them.
2. **Work the sections in order** — overview, volume and budget, format, execution, temporal.
3. **Then step back across the set**, which is where the actual value is. A single competitor
   worksheet tells you little; the pattern across five tells you what the category believes.
4. **Turn it into position** using the five strategic moves.
5. **Build the deck** if it is going to a client. **The five-slide presentation, and turning findings into position** (below).

## Answering well

**The gap is the deliverable, not the inventory.** Five filled worksheets are input. The output is
white space: *"angles, hooks, or claims that nobody in your space is making."*

**Read timing, not just creative.** Ads launched the same day are a test cohort. An ad that has run
for months is a candidate winner, subject to the warning above. This is the closest thing to
performance data available from outside.

⭐ **Repetition with variation is the strongest signal you can see.** *"If you see a competitor using
the same hook structure but with different visuals or products, they've probably found a winner
they're scaling."* It is behavioural: it is what a brand does when it has something it does not want
to lose. Rate it above raw run length.

**Elevate rather than copy.** *"When you see an angle that's clearly working, don't just copy it —
elevate it."* Operationally, and in Mirella's own words: *"make it more authentic, add stronger
social proof, connect it more with a unique value proposition, agitate the problem more."*

⚠️ **Engagement is not conversion.** TikTok Creative Center's Top Ads are *"often sorted by
engagement, not by actual business results."* And: *"when an ad has tons of likes but few substantive
comments, be skeptical. But when you see comments like 'Just ordered!' or 'This solved my exact
problem!' — that's a much stronger signal."*

**Set a cadence.** *"Spend an hour every month refreshing your competitive analysis."* An hour a
month is the whole ask, and it is worth saying to anyone treating this as a quarterly project they
never start.

## ⚠️ This is not Course 301's job

**301 reverse-engineers one winning ad into a reusable structure. This maps the landscape.** If the
question is "how do I make my own version of this specific ad", that is the other course. If it is
"who are we up against and where is the gap", it is this one.

## The Competitor Creative Analysis Worksheet

One per competitor. ACA's guidance is a set of **5 to 6**, chosen across price points rather than
only direct rivals.

⚠️ **ACA's own section numbering is broken.** Two consecutive sections are both labelled "SECTION 1"
(Competitor Overview, then Creative Volume & Budget Indicators) before it continues to SECTION 2.
This file numbers them 1 to 8, which is a renumbering rather than a change to the content.

---

### 1. Competitor overview

Competitor name · analysis date · brand positioning · target audience (if known) · primary platforms.

⭐ **"Analysis date" matters more than it looks.** Ad libraries show what is live now. A worksheet
without a date is unreadable in three months, and this is a document explicitly meant to be revisited
quarterly.

### 2. Creative volume and budget indicators

**Active creative count** — number of active ads observed, and the platforms they run on: Meta
(Facebook/Instagram) · TikTok · YouTube · Google Search/Display · other.

**Testing velocity**, as a four-way choice:

| Launch frequency | ACA's paired spend read |
|---|---|
| Multiple new ads weekly | **Very high** — extensive testing, premium placements |
| Weekly new creative | **High** — regular testing across multiple platforms |
| Bi-weekly new creative | **Moderate** — focused testing on 1-2 platforms |
| Monthly or less frequent | **Low** — minimal testing, infrequent new creative |

Plus free-text notes on volume and budget strategy.

⚠️ **This table infers spend from creative volume, and that inference is loose.** A brand can run few
ads on a large budget, or many ads cheaply. Volume tells you about **testing philosophy** reliably
and about **budget** only weakly. Report it as the former.

> "A brand running 50+ variations is probably doing systematic testing and has significant budget."

### 3. Format strategy

Percentage distribution, broken out per platform:

| Platform | Fields |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | Video % (and typical length range) · static image % · carousel % (and typical card count) · collection/instant experience % |
| **TikTok** | Native-style TikTok % (typical length) · repurposed content % (typical length) · other formats |
| **YouTube** | Skippable in-stream % · non-skippable % · bumper ads % |

⭐ **The TikTok split is the most diagnostic line in the whole worksheet.** *Native-style* versus
*repurposed* separates brands that make for the platform from brands that resize for it. A competitor
running mostly repurposed content on TikTok has left an opening that costs nothing to take.

### 4. Creative execution

Which hook styles appear most · editing styles and pacing · visual aesthetic · text overlays and
captions · **messaging themes and value propositions that repeat**.

> "This is where you'll find their creative 'formula'."

### 5. Talent approach

Who delivers the message — founders, paid creators, customers? What demographics? Consistent talent
or testing presenter types?

> "Really try to pay attention to what personas they seem to be speaking to."

### 6. Temporal patterns

How the creative has evolved · sticking with what works or constantly innovating · seasonal shifts ·
recent pivots.

> "Do you notice that during holiday season, Black Friday, Q1, they completely roll out a new set of
> creatives with new messaging?"

⭐ **Two timing signals do most of the work here**, and both are free to read:

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| **Ads launched the same day** | A creative test. They were putting these against each other |
| **Repetition with variation** | The strongest available signal. Same hook structure, different visuals or products, means they are scaling something they trust |

⚠️ **Long run length alone is weaker than either**, because an ad can run long through neglect,
contract, or an evergreen brand slot nobody reviews.

### 7. Creative swipe file

Where the actual examples live. **Capture the ad, not a description of it** — screenshots or links,
because ads come down and half of them will be gone when you revisit this.

### 8. Strategic insights and action items

The output section. See **The five-slide presentation, and turning findings into position** (below) for the five moves that fill it.

---

### Where to pull the ads

| Source | What it gives | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta Ad Library** | Free and comprehensive. Active ad counts, how long each has run, formats, how hooks evolve | The best single source for this worksheet |
| **TikTok Creative Center** | Trending formats, hook styles, talent use. Filter by industry and objective | ⚠️ Top Ads are *"often sorted by engagement, not by actual business results"* |
| **Google Ads Transparency Center** | Any brand's full YouTube ad history: what is running, when it started, which regions | ⭐ *"If a competitor is investing heavily in YouTube, they're usually playing a longer game with higher customer values"* |
| **Ad intelligence platforms** | Spend levels (aduniverse.ai), or organising and tagging over time (Foreplay, Motion) | Paid. The worksheet fills without them |

**What tagging over time reveals**, if you use a platform for it:

| Pattern | Means |
|---|---|
| Small tweaks of the same core creative | Optimization |
| Constantly different approaches | Exploration |
| Certain angles returning in new formats | Scalable winners |

---

### Filling it honestly

⛔ **Separate observed from inferred, in the document.** Roughly:

| Observed | Inferred |
|---|---|
| Number of active ads, start dates, formats, who is on screen, what the copy says | Budget level, testing philosophy, what is working, why they pivoted |

**Everything in the right-hand column is a hypothesis about a business you cannot see inside.** Mark
it `⚠️ inferred` in the cell. A competitor analysis gets excerpted into a pitch deck, and that is
where an inference quietly becomes a fact.

⚠️ **Percentages need a denominator.** "60% video" from a sample of five ads is not a format strategy.
Record how many ads the estimate is based on, in the section.

**A note on what you cannot see at all:** landing pages, offers, retargeting sequences, email, and
every ad that was turned off before you looked. The worksheet describes the visible surface of the
creative strategy, which is not the same as the strategy.

## The five-slide presentation, and turning findings into position

> "A simple 5-slide presentation template... perfect for when you need to share your competitor
> findings with clients or team members."

### The deck

| Slide | Contents |
|---|---|
| **1. Cover and scope** | The competitor list · **time period covered** · platforms examined |
| **2. Competitors 1 and 2** | Side by side: formats · angles · messaging · talent |
| **3. Competitors 3 and 4** | Same four rows |
| **4. Ad examples** | The creative itself, with the analysis attached |
| **5. Key patterns across competitors, and creative approaches** | Including **cross-category inspiration** |

**The four rows repeated on every competitor slide:**

| Row | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Formats** | Primary ad formats used — video, carousel, static |
| **Angles** | Main selling angles and approaches |
| **Messaging** | Key value propositions and claims |
| **Talent** | Type of people featured and their demographics |

⭐ **Slide 1's "time period covered" is the honesty field.** It is what stops a reader treating a
snapshot as the permanent state of the category, and it is the field most likely to be skipped.

⚠️ **Slide 5 is where the deck earns its existence.** Slides 2 to 4 are inventory. The pattern across
competitors is the finding, and a deck that stops at slide 4 has described the category without
saying anything about it.

### ⚠️ Two defects in ACA's shipped template

1. ⛔ **It contains Lorem ipsum placeholder text** on the cover slide. It will be presented to a
   client by someone who did not notice. **Delete it.**
2. **Its competitor slides are labelled "Competitor 1 & Competitor 2" then "Competitor 3 &
   Competitor 5"** — there is no 4. Renumber when filling it.

⚠️ **The template holds four competitor slots across two slides**, while the course recommends
studying 5 to 6. Add a slide rather than dropping competitors.

---

### The five strategic moves

The section the whole analysis exists to produce. Only the first two are about differentiating; the
rest are about borrowing well.

#### 1. Identify white space

> "Look for angles, hooks, or claims that nobody in your space is making. Sometimes the most powerful
> position is the one everyone else has overlooked."

**How to actually find it:** list every angle observed across the set, then list the angles the
category *could* use and does not. The second list is the deliverable.

⚠️ **An empty space is not automatically an opportunity.** Sometimes nobody makes a claim because it
does not work, is not credible, or is not legal in the category. **Say which reading you think
applies**, because "nobody is doing X" presented as a recommendation is how a client ends up testing
something four competitors already abandoned.

#### 2. Differentiate your positioning

> "If all your competitors are pushing 'affordable', maybe you emphasize 'premium quality'. If
> they're all about 'quick results', perhaps your angle is 'sustainable, long-term success'."

#### 3. Elevate proven approaches

> "When you see an angle that's clearly working, don't just copy it — elevate it."

⭐ **The four concrete moves**, from the delivered session:

- Make it **more authentic**
- Add **stronger social proof**
- Connect it more to **your unique value proposition**
- **Agitate the problem more**

That is what "elevate" means operationally, and it is the difference between this step and copying.

#### 4. Import cross-category inspiration

> "A B2B SaaS company might learn from how DTC fashion brands create urgency."

The method: describe the customer in one sentence that names no product category, then ask what else
that person spends money on.

⚠️ **ACA ships a dedicated prompt for this**, the Cross-Vertical Creative Inspiration Finder. Its
tracked-download list files it under Course 230, and this module names it as its own third resource,
so it is shared between the two. Where a skill for it is installed alongside this one, route there
rather than paraphrasing.

#### 5. Track creative evolution

> "If everyone suddenly shifts to a new hook style or claim, there's probably a reason — either the
> market is changing or they've all discovered something that works."

**This is the move that requires the cadence.** It only exists across repeated passes, which is why
the analysis date on each worksheet matters.

---

### Producing the deck

1. **Scope slide first** — competitors, period, platforms, and how many ads the analysis saw
2. **One row per competitor per dimension**, kept parallel so they can be read across
3. **Real ad examples**, captured rather than described
4. **The patterns slide**, with white space stated as a list
5. ⛔ **A limitations line.** What could not be seen: landing pages, offers, retargeting, spend,
   objectives, and every ad turned off before you looked

⚠️ **Mark inferences on the slide, not in the speaker notes.** A deck outlives its presenter. *"High
testing velocity"* is observed; `they are winning with UGC` is a guess about a business whose
numbers you do not have, and it needs to say so where a reader will see it.

> "The goal isn't to become a carbon copy of your competitors. It's to understand the landscape so
> you can carve out your own distinctive place within it — or disrupt it entirely."

### ⛔ The claim to carry into every use of this

> "Just because an ad is running doesn't mean it's working."

> "You never know what their conversion objective is or what their CPA goal is."

**This is Mirella's own myth-bust and it is the most commonly ignored line in the course.** Long
run length is evidence of budget and attention, never of profitability.

⚠️ **ACA's Course 301 leans the other way**, treating an ad that ran four months as *"your winner,
that's the pattern to study."* Both are defensible: 301 reads run length as the best signal available
from outside, and 302 warns the signal is weak. **Carry the tension rather than picking a side.**

### ⚠️ How old this material is

ACA stamps no date. Received into this library in **2026-W31**, a "no later than" rather than a
publication date.

⛔ **The ad libraries date fastest and they are load-bearing.** The Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative
