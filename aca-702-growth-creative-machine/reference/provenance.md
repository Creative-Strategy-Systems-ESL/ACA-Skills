## ⚠️ Every number here is unsourced

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| **50-100 new ads per week**, including iterations | ⛔ Attributed to *"the fastest growing performance advertisers"*. **No spend level, team size or category attached** |
| **Only 10% of ads last more than two weeks** | ⛔ *"Listen to this staggering stat"* — no source, no dataset, no platform |

⛔ **The 50-100 figure is the one most likely to be misused**, because it is memorable and has no
conditions. **It is not a target.** Course 703 derives volume from five variables including team
capacity; Course 601 indexes it on spend. Both are instruments; this is a description of what fast
growers do.

⚠️ **The 10% shelf-life stat is the load-bearing evidence for the whole volume argument**, and it is
the least supported thing in the course. ⭐ **Its nearest corroboration in the programme** is Course
602's *"on average, a new creative only performs at its peak for a few weeks"* — also unsourced, from
a different faculty member, which is weak agreement rather than confirmation.

## ⚠️ The parable

The photography-class story is from **Art & Fear** by David Bayles and Ted Orland, reaching Udi via
**Atomic Habits**. ⚠️ **It is widely retold and its details vary between tellings**, including whether
the professor's name and the grading scheme are as described. **It is a parable making an argument,
not a study**, and this skill carries it as one.

⭐ **It is still the most persuasive thing in the 700-Level**, because it names the actual failure
mode: the quality group failed by *planning*, which is what a team producing four polished ads a
month believes it is doing right.

## ⭐ Where this course quietly agrees with others

- **Its velocity example puts the sound first**, then builds visuals on it — which is exactly Course
  401's sound-first method, arrived at independently and never named as such here.
- **Its "identify the winning element and multiply it" decision tree is Course 703's MORE request**,
  described in different words by a different faculty member.
- **Its versions argument is Course 704's creative diversity argument**, made philosophically rather
  than mechanically.

⚠️ **None of these courses cites any other.** The agreement is real and it is unacknowledged, which
is worth more than a cross-reference would be: three people reached the same conclusion separately.

**Everything substantive is Udi's, quoted directly** — the parable, the three-ad demonstration, all
three parts and the three worked brand examples.

**This skill's organisation, not ACA's:** the two-file split, rendering the three ads and the three
winning-element examples as tables, and separating the argument from the numbers.

**This skill's additions, drawn from the material rather than stated by it:**

- ⛔ **The warning that the 50-100 figure has no conditions**, and that a real number comes from
  Course 703 or 601.
- ⭐ **The observation that the quality group failed by planning**, not by low standards — which is
  what makes the parable land on a team that thinks it is being rigorous.
- ⭐ **The point that both halves of the three-ad demonstration argue the same thing**: the winner was
  not predicted *and* was not born finished.
- ⚠️ **The consequence for creative review** — if experts cannot predict winners, a review that kills
  concepts on taste removes options from a process whose value is having options. Its job is
  compliance and brand safety instead. Udi never draws this out.
- ⭐ **The note that the three winning-element examples are deliberately different sizes** — a hook, a
  person, a script — which is what makes "identify the winning element" actionable.
